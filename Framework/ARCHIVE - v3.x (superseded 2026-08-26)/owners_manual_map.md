## The 13 principles mapped

### OM-1 — Partnership attitude
> "Although our form is corporate, our attitude is partnership."
> "…visualize yourself as a part owner of a business that you expect to stay with
> indefinitely, much as you might if you owned a farm or apartment house in partnership
> with members of your family."
> "If we have good long-term expectations, short-term price changes are meaningless for us
> except to the extent they offer us an opportunity to increase our ownership at an
> attractive price."
**Maps to**: Introduction (framework philosophy); Gate 7 behavioral rule ("Price
indifference is the behavioral test of the framework"; "NEVER sell purely on price decline").
**Coverage**: STRONG. This is the philosophical root of the whole framework; v3.0 should
open from it.

### OM-2 — Eat our own cooking
> "…most of our directors have a significant portion of their net worth invested in the
> company. We eat our own cooking."
> "We want to make money only when our partners do and in exactly the same proportion."
**Maps to**: Gate 3, Dimension 3 (Alignment: "significant personal wealth tied to long-term
equity — shares held, not options").
**Coverage**: STRONG and directly citable — better sourcing for Gate 3 alignment than the
framework currently uses.

### OM-3 — Maximize per-share intrinsic value gain
> "Our long-term economic goal… is to maximize Berkshire's average annual rate of gain in
> intrinsic business value on a per-share basis. We do not measure the economic significance
> or performance of Berkshire by its size; we measure by per-share progress."
**Maps to**: Gate 3, Dimension 2 (capital-allocation competence test); Gate 6 (per-share IV
as the valuation object).
**Coverage**: PARTIAL. The framework values businesses per-share but never states the
per-share-not-size test as a management-evaluation criterion. Candidate addition to Gate 3.

### OM-4 — Direct ownership preferred; depressed markets are good news
> "Our preference would be to reach our goal by directly owning a diversified group of
> businesses that generate cash and consistently earn above-average returns on capital."
> "So when the market plummets � as it will from time to time � neither panic nor mourn.
> It's good news for Berkshire."
**Maps to**: Gate 4 (above-average returns on capital as the quality bar); Gate 7 (buyer's
attitude toward declines).
**Coverage**: STRONG on both points.

### OM-5 — Consolidated numbers reveal little; segment reporting matters
> "…consolidated reported earnings may reveal relatively little about our true economic
> performance. Charlie and I, both as owners and managers, virtually ignore such
> consolidated numbers."
**Maps to**: Gate 2 rule ("Never assess a conglomerate as a single moat. Evaluate each
business segment independently."); Gate 4 (distrust of headline GAAP aggregates).
**Coverage**: STRONG — this is the primary-source anchor for the segment-level rule, which
the current framework asserts without citation.

### OM-6 — Accounting consequences don't drive decisions; look-through earnings
> "Accounting consequences do not influence our operating or capital-allocation decisions."
> "…we much prefer to purchase $2 of earnings that is not reportable by us under standard
> accounting principles than to purchase $1 of earnings that is reportable."
> "We consequently regard look-through earnings as realistically portraying our yearly gain
> from operations."
**Maps to**: Gate 4 (economic vs. accounting earnings).
**Coverage**: GAP. Look-through earnings appears NOWHERE in the current framework despite
being a priority search in Phase 2. v3.0 Gate 4 should carry it (it is the shareholder-side
analog of owner earnings for partial ownership).

### OM-7 — Debt used sparingly; float
> "We use debt sparingly. We will reject interesting opportunities rather than over-leverage
> our balance sheet."
> "(As one of the Indianapolis '500' winners said: 'To finish first, you must first
> finish.')"
> "…deferred taxes and 'float'… give us the benefit of debt � an ability to have more assets
> working for us � but saddle us with none of its drawbacks."
**Maps to**: Gate 4 checklist ("Is the balance sheet a fortress…"; survival through a bad
cycle).
**Coverage**: PARTIAL. Fortress balance sheet is covered; the float concept (a Phase 2
priority search) is absent — arguably out of scope for a stock-analysis framework except
when analyzing insurers. Note for Phase 3: a sidebar, not a gate change.

### OM-8 — No wish-list acquisitions at shareholder expense
> "A managerial 'wish list' will not be filled at shareholder expense."
> "Charlie and I are interested only in acquisitions that we believe will raise the
> per-share intrinsic value of Berkshire's stock."
**Maps to**: Gate 3, Dimension 2 ("the ability to say no to acquisitions and buybacks at
bad prices").
**Coverage**: STRONG and directly citable.

### OM-9 — The $1 retained / $1 market value test (with the 2009 self-correction)
> "We test the wisdom of retaining earnings by assessing whether retention, over time,
> delivers shareholders at least $1 of market value for each $1 retained."
> Corrected form: "(1) during the period did our book-value gain exceed the performance of
> the S&P; and (2) did our stock consistently sell at a premium to book…"
**Maps to**: Gate 3, Dimension 2 (capital-allocation record).
**Coverage**: GAP. The retention test — the single most operational management-competence
test Buffett ever published — is absent from the framework. Candidate addition to Gate 3.
Bonus: the passage is itself a model of confessing an error (supports Gate 3's candor
standard) — Buffett publicly corrected his own 1983 formulation in this document.

### OM-10 — Issue shares only for equal value
> "We will issue common stock only when we receive as much in business value as we give."
> "Owners unfairly lose if their managers deliberately sell assets for 80� that in fact are
> worth $1."
**Maps to**: Gate 4 (SBC-as-real-cost / dilution discipline); Gate 3 alignment.
**Coverage**: PARTIAL. The framework treats dilution via the 15% SBC rule [the threshold
itself is a suspected convention — Phase 1 target]; the issuance-for-value principle is the
proper primary-source ancestor.

### OM-11 — Reluctance to sell; no gin rummy
> "Regardless of price, we have no interest at all in selling any good businesses that
> Berkshire owns."
> "…gin rummy managerial behavior (discard your least promising business at each turn) is
> not our style."
> "To clean up some confusion voiced in 2016, we emphasize that the comments here refer to
> businesses we control, not to marketable securities."
**Maps to**: Gate 7 sell discipline.
**Coverage**: STRONG — but with a CAUTION the current framework ignores: the OM itself
says this principle applies to CONTROLLED businesses, not marketable securities. Berkshire
demonstrably sells stocks. v3.0 must not cite OM-11 as authority for never selling shares;
Gate 7's actual authority is the cost-of-activity line (Munger, Wesco 1999 — verify in
Phase 1/2) and Buffett's competitive-position monitoring standard.

### OM-12 — Candor
> "We will be candid in our reporting to you… Our guideline is to tell you the business
> facts that we would want to know if our positions were reversed. We owe you no less."
> "The CEO who misleads others in public may eventually mislead himself in private."
> "At Berkshire you will find no 'big bath' accounting maneuvers or restructurings nor any
> 'smoothing' of quarterly or annual results."
**Maps to**: Gate 3, Dimension 1 (Integrity — the candor test), including the specific
red-flag list (big-bath, smoothing).
**Coverage**: STRONG — this is the definitive candor source. The current framework cites
"the 1989 letter" for the candor test; the OM statement is cleaner and self-contained.
Phase 1 must check what the 1989 letter actually says.

### OM-13 — Silence on marketable securities
> "…we will discuss our activities in marketable securities only to the extent legally
> required. Good investment ideas are rare, valuable and subject to competitive
> appropriation…"
**Maps to**: Gate 1 (the informational-edge question — good ideas are rare and worth
protecting) — loosely.
**Coverage**: CONTEXT ONLY. Berkshire-specific disclosure policy; no gate change warranted.

## The two added principles

### OM-14 — Price tracking intrinsic value; fair over high
> "…we would rather see Berkshire's stock price at a fair level than a high level."
> "Our it's-as-bad-to-be-overvalued-as-to-be-undervalued approach…"
**Maps to**: Gate 7 ("Price exceeds intrinsic value by more than 20% → begin reducing");
Gate 6 fair-price philosophy [FA-Jul26: the Statute's "wonderful-at-fair" book].
**Coverage**: PARTIAL — good support for the overvaluation-reduction trigger.

### OM-15 — The S&P 500 yardstick
> "We regularly compare the gain in Berkshire's per-share book value to the performance of
> the S&P 500. Over time, we hope to outpace this yardstick. Otherwise, why do our investors
> need us?"
**Maps to**: nothing in the current framework.
**Coverage**: GAP (minor). An honest self-benchmarking discipline for the portfolio level;
candidate for the Workbook's position log / Appendix B, not a gate.

## The Intrinsic Value section (not numbered, but load-bearing)

> "Intrinsic value can be defined simply: It is the discounted value of the cash that can
> be taken out of a business during its remaining life."
> "…intrinsic value is an estimate rather than a precise figure, and it is additionally an
> estimate that must be changed if interest rates move or forecasts of future cash flows
> are revised."
> "Two people looking at the same set of facts… will almost inevitably come up with at
> least slightly different intrinsic value figures."
**Maps to**: Gate 6 (the DCF's primary-source foundation — this is Buffett's own published
definition, superior to citing Graham 1949 for the DCF concept); Gate 6 Step 6 sensitivity
grid (estimate-not-precise); [FA-Jul26] "sovereign rate floats" — the OM's "must be changed
if interest rates move" is the direct primary-source support for the rate-refresh rule.
**Coverage**: STRONG — and currently uncited by the framework, which is a striking miss:
the framework's Gate 6 epigraph reaches for Graham and a 1994 meeting quote while Buffett's
own canonical definition sits in the OM.

## Reverse map — gates with NO Owner's Manual grounding
- **Gate 1 (Circle of Competence)** — not an OM concept; sources are the 1996 letter and
  Munger's USC talk (verify both in Phase 1).
- **Gate 2 (Moat taxonomy)** — OM supports segment-level analysis (OM-5) but the
  four-type taxonomy is Morningstar/Dorsey, already half-confessed in the current text.
  Phase 1 must decide: confess fully as FRAMEWORK CONVENTION.
- **Gate 5 (Inversion)** — pure Munger; no OM analog.
- **Gate 8 (Reverse DCF)** — already confessed as "not a Buffett or Munger original."
- **Gate 6 mechanics** (fade structures, explicit-period lengths, 20%/30% MOS numbers,
  terminal growth 2%/3%, 10.5% standard rate) — no OM support; prime Phase 1 targets for
  the CONVENTION label.
