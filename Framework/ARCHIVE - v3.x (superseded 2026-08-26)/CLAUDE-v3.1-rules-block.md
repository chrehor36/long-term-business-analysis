## THE 2026-08-01 RULINGS — READ BEFORE ANY COMPANY RUN
These came out of running TJX, DOV, PNC, CMI, SBUX and CCB through the framework
on 2026-07-31/08-01. Full text and citations: Framework/PENDING RULINGS.md.

**THE GOVERNING PRINCIPLE (Ruling 4-C, point 4): the corpus is the CEILING AND
THE FLOOR.** Two symmetrical defects, both real, both found the same day:
- A convention SOFTER than the corpus is a defect — it lets the framework buy
  things Buffett would not. (Found in Ruling 4's discount spread, and in the
  margin of safety.)
- A convention STRICTER than the corpus is ALSO a defect where it would reject
  purchases Buffett actually made. (Found in my own Ruling 4-B drafting, which
  had closed the wonderful-business-at-a-fair-price path entirely.)
Check both directions on every constant. A framework assembled from someone's
writings must be neither more nor less willing to buy than that person.

**RULING 4-A — the certainty spread was misread from its own source.**
Ruling 4 set WIDE at +1% while its own note said it was "calibrated off E3-13's
7%->10% example." E3-13 says "at least a 10 percent rate" in "a world of 7
percent long-term bond rates" — that is a FLOOR, not a midpoint to discount
downward from, and Buffett adds "we have to feel pretty certain about any
business before we're even interested at all," so the gates have already done
the filtering before any spread is assigned. **Corrected ladder: WIDE +3%,
NARROW +4-5%, NONE +6%+, Gate-3 caveat +0.5-1% retained.** Also: certainty is
expressed in the NUMERATOR first per E4-01 (certainty is question one, "how
certain are you that there are indeed birds in the bush"; the third variable is
the BARE long-bond yield) — a run may not use an aggressive growth path and
claim certainty was handled in the rate.

**RULING 4-B — Book Two is a CONFESSED DEPARTURE. It may reject; it may not,
by itself, authorize.** Munger, 1996: "Warren talks about these discounted cash
flows. I've never seen him do one." Buffett: "if you have to actually do it on
— with pencil and paper, it's too close to think about... it ought to just kind
of scream at you." A DCF produces a number for anything; defensible tweaks move
the same business by half or double. So: a PRE-MODEL VERDICT is recorded before
Book Two runs and is binding; a wide sensitivity range is a PASS, not a puzzle
to solve (E4-01: "usually the range must be so wide that no useful conclusion
can be reached"); the sensitivity grid is dispositive, requiring a MAJORITY of
cells, not just the base case.

**RULING 4-C — "obvious" means obviously GOING TO WORK OUT, not obviously
cheap.** Correcting my own 4-B drafting. Munger's test is about the OUTCOME.
Written as a cheapness test it re-implemented the cigar-butt method the 1989
letter repudiates ("Time is the friend of the wonderful business, the enemy of
the mediocre"; "far better to buy a wonderful company at a fair price"). Test
case: Coca-Cola 1988 at ~15x earnings (~6.7% yield) against ~9% bonds FAILS
Book One outright — any reading that rejects Coke is not modelling Buffett.
So the pre-model verdict may be YES at a full price, and where it is YES a
favourable Book Two IS a legitimate authorization (the model confirms a
judgment rather than manufacturing one). The Coca-Cola clause is RETAINED
pending re-derivation, not retired.

**RULING 6 — THE PRICE LADDER. This is the required Gate 6 output and it is
what we are looking for on every run.** Never return a bare "WAIT" again.

    CHEAP    = IV x (1 - MOS)   buy with a cushion
    FAIR     = IV               pay value, earn exactly the discount rate
    CURRENT  = the quote        stated as a multiple of FAIR

  plus three diagnostics that make it decision-useful:
  1. **IRR at the current price**, stated as "X points of equity premium" over
     the sovereign. The most honest number in the framework — what you are
     actually paid for taking equity risk.
  2. **Implied year-1 OE growth** needed to justify the quote, beside what the
     base case assumes. (Gate 8's reverse-DCF logic, moved here because its
     value is HIGHEST on names that fail — it names what must change.)
  3. **Years for yield-on-cost to reach the sovereign**, when the current
     earnings yield is below it.

  And **TWO VERDICTS, stated separately and never merged: BUSINESS and PRICE.**
  e.g. "a wonderful business; not a fair price." Merging them causes both
  classic errors — rejecting a great business for being expensive today, and
  buying a mediocre one for being cheap.

  A WAIT with a ladder is a standing order: the business work is banked, the
  exit metric is pre-committed at Gate 5, and if the price arrives you act
  without re-litigating whether the business is any good.

**RULING 5 — ANSWERED AND CLOSED 2026-08-26 by Ruling 5-A. The 35% base
proposed here was REJECTED on the corpus; do NOT apply it. Text preserved for
the record.** The margin of safety (20%) is softer
than the corpus, which never states a number but illustrates one twice: the
1996 truck/bridge passage implies ~35% (9,800lb load, 15,000lb bridge) and the
1997 Grand Canyon variant implies ~60% when being wrong is catastrophic — and
makes the margin a FUNCTION of downside severity, which our fixed 20% ignores.
Proposal: 35% base (IV x 0.65) plus 10 points per Gate-5 inversion left
UNRESOLVED. Awaiting written approval; it changes every future buy price.
Also flagged by the same audit: the Coca-Cola clause (70% of hurdle) is an
unsourced softener needing re-derivation; the 4% hurdle floor and the size
premiums are invented but err STRICTER, so they survive the test.

**STILL OPEN — the real question behind all of this.** Book One is a static
yield test and is therefore structurally hostile to compounders whenever rates
are high; at a 5.28% sovereign it rejects almost every wonderful business.
Whether that is correct (rates are gravity) or whether the Statute needs a
growth-aware form for WIDE-moat names is unresolved. Settle it deliberately,
with Coca-Cola 1988 as the worked test case.
[PARTIALLY ANSWERED 2026-08-26 by Ruling 9: a large share of Book One's hostility
to compounders was never the static-yield design, it was the trough-anchoring rule.

## THE 2026-08-26 RULINGS — ALL FIVE RATIFIED, IN FORCE FOR EVERY RUN
Produced by a line-by-line corpus search the operator ordered after the TJX refresh.
Full text and verbatim citations: Framework/PENDING RULINGS.md. New ledger entries

**RULING 7 — THE WINDAGE RULE. This governs the construction of every valuation
and it decides three of the four questions that produced this block.** Buffett,
2004 meeting [E4-11], answering almost exactly the question we were asking:
"you take all of the variables and calculate them reasonably conservatively. **But
you don't try and put too much windage in at every level.** And then when you get
all through, you apply the margin of safety. So I would say, **don't focus too much
on taking it on each variable in terms of the discount rate and the growth rate and
so on.** But try to be as realistic as you can on those numbers, but with any errors
being on the conservative side. And then when you get all through, you apply the
margin of safety."
- **Conservatism is spent ONCE, at the margin of safety, at the end.** Every input
  above it is set to its realistic value, with genuine ties broken downward.
  "Errors on the conservative side" means breaking a tie, NOT selecting the worst
  observed value.
- **Certainty is priced in exactly one place: the Aesop spread in the rate**
  (Ruling 4/4-A). It may not ALSO scale the MOS.
- **A conservatism already applied at an earlier gate may not be re-applied at
  Gate 6.** This is what Ruling 9 corrects.
- **Every run states its windage count.** Conservatism at more than one place above
  the MOS must be named and justified in writing.

**RULING 5-A — THE MARGIN OF SAFETY IS A SEVERITY LADDER, AND IT STARTS AT 10%.**
Ruling 5's 35% base is rejected. Its bridge arithmetic was right but it quoted half
a passage: the same 1997 answer [E3-26] opens "if you understood a business
perfectly, the future of a business, you would need very little in the way of a
margin of safety," and the 2007 meeting [E4-12] answers our exact question about a
"dominant, long-standing, stable business" -- for See's or Coke "we don't think we
need a huge margin of safety" and "we will buy those as much closer to a dollar on
the dollar." The fat-man illustration in the same answer implies ~10%. Decisive
companion rule from [E4-12]: **a large MOS is NOT the remedy for uncertainty**
("we don't really try to compensate for that sort of thing by having some extra
large margin of safety. We really want to try to go on to something that we
understand better"). Walking away is the remedy.
    MOS = 10%  base: WIDE moat, 4/4 inversions resolved, Fortress passed with room
        + 10 pts  NARROW moat
        + 10 pts  per Gate-5 inversion left UNRESOLVED
        + 10 pts  Fortress passed but tight (cash < 2x debt due inside 24 months)
        floor 50% if Gate 5 identifies PERMANENT capital-loss risk (the Grand
                  Canyon case) -- and consider that this should be a Gate 5 FAIL
        ceiling 60% (the Grand Canyon figure); above that, an earlier gate failed
This is LOWER than the old flat 20% only for names that already cleared five gates,
and HIGHER for every narrow moat, unresolved inversion and tight balance sheet.

**RULING 8 — NO buyback caveat spread. It is a Gate 3 flag that binds SIZING.**
The corpus is unambiguous that repurchasing above IV penalizes continuing holders
[E4-10 "Buying dollar bills for $1.10 is not good business for those who stick
around"; E5-09 "Value is destroyed when purchases are made above intrinsic value"].
It is equally clear that the remedy is not repricing the business: Buffett attaches
a humility clause in the same breath [E4-13 "it is natural for CEOs to be optimistic
about their own businesses. They also know a whole lot more about them than I do"],
calls such infractions sometimes "innocent" because "many CEOs never stop believing
their stock is cheap" [E5-08], and when Coca-Cola, the Washington Post and Wells
Fargo all did it he recorded the reduced benefit and held all three.
- Score the 2011 two-condition test [E5-08] at Gate 3: (1) ample funds for
  operations and liquidity; (2) repurchases at a material discount to
  conservatively calculated IV. PASS / FAIL / UNKNOWN.
- A FAIL on (2) is a CAPITAL ALLOCATION FLAG and **must be stated with the humility
  clause attached**. It becomes a Gate 3 FAIL only if repeated and unrepentant over
  3+ years AND paired with an integrity or candor failure.
- **The flag binds Gate 7: size DOWN.** That is where the corpus lets the cost land.

**RULING 9 — THE TROUGH ANCHOR IS RETIRED. Book One reads NORMAL earning power.**
The corpus never anchors valuation to the worst year: See's 1983 [E2-24]
"conservatively representative of future earning power"; the 1982-87 acquisition
criteria [E2-25] "demonstrated consistent earning power"; 2016 [E5-10] "normal
earning power"; and decisively Wells Fargo 1990 [E3-24], bought mid-panic, valued
at "less than five times after-tax earnings" on CURRENT earnings, with the
catastrophe modelled in a separate paragraph: a break-even year "would not distress
us. In fact, at Berkshire we would love to acquire businesses or invest in capital
projects that produced no return for a year, but that could then be expected to earn
20% on growing equity." Our rule also DOUBLE-COUNTED: Gate 4's Fortress Test already
runs the bad year.
- **Book One anchor = MEDIAN of the verified annual OE tiers over the last 5 fiscal
  years.**
- **EXCLUDE years distorted by a government-mandated shutdown or equivalent
  externally imposed suspension of operations**, stating the exclusion and reason in
  writing. This is NOT a licence to drop bad years: recessions, demand cycles,
  competitive losses, margin compression and management error all STAY IN.
- **The trough tier is still computed and still reported** -- it moves to Gate 4 as
  the survival input, which is where the corpus puts it.
- Range anchoring survives in its original purpose: thresholds rise only on
  published filings, never on a quote.

**RULING 10 — OWNER EARNINGS: the WC term is required, and (c) is MAINTENANCE capex,
not total capex.** The 1986 definition [E2-23]: "(c) the **average annual amount** of
capitalized expenditures for plant and equipment, etc. that the business **requires to
fully maintain** its long-term competitive position and its unit volume. (**If the
business requires additional working capital to maintain its competitive position and
unit volume, the increment also should be included in (c).** However, businesses
following the LIFO inventory method usually do not require additional working capital
if unit volume does not change.)"
- **WC term: REQUIRED, and it is the structural requirement, not the raw annual
  balance-sheet swing.** Use the average annual cash effect of WC changes over the
  last 4 clean fiscal years (shutdown years excluded per Ruling 9).
- **CAPEX term: maintenance, not total.** Subtracting total capex understates OE for
  any unit-growing business. Since filers rarely disclose the split and Buffett says
  (c) "must be a guess," this is a MANDATORY DISCLOSURE, not a formula: every run
  states its assumption and the band, default **D&A as floor, total capex as
  ceiling** (D&A is a floor not an estimate -- 1986 letter, See's: capitalized
  expenditures exceed depreciation "simply to hold our ground competitively").
- **If the band changes the verdict, the verdict is WAIT** and the run says so.
- Labelled FRAMEWORK CONVENTION: the corpus mandates maintenance capex and supplies
  no method for estimating it from public filings.

**SAFETY CHECK ON ALL FIVE (Ruling 4-C, both directions).** These all loosen the
framework. Tested on TJX at $137.75 with every correction at its most generous
setting: Book One 3.48% vs a 5.19% hurdle, Book Two FAIR $83.91 vs a $137.75 price.
Still a double FAIL, still 1.64x fair. The corrections do not manufacture a buy.

**RULING 12 — EVERY GATE RETURNS ONE OF THREE VERDICTS: IN / OUT / TOO HARD.**
> "Charlie says, you know, 'We've got three boxes at the company: in, out, and too
> hard.' And a lot of things end up in the 'too hard' pile, and it doesn't bother us."
> -- Buffett quoting MUNGER, 2006 meeting (Annual Meetings/2006 Annual Meeting.txt:215)

- **OUT** is a judgment about the BUSINESS. It stops the run and is permanent.
- **AMENDED SAME DAY BY RULING 13 — "TOO HARD" SPLITS IN TWO.** The operator's standing
  instruction: use the technology to eliminate the researchable half.
  - **UNRESEARCHED** = the evidence exists and we have not got it. **A WORK ORDER, never
    a resting state.** Must name the artifact and where it lives. Nitori's English
    financial statements were on its own IR site the whole time; nobody opened them.
  - **UNKNOWABLE** = the evidence is in and the future is still indeterminate. Buffett's
    actual pile (2006: "the future is so likely to be different than the present").
  - **THE SEPARATING TEST, asked out loud: "Can I name the document that would resolve
    this?" YES -> UNRESEARCHED. NO -> UNKNOWABLE.**
  - **EVIDENCE LADDER:** (1) SEC XBRL, screening only (2) SEC EDGAR primary docs
    (3) company IR site, English (4) exchange filings TDnet/RNS/SEDAR+ (5) EDINET
    *(blocked, needs a subscription key)* (6) competitor filings for Gate 2. Where a rung
    is blocked, name the rung and the obstacle.
- **They must never be merged.**
- **TOO HARD IS THE DEFAULT WHEN EVIDENCE IS THIN.** A gate marked IN carrying
  "unverified", "general knowledge", "not independently confirmed" or "provisional" is a
  TOO HARD verdict wearing an IN label. This rule exists because the July 2026 Japan runs
  did exactly that and put unverified names into the portfolio.
- **THE 7-FOOT BAR RULE** (2005 meeting, Annual Meetings/2005 Annual Meeting.txt:977):
  "there's no degree of difficulty factor... we get paid, not for jumping over 7-foot
  bars, but for stepping over 1-foot bars... I'd rather have the universe be a little
  smaller than it really is, than being interpreted as larger than it is." **If a verdict
  only holds after narrowing assumptions or adjudicating a sensitivity grid, it is
  TOO HARD.**

**GATE 2 — THE COMPETITOR ROW IS NOW REQUIRED** (Ruling 11, [E3-28], 1996 meeting,
Annual Meetings/1996 Annual Meeting.txt:681): "I can't be an intelligent owner of a
business unless I know what all the other businesses in that industry are doing." A moat
is a claim about RELATIVE position and cannot be evidenced from one company's numbers.
Name 2-3 closest competitors, same metric, same window, filing-sourced. Where competitor
data is unavailable, the moat class is **PROVISIONAL** and takes the wider NARROW spread.

**GATE 3 — THE HALF-OWNER TEST** replaces the vague "integrity (binary)" line [E3-28]:
does this company's reporting tell me what a 50% partner, away for a year, would have
told me? A one-time item quantified separately at every line passes; the same item buried
in an adjusted figure does not.

**GATE 4 — TWO CHANGES.**
1. **Owner earnings on a MULTI-YEAR MEAN**, never a single year, per [E2-23]'s "the
   AVERAGE ANNUAL AMOUNT": OE = mean over 3 years of (Operating Cash Flow - SBC) - mean
   maintenance capex. **Maintenance capex is a DISCLOSED JUDGMENT, not an output** --
   Buffett says (c) "must be a guess". If the band changes the verdict, TOO HARD.
2. **The stress test is COMPANY-SPECIFIC, QUANTIFIED, AND CARRIES A PROBABILITY**, per
   the 1990 Wells Fargo passage [E3-24] (Shareholder Letters/1990 Letter.txt:326-332):
   name the specific mechanism by which THIS business gets hurt, quantify it from filed
   figures, state the outcome and the likelihood ("a low-level possibility, not a
   likelihood"). The generic 50%-decline-x-2yr test is the floor, used only where no
   specific mechanism can be quantified -- and if none can be named at all, consider
   TOO HARD.

**GATE 5 — EACH INVERSION CARRIES A STATED PROBABILITY** (likely / a real possibility /
a low-level possibility). A [U] on a mandatory inversion is a TOO HARD signal, not a
caveat to carry forward.

**GATE 6 — TWO CHANGES.**
1. **RULING 9-A IS SETTLED: Book One anchors on the MOST RECENT verified tier**, on
   "Wells Fargo CURRENTLY EARNS" [E3-24]. The 5-year mean is a consistency cross-check
   only. Where the two diverge materially and the divergence cannot be explained, the
   verdict is TOO HARD, not an average.
2. **INTRINSIC VALUE IS REPORTED AS A ROUND-NUMBER RANGE**, never a point estimate. 1986

## OPERATOR PROTOCOL — ANALYSIS RUNS (added 2026-07-14 after the first test-run audit)
These rules bind any session applying the framework to a company. They exist because
the 2026-07-14 test run violated the sequence rule and issued unauthorized verdicts
(see "Test Runs/2026-07-14 AUDIT of Test Run.md").

1. HARD GATE SEQUENCE. No Gate-6/7/8 output may be REPORTED for a company unless its
   run file shows Gates 1-5 each marked PASS (or a FAIL that stopped the analysis).
   A gate marked CONDITIONAL, TODO, or [U]-unresolved is NOT a pass.
2. COMPUTATION IS NOT CLEARANCE. Any valuation math produced before Gates 1-5 close
   must be labeled "COMPUTATION - NOT A CLEARANCE" in both the run file and the chat
   reply, and may never be accompanied by entry-permission language (no "starter
   permitted," no "buy below X," no "watchlist" - watchlist requires Gate 8 complete).
3. RUN FILE FIRST. Every company run starts by copying
   "Test Runs/_TEMPLATE - Company Run.md" to a dated file and filling it top to
   bottom. The template's tracker is the enforcement surface: sections are completed
   in order, and each section opens only when the prior section's verdict line is
   filled.
4. STRICT INPUT RULES apply from the first number: full OE formula (never NI proxy
   in a reported figure; WC term required and capex band disclosed per Ruling 10),
   Book One anchored to the 5-year MEDIAN tier with shutdown years excluded
   (Ruling 9, replacing "lowest verified tier"), year-1 growth
   anchored to the LOWER of recent OE growth or guidance, primary filings over
   aggregators (aggregator figures allowed only for live prices, flagged with date).
5. THE AUDIT IS PART OF THE RUN. A run is not complete until the file's self-audit
   checklist (bottom of template) is checked. Rule violations found later are
   corrected in an addendum, never by editing history.
6. THREE BOXES, NOT TWO (Ruling 12, 2026-08-26). Every gate returns IN, OUT or
   TOO HARD. A gate marked IN that carries an "unverified" or "provisional"
   caveat is a protocol violation -- write TOO HARD. TOO HARD closes the file
   without prejudice and is never reported as a pass.
7. THE PRICE LADDER IS THE DELIVERABLE (Ruling 6, 2026-08-01). No run that reaches
   Gate 6 may end on a bare BUY-ELIGIBLE / STATUTE-ONLY / WAIT. It must state
   CHEAP (IV x (1-MOS)), FAIR (= IV) and CURRENT in dollars; the IRR at the current
   price expressed as points of equity premium over the sovereign; the year-1 OE
   growth the price already implies versus what the base case assumes; and the
   years for yield-on-cost to reach the sovereign. It closes with TWO SEPARATE
   VERDICTS -- BUSINESS and PRICE -- never merged into one sentence. This is what
   we are looking for every time the system runs: not a yes/no, but a price to act
   at and an honest statement of what today's quote pays you.
