# THE HOLDINGS FRAMEWORK
# For businesses already owned. Adopted 2026-09-13 at the operator's instruction.

**Two frameworks, two questions.** `Framework/THE FRAMEWORK v4.md` answers **"should I buy
this?"** This document answers **"should I keep what I own?"** The corpus treats them as
different questions: Berkshire holds businesses it would not buy today, and sells businesses it
would still call good. The operator's instruction of 2026-09-13: *"the current framework is for
new purchases. we also need a holdings framework and this will be it once its built."*

**The rule that joins them: an add is a purchase.** Buying more of something already owned runs
the purchase framework, all six questions, at the add price. This document never approves an add. *(CONVENTION,
labelled 2026-10-05: no ledger row states that an add re-runs every purchase question; the v5 blind read found none
("no instance found in the v5 ledger"). The nearest corpus is the comparison of every new dollar with more of what is
already owned [E4-45] and the floor as a buying rule [E4-28]. The rule is ours and is kept.)*

**Where this came from.** Every rule below was already in the corpus and most were already in
the purchase framework's Q6, which carried the hold doctrine as a sub-part of the sell question.
This document gives that doctrine its own sequence, its own verdicts and its own template, and
settles one tension the Q6 text left open (section VII). **Nothing here is new evidence.** Each
rule carries its ledger id; anything without one is labelled CONVENTION.

# I. THE GOVERNING RULE

> "Sometimes, of course, **the market may judge a business to be more valuable than the
> underlying facts** would indicate it is. In such a case, we will sell our holdings. Sometimes,
> also, we will sell a security that is fairly valued or even undervalued because **we require
> funds for a still more undervalued investment or one we believe we understand better.** We
> need to emphasize, however, that **we do not sell holdings just because they have appreciated
> or because we have held them for a long time.** … We are quite content to hold any security
> indefinitely, **so long as the prospective return on equity capital of the underlying business
> is satisfactory, management is competent and honest, and the market does not overvalue the
> business.**" — **[E2-28]**, 1987 letter

Three conditions to keep holding. Two reasons to sell. Two reasons explicitly rejected.

**And the reason [E2-28] does not state, given at a later meeting [E4-17]:**

> "So now we sell - really when we think that we've - when we're **reevaluating the economic
> characteristics of the business.** [...] We probably had one view of the long-term competitive
> advantage of the company at the time we bought it, and we may have modified that. [...] And
> **those beliefs change quite gradually.**" — **[E4-17]**, 2002 meeting

**The holding period the corpus prefers, and its condition:**

> "when we own portions of **outstanding businesses with outstanding managements**, our favorite
> holding period is forever. We are just the opposite of those who hurry to sell and book profits
> when companies perform well but who **tenaciously hang on to businesses that disappoint.**"
> — **[E2-14]**, 1988 letter

"Forever" is conditional on the business and the management staying outstanding. The same
sentence condemns holding on to disappointment.

# II. THE VERDICTS

**Every hold question returns one of the four verdicts**, exactly as in the purchase framework:
**IN · OUT · UNRESEARCHED · UNKNOWABLE.** Ask aloud on every non-IN verdict: *can I name the
document that would resolve this?* YES is UNRESEARCHED; NO is UNKNOWABLE.

**The review ends in one of four outcomes:**

| outcome | meaning |
|---|---|
| **HOLD** | every hold question is IN. Keep the position; do nothing else. |
| **SELL REVIEW** | a question is OUT but the view has not yet crystallized, or a question is UNKNOWABLE. The review names the document that will settle it and when that document is due. |
| **SELL** | a question is OUT and the view has crystallized. Sell the whole position, promptly. |
| **HOLD, ADDS BARRED** | a note that travels with HOLD whenever the purchase framework does not clear today. It is the normal state of a holding and is not a warning. |

**A SELL REVIEW is not a parking place.** When its named document is read, the review concludes
HOLD or SELL. It may not be renewed on the same question without new evidence, because:

> "we made an **even more serious mistake in not selling them (at losses, if necessary) when our
> present views began to crystallize.**" — **[E2-40]**, 1979 letter

**UNRESEARCHED alone never forces a sale.** A holding with a gap in the record stays held while
the named document is fetched and read: *"if you really get a wonderful business with outstanding management [...]
when in doubt, keep holding. But it's no inviolable rule."* **[E5-66]**, 2009 meeting. The hold is already made and the
corpus says the relevant beliefs "change quite gradually" [E4-17]; selling on an unread document would be the frenetic
activity [E3-35] warns against. The gap must name its document and due date. *(Until 2026-10-05 this was a CONVENTION;
the v5 blind read found its row.)*

# III. BEFORE ANY QUESTION

**1. Every holding is a SECURITY.** *(Corrected 2026-10-05 from the PRIME RULE 5 case of that date, approved by the
operator. Until then this item read "Designation: SECURITY or PERMANENT", let a holding be designated PERMANENT in
writing and in advance, and exempted it from H4 on [E2-39]. The 1987 passage stands as history; its author withdrew
the treatment in 2016.)* The 1987 letter treated three marketable stocks as permanent:

> "our insurance companies own three marketable common stocks that **we would not sell even
> though they became far overpriced** in the market. In effect, we view these investments exactly
> like our successful controlled businesses - a permanent part of Berkshire [...] we would, **if
> absolutely necessary**, sell portions" — **[E2-39]**, 1987 letter

The 2016 letter withdrew it for every marketable security and amended the Owner's Manual to say so **[E5-64]**:

> "It is true that we own some stocks that I have no intention of selling for as far as the eye can see (and we're
> talking 20/20 vision). But **we have made no commitment that Berkshire will hold any of its marketable securities
> forever.** [...] **That principle covers controlled businesses, not marketable securities.** This year I've added a
> final sentence to #11 to ensure that our owners understand that **we regard any marketable security as available for
> sale**, however unlikely such a sale now seems." — **[E5-64]**, 2016 letter

So no holding is exempt from H4 on the corpus's authority. A holder who nonetheless designates a stock permanent does
so as a CONVENTION of his own, confessed in writing and in advance **[E1-02]**, and it still yields to the crystallized
view **[E2-40]**.

**The reluctance to sell sub-par businesses does not transfer.** The corpus's refusal to sell
weak businesses is stated for *"businesses that Berkshire owns"* and turns on feeling good about
*"their managers and labor relations"* **[E2-65]**, and it rejects *"gin rummy managerial
behavior"* for the same controlled businesses **[E2-32]**. A marketable security has no labor
relations to protect. For securities, **[E2-28]** and **[E4-17]** govern.

**2. The account.** Record whether the position sits in a taxable account or a tax-sheltered
one. It changes the switching bar at H5 and nothing else.

**3. The purchase thesis.** Find the run file that bought the position and what it said at Q2,
Q4 and Q6, including any exit metric set before entry **[E1-02]**. **If the position predates
the framework and no thesis is on file, say so**: H1 is then judged against the current purchase
run, and the absence is recorded.

**4. The filing gets read.** Operator rule 4 applies unchanged: the latest annual report and proxy
statement, with document, date and accession number, and one figure cross-checked.

# IV. THE FIVE HOLD QUESTIONS

**In order. Unlike the purchase framework, all five are answered**: a holding is already owned,
so every question bears on the outcome and none closes the file early.

## H1 - IS THE BUSINESS'S ECONOMIC POSITION INTACT?

The E4-17 question. **Compare today's evidence with the view held at purchase.**

- **The instrument is the purchase framework's Q2**, re-run or re-read on current filings: the
  competitor row, pricing power **[E2-44]**, durability **[E4-04]**.
- **The monitoring question:** *"whether this erosion is just part of an **aberrational cycle**
  ... or whether the business has **slipped in a way that permanently reduces intrinsic business
  values**"* **[E3-30]**.
- **Physical volume is the evidence price cannot hide.** Munger on a unit decline masked by a
  price rise: *"a serious reverse, not likely to disappear in some bounce back effect"*
  **[E4-55]**.
- **Key-person dependence is a moat defect, not a management note** **[E4-23]**.

**Verdict.** IN: the position is as strong as believed at purchase, or stronger. OUT: the
position is weaker than believed at purchase, on filed evidence, and the weakness is not
aberrational. UNRESEARCHED: the document that would settle it is named. UNKNOWABLE: no document
can settle it.

**A purchase-framework Q2 of OUT is evidence for H1, not the verdict of H1.** H1 asks whether the
*view has changed and crystallized*. Two cases differ:
- **The business was judged a franchise at purchase and is now judged not to be one.** That is
  exactly the reevaluation **[E4-17]** names as the reason Berkshire sells. Slow to conclude,
  because *"those beliefs change quite gradually"*; once concluded, H1 is OUT and the outcome is
  SELL **[E2-40]**.
- **The business was never judged a franchise, and was held for its returns.** H1 then asks only
  whether the economics that were relied on still hold, and H2 carries the weight.

## H2 - IS THE PROSPECTIVE RETURN ON EQUITY CAPITAL SATISFACTORY?

The first condition of **[E2-28]**, measured the corpus's way:

> "The primary test of managerial economic performance is the achievement of a **high earnings
> rate on equity capital employed (without undue leverage, accounting gimmickry, etc.)**"
> — **[E2-01]**, 1979 letter

- **Use return on capital employed where buybacks or leverage have thinned book equity**, and
  say why; raw return on equity is meaningless on a negative or buyback-shrunk base **[E2-01]**.
- **Owner earnings, not reported earnings**, rebuilt the purchase framework's way (Q4), over its
  default window.
- **Why the business's return is the owner's return over time:** *"Over the long term, it's hard
  for a stock to earn a much better return than the business that underlies it earns"*
  **[E3-17]**.
- **The retention test, where earnings are retained:** does retention deliver at least a dollar
  of market value per dollar retained over a rolling five years **[E3-54]**, applied in the form its author corrected
  in 2009: *"The five-year test should be: (1) during the period did our book-value gain exceed the performance of the
  S&P; and (2) did our stock consistently sell at a premium to book, meaning that every $1 of retained earnings was
  always worth more than $1? If these tests are met, retaining earnings has made sense."* **[E5-65]**, 2009 report,
  which calls the 1996 wording *"the test as I improperly formulated it"* because a market decline fails it
  mechanically. For a holding, the two legs are the business's own progress against the index over the period and the
  market's valuation of the whole consistently above the capital put in. *(Added 2026-10-05 from the PRIME RULE 5 case
  of that date; the hold reads of 2026-08-28 applied the 1996 form and receive dated notes.)*
- **"Prospective" means forward.** A satisfactory past with a filed, non-aberrational decline
  under way **[E3-30]** is not satisfactory.

**Verdict.** IN: the forward return on the capital the business employs is satisfactory on the
filed record. OUT: it is not, and the shortfall is not aberrational. UNRESEARCHED / UNKNOWABLE as
defined.

## H3 - IS MANAGEMENT COMPETENT AND HONEST?

The second condition of **[E2-28]**.

- **Honesty is a binary.** Run the purchase framework's Q3 flags on the latest proxy and annual
  report. A disqualifying finding makes H3 OUT.
- **Competence is judged on capital allocation, and the damage to watch is loss of focus:**
  *"when the management of a great company gets sidetracked and neglects its wonderful base
  business while purchasing other businesses that are so-so or worse"* **[E3-40]**.
- **Share issuance** is *"one of the surest indicators of a promotion-minded management"*
  **[E5-15]**.
- **Good managers cannot rescue bad economics** **[E2-38]**, so H3 IN never offsets H1 or H2 OUT.

**When H3 is OUT on capital allocation, the corpus says leave, not argue:**

> "if you really think you're in with people that have got a good business, but **they're going
> to keep doing dumb things with your money, you'll probably do better to get out**" — Buffett;
> Munger on their record at persuading managements: *"Worse than poor."* — **[E4-24]**, 2001

**Verdict.** IN: no disqualifier and capital allocated sensibly. OUT: a disqualifier, or capital
being misallocated and expected to continue. UNRESEARCHED: the document is named (for a new chief
executive, usually the next proxy statement). UNKNOWABLE as defined.

## H4 - DOES THE MARKET NOT OVERVALUE THE BUSINESS?

The third condition of **[E2-28]**: *"the market may judge a business to be more valuable than
the underlying facts would indicate it is. In such a case, we will sell."*

**The test is the purchase framework's valuation arithmetic, with the floor removed.**
- **Value the business as a range**, against the government bond for its earnings currency
  **[E4-21]**, with the growth its own record supports **[E4-35, E4-44]**.
- **Price above the whole range: overvalued.** The market judges it more valuable than the facts.
  H4 is OUT.
- **Price inside the range: not overvalued.** *"Usually, the range must be so wide that **no
  useful conclusion can be reached**"* **[E4-25]**, and no useful conclusion is not a reason to
  sell. H4 is IN.
- **Price below the range: not overvalued.** H4 is IN.

**The ~10% floor does not apply to a holding.** The floor is stated as a buying rule: *"we don't
want to **buy** equities where our real expectancy is below 10 percent"* **[E4-28]**. A holding
that would not clear it as a purchase can still be held; it simply cannot be added to.

**Appreciation is not overvaluation.** A price that has risen but still sits inside the range
is the case **[E2-28]** names directly: *"we do not sell holdings just because they have
appreciated."*

**No holding skips H4.** *(2026-10-05: this line read "PERMANENT holdings skip H4 [E2-39]" until the PRIME RULE 5
case of that date; the author withdrew the permanent treatment for marketable securities in 2016 [E5-64], section III.)*

**Verdict.** IN: price inside or below the range. OUT: price above the whole range. UNRESEARCHED:
the input that would set the range is named. UNKNOWABLE: no range can be built, in which case H2
is also in question.

## H5 - IS HOLDING STILL THE BEST USE OF THIS MONEY?

The second sell reason of **[E2-28]**: *"we require funds for a still more undervalued investment
or one we believe we understand better."*

**H5 is OUT only against a named replacement that has cleared the purchase framework**, all six
questions including the ~10% floor **[E4-28]**, at its current price. A general sense that
something better exists is not a replacement.

**The replacement must clear the switching bar**, computed for the account that holds the position:
- **Tax**, in a taxable account: the corpus's arithmetic of realising gains every year against
  compounding once **[E2-46]**, and Munger's per-annum form **[E3-64]**. In a tax-sheltered account
  this term is zero.
- **Friction**: the costs of getting in and out *"can easily reach 3 percent"* a year when activity
  is frequent **[E3-67]**.
- **A material gap**: *"8 1/2 wouldn't tempt you"* from a familiar, certain 8 **[E4-45]**.
- **And the counterweight**: *"trying to minimize taxes too much is one of the great standard
  causes of really dumb mistakes"* **[E3-64]**. Tax never vetoes a sale that H1 to H4 require.

**Verdict.** IN: no named replacement clears the purchase framework and the switching bar. OUT: one
does, and it is named with its run file.

# VI. WHAT IS NEVER A REASON

- **The price has risen.** *"We do not sell holdings just because they have appreciated"* **[E2-28]**.
- **It has been held a long time.** Same sentence **[E2-28]**.
- **It has become a large position.** *"The weeds wither away in significance as the flowers
  bloom. Over time, it takes just a few winners to work wonders."* **[E5-14]**. Trimming a winner
  because it grew is not a hold question.
- **Something should be done.** *"Inactivity strikes us as intelligent behavior"* **[E3-15]**;
  *"activity does not correlate with achievement"* **[E3-35]**; *"we neither bought nor sold a
  share of five of our six major holdings"* **[E3-01]**.
- **The purchase framework would not buy it today.** That bars adds. By itself it is not a sale:
  it becomes one only through H1, H2, H3, H4 or H5.
- **The price has fallen.** A fall moves H4 further toward IN. A fall is a prompt to re-read H1
  and H2, never a verdict.

# VII. THE TENSION THIS DOCUMENT SETTLES

**The purchase framework's Q6 described the three [E2-28] hold conditions as "Q2, Q3 and Q5
restated."** The text does not say that, and PRIME RULE 2 says the text wins:
- **The first condition is a return test, not a franchise test.** *"The prospective return on
  equity capital of the underlying business is satisfactory"* **[E2-28]** asks about returns. A
  business can fail the franchise test and still earn a satisfactory return.
- **The franchise question enters holding through a different passage**: the reevaluation of
  competitive advantage **[E4-17]**, which is H1 here.
- **The third condition is not Q5.** Q5 carries the ~10% floor, which is a buying rule **[E4-28]**.
  H4 tests overvaluation against the range, without the floor.

So a holding needs **both** H1 (the advantage believed at purchase is intact) **and** H2 (the
return is satisfactory). The earlier hold reads on file tested H2 and not H1 as a separate
question. **Each holding's first review under this framework asks H1 explicitly.**

# VIII. WHEN TO REVIEW

*CONVENTION: the corpus sets no calendar, and says beliefs about a business change gradually
[E4-17]. A review is owed when evidence arrives, not on a schedule:*
- **each annual report** (H1, H2, H4);
- **each proxy statement** (H3);
- **any pre-committed tripwire firing**, set at the previous review **[E1-02]**;
- **a price alert firing** (H4 only, then H1 and H2 if the move is large);
- **before any add** (which runs the purchase framework instead);
- **when a replacement clears the purchase framework** (H5).

**Every review ends by setting the next review's tripwires in writing**, before the act **[E1-02]**.

# IX. WHAT THIS FRAMEWORK DOES NOT CLAIM

- **It does not claim that holding beats selling, or that the purchase framework beats the
  market.** The honest record in `Framework/OPERATOR-PROTOCOL.md` (operator rule 7; in `CLAUDE.md` until 2026-09-25) governs both documents: the
  market-beating claim is unproven.
- **It does not cover funds.** Index funds and ETFs are not businesses; no review applies.
- **It does not approve adds.** An add is a purchase.
- **It does not replace judgment.** Every verdict is a judgment made by the AI running it and
  justified by ledger id, under the division of labor in the operator protocol (`Framework/OPERATOR-PROTOCOL.md`).

**The review surface is `Test Runs/_TEMPLATE - Holding Review.md`.**
