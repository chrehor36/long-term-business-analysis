# CASE E — business description (de-identified, from the filing of record at the anchor)

The company is one of the largest food and beverage companies in the world, selling in approximately 190 countries and territories. It manufactures and markets condiments and sauces, cheese and dairy products, shelf-stable and frozen meals, meats, refreshment beverages, coffee and other grocery products under a portfolio of brands the filing describes as iconic, several of them more than a century old.

The company in its present form is new: it was created in mid-2015 by the merger of two long-established packaged food companies, each with over 100 years of history. Before the merger, one of the two had been taken private in 2013 by a pair of controlling sponsors, a large publicly traded investment holding company and a private-equity firm, and the merged company's most recent fiscal year is the first full year of combined results. It reports four segments: its home country, a neighboring country, one overseas developed region, and a rest-of-world grouping of emerging regions. Product categories contributing 10% or more of consolidated sales were condiments and sauces at roughly 26%, cheese and dairy at roughly 21%, and meats and seafood at roughly 10%; ambient meals and frozen and chilled meals contributed roughly 9% and 8%.

Products are sold through the company's own sales organizations and through independent brokers, agents and distributors, to grocery chains, wholesalers, cooperatives and independents, convenience, drug and value stores, mass merchants, club stores, bakeries, pharmacies, foodservice distributors and institutions. Customer concentration is significant: the largest customer, a mass-market retailer, represented approximately 22% of net sales in the latest year, and the five largest customers accounted for approximately 49% of home-country segment sales, 76% of the neighboring country's segment sales and 31% of the overseas developed region's segment sales.

Raw materials are agricultural commodities: dairy, meats, coffee beans, nuts, tomatoes, potatoes, oils, sweeteners, corn and wheat, plus resins, cardboard and energy for packaging and operations. Procurement is centralized to leverage scale; hedging limits but does not eliminate commodity exposure, and the filing states supply is generally adequate and available from numerous sources.

On competition, the filing states the company faces competition in all aspects of its business, from large national and international food and beverage companies and numerous local and regional companies, and from branded, generic and retailer-label products. Competition is on product quality and innovation, brand recognition and loyalty, service, the ability to identify and satisfy consumer preferences, new product introductions, advertising and marketing effectiveness, distribution, shelf space, merchandising support, and price; improving market position or introducing a new product requires substantial advertising and promotional spending. Trademarks are described as material and among the company's most valuable assets; some brands are licensed from third parties. Research and development spending is modest relative to sales. The company has approximately 41,000 employees and is subject to extensive food safety, labeling, employment and environmental regulation in its markets.
