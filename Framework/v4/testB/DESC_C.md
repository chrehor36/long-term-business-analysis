# CASE C — business description (de-identified, from the filing of record at the anchor)

The company is a global information technology enterprise, incorporated in its home country roughly a century ago, operating in more than 170 countries. It creates business value for enterprise clients through integrated solutions that combine consulting, delivery and implementation services, enterprise software, computer systems and financing. Its stated strategy over the past decade has been to shift its business mix toward higher-value areas, exiting commoditized segments while acquiring over 100 companies, and to run itself as a single globally integrated enterprise with shared processes and delivery centers located where labor and skills make business sense. Named growth initiatives are emerging geographic markets, business analytics, cloud computing, and instrumented "smart" infrastructure solutions for industries.

It reports five segments. Two are services segments: one provides IT infrastructure outsourcing, business process services, project-based technology services and maintenance, running what the filing calls the world's largest privately owned IT infrastructure across roughly 430 data centers; the other provides consulting, systems integration and application management. About 60% of external services revenue is annuity-based from outsourcing, maintenance and application management contracts, and a backlog provides a base of revenue entering each year. The software segment consists primarily of middleware and operating systems; about two-thirds of its external revenue is annuity-based from recurring license, subscription and support charges. The systems segment sells servers, storage, retail point-of-sale systems and semiconductors; about half of server and storage sales flow through business partners. A captive financing segment provides leases and loans to end users for terms of two to seven years, short-term inventory and receivables financing to dealers, and remarketing of returned equipment; the filing says its deep knowledge of the client base and of the products financed mitigates the credit and residual-value risks normally associated with financing.

Clients span financial services, the public sector, industrial, distribution and communications industries, plus smaller businesses. The company invests heavily in research and development focused on high-value opportunities; it states it received more patents in its home country than any other company for the eighteenth consecutive year, and that licensing its intellectual property produces meaningful annual income, though no single patent or license is material.

On competition, the filing counts hundreds of competitors worldwide, from large multinationals to niche players, and states the company is the leader or among the leaders in each of its segments. Services rivals include global IT and consulting firms, offshore service providers based in one large emerging market, and the consulting arms of accounting firms; software rivals are large platform and database vendors; systems rivals are large server and storage makers; the financing arm competes with other captive financiers, industrial finance companies and banks. Principal competitive methods are technology innovation, performance, price, quality, brand, breadth of capabilities, client relationships and service.
