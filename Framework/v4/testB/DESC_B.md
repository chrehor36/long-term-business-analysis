# CASE B — business description (de-identified, from the filing of record at the anchor)

The company designs, manufactures and markets consumer electronics hardware: smartphones, multi-purpose tablets, desktop and portable personal computers, a recently introduced wrist-worn wearable device, a television set-top device and portable digital music players. It also sells related software, services, accessories, networking solutions and third-party digital content and applications. It develops its own proprietary operating systems for each hardware line, a suite of consumer and professional application software, a cloud service that synchronizes content across a user's devices, fee-based extended service and support plans, and a mobile payment service currently available in two countries.

Its stated strategy rests on a claimed unique ability to design and develop its own operating systems, hardware, application software and services, delivering products with innovative design, superior ease of use and seamless integration. It operates online marketplaces for the discovery and delivery of digital content and applications, and supports a large third-party developer community that builds software, hardware and content complementing its platforms.

It manages the business on a geographic basis, with five reportable segments covering its home region and several international regions, one of which consists of a single large emerging market and another of a single developed market. In the most recent fiscal year the home country produced 35% of net sales and international markets 65%. Customers are consumers, small and mid-sized businesses, education, enterprise and government. Direct channels, meaning its own retail stores, online stores and direct sales force, produced 26% of net sales; indirect channels, meaning cellular network carriers, wholesalers, retailers and value-added resellers, produced 74%. No single customer exceeded 10% of net sales. Its fiscal year ends just before the holiday season, so the holiday quarter is its first and historically strongest quarter.

The filing describes markets that are highly competitive, with frequent product introductions, rapid technological advance, and aggressive price cutting by competitors selling devices based on other operating systems, producing industry-wide downward pressure on gross margins. Competitive factors include price, features, quality and reliability, design innovation, a strong third-party software and accessories ecosystem, marketing and distribution capability, service and support, and reputation. The company believes it offers superior innovation and integration of the entire solution of hardware, software and services; it notes some competitors have substantial resources and may sell at little or no profit. Its digital content services face significant competition, including free peer-to-peer alternatives.

On supply, many components come from single or limited sources, including custom sole-sourced parts; substantially all hardware is assembled by a small number of outsourcing partners concentrated in one overseas region, often in single locations, and industry-wide component shortages and price swings occur. Purchase commitments typically cover about 150 days. It holds a large worldwide patent portfolio but states it relies primarily on the innovative skills of its personnel. It has approximately 110,000 full-time-equivalent employees.
