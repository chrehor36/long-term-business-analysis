# CASE A — business description (de-identified, from the filing of record at the anchor)

The company is a diversified financial services holding company in a large developed economy. It is organized as a bank holding company that has elected financial holding company status under its home country's banking law, permitting it to affiliate with securities and insurance firms and to engage in activities that are "financial in nature." Its principal business is to act as a holding company for its subsidiaries. A single national bank subsidiary holds roughly 89% of consolidated assets. By assets, the filing states the company is the third-largest bank holding company in its home country.

The company provides retail, commercial and corporate banking services through banking locations and offices, the internet and other distribution channels, to individuals, businesses and institutions in every state of its home country and in other countries. Through subsidiaries it also conducts wholesale banking, mortgage banking, consumer finance, equipment leasing, agricultural finance, commercial finance, securities brokerage and investment banking, insurance agency and brokerage, computer and data processing services, trust services, investment advisory services, mortgage-backed securities servicing and venture capital investment. For management reporting it has three operating segments: community banking; wholesale banking; and wealth and investment management. It has approximately 269,000 active full-time-equivalent employees.

On competition, the filing states the financial services industry is highly competitive. Its subsidiaries compete with banks, savings institutions, credit unions, finance companies, mortgage banking companies, insurance companies, investment banks and mutual fund companies, and face increased competition from nonbank institutions such as brokerage houses, private equity firms and online lending companies. Many competitors enjoy fewer regulatory constraints, and some may have lower cost structures. The filing notes that technological advances may diminish the importance of depository institutions in the transfer of funds.

Regulation receives the greatest weight in the filing's business discussion. The parent is supervised by the central bank; bank subsidiaries are examined by the national bank supervisor, the deposit insurer and a consumer financial protection agency. The framework is designed to protect depositors and the banking system, not investors in the holding company. Subsidiary banks' ability to pay dividends to the parent is limited by statute and by supervisory capital rules; the parent must submit annual capital plans and pass stress tests before making capital distributions. The company is designated a global systemically important bank, carrying an additional capital surcharge, enhanced leverage requirements taking effect within two years, and total loss-absorbing capacity requirements. Comprehensive financial reform legislation enacted in its home country in 2010 has affected, and is expected to continue to affect, most of its businesses directly or indirectly. Deposit insurance assessments include a temporary surcharge on large banks. Earnings are significantly affected by the fiscal and monetary policies of the home-country government and central bank. The filing states that further legislative and regulatory change could alter its operating environment in substantial and unpredictable ways.
