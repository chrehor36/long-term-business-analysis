# CASE 1 — [E4-04] IS A PERIMETER LIMIT, NOT A FRANCHISE FINDING

## What Q2 says today
Q2 carries [E4-04] from the 2007 letter: *"Our criterion of 'enduring' causes us to rule out companies in
industries prone to rapid and continuous change. [...] A moat that must be continuously rebuilt will
eventually be no moat at all."* Runs have applied it as a **business finding at Q2**: a name that passes
[E3-03]'s three franchise conditions can still be marked **Q2 OUT, on the business** because its industry
changes fast. TSM is the case: it passed [E3-03] on its customers' own filings and closed *"OUT, ON THE
BUSINESS, ON [E4-04]"*.

## What the corpus says
1. **The reason given for the rule is epistemic, in the rule's own sentence:** creative destruction
   *"precludes investment certainty"* (2007 letter, [E4-04]). The 1999 letter says it without ambiguity:
   *"Our problem -- which we can't solve by studying up -- is that we have no insights into which participants
   in the tech field possess a truly durable competitive advantage"* **[E4-57]**, and *"Predicting the
   long-term economics of companies that operate in fast-changing industries is simply far beyond our
   perimeter"* **[E4-58]**. The 1987 acquisition criteria state the reason as competence inside the list
   itself: *"simple businesses (if there's lots of technology, we won't understand it)"* **[E2-75]**.
2. **The unit of the moat test is the company, not the industry:** *"The key to investing is not assessing
   how much an industry is going to affect society, or how much it will grow, but rather determining the
   competitive advantage of any given company and, above all, the durability of that advantage"* **[E4-08]**.
3. **Munger says the avoidance is theirs, not the business's:** *"we tend to avoid that stuff, based on our
   personal inadequacies"* **[E3-72]**; *"The fact that we don't think we're very good at it and have pretty
   well stayed out of it doesn't mean that it's irrational for you to do it"* **[E3-73]**.
4. **The purchase record qualifies the rule on the record, twice.** BYD: *"BYD is surfing along on the
   developing edge of new technology, and that has not — we have always bragged about avoiding that. [...] And
   yet here we are. I think it's because we've shown some capability for learning"* **[E5-51]**. Apple: *"I
   didn't go into Apple because it was a tech stock in the least. [...] the value of an ecosystem and how
   permanent that ecosystem could be [...] It's much more the nature of consumer behavior"* **[E5-52]** —
   against the 1991 letter's *"high-technology (Apple) [...] We will never develop the competence to spot such
   businesses early"* **[E3-75]**.
5. **On TSMC the text is one-sided.** The only passage on the shelf: *"one of the best managed companies and
   important companies in the world. And I think you'll be able to say the same thing five, or ten, or 20 years
   from now. [...] I don't like its location. [...] there's nobody in the chip industry that's in their league"*
   **[E5-53]**. The business verdict is affirmative on a twenty-year horizon; the sale is jurisdictional.
6. **Where the text is genuinely divided, it supplies its own reconciliation.** [E4-04] says a moat that must
   be *rebuilt* will be no moat; Buffett in 2018 says every owner *"should be working at improving your own moat
   and defending your own moat all of the time"* **[E5-23]**, and Munger credits Gillette for *"surfing along
   new technology"* while holding its moat (Talk 02, line 437). The distinction the text draws is **rebuild from
   zero** against **maintenance**. The have-to-stay-smart business is, in Buffett's own example, *retailing*,
   not technology **[E3-74]**.

## The reading the text supports
**[E4-04] is a statement about where durable advantage is usually absent and about the limits of the
analyst's own sight. It is not a finding that a company which has passed [E3-03] lacks a franchise.** Where
a fast-changing industry leaves the durability question unanswerable, the corpus-native verdict is the 1999
letter's: *far beyond our perimeter* — a competence close, which in this framework's four verdicts is
**UNKNOWABLE at Q2 (closed without prejudice), or Q1 OUT where the business itself cannot be understood** —
never *OUT, on the business*. Where the run CAN answer it from filings (as TSM's did, on its customers'
own words), [E4-04] asks one further question: **must the advantage be rebuilt from zero each generation, or
maintained?** A rebuild-from-zero record fails [E4-04] on the business. A maintained lead does not.

## The change to `THE FRAMEWORK v4.md`, if approved
In Q2, beside [E4-04]: *"[E4-04] is applied as a competence limit, not as a fourth franchise criterion. A
name that passes [E3-03] and whose durability cannot be judged from filings closes UNKNOWABLE at Q2 [E4-58],
never OUT on the business. A name whose advantage must be rebuilt from zero each generation fails [E4-04] on
the business; a maintained lead does not [E5-23, E5-51, E5-52]. The unit of the test is the company
[E4-08]."* Section VI gains no convention: every clause above is corpus.

## Verdicts of record this would touch
- **TSM** — the only register entry whose Q2 OUT rests on [E4-04] alone. Under the reading above it becomes
  **UNKNOWABLE at Q2 or a maintained-lead IN**, to be decided by re-reading its own file's rebuild evidence
  (it found capex above depreciation in eleven of eleven years, which is the rebuild question exactly).
  Corrected by addendum to the run file and a dated note in the register, never by editing either.
- **NVDA** — closed on [E4-04] *with* [E3-03] criterion 2 as the customers' filings test it; the criterion-2
  finding stands on its own, so the verdict does not move, and the [E4-04] clause is re-read.
- **Nineteen other Q2 OUTs cite [E4-04] alongside an independent [E3-03] criterion-2 failure.** None moves.
- **No gate-clearer changes.** Nothing at Q5 changes.

# CASE 2 — A BANK IS A BUSINESS, AND THE LOW-COST DOOR IS THE ONLY ONE

## What Q2 says today
Q2 carries [E3-03] (needed or desired; no close substitute; not price-regulated) and [E2-58] (the wide and
sustainable cost advantage as the exception for commodity products). It says nothing about banks. Five banks
were run on 2026-09-19: CCB and SOFI failed criterion 2 on their counterparties' filings; ACNB passed through
[E2-58] on the cost of deposits; JPM passed through [E2-58] on the cost of operations; TFC failed on regulator
data. Three of the five sourced *"a bank is a business, unlike a franchise"* to [E3-43], which is said of
newspapers, television and magazines, not banks (`Test Runs/ADDENDUM 2026-09-20 - E3-43 is not said of banks
...`).

## What the corpus says
1. **The product is a commodity, in Buffett's words:** banks are *"dealing in what is basically a commodity —
   money"*, and their high returns *"would be self-neutralizing"* **[E4-59]**. The 1982 letter puts financial
   institutions inside the commodity-and-administered-prices frame by name: *"deposit costs for financial
   institutions"* among government-administered costs **[E2-59]** — the same letter Q2 already cites for
   [E2-58].
2. **The door, in a bank's own terms, twice:** *"very little risk on the asset side and very cheap money on the
   deposit side"* **[E4-60]**; the Bank of Granite's *"2.58 percent on assets [...] and [...] a 33 percent
   efficiency ratio"* against larger banks, *"There's no magic to it. You just have to stay away from doing
   something foolish"*, and *"we like businesses like banking, if we've got somebody in charge of them that is
   going to run them right"* **[E3-76]**.
3. **What makes it a business and not a franchise is management dependence:** *"leverage of 20:1 magnifies the
   effects of managerial strengths and weaknesses"* **[E3-29]**; *"you should know something about the culture
   of the management and the institution to make a firm buy decision on a bank, and that's hard to do for 99
   percent of the banks"* **[E4-63]**; the economics rest on *"you get your money so cheap and [...] the implicit
   federal guarantee, and you do get to leverage up"* **[E5-54]**; and the return is set from outside: *"You can
   change the math of banking, and the attractiveness of banking, totally, by capital requirements"* **[E5-55]**.
4. **The text refuses the opposite reading too:** *"I would not characterize all banks as the same"* **[E4-61]**;
   Munger: *"Warren and I have failed to properly diagnose banking [...] we underestimated the general good
   results"* **[E4-62]**. And the deposit base is treated as a non-wasting asset once: *"Yet core deposits
   regularly increase"* **[E5-56]**.

## The reading the text supports
**A bank sits on the "business" side of the 1991 dichotomy [E3-43] because its product is a commodity
[E4-59, E2-59] and its returns are management-made [E3-29, E4-63] and regulator-set [E5-55]. It passes Q2
only through [E2-58], and the corpus names the two forms that door takes for a bank: the cost of its funding
[E4-60] and the cost of its operations [E3-76]. A bank with neither is OUT. Because the advantage is
management-made, it is perishable, which is why Q3 is the deciding gate for a bank [E3-29] — Q2 IN for a bank
is always NARROW.** Four runs reached this by different routes; the corpus says it in one place.

## The change to `THE FRAMEWORK v4.md`, if approved
In Q2, after [E2-58]: *"For a bank, [E2-58] is the only door [E4-59, E2-59], and it has two forms: the cost of
funding [E4-60] and the cost of operations [E3-76]. Both are management-made [E3-29, E4-63] and regulator-set
[E5-55], so a bank's Q2 IN is NARROW by construction and Q3 decides the file. [E3-43] supplies the test a bank
is measured against; it does not classify banks."* Section VI gains no convention.

## Verdicts of record this would touch
- **None.** CCB, SOFI and TFC failed on filed evidence that stands under this reading; ACNB and JPM passed
  through the door the reading names; MKL, WTM, L, CB, TRV and AIG are insurers and unaffected. The three
  mis-sourced sentences are corrected by the addendum above.
