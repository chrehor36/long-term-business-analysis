# RE-RUN — the six live names under the Q3 weight rule
**2026-08-27.** The manager standard added a step that runs *before* any Q3 finding: declare
how much Q3 weighs, because the business sets it **[E3-38, E1-16, E3-29]**. This applies that
step to the existing opportunity set. **No valuation changes here** — the ranking in
`REGRESSION - the six live names under v4.md` stands. What changes is whether Q3 is a
qualitative overlay or a binary gate, and for one name it changes the answer.

## The rule being applied

**How much damage can this manager do before you can react?**

| determinant | raises Q3 to a gate | source |
|---|---|---|
| Daily execution | have-to-be-smart-**every-day**, not have-to-be-smart-once | **[E3-38]** |
| Control | whole business, no exit | **[E1-16]** |
| Leverage | small asset errors destroy equity | **[E3-29]** |

**None → overlay; price does the work. Any one → gate, and no price compensates.**

## TJX and NCLTY — retailers, and the corpus names the case

Buffett's own example is not an analogy here, it is the same industry:

> "if you were smart enough to buy a network TV station very early in the game, you could put
> in a shiftless and backward nephew to run things, and the business would still do well for
> decades… **For a retailer, hiring that nephew would be an express ticket to bankruptcy.**"
> — **[E3-38]**, 1995 letter

Off-price retail is a buying-and-turns business re-won every season. **Q3 is a gate for both.**
NCLTY's Q3 cannot close at all until the FY2024 statements and the competitor row are in —
already recorded as UNRESEARCHED with named artifacts.

## HRB — the finding that matters, and it lands on the top-ranked name

**H&R Block carries $3,256M of assets against $117M of equity: 27.72:1.** That is *above* the
twenty-to-one the corpus calls "a common ratio in this industry" when describing banks, and
the mechanism it names applies arithmetically:

> "When assets are twenty times equity … **mistakes that involve only a small portion of
> assets can destroy a major portion of equity.** … Because leverage of 20:1 magnifies the
> effects of managerial strengths and weaknesses, **we have no interest in purchasing shares
> of a poorly-managed bank at a 'cheap' price.**" — **[E3-29]**, 1990 letter

**Two honest qualifications, because this is a judgment and not a mechanical rule:**

1. **HRB is not a bank.** Its assets are seasonal cash and receivables, not a levered loan
   book. It is not taking credit risk with depositors' money, which is the risk the 1990
   passage is actually about.
2. **The thin equity is a deliberate capital-structure choice, and it is the *good* kind.**
   HRB has retired shares at **−6.63%/yr since 2015 and −7.12%/yr over five years** — the
   fastest of the six by a wide margin, and precisely the behaviour **[E5-01, E5-08]** praise.
   The buybacks *created* the thin equity.

**So the same fact reads as discipline through one lens and fragility through the other.**
That is exactly why v4 deleted the 10:1 ceiling: a ratio cannot make this call.

> ## CORRECTION 2026-08-27 — the leverage ruling below was WRONG, and Test A caught it
>
> This section originally ruled Q3 a gate **because of** the 27.72:1 ratio. Two independent
> analysts, running HRB blind under Test A with no sight of this file, both rejected that
> and both gave the same reason: **the ratio fires but the mechanism does not.**
>
> **[E3-29]** is about *"mistakes that involve only a small portion of assets"* destroying
> equity. That requires an asset book in which to make such mistakes. H&R Block has none —
> net debt is **$532.8M against $1,056.9M of EBITDA (0.50x)**, interest cover **13.1x**, and
> the thin equity is an artifact of ~$500M/yr of buybacks retiring stock, not of asset risk.
> Analyst 1: *"no asset book magnifies managerial acts."* Analyst 2: *"elevated but not
> binding."*
>
> **The verdict does not change; the reason does.** Q3 is still a **GATE** for HRB — but on
> **daily execution [E3-38]**, the same determinant as TJX and NCLTY: 6,802 offices and
> ~73,400 seasonal hires, with all revenue earned in a ten-week window and no way to recover
> a botched season. That is the retailer case Buffett names, not the bank case.
>
> **Applying a determinant on its arithmetic while ignoring its stated mechanism is exactly
> the error the deleted 10:1 ceiling made.** Recorded rather than silently edited.

**The ruling: Q3 is a GATE for HRB on daily execution [E3-38], and no price compensates.**
The leverage ratio is recorded as context, not as the trigger — see the correction above.

## ASML — a gate, on Munger's own reasoning

Not because of leverage (2.58:1) but because the business must stay technically superior
indefinitely. Munger drew this exact distinction about Precision Castparts:

> "in the early days… **we buy a business an idiot can manage, because sooner or later, an
> idiot will**… But the world has gotten harder… **A business like Precision Castparts
> requires a very superior management that's going to stay superior for a long time.**"
> — 2016 meeting

EUV lithography is that class. **Q3 is a gate.** ASML also sits at 1.4× the most generous
value, so nothing is actionable regardless.

## V — the one clear overlay

A payment network already built is close to the have-to-be-smart-once case: the rails, the
scheme rules, and the two-sided network are in place, and quarterly execution does not
re-decide them. Leverage is low.

**Q3 is an overlay for Visa.** A mediocre manager would be survivable; price does the work.

**Two refusals, both reported rather than worked around:**
- **`no diluted share count in XBRL`** — Visa tags diluted shares dimensionally by share
  class, so no flat total exists. Same refusal `screen.py` gives, for the same reason. The
  count must be read off the 10-Q cover by hand.
- The equity figure above resolves to **FY2011** for the same dimensional reason. **It is
  stale and is not used** — the overlay ruling rests on business type, not on that number.

## TBTC — and a correction

**Table Trac Inc is a casino management systems company, not a bank.** An earlier note in
this session called it "a small bank"; that was wrong and nothing was built on it. Assets are
**$14M against $13M of equity, 1.12:1**. No leverage tick.

**Q3 is an overlay on the weight rule.** But the succession question that has been sitting in
TBTC's file is **misfiled, and the standard says where it goes:**

> "**if a business requires a superstar to produce great results, the business itself cannot
> be deemed great.** … The partnership's moat will go when the surgeon goes."
> — **[E4-23]**, 2007 letter

At $14M of assets with a founder-led operation, key-person dependence is a **Q2 moat defect**,
not a Q3 manager finding. **Moving it to Q2 makes it stop the run earlier and for the right
reason** — and it means a good answer on the manager would not have cured it, which is the
whole point of **[E4-23]**.

TBTC is also **the only one of the six issuing shares** — `+0.42%/yr` since 2017, `+0.74%/yr`
over five years. That is SBC dilution, not the *"hell-bent on issuing shares"* of **[E5-15]**;
recorded as a prompt to read, not a flag fired.
