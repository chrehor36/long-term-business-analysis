# INVENTIONS — WHAT WAS DELETED, AND WHAT IT COST
**2026-08-26.** The audit trail for v4.0's founding decision: *every rule traces to a
verbatim quote, or it goes.*

v3.1 carried **66 numeric or mechanical rules**. Classified against the ledger:

| | count |
|---|---|
| **CORPUS-SOURCED** — a ledger row states this number or rule | **9** |
| **DERIVED** — the corpus states a related but different number | **14** |
| **INVENTED** — no corpus basis at all | **43** |

All 43 are deleted. This file records what each one did, so that if something later
breaks, the cause is findable rather than mysterious.

**Two of the four things the 2026-08-26 audit said demonstrably work rested on invented or
mis-cited numbers.** That is the uncomfortable finding this register exists to preserve.

# I. THE DELETIONS THAT COST SOMETHING REAL

## 1. The 10:1 leverage ceiling — the best mechanical rule in the framework
**What it did.** Assets ÷ equity > 10:1 was an automatic FAIL for financial businesses. It
caught Citigroup at mid-2007 (19:1) with no judgment required, plus Lincoln National
(17.5:1), State Street (11.2:1), Principal (20.8:1) and a sweep of banks.

**Why it goes.** It cited `[E3-02 context]` — and E3-02's quote is the *institutional
imperative* sentence that happens to follow the leverage passage in the same paragraph.
**The ceiling never had a source.** The real passage is now rowed at **[E3-29]**, and it
says something different:

> "The banking business is no favorite of ours. **When assets are twenty times equity — a
> common ratio in this industry** — mistakes that involve only a small portion of assets can
> destroy a major portion of equity. […] Because leverage of 20:1 magnifies the effects of
> **managerial strengths and weaknesses**, we have no interest in purchasing shares of a
> **poorly-managed** bank at a 'cheap' price. Instead, our only interest is in buying into
> **well-managed** banks at fair prices." — 1990 letter

Buffett states no ceiling. He calls twenty-to-one *normal for the industry*, concludes about
**management quality**, and then buys Wells Fargo at roughly that leverage.

**And the arithmetic traps it.** At the corpus figure of 20:1, **Citigroup at 19:1 passes** —
so the rule cannot be moved to its own source without destroying the case that validated it.
It was never a stricter version of a corpus rule; it was a different rule wearing a citation.

**What replaces it.** Q3 management quality, plus Q4's named, quantified failure mechanism
and the three strengths of staying power **[E5-11]**. Leverage is judged and stated, not
screened. **See the verification note at the end of this file: Citigroup 2007 must be
re-run without the ratio, and the answer recorded either way.**

## 2. The 50% × 2-year stress test — Gate 4's only mechanical test for non-financials
**What it did.** "Survives a 50% owner-earnings decline for two consecutive years" was the
survival test for every non-financial business.

**Why it goes.** Self-confessed as invented, and it applied the same test to a retailer, a
bank and a software company. The corpus does the opposite **[E3-24]**: it names the specific
mechanism, quantifies it from filed figures, and attaches a likelihood — 10% of the loan
book impaired at 30% severity, producing roughly break-even, *"a low-level possibility, not
a likelihood."*

**What replaces it.** Q4 now requires the mechanism to be **named**, **quantified** and
**given a likelihood**. If none can be named, the honest verdict is UNKNOWABLE. This is
harder and slower, and it is what the corpus actually does.

## 3. The 4% hurdle floor and the +1% / +2% size premiums
**What they did.** The floor set a minimum hurdle when sovereign yields were low; the
premiums raised the bar for small and micro-caps. Together they set the pass mark for every
mechanical screen ever run in this project.

**Why they go.** Both self-confessed as invented. The size premiums also ran *against* the
corpus, which flexes the rate on **certainty**, not size **[E3-13]** — and they
double-counted against the certainty spread, a windage violation nobody had flagged.

**What replaces them.** Nothing, and nothing is needed. Q5 **ranks** rather than setting a
threshold **[E4-21]**, so there is no pass mark to floor. A micro-cap with a thin premium
simply ranks below something better.

## 4. The Coca-Cola clause (70% of hurdle for a generational moat)
**What it did.** Let a great business pass at 70% of the hurdle. It was the only mechanism
keeping the wonderful-business-at-a-fair-price path open, and without it Coca-Cola 1988
fails the framework outright.

**Why it goes.** 70% is reverse-engineered from KO 1988 itself (6.7% against ~9% bonds =
74%), which is curve-fitting to a single case. It was also formally in force with **no field
in the v3.1 template** — a rule nobody could actually apply.

**What replaces it.** The ranking. A wonderful business at a fair price ranks where it
ranks; if it is the best available, you take it. **See the verification note: KO 1988 must
still be buyable under v4, or the deletion is wrong.**

## 5. The margin-of-safety ladder
**What it did.** 10% base, +10 narrow moat, +10 per unresolved inversion, +10 tight
fortress, 50% floor for permanent-loss risk, 60% ceiling.

**Why it goes.** Only the **60% ceiling** has a verbatim source (the Grand Canyon
arithmetic, 4,000lb on a 10,000lb bridge **[E3-26]**) and it survives. Everything else was
invented, and the `+10 NARROW` line **contradicted the ruling printed directly above it**:
certainty is priced once, in the spread, and moat class *is* certainty.

**What replaces it.** The corpus's own illustrations as reference points — the bridge at
~35% **[E3-25]**, "closer to a dollar on the dollar" for a business you understand
**[E4-12]**, "dollar bills for 80 cents" **[E5-09]** — and a run that states the margin it
used and which illustration it sits nearest.

# III. WHAT SURVIVED, AND WHY

**Corpus-sourced, kept as-is:**
- The owner-earnings formula, all four constraints **[E2-23]**
- The franchise test, three conditions **[E3-03]**
- The two buyback conditions, with "material discount" left unquantified **[E5-08]**
- The humility clause on any buyback finding **[E4-13]**
- Integrity as binary, permanent and filings-based **[E2-26]**
- Conservatism spent once, at the end, with a windage count **[E4-11]**
- The 60% margin ceiling **[E3-26]**
- Likelihood vocabulary instead of numeric probabilities **[E3-24]**
- Value reported as a range, not a point estimate **[E4-01]**
- Pre-committed exit metric **[E1-02]**

**Derived, kept with its arithmetic shown:**
- The **+3%** certainty-spread floor — 10% less 7% in **[E3-13]**. The only spread the
  corpus supplies. Wider spreads follow the direction he states, but he gives no ladder, so
  a run states its spread and its reason.

**Invented, kept and confessed** — the full list is Section VI of `THE FRAMEWORK v4.md`.
The main ones: splitting "too hard" into UNRESEARCHED and UNKNOWABLE; the evidence ladder;
computing owner earnings via operating cash flow; the maintenance-capex band; round-number
formatting.

# IV. WHAT THE DELETIONS MEAN FOR THE SCREENER

**The wide mechanical screen loses its pass mark.** Every historical screen in `Screens/`
was scored against a hurdle built from the 4% floor and the size premiums, both now gone.

**This is not fatal, because the screen was never the edge.** On corrected data the
mechanical layer wins 49.6% of 944 one-year holdings, with the median pass trailing the
index by 1.45 points. A coin flip does not need a precise pass mark.

**What the screener does in v4:** it computes owner-earnings yield against the correct
sovereign for each name's earnings currency and **ranks**. There is no pass/fail column.
The output is an ordered opportunity set, which is what **[E4-21]** asks for, and the top of
that list is where the six questions get spent.

# V. VERIFICATION OWED

Two cases decide whether the deletions in Section I were right. **Neither is optional, and
both must be recorded whichever way they come out.**

1. **Citigroup, mid-2007, without the leverage ceiling.** Does Q3 (management quality, the
   institutional imperative) plus Q4 (staying power's three strengths, the named failure
   mechanism) still reject it? If yes, the ceiling was redundant and the deletion is free.
   If no, v4 has a hole where the ratio used to be, and that hole must be written down here.

2. **Coca-Cola, 1988, without the Coca-Cola clause.** At roughly 15× earnings against ~9%
   bonds, does the wonderful-business-at-a-fair-price path stay open under the ranking? If
   it does not, the deletion closed a path the corpus explicitly endorses **[E2-16]** and
   must be reconsidered.

A third check is standing rather than one-off: **the six live names** (TJX, V, HRB, TBTC,
NCLTY, ASML) must reproduce their v3.1 conclusions under v4, or each difference must be
explained. HRB is the sharpest test — it is the only one that currently clears its
conservative case.
