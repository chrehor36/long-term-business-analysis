# CASE 2026-10-26 - ASML at Q1: a technology monopoly with a service annuity, for the operator's decision

*Written by the dispatching session at the operator's request, after the operator questioned the box of
`Test Runs/2026-10-26 Run - ASML ASML Holding.md` (TOO HARD (NATURE) at Q1): "they have no major competition, by a large
margin. they are the only game in town and most advanced." **This is not a rule change, and none is proposed**: the
operator said so ("we dont want a rule change"). The question is narrower: did the run read the existing Q1 correctly for
this one name? The facts behind it are in
`Test Runs/_research 2026-10-26 ASML/AFTER THE FOLD - system and service sales 2014-2025.md`. Every quote below is the
framework's own, from `Framework/THE FRAMEWORK v5.md`, Q1, with its ledger id.*

## The question

Q1 asks for "a reasonable fix on about what the earning power and competitive position will look like in five or 10
years" **[M2012-065]**. The run answered the competitive half (ASML is the only maker of EUV scanners; Canon and Nikon
compete only in DUV) and found the earning-power half out of reach: how much lithography future processes use, the price
and adoption of High NA, and export rules on China. It called the call close.

## The case for reading Q1 as IN

1. **The winner can be named**, which is the test the rows set for an industry: "there's industries we know that may have a
   wonderful future, but we don't have the faintest idea who the winners will be" **[M2012-067]**. Here the winner is not in
   doubt.
2. **The product can stay opaque if the economics are clear.** "I never would understand the chemistry of it, but I — but
   that's not necessarily vital. What is important is that **I understand the economic dynamics of the industry.**"
   **[M2011-014]**.
3. **How far off could the analyst be?** The rows ask for "some model in our mind of how far off we can be"
   **[M2011-084]**. On the filed record, system sales fell in no year from 2014 to 2025 (flat in 2015 and 2024), and service
   sales, about a quarter of the total, fell once, by 2.1%. The errors in the insiders' forecasts were all on the low side.
4. **An omission inside the circle is an error.** "What's an error is when it's something we understand, and we stand there
   and stare at it, and we don't do anything." **[M2001-006]**.

## The case for the run's box

1. **The technology filter is the first filter.** "if something comes in where there's a technological component that's of
   significance [...] it won't make it through the filter." **[M1998-008]**. ASML's position rests on the most advanced
   technology in its industry, which the operator's own words confirm.
2. **Understanding the product is not understanding the economics.** "Oh, **we understand the product.** We understand what
   it does for people. **We just don't know the economics of it 10 years from now.**" **[M2000-104]**. The operator's point
   (the only game in town) and the run's verdict (economics out of reach) are both inside this one sentence.
3. **The insiders did write a forecast down, and it missed by half.** Test 5 asks whether the insiders would write the
   forecast down **[M2000-105]**. ASML did: 2025 revenue modelled in 2018 at EUR 15 to 24B, actual EUR 32.7B; 2026 guidance
   moved from EUR 34 to 39B to EUR 44 to 45B inside nine months. A forecast missed by that much, even on the happy side, is
   not a fix on the earning power.
4. **The analyst called it close, and doubt decides.** "if you have doubts about something being into your circle of
   competence, it isn't." **[M2002-092]**.
5. **The past record is not the ten-year view, and study does not cure it.** "We couldn't solve this problem, moreover, even
   if we were to spend years intensely studying those industries." **[L1993-023]**. The service series above is study of
   exactly this kind.
6. **The service annuity does not decide.** By Q1's paragraph on parts, the part that matters is the one with more than half
   of five-year operating profit, or else the largest; system sales are about three quarters of the total.

## What a different reading would and would not change

- **It would not make ASML a purchase.** The run's own computation, at EUR 1,424.80, gives 1.6% to 2.1% expected at the
  price against the floor of about ten percent; even the 2020 to 2025 sales growth of 18.5% carried ten years gives about
  6.6%. Read IN at Q1, the file would most likely close OUT at Q7, with a fair price near EUR 272.
- **It would need Q2 to Q6 answered,** which the run did not reach. A reading of Q1 alone does not produce a box.

## The options

- **A. The box stands: TOO HARD (NATURE) at Q1.** The facts are kept on disk; no further work. *Recommended.* Five rows of
  Q1 bear directly on this name and four of them (the technology filter, the product-versus-economics row, the doubt row and
  study-does-not-cure-it) point to the run's box; the record of steady sales is the past, and the question is the future.
- **B. A second blind run on ASML,** to test whether another analyst reads Q1 the same way, as the framework's two-session
  rule does for held names (section I). If it closes IN at Q1 and runs on, the likely end is OUT at Q7 on price, and an
  alert at the fair price would be armed at its fold. Cost: one full run.
