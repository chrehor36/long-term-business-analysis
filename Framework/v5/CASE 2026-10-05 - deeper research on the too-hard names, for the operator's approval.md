## Why this exists

"unknowable" ones."* HRB had just been kept on WATCH, with NO ADD, because both v5 Test A' analysts closed its purchase run

work is done, and say why more work would not help", and "Study does not cure it", on three rows. This case asks whether

## What the corpus says

**1. The rows name two causes of "too hard", and only one is the industry.**

> "In many industries, of course, Charlie and I can't determine whether we are dealing with a "pet rock" or a "Barbie." We
> couldn't solve this problem, moreover, even if we were to spend years intensely studying those industries. **Sometimes our
> own intellectual shortcomings would stand in the way of understanding, and in other cases the nature of the industry would
> be the roadblock.** For example, a business that must deal with fast-moving technology is not going to lend itself to
> reliable evaluations of its long-term economics." — **[L1993-023]**, 1993

The same row v5 cites for "study does not cure it" separates the analyst's shortcoming from the nature of the industry. The
first is a fact about the reader; the second is a fact about the business.

> "I haven’t done the work and I’m not sure if I did the work I would understand them. I think they are harder to
> understand, frankly, and to develop a fix on, than our kinds." — **[M1994-026]**, 1994

Here both states are in one sentence: the work not done, and the doubt whether doing it would help. The speaker does not
treat the first as settling the second.

## The reading
The rows support three things and refuse a fourth.
- **Supported:** "too hard" has two causes, the reader's and the industry's **[L1993-023]**, **[M1994-026]**.
- **Supported:** for the reader's cause, the remedy is work of a named kind: list what is not known **[M1999-129]**, keep only
  what is knowable and important **[M2006-076]**, read first, then go to competitors, customers, suppliers and employees
  **[M1998-144]**, and aim it at rejecting the hypothesis, not confirming it **[M1998-144]**.
- **Supported:** for the industry's cause, no work helps **[L1993-023]**, **[L1999-018]**, **[M2008-086]**, and the test of
  which cause applies is whether insiders would write the forecast down **[M2000-105]**.
- **Refused:** research as a way to widen the circle because the circle has nothing cheap in it **[M1995-018]**, or to rescue a
  name the analyst wants to own **[M2002-092]**. That is operator rule 9 in the speakers' words.

**What AI changes, and what it does not.** The speakers' scuttlebutt was the expensive last 10 or 20 percent: travel, calls,
competitors' offices. Much of what it found is now on paper and cheap to read: competitors' filings, government statistics,
regulators' records, published complaints, hiring, pricing. An AI cannot "talk to competitors" in the speakers' sense; it can
read what they have filed and said in public. So AI lowers the cost of curing the reader's cause. It does nothing to the
industry's cause: a forecast nobody inside the industry would write down stays unwritten however much is read. The risk runs
the other way too. Cheap reading makes it cheap to collect support for an answer the analyst already wants, and for a held name
the analyst wants one answer (operator rule 9, **[E4-26]**).

## The change, if approved

**A. The TOO HARD box records its cause.** Every TOO HARD in a run or review states one of two causes (CONVENTION, ours; the
rows name both causes **[L1993-023]** but give them no labels):
- **TOO HARD (WORK):** the question is knowable and important, and the work has not been done **[M1994-026]**, **[M2006-076]**.
  This is an open file, not a verdict.
- **TOO HARD (NATURE):** the deciding question is a forecast that the industry's own insiders would not write down
  **[M2000-105]**, **[L1993-023]**, **[L1999-018]**. This is the closed box v5 already has. No further work is done.

**B. A TOO HARD (WORK) file gets a deep-research pass, pre-registered.** Before any reading:
1. **The list.** "What do I not know that I need to know?" **[M1999-129]**, written as numbered questions, each marked knowable
   or not **[M2006-076]**. If the deciding question is not knowable, the file is TOO HARD (NATURE) and the pass stops here.
2. **The disconfirming answer, written first.** For each knowable question: what evidence would answer it, where that evidence
   is, and **what answer would close the file OUT**, so that the research is "to possibly reject your original hypothesis"
   **[M1998-144]**.
3. **The reading.** Primary documents first (the company's and its competitors' filings, government and regulator data), then
   the documentary scuttlebutt: competitors, customers, suppliers, former and current employees, as far as they are on the
   public record **[M1998-144]**. Every fact carries its source; aggregators flagged.
4. **The close.** The file returns IN, OUT, or TOO HARD (NATURE). It may not return TOO HARD (WORK) twice on the same question:
   a second pass that cannot answer it is the evidence that the question was not knowable **[M2008-086]**.
5. **For a held name, a second analyst.** Where the operator holds the name, the pass is run by a session that does not see
   `PORTFOLIO.md` or the holding review, and its result is compared with the first; the incentive is the analyst's
   **[M2002-092]**, **[E4-26]**.

**C. What it does not do.** It does not widen the circle to find something to buy **[M1995-018]**. It does not reopen a TOO
HARD (NATURE) because the price fell **[M2000-038]**. It does not let research outvote a doubt: a pass that ends with the analyst
"not sure whether you understand it or not" ends TOO HARD **[M2002-092]**.
