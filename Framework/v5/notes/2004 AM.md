## What this session taught
The FY2003 letter and the morning both return to promises booked now and paid later: long-tail insurance earnings are whatever the CEO wants and gatekeepers rarely catch it (L2003-015); Freddie Mac misstated billions under able directors, an auditor and many analysts (M2004-025); traders on both sides of one contract book profits, and bonuses paid now outlast consequences that arrive decades later (M2004-026, M2004-031).
Buffett's confessions are about delay and price anchoring: he dithered over closing Gen Re Securities, whose troubles would correlate with Berkshire's and claim capital when it was most valuable (L2003-018), and he stopped buying Walmart when the price rose a little, now put at $10 billion (M2004-038, M2004-039). The letter adds a third, not selling during the Bubble (L2003-021).
On valuation, the meeting gives a method: realistic inputs with errors on the conservative side, one margin of safety at the end, and no companies that need extreme assumptions (M2004-055, M2004-056); a growth rate above the discount rate gives infinity, so growth must be assumed to slow and stop (M2004-023, M2004-024).
On allocation, categories and percentage targets are rejected in favour of a short-term default and action on individual understood bargains, with heavy buying when one is found (M2004-019, M2004-022, M2004-041, M2004-045).
On debt, the letter allows it for riskless liquid positions, sound receivables and utilities whose stable earnings would service it in harsh times (L2003-016, L2003-017, L2003-019), while the meeting's closing answer gives the reason for aversion: leverage is what stops a sound hand from being played out (M2004-065).
On boards and pay, the letter defines independence as willingness to challenge a forceful CEO, ranks business savvy above the other qualities, and gives fund directors two declarations to make (L2003-010 to 012); the meeting adds that checklists are no substitute for thinking about the right CEO (M2004-005).
On competition, an improvement every rival can copy goes to customers, with the textile machinery as the narrated case (M2004-053, M2004-054); in insurance nothing structural protects a firm, so managers decide the result (L2003-014).
