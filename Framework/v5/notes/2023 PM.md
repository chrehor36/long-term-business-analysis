## What this session taught
The afternoon opened on banks and kept returning to how fast money can leave. Fear is contagious and deposits are no longer sticky, so a run takes seconds (M2023-047); the answer for a bank is to compare what it owns with what can be demanded of it tomorrow (M2023-103), to read its 10-K for one-sided options handed to borrowers (M2023-049), and to be cautious about owning one when the dynamic has changed in a way nobody can measure (M2023-054). A sound bank is one that declines what other banks do (M2023-053), and Munger would judge it by whether its people want to avoid trouble or to get rich (M2023-052, M2023-055). Penalties must fall on those who caused the damage, or behaviour does not change (M2023-050).
Options recur as a hazard on both sides of a deal: the seller's option at Pilot (M2023-060), the free option a public bid gives the target (M2023-087). Several tests separate appearances from economics: earnings grown by reinvested capital at an unchanged return (M2023-081), owned sites against market share (M2023-061), the same industry name covering different businesses (M2023-082), winners at a given time with no permanent place (M2023-100), and the talent that captures the money in entertainment (M2023-080). On brands: customers who ask by name (M2023-073), gifts that are not price-shopped (M2023-075), and the See's experiments that kept failing because the economics tempted every manager (M2023-099).
On conduct, the session gives a cluster of personal rules: avoid mistakes that take you out of the game (M2023-065), underspend and avoid costly debt (M2023-066, M2023-069), remove unreliable people fast (M2023-071), and do not pretend to understand what you do not (M2023-057). Munger adds that he prefers the easy, identifiable job to the impossible one (M2023-085), and his own early blindness at US Steel as a lesson in observing a business's basic economics (M2023-106).
