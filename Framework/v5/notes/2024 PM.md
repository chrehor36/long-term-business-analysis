## What this session taught
The first afternoon without Munger is shorter and more personal than the morning. On capital, Buffett moves all allocation, businesses and stocks alike, to the next CEO, and says his own view changed as the sums grew (M2024-040); the CEO must be able to act when others with money are paralyzed (M2024-041). Operating managers need not be capital allocators, and the owner has to tell which ones are (M2024-048). He regrets only the big things he could have understood and missed (M2024-049), and says quick decisions rest on years of thinking about the parameters (M2024-050), with the moment of action coming when observations crystallize, which can be prepared for but not forced (M2024-052). Two losses are owned openly: the Baltimore store taught consumer behavior, not retailing (M2024-051), and Paramount made him "smarter" and "poorer" (M2024-055). On BNSF he concedes the 2008 to 2009 return came from when the money went in as much as where (M2024-057), and repeats that an essential, unbuildable asset can pay well on a price far below replacement value (M2024-058). On advisers: anyone approaching a holder of large resources wants to use them (M2024-062), and the answer is one commission and owning the stock (M2024-063). The last third of the session is about living: luck, wills, heroes, kindness.
