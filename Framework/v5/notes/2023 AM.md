## What this session taught
The FY2022 letter is short and mostly repeats, but adds a few clean lines. Berkshire runs on trust more than rules, forgiving business mistakes and never personal misconduct (L2022-001). Market prices are foolish both ways while controlled businesses are almost never cheap, because an owner not under duress never sells at a panic price (L2022-002). Buffett grades most of his own allocation decisions "so-so" and credits a dozen good ones plus long holding (L2022-003, L2022-004), warns that even his favoured operating figure can be manipulated (L2022-006), and binds future CEOs to own shares bought with their own money (L2022-007). Half the letter's lessons are Munger's, printed as a list (L2022-008 to L2022-020).
The morning session's recurring theme is judging people and businesses apart. A good business hides poor management for years, so successor management is hard to judge (M2023-045). Boards should not take Wall Street's view (M2023-046). Hiring goes on the person, not schooling (M2023-025), and talent is often already in the company (M2023-026). On investing: opportunity comes from others' dumb things (M2023-010) against Munger's "get used to making less" (M2023-009). Concentration is safe only for one who knows the edge of his competence (M2023-036, M2023-037). The Japanese houses were bought on a 14% earnings yield against half a percent of financing (M2023-031). Insurance sells the certainty of payment (M2023-013, M2023-014). Buffett admits two errors: keeping an aging manager (M2023-006) and selling Apple for a tax reason (M2023-035).

## What in the brief was wrong or unclear
Section 6 says a transcript row's `heading` is "the full `### N. ...` line the passage sits under", and the conventions cover a heading whose reply answers the previous question. They do not cover a question title printed without its `### N.` marker. This transcript prints "′ What gives you opportunities is other people doing dumb things’" at line 287 with no `### 12.`, the file has no `### 12.` line, and the helper's `unit` output silently lists headings 11 then 13, so nothing flags the gap. I gave the three rows from that passage (M2023-009 to 011) the `### 11.` line, the last `### N.` line above them, and put a HEADING FLAG in each row's notes and in the Rows list. I kept every elision inside lines 289-335 so that no quote crosses the unmarked title. The operator may prefer the printed title itself as the heading, or a rule that an unmarked title counts as a heading boundary.
