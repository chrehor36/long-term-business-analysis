## What this session taught
The afternoon is mostly about the edges of competence and the limits size puts on returns. A circle of competence is built by reading widely, finding where one's understanding differs from competitors', and being realistic about its perimeter; inside it one must master how change affects the business, and learning one is incompetent somewhere is a gain (M2019-053, M2019-083). Knowing who is winning in technology is not enough when others know the game better, and judgment is never subcontracted (M2019-048); a high batting average matters more than catching the biggest winners (M2019-049).
On decisions: there is no formula for risk; the first question is whether one is reasonably sure of knowing what one is doing, then gain against affordable loss (M2019-077); staffs, committees and spreadsheets return the answer the boss wants, just over the hurdle (M2019-076), and an acquisition department will acquire something (M2019-069).
On capital: cash is held because the chances to use it come in clumps, when others will not allocate (M2019-060), and Munger warns against committing the last ounce at the top (M2019-061); a niche business's earnings go wherever they earn best, since money is fungible (M2019-086); large sums and large staffs erode great records (M2019-084, M2019-085).
On owners: run the company for owners with nearly all their net worth in it, so that no one who buys near intrinsic value loses permanently (M2019-062), and treat their money as one's own (M2019-073); report to them what one would want in their place (M2019-066).
On honesty: earnings lifted by paying people in unexpensed options are a lie about the accounting (M2019-078), and a director whose fees matter to him is not independent (M2019-058).
