## What this session taught
Risk is put in plain terms twice: volatility and beta are not risk (M2007-075, M2007-076), risk comes from a business's economics and from not knowing what you are doing (M2007-077), the one large loss came from a misjudged business (M2007-078), and a catastrophe book is judged as the owner of many roulette wheels judges it (M2007-080).
On management, the afternoon gives checks from documents alone: the industry's most important figure left out of the report (M2007-082), a letter written by investor relations (M2007-083), and people who give themselves away by what they treat as important (M2007-090); Munger ranks business quality above management quality (M2007-085), and Buffett adds his own twenty years in textiles (M2007-086).
On price, the government bond is named as the base yardstick with a fuzzy premium above it (M2007-095, M2007-096), and Munger sets opportunity cost against the hurdle rate (M2007-097, M2007-098), which Buffett carries into how often a holding is reviewed (M2007-104, M2007-105).
On acquisitions, a long passage on boards and stock deals: deals reach the board already decided (M2007-121), big deals usually hurt the acquirer (M2007-122), and the question in a stock deal is what is given against what is got, not dilution (M2007-123), with Dexter as the worked case (M2007-124) and the accounts hiding it (M2007-125).
On business type, capital intensity caps returns (M2007-112, M2007-130), easily shipped high-labor products erode (M2007-116 to M2007-118), and a product can be worth its price yet lose to a free substitute (M2007-134).
Several personal-conduct rules appear in the young people's questions (M2007-108, M2007-109, M2007-113 to M2007-115).
