## What this session taught
The FY2000 letter states the valuation formula outright for the first time in the L rows: three questions (how certain the birds, when and how many, the risk-free rate, taken as the long-term US bond yield), giving the maximum price (L2000-021), applied to every outlay so all uses of capital can be ranked (L2000-022).
The usual yardsticks, P/E, yield, book and growth, are only clues to the amount and timing of cash, and growth can destroy value (L2000-023); the meeting gives the condition: growth adds value only when the added capital returns cash well above the interest rate (M2001-018), with airlines and See's as the two poles (M2001-019).
Estimates are a range; usually too wide to conclude anything, occasionally showing a price startlingly low even on conservative figures (L2000-024, L2000-025). Where no range gives conviction, as in new or fast-changing industries, the commitment is speculation (L2000-026, L2000-027).
The meeting's screen is said twice in nearly the same words: something understood, a durable competitive advantage, management liked, a sensible price (M2001-024, and M2001-011 for advantage plus people).
Mistakes of omission are defined as the costliest and are counted only inside the circle of competence, with the eyedropper purchase as the worst form (M2001-004, M2001-006, M2001-007).
Management reporting is read for warning signs: EBITDA, vague accounting, public growth predictions, big baths, pension return assumptions (L2000-036, L2000-037 to 040, M2001-026, M2001-027).
In insurance, the cap on exposure is absolute (L2000-005, M2001-032), float cost ranks above float growth (M2001-029, M2001-030), and an uncapped hazard like asbestos disqualifies an otherwise attractive purchase.

## What in the brief was wrong or unclear
Section 6 says a letter row before the first section title takes "the letter's own title line", or "the file's first printed line when it has no title"; the FY2000 letter file's first printed line is a note about the performance table ("Note: The following table appears in the printed Annual Report..."), not the letter, so following the brief literally would have given a heading that is not the letter's. I used `BERKSHIRE HATHAWAY INC.` at line 105, the line the letter itself opens with, as 1999 AM and 2000 AM did. Second, the matcher the helper runs ignores case and punctuation, so a quote whose first letter I capitalised (M2001-031, "There’s" for the printed "there’s") passed; the brief says quotes are exact but the tool does not hold them to case, and section 7 gives no way to correct a passed quote other than a new row; I recorded it in Rows as a slip rather than writing a duplicate row. Third, the brief does not say whether a letter passage that repeats an earlier year's letter in substance but not word for word (here the full-reporting and calendar passages) is rowed; I treated close restatements as repeats and listed them with the ids they repeat, and rowed passages that add a new test (EBITDA, the 80% tax point).
