## What this session taught
The afternoon is about what not to stretch for. Do not guess commodity prices, do not reach for basis points on short-term money, do not borrow in a currency you cannot control, do not issue shares or sell a business to buy another, and never trade what you have and need for what you do not need (M2011-044, M2011-077, M2011-088, M2011-089, M2011-099, M2011-100). Risks that could threaten the whole are never borne, and the chief executive owns that judgment; Munger adds that a tiny chance of a dent in one part must be endurable (M2011-078, M2011-079).
On valuing: judge a business on tangible returns and its buyer on the full price; growth counts only at a high return on added capital; hold a mental model of the future and of one's possible error, and ignore the seller's banker's projections (M2011-060, M2011-081 to M2011-084). In inflation the winners need little capital and can price; a capital-heavy asset is protected by its replacement cost (M2011-054, M2011-055).
On deals: each commitment is measured against what else is available that day, not against the best deal ever made; no auctions; a cyclical business is bought like a seasonal one, cheap enough to share in all the years (M2011-071, M2011-073, M2011-074, M2011-101, M2011-102).
On insurance: break-even underwriting is the conservative assumption, the business tempts stupidity, and reinsurance looks easy because the rare loss is absent most years, a lesson Berkshire took fifteen years to learn (M2011-051, M2011-053, M2011-094).
On people: rationality, love of the work, the institution put first, values and capital allocation in a successor, and pay that sets the strike at what the business is worth and rises with retained money (M2011-056 to M2011-058, M2011-067, M2011-068). Munger's Costco and General Motors pair the right system with the danger of success turning into a disadvantage (M2011-063, M2011-064).

## What in the brief was wrong or unclear
The convention that "a two-speaker exchange is one row under the speaker who speaks first" assumes the first speaker states the lesson. At heading 19 Munger speaks first with a quip ("we were not going to participate in the transaction if they didn’t do it our way") and Buffett then states the rule ("we basically don’t participate in auctions"). I followed the convention as written and filed M2011-071 under MUNGER, with Buffett's statement inside the quote and the exchange named in the notes, but a synthesis that counts lessons by speaker will credit Munger with a rule Buffett stated, and M2001-064 (Buffett, no auctions) shows the same rule under the other speaker. The brief does not say whether "speaks first" means first in the exchange or first to state the lesson. A second, smaller gap: the brief gives no form for a speaker's restatement that falls between two passages of the other speaker carrying the same lesson (Munger's "We are very reluctant to issue shares", line 1233, inside Buffett's answer); I listed it under Rows and named it in M2011-100's notes rather than rowing it.
