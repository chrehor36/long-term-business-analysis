## What this session taught
The FY2007 letter sets out a full taxonomy of businesses: a great one has an enduring moat protecting high returns on capital, does not depend on a superstar, sits in a stable industry and needs little capital to grow; a good one earns well but must keep putting up more; a gruesome one grows, consumes capital and earns little, and its growth should repel (L2007-004 to L2007-010). The three savings accounts are the compact form.
The confessions sit beside the taxonomy: nearly losing See's over $5 million, and declining a See's-like station a trusted expert offered, are errors of omission by someone who knew the economics (L2007-011, L2007-012).
The letter's arithmetic tests of other people's numbers are new: a pension return assumption checked against what bonds can earn, an expected equity return translated into the index level it implies, and the proof that the helper-aided class must trail the passive one (L2007-017 to L2007-020).
The meeting carries that last point into advice: the know-nothing investor should diversify and index, the professional should load up on rare cinches, and the danger in concentration is leverage, as LTCM showed (M2008-048 to M2008-052, M2008-055 to M2008-057).
On buying, the session adds that bargains come from the market and never from private owners, that nothing is done "regardless of price", and that a manager is judged by passion and a long record, not by an interview (M2008-004 to M2008-006, M2008-041).
Much of the morning is personal conduct (passion, spouse, communication, an internal scorecard, living within income); these are rowed as personal conduct for the synthesis to keep or drop.

## What in the brief was wrong or unclear
The settled convention says new text inside an otherwise repeated reprint is rowed under its own printed heading, but the whitelist does not allow opening the previous year's report, so the reader cannot always tell whether a phrase is new. This unit's Acquisition Criteria reads "at least $75 million of pre-tax earnings unless the business will fit into one of our existing units"; the 2007 AM note quoted only the $75 million floor, R1997-001 fails against both reprints because of the old $25 million figure, and no earlier row or note carries the "existing units" clause, so I could not establish whether it is new this year. I did not row it, because it restates the tuck-in rule L2006-004 in substance, and recorded the doubt under Rows.
