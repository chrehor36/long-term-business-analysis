## What this session taught
The FY2017 letter is short and its new lessons are about discipline when others lose it. It names five qualities sought in a whole business, adding returns on net tangible assets and room for internal growth (L2017-002); it describes why acquisitive CEOs overpay, from the job selecting for doers to subordinates who gain from size (L2017-003); it warns that cheap debt makes even a high-priced deal accretive (L2017-004); and it states the guideline that the less prudent others are, the more prudent one must be (L2017-006). The bet supplies three lessons: look unimaginative or foolish for a long time if need be (L2017-009), do not measure risk by the bond share of a portfolio (L2017-010), and make few big easy decisions, reading a bond as a price-earnings multiple (L2017-011).
The report adds nothing new: the Owner's Manual, the Acquisition Criteria and the reprinted bet section repeat earlier text.
The morning's centre is incentives and candour: ignoring a faulty incentive system is worse than having one (M2018-012), scandals at sound companies have produced some of the best purchases (M2018-013), and holdings' value changes do not belong in the income account (M2018-019).
On businesses: an industry moat is not each company's moat (M2018-041), more moats are open to invasion and must be defended all the time (M2018-042), low cost is a moat (M2018-043), a distributor between giants has little room (M2018-021), and whoever takes cost out of a layered system wins, slowly (M2018-039).
On conduct: do not push terms to the limit against a counterparty with nowhere else to go (M2018-016); walk away from an inadequate price whatever the pull of employees and plants (M2018-020); bureaucracy damages decisions as well as costs (M2018-038).

## What in the brief was wrong or unclear
The 2017 convention says a restatement inside the same session "is named in the first row's notes and not re-rowed", but section 7's write-early rule means the first row has usually been appended and committed before the restatement is read, and the only tool for changing a committed row's notes is `erratum`, which section 7 reserves for notes that "turn out wrong". The convention therefore works only for a restatement inside the same heading or batch. I applied it there (M2018-012 and M2018-015 name the restatements at lines 185 and 201 of the same answer), and where a later heading restated an earlier row of this session while adding something new (M2018-019 against M2018-001, M2018-045 against the letter's L2017-001), I rowed it and named the earlier id in the new row's notes; no erratum was used to add a forward reference. The brief does not say whether a pure cross-heading restatement in one session should get an erratum on the first row, a new row, or only a line under Rows.
