## What this session taught
- The letter and the meeting restate the method in one line several times: sensible price, good and enduring economics, able and honest owner-minded managers, inside what one understands. The meeting adds the order Buffett asks in: understand it first, then economics and returns on capital.
- The moat is described in the speakers' own words: a wide, long-lasting moat around an economic castle with an honest lord. Every moat is under attack and most are worthless; the work is to ask why the castle still stands and how much depends on the lord. Munger translates it as scale advantages and low agency cost.
- Businesses that need their owner to stay smart are separated from those that needed him to be smart once; between a terrific business and a terrific manager, the business wins.
- Value is discounted cash, but the decision should be obvious enough not to need a written model; projections from interested parties are dismissed outright.
- Capital is never free: managers are charged and credited for it, pay is tied to what they control, alignment must run both ways, options are compensation, and shares issued are judged only by value received.
- On losses: a mistake is made at the time of decision; a loss need not be won back where it was lost, and trying to is usually a mistake rooted in human nature. USAir is narrated as a mistake with an explicit economic lesson about commodity costs.
- Insurance serves as a model for thinking about rare ruin: value float by size and cost, size risk by correlated worst case, solvency under extreme conditions is the only test a buyer should care about, stop writing when prices are silly.

## Tensions seen
- M1995-050 (never looked at a projection) and M1995-049 against M1995-032 and L1994-007 (value is discounted future cash). Munger states both halves himself at line 645: projections are "logically required by the circumstances" yet do more harm than good. The speakers resolve it by wanting the decision obvious without the numbers. I did not row a separate tension because no single passage was framed as one.
- M1995-034 (sat through dry spells, closed the partnership when nothing made sense) beside L1994-004 (do not let fear of unknowns defer deployment) and M1994-065 (no cash hoarding for a fall). The letter says do not wait on macro fear; the meeting says do wait when nothing makes sense. The difference is price against forecast, and the speakers do not draw it out here.
- M1995-044 (will not sell good businesses for an extra point a year) against L1994-013 (every capital decision judged by per-share value). Buffett names the cost and accepts it openly; it reads as a stated tradeoff rather than a contradiction.
- M1995-052 (judge by the past record, miss the bright-future turnaround) beside L1994-031 (buy the predictable long-term future). Not a contradiction; noted for synthesis.
