## What this session taught
The FY2005 letter turns twice to how a business's position changes without anyone seeing it: the moat widens or narrows daily through small acts (L2005-010), and short-term targets met by damaging cost, service or brand cannot be repaired later (L2005-011); the same letter confesses the opposite failure in management, years spent trying to sell Gen Re Securities instead of closing it, with "the time to act is now" (L2005-009).
On owners' arithmetic, the letter states for the first time in the letters read that owners as a group earn only what their businesses earn, less friction (L2005-016), and the Gotrocks parable turns it on advisers, consultants and performance fees (L2005-017, L2005-018); the meeting repeats it for professionals as a class (M2006-043 to M2006-046).
On pay, the letter gives a worked test of fixed-price options, which reward withholding dividends even from a stagnant business (L2005-014), names comparative pay data as the ratchet (L2005-015), and the meeting adds paying only for what the manager controls, with copper and oil as the cases (M2006-004).
On risk, the letter adds that diversity of uncorrelated earnings protects debt better than a high coverage ratio (L2005-019) and the "single zero" line (L2005-021); the meeting adds the hurricane case: a long history is the worst guide when conditions change, and one quiet year proves no price right (M2006-030, M2006-033), with Pascal's wager as the letter's rule for pricing under that ignorance (L2005-007).
On markets, Buffett describes how fundamentals turn into speculation through price history and envy (M2006-018), says he cannot judge a boom's end and buys on fundamentals instead (M2006-019, M2006-020), and confesses not selling Coca-Cola at 50 times earnings (M2006-029).
Several checks are new in this read: suspicion of a growth rate's base year (L2005-003), whether a holding company adds anything over owning the parts (L2005-002), the decline of self-assessment with age (L2005-022), and a larger margin where one's objectivity is weaker, as in buying one's own shares (M2006-039).
