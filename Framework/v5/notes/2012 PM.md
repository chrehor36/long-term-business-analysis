## What this session taught
The afternoon keeps returning to what to stay away from. Declining businesses, however cheap the multiple, are avoided because the same effort spent elsewhere pays more; Berkshire's own start in three failed declining businesses is the confession behind it (M2012-061 to M2012-064). New issues, high-commission products and industries whose winners cannot be named are never considered; ideas pass quick filters first, and to understand a business means a reasonable fix on its earning power and competitive position five to ten years out (M2012-065 to M2012-069, M2012-091).
Risk is treated as the avoidance of ruin, not a calculation: no magic cash number, no sigma, no tiny chance of going broke for a chance of doubling, never risk what you have and need, and accept being too conservative ninety-nine years in a hundred (M2012-079 to M2012-082, M2012-102). The mathematicians failed through the hammer-and-nail error and ignorance of history; the remedy is the study of other people's disasters (M2012-083, M2012-084, M2012-104).
On businesses: cash-consuming businesses must earn a reasonable return on what they consume; barriers are bought, not built, and tested by whether billions could knock off the leader; one competitor can ruin a business; an essential asset bought below replacement cost has a barrier; size is not a measure, only growth in value (M2012-057, M2012-087, M2012-105 to M2012-109). Insurance value comes from bargain float and a low-cost producer, or from one person's skill judged over many deals (M2012-110 to M2012-112).
On people: managers who no longer need money stay for autonomy; the owner's job is a "negative art" of not destroying their passion; pay is tailored, tied to logical measures, and kept out of the consultants' ratchet; investment managers are chosen for how the record was made and paid on excess return over rolling years (M2012-056, M2012-076, M2012-089 to M2012-095).
