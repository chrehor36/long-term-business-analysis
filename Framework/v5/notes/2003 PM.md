## What this session taught
Intrinsic value is the cash an asset yields to Judgment Day at one rate common to all assets; a foreseeable decline is already in it, uncertainty goes into the cash estimate and not the rate, and where no reasonable estimate exists one moves on (M2003-073, M2003-078, M2003-135, M2003-147). Buffett's own Dexter error is named as projecting past competitive conditions forward (M2003-077), and the purchase test is three questions: what cash, how sure, against what price (M2003-138).
Ideas come from public documents and an accumulated database, not from managements, analysts or projections; one needs few ideas and no big mistake, and should buy heavily when one comes, since fading on a small price rise was the costliest error (M2003-064 to 070, M2003-144 to 146).
The circle is drawn by foreseeable economics, not by knowing what a business does: telecom is outside, Wrigley and Coca-Cola inside; the occasional exception is a bond bet on people or an intelligent gamble, marked as unusual (M2003-092 to 098).
Risk: institutions die in gaps the models call six-sigma; a catastrophe payer may be hit by the same catastrophe; a hedge inherits the counterparty's conduct; count on your own resources; interlinked institutions can fall like dominoes (M2003-082 to 086, M2003-133).
Accounting: depreciation is reverse float, EBITDA is worth looking at only if the presenter pays the capex, pensions are the remaining lollapalooza, and conservative accounting protects decisions; operating problems are never solved by numbers (M2003-102 to 112).
Capital: the ideal business compounds at high returns on more capital but almost none do; great franchises throw off cash that must be moved, and the record of doing so is poor; a merely good business whose profit "sits in the yard" is hated (M2003-119 to 123).
Hurdles: no cost-of-capital figure, only opportunity cost, personal to one's own circumstances; a 10 percent floor held whatever short rates are, and cash at a low yield over a long bond that would lose principal (M2003-116, M2003-117, M2003-148 to 155).
Incentives run through pay (simple, fair, on the unit one controls, capital charged where it matters), Clayton's dealers who keep the loss on their paper, and D&O costs that land on no wrongdoer (M2003-100, M2003-126 to 132, M2003-140).
