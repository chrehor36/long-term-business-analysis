## What this session taught
The FY2008 letter's centre is the failure of history as a guide: insured bonds priced on an uninsured record, mortgage securities priced on a calm past, back-tested models projected onto a different universe, and long options priced on past volatility (L2008-015 to L2008-018, L2008-025, L2008-026). The check it offers is to ask whether the past and the present are the same kind of universe, and to push a formula to an extreme to see if it breaks.
Lending is reduced to the payment against actual income, a real down payment and no reliance on refinancing or resale, with credit scores beside the point (L2008-011, L2008-012), and a promise is judged by the strength of the one who makes it (L2008-009).
On safety, the letter and the meeting agree from several sides: more than ample cash, never dependent on others' money, no collateral-posting contracts, no position where a lender or one's own fear can force a sale (L2008-019, L2008-024, M2009-030, M2009-051).
The meeting adds a cluster on method: investing is cash now for more cash later compared against every other bush; if the case needs a calculator, pass; higher mathematics is dangerous; ratings are never a substitute for one's own credit judgment; "everybody else is doing it" is never a reason (M2009-004, M2009-005, M2009-007, M2009-011, M2009-013, M2009-025).
Two confessions sit side by side: thumb-sucking when new facts should have prompted action (L2008-003) and the Irish banks, where the information was available and Buffett "just plain wasn't paying attention" (M2009-057).
On selling, the morning states the four reasons to sell a stock and the two exceptions for a whole business, and concedes that keeping the Buffalo News would be a mistake for a trustee (M2009-040, M2009-041, M2009-046).
