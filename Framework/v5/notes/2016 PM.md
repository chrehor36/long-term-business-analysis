## What this session taught
Acquisitions go wrong on the future economics of the industry, never on the checklist, and the judgment that matters most about a seller is whether he will run the business the same way once he has the cash; Munger adds the human quality of the managers who stay (M2016-065 to M2016-067). Negotiation is kept moving with trust, without trying to win every point (M2016-068).
Pay is the afternoon's longest theme: you get what you reward; GEICO pays on two variables employees control, growth in policies in force and profit on seasoned business, because profit alone would shrink the business; plans are built business by business, the manager may write his own, and consultants gain by complexity (M2016-083 to M2016-091).
Buybacks must be tied to valuation by a demonstrable margin, and a full wallet urges spending (M2016-046 to M2016-049). Cost cutting is judged by whether it is smart or dumb, measured against fat competitors over years; sloppy thinking in one area suggests it elsewhere, and a wonderful business hides it (M2016-072 to M2016-076).
On reading figures: deferred tax on unrealized gains is not cash; depreciation can fall short of real maintenance spending; adjusted earnings almost always inflate; no restructuring charges (M2016-058, M2016-092, M2016-094, M2016-095).
On the mind: the worst anchor is one's own previous conclusion; state the other side's case better than its holder before disagreeing; avoid the standard stupidities; know that you do not understand what no one understands (M2016-054, M2016-055, M2016-060, M2016-079).
On holding: great businesses weaken, yet a portfolio cannot be changed on every slippage, only on what changes the game, with each threat graded minor, major or life-threatening (M2016-080, M2016-081). The climate proposal gave the risk tests in meeting form: one-year repricing, holding period against hazard, risk to the world against risk to the business (M2016-102 to M2016-106).
