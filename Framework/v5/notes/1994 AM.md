## What this session taught
- Risk is defined twice, consistently: the possibility of loss (letter) or harm (meeting) over the holder's horizon, never volatility. The letter turns it into five factors: the business, the managers' ability, their honesty to owners, the price, and tax and inflation.
- The managers are judged on two yardsticks, how they played the hand dealt and how they treat owners, and Buffett says this can be read from reports and proxy statements without meeting anyone.
- Understandability is a hard first screen (95 percent of businesses fail it), and fast-changing technology fails it by nature, not for want of study.
- Price discipline appears identically in insurance and in securities: write or buy at sensible prices, accept low volume or idle cash otherwise, and price to exposure, not recent experience.
- Several rows are about incentives of the other party: sellers' projections, managers who gain from size, LBO funds with upside only, single-line insurers with no alternative use of capital.
- One plain mistake is narrated with its lesson: USAir, a business needing its cost structure fixed.
- Accounting is a starting point; the owner recasts it to economics, and a management preoccupied with it is a negative.
