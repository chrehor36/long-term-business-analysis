## What this session taught
The FY2018 letter retires book value as the yardstick and gives its reasons: most value now sits in operating businesses carried below worth, and buybacks above book but below value raise value while lowering book (L2018-001). It values Berkshire by a few groves, deducting tax only on what will be sold, because good businesses are too scarce to sell (L2018-002), and it ties the absence of any quarterly number at the top to deciding for the owners who stay (L2018-003). Buffett admits his own years of preaching doom about deficits (L2018-004).
The report reprints no signed text this year, so the Owner's Manual and the Acquisition Criteria are absent from it.
The morning is about price and about trust. Price: overpaying spoils even a wonderful business, a low price cannot rescue a poor one, and a growing brand is the easier one to overpay for (M2019-013, M2019-015, M2019-043); brands sold through intermediaries are weaker than those that reach the customer directly (M2019-014, M2019-041); all investing is the same calculation of birds in the bush, including who will try to take the bush away (M2019-022, M2019-023).
Trust: paying every claim of two failed small insurers is why counterparties accept Berkshire's word, an asset no competitor can buy (M2019-027, M2019-033); a buyback owes the selling partner the facts (M2019-007); a money manager should take only clients whose expectations fit (M2019-034).
On risk: the fully safe stand-alone insurer is a poor business, and reinsurance may fail in the worst case it was bought for (M2019-025); insurance is mediocre unless one is far better than average (M2019-026); private-equity returns leave out idle committed money (M2019-021). On mistakes: Google's advertising was visibly working in their own GEICO operation and they did nothing (M2019-024).

## What in the brief was wrong or unclear
The brief does not cover a report that reprints no signed section at all. Section 4 and the 2013 convention assume a reprint that drops a sentence: they ask for the drop to be recorded under Rows and an erratum, "not reprinted in the FY<year> report", appended once to each dropped row. The FY2018 report omits the whole Owner's Manual and the Acquisition Criteria (its contents page lists only the letter, the 10-K and the appendices), and the helper's last line reports "0 MATCH, 49 DRIFT" with every id "opening not found (dropped?)", which reads as 49 dropped principles. Applied literally, the convention would append "not reprinted in the FY2018 report" to every R-row that does not yet carry such an erratum, though nothing in this year's file says any principle was withdrawn; the manual was simply not printed in this report. I recorded the absence once under Rows, wrote no erratum, and left it to the operator to say whether a wholesale absence counts as 49 drops, as one note line, or as a reason for the helper to stop reporting DRIFT against a report with no signed sections. The dispatch for this unit anticipated "no signed sections by heading", so the gap is in the standing brief, not in the dispatch.
