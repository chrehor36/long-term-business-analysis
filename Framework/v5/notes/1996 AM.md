## What this session taught
- The letter and the reprinted principles carry most of the method this year; the meeting spends its first half on the Class B issue and turns it into lessons about issuing and buying back stock: both are sales or purchases of slices of every business, judged only against intrinsic value (L1995-028, R1995-010, M1996-002, M1996-013).
- Intrinsic value is defined twice in plain words: all future cash discounted (M1996-011), held as a range not a number (M1996-009), and book value tracks only its movement (M1996-008). Breakup and peer-multiple views are named as errors (M1996-010, M1996-016).
- The discount-rate answer is the clearest statement so far of how the rate is chosen: the long-term government rate, risk kept out of the rate and handled by staying within understanding and buying only at a significant discount (M1996-024, M1996-025).
- Float is explained three ways (letter, meeting, insurance table): money held but not owned, valuable only if cheap, and cheap only with a competitive advantage; an ordinary insurer is not a good business (L1995-014 to 016, M1996-022, M1996-023).
- Several narrated errors share one shape: selling or under-owning an identifiably wonderful business (GEICO 1952, Disney 1967, Cap Cities, Gillette preferred instead of common) (L1995-012, L1995-026, L1995-032).
- Durability is discussed as erosion by notches: newspapers as customers gain choices, World Book under electronic delivery, retailers who must stay smart every day (L1995-008, L1995-009, L1995-023, M1996-026).
- Reading is described as the work itself: every competitor's reports, outside information, no Wall Street ideas, an hour per report (M1996-027 to M1996-031).
