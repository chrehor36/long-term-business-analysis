## What this session taught
Value is what an asset produces in cash over its life; investment looks to that, speculation to the next buyer, and the quote is ignored (M2002-078 to 080). Gold and correlations are no yardstick; bonds are expected to return their yield (M2002-075, M2002-077).
Bubbles are recognizable but not timeable, so being right about frauds does not justify shorting; shorting is asymmetric, capped in size and fatal on one error, while an unmargined long can wait (M2002-081, M2002-082, M2002-089, M2002-090).
Options run through the afternoon: every option has value, the receiver knows it better than the giver, it is both an expense and a dilution, diluted EPS hides it, formula values are shaded and suit only short terms, and the two-farms test makes the cost plain (M2002-083 to 087, M2002-109 to 113).
The circle of competence: doubt means outside; stay well inside; understanding each transaction is not understanding the enterprise; a competence knows its edge (M2002-092 to 095).
Business quality: capex needed to stand still against pre-depreciation margin; price-switching customers make a business decent, not wonderful; the value sits in the trademark people ask for by name; where price is part of a product's integrity it never goes to a discounter (M2002-061, M2002-062, M2002-091, M2002-129).
Avoidance: quitclaim fields where the tort system punishes a deep pocket; avoid fields with no edge and a tax disadvantage; prefer simple, auditable, cash-producing businesses (M2002-102, M2002-103, M2002-106, M2002-107, M2002-121, M2002-122).
Mistakes narrated: the missed drug basket, General Re's cultural drift, Dexter's tenfold labor gap paid in stock, See's failure outside the West, the missed RTC years; Munger adds that systems never end mistakes (M2002-060, M2002-068, M2002-116, M2002-117, M2002-128, M2002-108).
