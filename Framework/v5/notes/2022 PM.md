## What this session taught
The afternoon is about honesty in numbers and in conduct more than about buying. Forecasting earnings is the thing never to start, because a repeated target becomes a creed the successor cannot retract and the figures get bent to meet it (M2022-054); employees see a changed figure, so one altered number at the top makes lying the culture, and no process survives that (M2022-055); an organization run on process treats whatever is permitted as acceptable (M2022-056). Accounting cannot be written so it is not gamed, and decent neighbours still play with numbers (M2022-070); compare both counterparties' values for the same contract (M2022-072); a fairness opinion is a fee ritual paid by owners (M2022-073), and a holder on both sides lets the other owners decide (M2022-074).
On capital: inflation forces a capital-needing business to reinvest just to stand still (M2022-050), nobody knows future inflation and forecasters are paid for the claim (M2022-051, M2022-069), and an episode never repeats the same way because the first changes attitudes (M2022-068). Buybacks need both a relative and an absolute test (M2022-083), and large profitable ones need a market that thinks the buyer wrong (M2022-082). A workout is defined and weighed as a capped gain against a deal break (M2022-057, M2022-058). Assets have value only if they deliver something (M2022-076).
On conduct: stay off divisive subjects because owners and employees pay (M2022-046); do not use the company as a weapon (M2022-047); raise the stakes against a counterparty that relies on the small claim not being worth the fight (M2022-075); avoid what you are bad at (M2022-080).
