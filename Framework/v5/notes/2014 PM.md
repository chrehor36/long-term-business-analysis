## What this session taught
The afternoon is about change and how an owner meets it. Every business is subject to change, slow change is the dangerous kind, and the owner asks what can break the model; yet a real threat such as self-driving cars is no reason to sell a business one trusts, because such changes often come more slowly than predicted and the better mousetrap usually wins (M2014-038, M2014-048, M2014-049, M2014-096). Where the winners cannot be picked, one watches without temptation (M2014-097).
The mistakes are several and specific: the EFH gas-price assumption, cash spent too early in 2008, retail outside the circle, GEICO's distracting acquisitions, Fannie and Freddie's earnings-per-share game, and a grandfather's "The day of the chain store is over" (M2014-037, M2014-042, M2014-052, M2014-075, M2014-090, M2014-041). Munger's answer to all of them is to scramble out (M2014-040).
On valuation Buffett gives intrinsic value as the present value of all cash distributed until judgment day and the Aesop questions that estimate it, and confesses starting too quantitative (M2014-067 to M2014-069). He then measures a growing utility's return on added capital by operating earnings after depreciation, not by cash after capital spending, and Munger says the same numbers would be hated in a declining store (M2014-086, M2014-088).
On deals, the forces toward activity inside a company (animal spirits, strategy staff, bankers) produce dumb deals, and the defence is to be eager only for a deal that makes sense, in a setting that does not press (M2014-074, M2014-076, M2014-077). Partners are judged by kept deals and no overpromising, and one gets good ones by deserving them (M2014-091, M2014-092).
The circle of competence is defined as realism about one's own talents, learned partly by asking friends, and Munger adds that competence is relative to the competition (M2014-051, M2014-053, M2014-054).

## What in the brief was wrong or unclear
Two points. First, the political test ("whether the passage instructs an investor or owner in what to do or check") decided heading 19 against rowing, because Buffett and Munger are telling prosecutors to charge individuals; but the reason they give, that behaviour changes when the fear "will hit home to the individual" and far less when "the only fear is that the company’s going have to write a big check" (line 1085), is a statement about incentives that an owner could apply to his own managers, and the brief does not say whether a general principle carried inside a policy answer, but not addressed to an owner, is rowed as the principle (as section 5's first clarification does for a rule inside a political answer) or dropped with the policy. I dropped it and listed it under Rows with its line.
