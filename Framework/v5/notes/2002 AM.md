## What this session taught
The FY2001 letter turns September 11th into underwriting rules: three principles (evaluate only what you can, with an expectancy of profit; no aggregation that threatens solvency; no moral risk), and Buffett's own failure under the first two (L2001-007 to 010, L2001-012).
The central error named in letter and meeting alike is pricing from experience rather than exposure, risks seen rather than risks possible (L2001-010, L2001-011, M2002-013), with the Noah rule as the cure: seeing a risk counts for nothing unless acted on (L2001-012).
Reserving is treated as a test of honesty and of knowing one's costs: underreserving makes the product look cheap, so it is underpriced and bonuses are paid on false profits; "loss development" is an estimation error; struggling insurers grade themselves softly (L2001-015, L2001-020 to 024).
Terminology is read as a warning: EBITDA, pro forma, loss development, reserve strengthening (L2001-019, L2001-021, M2002-026); depreciation is "the worst kind of expense", the reverse of float (M2002-027).
On buying businesses, the meeting gives fast tests: what the customers need and whether the business delivers it, whether a competitor could divert them, whether the business can be understood in minutes or never, and whether the seller cares for the people he leaves (M2002-029 to 032). Quick decisions work because only the no-brainers are taken (M2002-033); diligenced deals by others earned a quarter of projections (M2002-034).
On moats, the speed of building one depends on the industry; quick gains are quick losses; the proof of a franchise is that everyone knows its profits and still cannot take its share (M2002-048, M2002-050, M2002-051), and the obvious ones are priced high (M2002-049).
Selling is explained as a downgraded judgment of competitive advantage, with newspapers as the case of a franchise that eroded slowly (M2002-004, M2002-005).
The investor needs realism and discipline, detachment from the crowd and an accurate circle of competence, not IQ (M2002-018, M2002-039, M2002-041); confirmation bias is guarded by honest self-reporting and a partner who is not subservient (M2002-056, M2002-057).
