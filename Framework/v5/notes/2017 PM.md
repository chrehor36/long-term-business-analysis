## What this session taught
Buffett prefers to buy businesses already run lean rather than do the cutting himself, and Munger adds that being right is not enough reason to act when the reaction will cost more (M2017-066, M2017-067). Cash that cannot be used in a reasonable time shifts the burden of proof to management, and buybacks are fair to sellers only when the stock is undervalued and nothing is done to depress it (M2017-068, M2017-072, M2017-081).
On valuation: value is the present value of all the cash a business will distribute, no formula reduces it, future interest rates matter most and the current long rate is hard to better; book value fades as a guide once value builds up in operating businesses (M2017-076, M2017-083, M2017-084). Depreciation is reverse float, EBITDA flatters, and one should ask whose interest a popular measure serves (M2017-099 to M2017-101).
On learning: run a lousy business once to learn how little intelligence can do against bad economics; a slightly better bad business is the more dangerous one to own because you hang on; the scuttlebutt question (which competitor would you own for ten years, which would you short) reveals an industry's economics (M2017-075, M2017-086, M2017-091).
On people and structure: the best people ask what they can do for the enterprise; most headquarters staff only pretend to think like shareholders; independent subsidiaries contain a single disaster; a reputation for quick, simple dealing brings deals; success at harmful work breeds a self-delusion that should never be underestimated (M2017-070, M2017-071, M2017-088, M2017-093, M2017-097).
