## What this session taught
The afternoon is about what to own when the ideal is scarce and how to read a business from its customers. Capital-light businesses earning high returns remain first choice, and good capital-intensive ones at sensible prices are a second-best that never displaces an available first (M2018-055); Munger adds that satisfactory is good enough and perfection is not on offer (M2018-056), and that when leveraged buyers set crazy prices one waits (M2018-077).
Consumer behavior is the evidence: products whose buyers lose price sensitivity (M2018-072), customers who stay through a scandal (M2018-073), delighted customers (M2018-075), and the scuttlebutt method, with the rule that nothing is foreseen without a lot of evidence (M2018-074). Apple is judged by ecosystem permanence and capital use, not as a tech stock (M2018-086), and the test is the durability of the advantage plus one's edge in judging it (M2018-084).
Against formulas: Munger's mix of factors and value-price gap (M2018-050), physics envy and slowly improved judgment (M2018-082), and Buffett's distrust of an "elegant" theory (M2018-081); investing needs discipline and a few fundamentals, not advanced learning (M2018-078, M2018-080).
Errors told with lessons: the newspapers misjudged partly from love of them (M2018-057), the 401(k) left to delegation (M2018-066), a wrong macro forecast with right investing (M2018-071), and missing Amazon and Google, kept inside the strike-zone rule (M2018-087).
On owners and managers: repurchases only below worth (M2018-051), buybacks to prop the price immoral (M2018-054), no hostile bids though owners may challenge a management (M2018-076), and pay scaled to size and consultants' plans work against human nature (M2018-089).
