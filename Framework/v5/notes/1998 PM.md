## What this session taught
- Value is the net cash a business can return, discounted at the long Treasury rate as a common yardstick; gross cash flow and EBITDA get no credit, and depreciation roughly stands in for required capital spending in most companies (M1998-080, M1998-082, M1998-086, M1998-127). The Frozen Corporation makes the point: a business that can never pay out is worth nothing to its owner (M1998-084).
- Businesses are graded by what growth costs them: the best needs no capital, the second earns well on what it takes, the worst is forced to spend at low returns just to stay in the game and is "a real trap" (M1998-081, M1998-128, M1998-129, M1998-132). Munger's version: the good business throws up easy decisions, the bad one agonizing ones (M1998-131).
- Risk is stated as a go/no-go valve, not a discount-rate adjustment: what cannot be foreseen is risky "for us" and is given up; past the threshold one rate applies to all; volatility in a business whose outcome is known is preferred (M1998-150 to M1998-154).
- The checklist is given in order: understand the business, judge its earning power five to fifteen years out, the people, then the price (M1998-088). Book value and price-to-book carry no weight; a low price-to-book more often marks a poor business (M1998-115, M1998-116).
- Research starts from a strong presumption (the seven-footer) and uses scuttlebutt to try to reject it; the questions are whom competitors fear, why a thrifty customer pays more, why customers switch and how permanent that is (M1998-143 to M1998-147).
- Waiting has no time limit and cash may pile up; "you don't get paid for activity, you only get paid for being right" (M1998-136 to M1998-139). A price too high to add at is not a price to sell at (M1998-160).
- In acquisitions the people and the economics are "99 percent"; due diligence is boilerplate, often a crutch for a deal already wanted, and the bad deals (Hochschild Kohn) failed on economics (M1998-175 to M1998-177). Honesty is read from behavior over months and from track records (M1998-166 to M1998-168).
- Subsidiaries are run at near-abdication: capital is centralized, nothing else; a failing operation is fixed by changing the manager, not by adding systems (M1998-074 to M1998-079, M1998-106).
