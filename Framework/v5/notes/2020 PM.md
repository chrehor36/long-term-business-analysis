## What this session taught
The afternoon is the morning's lecture put to work on questions. Buffett treats a stock position as a whole business: buy as much as you can of what you like, never trim, and when the view changes sell everything (M2020-020); a business that will keep losing money is sold or closed, and shareholders' money is never used to subsidise it (M2020-026, M2020-028). Preparation is for cascading problems, with a worse worst case than most people use (M2020-021), and liquidity means nothing due on a given date, no collateral, Treasury bills only, even though the bills are "a terrible investment" held to pay for the opportunity when the world stops (M2020-027, M2020-036, M2020-039).
On owning stocks the test is the owner's psychology and understanding: hold like a farm through a 50 percent fall, never on borrowed money (M2020-022, M2020-023); repay 18 percent debt before investing (M2020-048).
On the business itself: capital-light growth beats capital-intensive growth, and insurance needs capital available rather than consumed (M2020-040, M2020-041); a regulated utility should earn decent, not super, returns (M2020-034); an oil producer is a bet on the price of oil (M2020-035).
On capital allocation: a buyback is a distribution to the owners who want cash, done only after growth is funded, with a margin for surprises, and below value measured now rather than against an old price (M2020-043 to M2020-045, M2020-047). On advisers and managers: fees on one side make advice partial, and the money in investment management is in selling (M2020-032, M2020-037); wisdom is preferred to IQ for judgment (M2020-049).
