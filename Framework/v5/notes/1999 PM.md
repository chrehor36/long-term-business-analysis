## What this session taught
- Predictability is chosen over payoff: change is read as a threat, a business facing much change is passed nine times in ten, and the cost is missing big winners in exchange for very few big losers (M1999-063, M1999-064, M1999-072, M1999-073). The circle of competence is knowing which businesses you understand and when you are inside it (M1999-074, M1999-093).
- Moat and management carry no separate price; they enter value through the certainty and amount of the cash stream, which is discounted at the Treasury rate only for comparability (M1999-103, M1999-104). The Wrigley questions and the share-of-mind question for Coke show what evaluating a moat means in practice, and no formula measures one (M1999-105, M1999-107, M1999-108). A big enough moat needs less management (M1999-106).
- Arithmetic as a check on expectations: investors get only what businesses earn, less friction; trace the mathematics to its end, and compare the earnings a price implies with how few companies ever reach them (M1999-066, M1999-067, M1999-076, M1999-068).
- Holding and adding are separate decisions: a price too high for new money is no reason to sell, and trading in and out of a wonderful business needs two right decisions and a tax (M1999-077 to M1999-079).
- Research is journalism: read everything, immerse as if buying the whole business, ask competitors the silver-bullet question, write the story of what the company is worth, and prefer companies whose story is easy (M1999-128 to M1999-133). Munger: an easy decision is what is sought (M1999-121).
- Mistakes are audited: postmortems, sales judged against purchases, a batting average, and the McDonald's sale priced at over a billion dollars (M1999-123 to M1999-125). An insurance loss of unknown size cost more than accounting showed by forcing caution when stocks were cheap (M1999-097).
- Owners' money is the owners': no CEO pet charities, no second hat as a money manager, never handed to outside managers, every share class treated proportionally, and reporting without spin (M1999-069, M1999-087, M1999-088, M1999-122, M1999-140).
- Insurance: intrinsic value growth turns on float growth and float cost; long-tail surprises come big and late; an industry with more capital than it can use stays average (M1999-086, M1999-098, M1999-113).
