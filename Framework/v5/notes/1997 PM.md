## What this session taught
- Intrinsic value is stated as a bond with unprinted coupons, discounted at the long government rate, but the rate is a yardstick, not a hurdle: risk is handled by buying at a big discount from that value, and Buffett concedes Munger's point that he never actually does the discounting (M1997-050, M1997-053, M1997-086, M1997-126).
- Opportunity cost is the filter stated most often, three times by Munger and once as the "more Coca-Cola?" question: every stock first against the government bond, then against the best idea already owned (M1997-055, M1997-056, M1997-089, M1997-090, M1997-147).
- The two first filters are named in order: can we understand it, then can it have a sustainable edge (M1997-148). Understanding means seeing the competitive forces ten to thirty years out, and preferring businesses that change little so the knowledge piles up (M1997-051, M1997-154).
- The consumer franchise is share of mind that money cannot buy, kept up by the last customer served and defended by spending whose effect cannot be traced; the hundred-billion question tests it (M1997-099, M1997-101 to 103). Loyalty is a property of the industry: blades, not hamburgers (M1997-139, M1997-140).
- The repeated mistake is omission inside the circle: not paying up, stopping a purchase over an eighth, and See's nearly lost over $100,000 (M1997-083, M1997-104, M1997-120). The lesson Munger draws is to keep learning; Buffett's is the pond over the swimming (M1997-105).
- Retained earnings must each become more than a dollar of market value, otherwise pay them out; buybacks in a super business beat dividends (M1997-091 to 094).
- Leverage and options are refused on a stated ground: never risk what you have and need for what you neither have nor need, and never enough debt to lose sleep over obligations (M1997-122 to 125, M1997-156).
- People are filtered by visible flags; no auctions, no stories, no brokerage reports (M1997-149 to 152).
