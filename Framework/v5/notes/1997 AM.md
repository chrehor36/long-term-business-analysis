## What this session taught
- The 1996 letter is the year's teaching text, and the morning is largely about it: The Inevitables against the Impostors (L1996-029, L1996-031), applied straight away to McDonald's (M1997-001) and qualified at once by the overpayment warning (L1996-032, M1997-002).
- Understanding is given an operational meaning: a pretty good idea where the business will be in ten years (M1997-021), with the circle of competence defined by its boundaries rather than its size (L1996-035), and the danger named as believing one understands when one does not (M1997-024).
- Risk is defined three times and never as volatility: business risk from capital structure and the nature of the business (M1997-009), a substantial chance of something going horribly wrong (M1997-013), and the beta paradox at the Washington Post (M1997-014). Overpaying for a good business is called a risk of time, not principal (M1997-011).
- Change is treated as the enemy of certainty: favour businesses unlikely to change (L1996-026), test whether the reasons customers buy are unchanged (L1996-028), avoid industries where companies get left behind (M1997-023).
- The Owner's Manual, reprinted whole in this report, adds commentary to the 1983 principles: a share as part of a business held indefinitely (R1996-001), a falling market as good news (R1996-005), and the added principle that price should track value both ways (R1996-017).
- Compensation runs through all three documents: GEICO's two-variable plan and the three tests of a goal (L1996-018), pay for the area one controls (M1997-042), options with a step-up for retained capital (M1997-043), and the mediocre manager as the real sin (M1997-045).
- Due diligence is set aside in favour of judging the economics and the manager (M1997-036, M1997-037, M1997-039).

- M1997-003 against M1997-002 and L1996-032, all from this unit: certainty about a wonderful business matters more than 5 or 10 percent on price, yet one can pay too much even for the best. Buffett holds both in one answer; rowed as mistake-and-lesson, with the pull named in its notes.
- M1997-046 (Coca-Cola stagnated under earlier management with the same product) against M1996-037 (a great business needs no management) and against the letter's "you need only monitor whether these qualities are being preserved" (L1996-024). Noted in M1997-046's notes, not rowed separately.
- L1996-025 (do not trim a dominant winner) sits beside L1996-032 and M1997-002 (overpayment risk is high now, The Inevitables included); in the same answer Buffett says he would not sell Coke or Gillette at considerably higher prices (M1997-002 notes). Different acts, buying and selling; noted, not rowed.
- M1997-015 says reported earnings plus amortisation of intangibles are a good representation of owner earnings for Berkshire's businesses, while R1996-023 says depreciation is a real cost and EBITDA meaningless. Not a contradiction (depreciation stays in), recorded because a reader could take one for the other.
