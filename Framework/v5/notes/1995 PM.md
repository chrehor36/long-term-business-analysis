## What this session taught
- Retention and repurchase are judged by one yardstick, stated three times in this session: keep a dollar only while it makes more than a dollar of market value over time, measured over a couple of years, and buy back below intrinsic value once the business's own needs are met (M1995-054, M1995-067, M1995-099).
- The best available holding is the hurdle for any new purchase. Buffett asks "why would I rather have this than more Coca-Cola?" and Munger calls the measuring stick a thought conserver that screens out 99 percent (M1995-105, M1995-106).
- Durability is attacked from outside the business: technology a monopolist welcomed as a cost cut let rivals copy it (Title Insurance and Trust), branches lose value as newcomers go around banks, and a newspaper franchise stays rich while its certainty falls (M1995-061, M1995-102, M1995-058).
- Accounting is read for character. Confusing accounts mean walk away, and swelling deferred assets or inventories out of line with sales mean look twice (M1995-063 to 065).
- People: no good deal with a bad person, and no contract protects against one; candour, intelligence and focus are what they look for; agency cost comes as self-dealing or folly (M1995-084, M1995-111, M1995-109).
- Valuation is prospective and imprecise on purpose. Today's multiple counts only together with the reinvestment rate, interest rates count in large moves, and the margin of safety should make decimals unnecessary (M1995-113 to 115, M1995-073).
- Reported returns mislead in known ways: equity shrunk by liabilities, omitted option costs, averages that drop the losers, capital outgrowing the business (M1995-086 to 088, M1995-069).
