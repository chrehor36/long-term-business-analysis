## What this session taught
The FY2009 letter is written as a primer for the BNSF newcomers and its centre is inversion: what Berkshire does not do (L2009-004 to L2009-009). Avoid businesses whose future cannot be evaluated, since seeing an industry's growth says nothing about its margins once competitors arrive; keep liquidity that dwarfs any conceivable need; accept the visible cost of a few bad decisions over bureaucracy's invisible ones; keep the centre to four jobs.
Measurement comes first: a yardstick fixed in advance, never drawn around the result, and a warning that even a decade's record can be distorted by a foolish price at either end (L2009-001, L2009-002).
The letter's longest argument is on paying with stock: if you would not sell the whole company at the market price, do not sell part of it at that price in a merger; an undervalued acquirer cannot make a sensible all-stock deal; ask what is given, and who gains; hire a second adviser paid only if the deal fails (L2009-019 to L2009-022).
Capital use shifts openly: the best businesses still need little capital, but a company with too much cash will now accept capital-heavy businesses at decent incremental returns, and the morning adds that the wonderful businesses are wonderful partly because they cannot absorb more (L2009-012, M2010-016, M2010-017).
The report's one new lesson is a correction: the retention test as written in 1983 failed at a market low, and Buffett restates it after a shareholder's question at the 2009 meeting (R2009-002).
The morning's longest thread is conduct: legality is not the standard, never trade reputation for money, "the other fellow is doing it" is no reason, close to the line is probably over it, and problems are cured if heard of early (M2010-007, M2010-032 to M2010-036).
On risk-taking: judge the risk and set the price, never the counterparty; stop writing when premiums are wrong while others take more risk; know enough to lend before knowing enough to own; the willingness to endure lumpy results is itself an advantage others know of and do not copy (M2010-003, M2010-004, M2010-008, M2010-018, M2010-038, M2010-039).
