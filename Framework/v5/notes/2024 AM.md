## What this session taught
The FY2023 letter is Buffett's first without Munger, and its new lines are about limits. Judging managers is harder than judging businesses because "Sincerity and empathy can easily be faked" (L2023-003). The owners' money "does not belong to us" (L2023-004), and "Never risk permanent loss of capital" is set down as the one unchanging investment rule (L2023-005). A railroad that spends more than depreciation pays its owner less than it reports, and can still be a fair buy at a fraction of replacement value (L2023-009). He admits never considering that a utility's regulatory bargain could break (L2023-011) and refuses to throw good money after bad (L2023-010). The letter also favours the rare business that can reinvest at high returns, against his earlier preference for businesses that need little capital (L2023-002).
The morning session is mostly Buffett restating his core: stocks as businesses, learned from one book after years of error (M2024-005); never a fatal mistake (M2024-002); cash left idle until a pitch he likes comes (M2024-034); size kills returns (M2024-035). The insurance answers add tests: what is one event (M2024-021), fashionable lines sold on commission (M2024-022), and insurance looks easy because the money comes first (M2024-036). Two admissions stand out: GEICO's low costs masked its lag in pricing risk (M2024-014), and he and Munger let managers coast because they were doing well themselves (M2024-025). On succession the board's job is almost all in picking the CEO, and in being able to correct a wrong pick (M2024-028), with the right business ahead of the great manager (M2024-030).

## What in the brief was wrong or unclear
Section 7 step 5 says "Cross-references cite only ids the helper has already printed. Never guess the next id", and the 2007 to 2010 conventions say an earlier id must be read before it is cited, "never taken from a keyword hit". Neither says how to cite a range. I wrote "M2003-065 to M2003-070" in M2024-034's notes from a keyword list in which those ids sat together, assuming a run of rows on one passage; only M2003-070 states the pitch rule. The erratum corrects it, but the brief's wording reads as if each id is checked one by one, and a range invites skipping the check for the ids inside it. I treated a range as a list of ids each of which must be read. Separately, the parent's dispatch for this unit (an unmarked question title is a heading boundary, rows under it take the last marked `### N.` line with a HEADING FLAG) is not yet in the prompt file; I applied it at line 1129, where `### 19.` is missing, because the 2023 AM note proposed it and the dispatch adopted it, but a headless cycle reading the prompt alone would not know it.
