## What this session taught
The FY2015 letter's new lessons are about seeing true costs and true risks: stock-based compensation is compensation and analysts who repeat managers' adjusted figures propagate deception; depreciation can fall short of the spending needed merely to keep a railroad running, so reported earnings overstate; a catastrophe-swung earnings stream is counted at break-even until it steadies (L2015-002 to L2015-004).
On risk it gives a three-part test, probability, range of cost and timing, against the 10-K's catalogue; a small yearly probability compounds toward certainty; Noah's Law says build the ark before the sky clouds; and an insurer's exposure to rising losses depends on whether it reprices each year (L2015-008 to L2015-011).
The mortgage chain without skin in the game, set against Clayton's 100 percent retention, is the letter's mistake-and-lesson (L2015-005); lending long on short money is called risky except with an offset (L2015-006).
The morning session's centre is the bet against the hedge funds: the passive half must beat the active half after costs, consultants are paid never to recommend the index, Wall Street earns more by salesmanship than by skill, and the few good managers are a needle in a haystack (M2016-035 to M2016-039).
On people: choose a manager one would make trustee of one's will, Kiewit's intelligence, energy and integrity with the last indispensable, and pattern recognition, above all the disguised chain letter (M2016-032 to M2016-034).
On businesses: the ideal one grows without capital, and size forces Berkshire into capital-intensive ones; Munger frames the shift as plan B, buying businesses that need a durable superior management rather than ones an idiot could run; Buffett names the qualities of a Precision Castparts (M2016-003 to M2016-006).
On adapting: think about what a powerful trend will make the world look like in five to twenty years, do not try to beat Amazon at its own game, and get out of what cannot be fixed (M2016-012 to M2016-015, M2016-031).

## What in the brief was wrong or unclear
Section 7 says to write the commit message "to a file with the Write tool" and then commit; it does not say the message file must be rewritten before every commit, and the inbox keeps one shared `_msg.txt`. On my second commit I reused the file unchanged, so commit 6aee2130 (which holds M2016-001 to M2016-010, headings 5-10) carries the letter commit's message. The rules forbid rewriting history, so the slip is recorded under Rows and in the next commit's message rather than amended. A per-commit message file named for the batch (for example `<key>-<NN>.msg`) would make the slip impossible. A smaller point: section 5 forbids rowing "a forecast", and L2015-008 ends with a sentence about the future ("A threat that will only surface 50 years from now may be a problem for society, but it is not a financial problem for today's investor"); I rowed it as part of a test, since it tells the investor what to weigh, but the brief does not say where a stated principle about the future ends and a forecast begins.
