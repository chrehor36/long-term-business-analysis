## What this session taught
The FY2011 letter's essay on the three kinds of investment turns the 2011 meeting's sorting into definitions: investing is giving up purchasing power now for more after tax later, and risk is the reasoned probability of losing purchasing power over the holding period, so a non-fluctuating asset can carry great risk (L2011-012 to L2011-014). Unproductive assets need an ever-growing pool of buyers, rising prices recruit their own believers, and the crowd runs to cash and gold at the worst moments (L2011-016 to L2011-018).
The best productive asset meets a "double-barreled test": it keeps its output's purchasing power in inflation while needing little new capital; utilities fail it because to earn more their owners must invest more (L2011-019, L2011-020).
On capital allocation the letter names a "first law": what is smart at one price is dumb at another, for acquisitions and buybacks alike (L2011-003); the meeting adds that most buybacks are for ego, buying high and issuing options low, and that sellers should be told when they are selling too cheap (M2012-009 to M2012-011).
On insurance, float is a revolving fund whose true liability is far below the accounting one when it is costless and long-enduring, insurance goodwill is worth what similar float would cost, and an insurer is worth more than net worth plus float only with sustained underwriting profit and growth (L2011-005, L2011-006, M2012-033). Catastrophe pricing assumes the worst and writes on a negative hypothesis (M2012-018, M2012-019).
On risk at the top, the CEO is the chief risk officer and adds up how the units' risks accumulate; Value at Risk and the Gaussian curve are rejected; no position may call for cash tomorrow morning; the first rule is to be able to play tomorrow (M2012-001 to M2012-003, M2012-029, M2012-032).
The year's confessions: Energy Future's bonds, the early cheering of rising markets, small companies, lateness to IBM and the railroads, stock issued for BNSF, and the internet's effect on GEICO (L2011-002, L2011-004, L2011-009, L2011-010, M2012-021, M2012-030, M2012-045).
On businesses facing technology: news is what you do not know and want to know, a paper lives by staying primary for something local, charging for a product while giving it away free does not last, and whatever can be bought from a home computer is exposed (M2012-043 to M2012-047).
