## What this session taught
Risk dominates the afternoon. A ruinous outcome is to be none, not slim, and the chief executive owns that judgment himself (M2008-061, M2008-084); executives near retirement and on annual bonuses have little reason to fear a rare collapse (M2008-062); a business that borrows every day lives at the world's goodwill, and Munger answers with two layers, unquestioned credit and liquidity to outlast a loss of it (M2008-066, M2008-081).
Munger names the model-builder's error: craving computation until reality is tortured into a model that fails in the extremes (M2008-082), and Buffett asks for a manager who can imagine risks never yet experienced (M2008-079, M2008-080).
On deciding, the session is about speed by exclusion: talk to no one and read the report (M2008-067), want a gap so wide no refinement matters (M2008-068), drop what the figures do not settle (M2008-069, M2008-086), and refuse whole categories such as startups (M2008-088). Conventional due diligence is said never to have caught one of his big mistakes (M2008-072).
On businesses: brands as promises and the penny-cheaper substitute test (M2008-075, M2008-076); banks judged one by one by their DNA (M2008-089, M2008-090), against pharma bought as a group when the winner cannot be picked (M2008-113); the dividend test of a dollar retained (M2008-104, M2008-105).
The crisis material gives three warnings that outlast it: correlated junk pooled is not triple-A (M2008-098), an instrument that takes 750,000 pages to read cannot be valued (M2008-099), and side bets larger than the thing insured create an incentive to cause the loss (M2008-103).
Personal-conduct rules recur in the questions from young people and teachers (M2008-092, M2008-093, M2008-106, M2008-107, M2008-112, M2008-115).
