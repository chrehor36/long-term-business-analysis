## What this session taught
The letter, Buffett's last as CEO, turns on mistakes: name them, correct them fast, and distrust any management that will not say the word, because fooling owners ends in fooling oneself. Its insurance section is about matching commitment to what can be known: short contracts for growing and unpredictable risks, prices set in advance to absorb losses, and owners' money at stake that buys caution but not foresight. The meeting's first half is about how capital waits: spend only on what is understood, cheap and safe, irregularly; patience is waiting for the call and then acting the same day, and Abel adds that the waiting time is for preparing. Size limits even the holdings Buffett loves. Currency appears three ways: governments debase money by their nature, a country with a real chance of runaway inflation is avoided, and Abel's check is comfort with the currency the value will be realized in. Jain twice declines to chase what others are doing, AI spending and leveraged life insurance, and waits. Much of the late morning is personal conduct: associates, reciprocity, employers, setbacks. The last answer warns against reading a big change through its spectacle or one research report: look at the math of the business, act on probabilities, and expect to be surprised.
