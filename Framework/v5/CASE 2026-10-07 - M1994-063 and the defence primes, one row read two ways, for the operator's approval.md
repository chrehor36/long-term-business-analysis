# CASE 2026-10-07: [M1994-063] and the defence primes, one row read two ways, for the operator's approval
*A PRIME RULE 5 case. Written 2026-10-07 at the operator's instruction after two blind runs of 2026-10-06 read the only row
about a defence prime in opposite directions. Nothing here changes a run of record; both runs bind as written.*

## Why this exists
Operator rule 8 says every judgment is justified from the corpus by ledger id, or it is an opinion. On 2026-10-06 two blind
analysts, run on the same day under the same text, each cited **[M1994-063]** and reached opposite boxes for two companies in
one industry:

- **LMT** (`Test Runs/2026-10-06 Run - LMT Lockheed Martin.md`) made it "the deciding row": "The speakers spoke to this
  business by name of kind", read the sentence as the speakers' verdict that a defence prime's twenty-year future cannot be
  judged, and closed **TOO HARD (NATURE) at Q2** on it with **[M2000-014]**, **[M2000-019]** and **[M2006-013]** beside it.
- **GD** (`Test Runs/2026-10-06 Run - GD General Dynamics.md`) read it as "a holding-period judgment, not a statement that its
  economics could not be foreseen", said of the General Dynamics of 1993, weighed it at Q2 and Q7 and read the castle **IN**
  on the filings.

One sentence, two boxes. The case asks which reading the corpus allows.

## What the v5 rows say
The row itself, from the 1994 meeting, under the heading the blind reader gave it:

> "We think the management of General Dynamics has done an absolutely sensational job. Obviously, also it isn’t the kind of
> business, basically, that we have a 20-year view on, or something of the sort." **[M1994-063]**, 1994 meeting

Its setting, which the row's elision leaves out: a shareholder had asked why Berkshire sold a fifth of its stake after praising
the company and giving management its proxy the year before. Buffett first said it was "probably inappropriate to be talking
about what we’re buying or selling", then gave the sentence above. The shareholder pressed, and the answer is the row added
for this case on 2026-10-07 (PRIME RULE 6, the row before the rule), three lines below the first:

> "Should I take from your comment that you have changed your view about the business itself? [...] No, no. I think you take
> my comments as saying just what I’ve said." **[M1994-086]**, 1994 meeting

The blind reader's own concept line for **[M1994-063]**, written during the read: "Excellent management does not by itself make
a business one to hold for twenty years; the business's own durability sets the holding view", with the note "Said while
declining to explain a partial sale. Consistent with L1993-014, which reserves tenacious holding for durably wonderful
businesses."

The rows that decide where a twenty-year view comes from say nothing of defence as a kind. The long view is reserved for the
business whose future is not expected to change: "Most of our decisions relate to things where we expect the future not to
change much." **[M2000-034]**; the holding is kept for "an interest in a business that is both understandable and durably
wonderful" (**[L1993-014]**, as the ledger notes it). The Q2 rows that send a castle to TOO HARD do so on the evidence about the
business: "there are many businesses — industries where it’s very hard to evaluate moats. There — those are the businesses of
rapid change." **[M2001-069]**; "We don’t know how to valuate that" **[M2000-019]**; "we don’t have any idea what that world is
going to look like in 10 or 20 years" **[M1996-059]**, said of Microsoft. A search of the ledger for defence, the Pentagon,
government contracts, military and aerospace finds no row on the government-customer business as a kind; the nearest are the
regulated-return rows **[M2009-104]**, **[L2009-012]**, which carry a utility to Q3 as a second best, and the Teledyne row
**[M2015-032]**, which is about incentives, not the industry.

## The readings
1. **The kind reading** (LMT). "The kind of business" names defence primes; the speakers had no twenty-year view of the kind;
   a defence prime's castle therefore cannot be judged, and the box is TOO HARD (NATURE) at Q2 (or at Q1, as a business whose
   economics cannot be foreseen).
2. **The holding reading** (GD). The sentence states the speakers' holding view of one company at one date, made while
   declining to explain a sale; the speaker refused, when asked, to extend it to "the business itself" **[M1994-086]**. It is
   evidence of the test the speakers apply (durability sets the holding view) and of their view of that company then; it is
   weighed, and the castle is judged on the question's own tests and the filings.
3. **The circle reading.** The sentence says what the speakers chose to hold for twenty years, which is their circle, not the
   analyst's; a business outside their long view may be inside another analyst's **[M1997-021]**, and the row carries nothing
   to a run but the test.

## What the corpus decides, and what it leaves open
**Decides against reading 1.** Asked in the same exchange whether the remark meant a changed view of the business itself, the
speaker said "No, no" and that the comments say "just what I’ve said" **[M1994-086]**. A sentence its speaker refused to extend
to the business cannot be extended by an analyst to the business's industry. The row says that the speakers did not hold a
twenty-year view of General Dynamics in 1994 and that management alone does not make one; it says nothing of whether a defence
prime's castle can be judged. The rows that do send a castle to TOO HARD do it on the business's own evidence of change
**[M2001-069]**, **[M2000-019]**, and the LMT run has those rows too; what the case removes is the row it called deciding.

**Leaves open** whether defence primes are TOO HARD as a kind. That is a Q2 question on the filings (the programme re-won on the
customer's terms, the customer's right to terminate for convenience, the entrants the customer admits), answered name by name,
as the two runs in fact answered it differently on the evidence beneath the row: LMT on a $950M estimate swing inside a
classified programme and a lost down-select, GD on a submarine franchise with a procurement plan past the horizon.
