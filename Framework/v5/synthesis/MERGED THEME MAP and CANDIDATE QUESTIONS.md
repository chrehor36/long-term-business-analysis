**Where they differ.**
1. Advantage before understanding: M2008-039 names the durable advantage first and understanding second; M2005-043, M2010-061 and L2017-002 omit understanding and lead with the advantage; M2017-008 joins the two ("we can look out five, or 10, or 20 years and decide that the competitive advantage that it had at the present would last over that period"). 2. ## 7. Steering noticed, and the limits of this merge

The auto-loaded six shaped this merge in ways I can name. The candidate questions Q1, Q2, Q5, Q7, Q9 and Q11 each have a twin among the six, and the familiar sequence made the section 2 ordering feel natural before I tabled it; I checked it against the eleven ordering passages that put understanding first and the fifteen general lists that put price last, which I think would hold without the six, but the decisions to make survival (Q9) and selling (Q11) numbered questions follow the six and the row counts more than any speaker's checklist, and I have said so at each. Where the six had no slot I tried to give the rows their weight: Q6 (709 rows on what management does with money and owners) is the largest question in the set, and the preamble carries 735 rows that no purchase list orders. Placing Q4 (accounting) between the business and the people, Q9 after price, and splitting Q8 from Q7 are my choices; the rows do not order them. The merge itself was done from the slice maps' theme headings, counts, sub-themes and strongest quotes; the slice agents built those maps mostly from one-line concept summaries and read full quotes for a fraction of rows (A says about 170 of 1,081), and I read in full only the roughly 230 rows quoted here, so a row's theme placement is inherited, not re-checked. Some slice themes straddle two merged themes (B-T13 holds stock-paid acquisitions; C-T10 holds both pay and boards; A-T03 holds macro; A-T14 and B-T07 hold life conduct), so several merged totals are understated or overstated by the inside sub-themes named in each entry. Two choices could go either way and change the ranking: joining B's moat, erosion and commodity themes made M01 the largest theme (361), where the castle rows alone would total 291; joining boards to pay made M04 (269) larger than understanding (231). I could not reconcile two things from the maps alone: whether the advantage-before-understanding lists (M2008-039, M2005-043, M2010-061, L2017-002, M2017-008) reorder the tests or assume the first one, and whether the 2016 cheap-money row (M2016-078) retires the 2003 10 percent floor; both are left to the operator.
