# RECONCILIATION part 4 — THE HOLDINGS FRAMEWORK (v4) against the v5 holdings draft. 2026-10-04

**Status.** Part 4 of the reconciliation pre-registered in `Framework/v5/PREREGISTRATION - v5 blind read and comparison.md` ("Pre-registered standards", "The reconciliation table"), which places "every rule of v4.1 and of the holdings framework" in a class. Parts 1 to 3 covered the purchase framework; this part covers `Framework/THE HOLDINGS FRAMEWORK.md`. This step is not blind: both documents were read. It decides nothing; adoption is the operator's, on a ruling case.

**Sources read.** In force: `Framework/THE HOLDINGS FRAMEWORK.md`, all nine sections. Draft: `Framework/v5/DRAFT - THE HOLDINGS FRAMEWORK v5.md`, all seven sections and its "Steering noticed" note. The Q11 (holdings) sections of `Framework/v5/tests/R V - run.md`, `R NCLTY - run.md`, `R MITSY - run.md`, `R TBTC - run.md`, `R ASML - run.md` and `A HRB - analyst 1.md`, with each run's box. Sections 2 and 3 of `Framework/v5/RECONCILIATION - v5 against v4.1.md`, and rows 29 to 44 and 65 to 70 of part 3, which already classed the holdings doctrine that v4.1's Q6 carries; where this part and part 3 class the same rule, this part says so. Every E-id below was opened in `principle_ledger.csv` and every M, L and R id in `principle_ledger_v5.csv` before it was cited.

**How a row is classed.** The four pre-registered classes, plus PARTLY as the purchase reconciliation used it. REPRODUCED: a v5 row states the same substance. PARTLY: part of the rule is reproduced and a named part is absent or weighed differently; the note says which. ABSENT: worded "no instance found in the v5 ledger (searched: ...)", the searches being regular-expression searches of the `quote_verbatim` column of all rows of `principle_ledger_v5.csv`, run 2026-10-04, unless another sweep is named. CONTRADICTED: a v5 row cuts against the rule with no resolution in the meetings or the reports. **Load-bearing** is the Test D definition the preregistration keeps: stated three or more times across the source, or framed by the authors as central, AND capable of changing a review's outcome. A rule the holdings framework states with no id, or labels CONVENTION, is by its own form not stated in the source; it meets the first limb only where the framework cites corpus for it.

**One structural fact governs the whole comparison.** The rule in force returns one of four named outcomes from five questions answered IN, OUT, UNRESEARCHED or UNKNOWABLE. The draft returns no outcome at all: its questions are STOP or WEIGHING, and it records the absence of named outcomes as a finding about the rows: "The rows give verbs, not a list of verdicts" (draft, section VI, item 2). Many rows below are therefore REPRODUCED in substance and PARTLY in force: the reason to sell is the same, but in the draft it is weighed, and nothing converts the weights into an act.

## C. Contradictions and the meetings' own resolution

**1. The PERMANENT designation for marketable securities: CONTRADICTED, and resolved by the authors against the rule in force.**

The rule in force (section III.1, H4 and section V) rests on the 1987 letter:

> "However, our insurance companies own three marketable common stocks that we would not sell even though they became far overpriced in the market.  In effect, we view these investments exactly like our successful controlled businesses - a permanent part of Berkshire rather than merchandise to be disposed of once Mr. Market offers us a sufficiently high price.  To that, I will add one qualifier: These stocks are held by our insurance companies and we would, if absolutely necessary, sell portions of" — **[E2-39]**, 1987

The meetings first hold both sides in one answer:

> "Well, there are things that we think there’s no price for. [...] at some price, you have to be willing to sell something that’s a marketable security, forgetting about a controlled business. [...] But if you ever attend a meeting here, and there are 60 or 70 times earnings, keep an eye on me." — **[M1996-095]**, 1996

The 2016 letter and report then withdraw the commitment in terms:

> "It is true that we own some stocks that I have no intention of selling for as far as the eye can see (and we're talking 20/20 vision). But we have made no commitment that Berkshire will hold any of its marketable securities forever. [...] That principle covers controlled businesses, not marketable securities. This year I've added a final sentence to #11 to ensure that our owners understand that we regard any marketable security as available for sale, however unlikely such a sale now seems." — **[L2016-010]**, 2016

> "We continue to avoid gin rummy behavior. [...] To clean up some confusion voiced in 2016, we emphasize that the comments here refer to businesses we control, not to marketable securities." — **[R2016-001]**, 2016

The resolution is the authors', dated, and keeps the intent while removing the exemption: an intention to hold a marketable security may be written down ("no intention of selling for as far as the eye can see"); an exemption from the overvaluation trigger may not. The class is CONTRADICTED and not load-bearing by the definition (one 1987 letter, with **[E2-74]** in the same letter, not framed as central), though it is capable of changing a review, since it turns an H4 SELL into HOLD. Three consequences for the rule in force: section III.1's PERMANENT designation, H4's "PERMANENT holdings skip H4", and the outcome row "H4 OUT, holding is PERMANENT: HOLD unless absolutely necessary" all rest on the withdrawn passage. Section III.1's next paragraph, that the reluctance to sell does not transfer to securities, is confirmed by the same two rows (A row 23). The finding stands whatever becomes of v5, as `Framework/v5/RECONCILIATION - v5 against v4.1.md` section 3 item 1 already records.

**2. The retention test's formulation, corrected by its author in 2009 (PARTLY, A row 40).** The rule in force quotes the 1996 Owner's Manual (**[E3-54]**; in the v5 ledger the 1995 text, **[R1995-009]**): "at least $1 of market value for each $1 retained [...] on a five-year rolling basis." In 2009 the author wrote: "I should have written the "five-year rolling basis" sentence differently [...] When the stock market has declined sharply over a five-year stretch, our market-price premium to book value has sometimes shrunk. And when that happens, we fail the test as I improperly formulated it." **[R2009-002]**. His replacement test is specific to Berkshire (book value against the S&P, and a premium to book); the general lesson the draft takes from it is to read the earnings leg beside the market leg **[M2011-072]**. This is not CONTRADICTED, because the test survives, but H2's market-leg-only wording is the formulation its author called improper. The NCLTY run is the case: the market leg fails across a falling market and the earnings leg fails too, so the answer is the same there; it would not be for a holding whose earnings kept pace in a falling market.

**3. Selling at an overvalued price: SELL in force, a weighing in the draft (PARTLY, A rows 3 and 49).** The rule in force sells a SECURITY whose price is above the whole range. The draft weighs a "silly price" and carries it OPEN: **[M2007-089]**, **[L2003-021]**, **[L2004-011]** and **[M2006-029]** against "I would worry, frankly, if I sold a bunch of things right at the top, because that would indicate that, in effect, I was practicing the bigger fool-type approach" **[M1998-101]** and "we’re not good at the game of [...] figuring out how far a speculative boom will go." **[M2006-019]** (draft V, item 2). For marketable securities the reports lean toward the rule in force: not selling in the bubble was "a big mistake" **[L2003-021]**, and every marketable security is "available for sale" **[L2016-010]**. The hold-through rows are about the timing of a top, not about overvaluation against a range, so they cut against a sale timed to the peak, not against the rule in force. Not CONTRADICTED; the rule in force has a range and the draft has none (A row 48).

**4. UNKNOWABLE on H1 to H3 sends a holding to SELL REVIEW; the draft says "when in doubt, keep holding" (PARTLY, A row 61).** The rule in force cites **[E4-12]**, whose v5 twin **[M2007-022]** is a statement about what Berkshire buys ("We favor the businesses where we really think we know the answer"). For a holding the rows give the opposite tilt: "when in doubt, keep holding. But it’s no inviolable rule." **[M2009-040]**, stated for "a wonderful business with outstanding management — but mostly the wonderful business part of it". The two can be read together (SELL REVIEW is a held state, not a sale), but the citation the rule in force gives for it is a buying row. Four of the six test runs met this case: a held name the purchase questions now find TOO HARD (A row 27).

**5. Compare with the view at purchase, against the anchoring warning (not a contradiction).** H1 compares today's evidence with the purchase view **[E4-17]**. The draft keeps the comparison (**[M2002-004]**, **[M2000-054]**) and adds: "We try and avoid the worst anchoring effect, which is always your previous conclusion. We really try and destroy our previous ideas." **[M2016-054]**. The rows ask the reviewer to use the purchase view as the benchmark and to distrust it as a conclusion; the rule in force has the first half only.

**6. "An add is a purchase", all six questions, against an add tested on price and comparison (PARTLY, A row 2; B row 32).** No v5 row cuts against re-running every question; the rows that exist assume the knowledge carries over: "If a business is attractive enough to buy once, it may well pay to repeat the process." **[L1994-032]**; "those are companies we know, understand, obviously like to some degree." **[M2007-107]**. **7. The account (ABSENT, A row 25).** No row cuts against an account-specific tax term; **[L1993-008]** keeps buy-and-hold for a tax-exempt holder, which limits how much the term can matter.

**8. Honesty as a binary that forces a sale (PARTLY, A row 42).** The rule in force makes a disqualifying honesty finding H3 OUT and then SELL. **Plainly.** The v5 draft returns no named outcome on any of the six, by design (draft VI, item 2; VII, item 11). It is not true that every held name came back "keep, no add": four did (V and MITSY as findings; TBTC and HRB only by the default, with no reason to sell established either way), NCLTY came back with two questions weighing toward sale and no position taken, and ASML came back "keep" on the holdings questions alone with Q6 pressing toward sale. No run returned a sale and no run returned an act other than "no add". Read through the outcome vocabulary in force, the same facts give one HOLD, ADDS BARRED (V), one that could be either (MITSY), and four SELL REVIEWs, one of which (ASML) could be SELL.

**Defect or faithful reading.** Both. It is faithful: the draft searched for named outcomes and an intermediate state and found none (VI, items 2 and 3), and its burden rule, "when in doubt, keep holding" **[M2009-040]**, is a row. It is a defect in use: a review that ends in weights cannot be acted on, cannot be compared from one review to the next, and has no state for "this weighs toward sale and the deciding document is not yet read", which is exactly the state the rule in force calls SELL REVIEW and which four of the six runs reached. The runs themselves record the cost: the TBTC run says "the draft does not say what a holder does with a name the purchase draft now puts in that box"; the ASML run says the draft "does not say what a purchase-side verdict means for a name already held".

## E. The verdict for this part, as pre-registered, and the limits

**By the pre-registered rule, the hypothesis does not fail on the holdings framework.** No load-bearing rule of the holdings framework in force is ABSENT or CONTRADICTED. The one CONTRADICTED rule, the PERMANENT designation (A rows 21 and 52), is not load-bearing by the definition and is resolved by the authors themselves against the rule in force (**[L2016-010]**, **[R2016-001]**); it is a finding against the rule in force whatever becomes of v5. The ten ABSENT rules are confessed conventions, rules with no id, protocol or project record, or single rows from outside the v5 sources (**[E4-55]**, **[E1-02]**).

**What the pre-registered test does not catch, stated as part 3 stated it.** The definition is doing the work. The absences that mattered when the draft was used are structural, and a structural rule has no source statements to count, so it can never be load-bearing:
1. **No named outcome** (A rows 14 to 17). Every run ended in weights; none could be acted on.
2. **No SELL REVIEW state** (A row 15). Four of six runs reached it in substance.
3. **No rule for a held name the purchase questions now reject** (A rows 27 and 65; C item 4). TBTC, ASML, MITSY and HRB each met it and resolved it differently: ASML read it through Q6 as weighing toward sale, HRB and MITSY gave it no force, TBTC left Q6 UNDECIDED.
4. **Q5 and the forcing check need the portfolio** (B rows 2 and 27). Every run marked H-Q5 UNDECIDED or incomplete, and none could confirm the holder could not be forced to sell, because the test protocol barred `PORTFOLIO.md`. This is a limit of the tests as much as of the draft, but the draft's Q5 cannot be answered from the holding alone, where the rule in force's H5 can (it is IN unless a named replacement has cleared).
5. **Adds to a name the purchase questions find TOO HARD.** The draft tests an add on price and comparison with the holding's answers "standing in" for Q1 to Q6 (section IV); the purchase draft counts "more of what I already own" as an ordinary purchase. The NCLTY run notes the two can diverge for a name that is OUT at Q2 yet cheap.

Ten load-bearing rules are PARTLY (A rows 3, 7, 34, 36, 40, 42, 48, 49, 54, 60). In each the reason is reproduced and the force is not: the draft weighs what the rule in force acts on (overvaluation, the return condition, a lost franchise, honesty, a better use). Whether a load-bearing PARTLY counts against the hypothesis is the operator's decision, as part 3 left it. This part's view: rows 40 (the author's 2009 correction) and 48 (no valuation instrument for the overvaluation sale) are the two where a review under the draft would most plausibly reach a different act from a review under the rule in force on the same facts.

**Findings against the rule in force, for addenda at the operator's call.** (1) The PERMANENT designation for marketable securities rests on a passage its authors withdrew in 2016 (section C item 1). (2) H2's retention test quotes the formulation its author called improper in 2009 (**[R2009-002]**; section C item 2). (3) "An add is a purchase", the sentence that joins the two frameworks, carries neither an id nor a CONVENTION label (A row 2). (4) Section V's UNKNOWABLE row cites a buying row (**[E4-12]**) for a holding rule (section C item 4). (5) Two confessed CONVENTIONs now have rows: UNRESEARCHED never forces a sale (**[M2009-040]**) and sell the whole position (**[M2020-020]**).

**Limits.** (1) The v4.1 holdings outcomes of record for the six names were not read; section D's in-force column is a reading of the v5 runs' facts, not a record. (2) The ABSENT searches are regular-expression searches of quote text; a row that states a rule in other words would be missed, and the draft's own sweep (its section VI) was relied on where named. (3) Load-bearing is judged by counting the rows found; a different reader could count the E2-28 conditions (rows 7 and 42) as one rule or three. (4) The 77-rule count depends on how the rule in force was split: duplicates across sections were folded into the first place they appear (overvaluation at rows 3 and 49 is the one exception, kept as reason and as test). (5) Only Q11 sections and boxes of the six runs were read, not their purchase questions in full. (6) This part read `principle_ledger_v5.csv` by search and by the ids the draft and the runs cite; it did not read the v5 ledger end to end.
