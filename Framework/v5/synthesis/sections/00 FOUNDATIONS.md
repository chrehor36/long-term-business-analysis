## THE FOUNDATIONS (the preamble, not a gate)

Before they name any test of a business, the speakers name the ground the whole activity stands on. Buffett calls three ideas, to look at stocks as businesses, to have the proper attitude toward the market, and to operate with a margin of safety, "the three basic ideas that underlie successful investing" **[M1997-135]**; he sets them out one by one from the book he learned them in **[M1996-085]** and returns to them a decade later as "three big lessons" **[M2008-001]**. To the three ideas the rows add a condition of the person: "the temperament is all important" **[M2004-035]**. None of these is a step in a purchase. They decide how the analyst reads every price, forecast, adviser and conclusion that the questions after them will meet, and no business passes or fails on them. The merged map adds four companions the rows press as generally: no macro forecast, who is paid to tell you, the index, and the analyst's habits.

### A share is a business

> "And I really think it is not a bad mindset, whenever you buy a stock to say, “**Would I be happy buying this stock if the market closed for five years?**” Because then **you’re buying a business**, if you say yes to that." — **[M1997-109]**, 1997 annual meeting

A share is bought for the cash the asset itself will produce, "Not by selling it to somebody else" **[M1997-052]**. The speakers call the other focus, on what the next fellow will pay, speculation, and decline to play it because they "bring nothing to the party" **[L2000-027]**; Buffett says he is "unable to speculate successfully" **[L2013-007]**. What decides the outcome is "the prospects of the businesses" **[M1998-099]**, so the analyst asks what will happen to the business and never when the price will move **[M1994-038]**, and stands to be right or wrong about the business, not about the quotation **[M1997-052]**.

**The test:** would I be content to own this if the market closed for years **[M1997-109]**, or if the stock were delisted and reopened long after **[M1996-067]**? A "no" means "you may not be focusing on the proper thing" **[M1997-109]**.

**Rules out:** buying because the price has risen, since appreciation "is never a reason to buy it" **[L2013-007]**, **[M2011-028]**; buying on what the next buyer will pay **[L2000-027]**; reading potential from a low price per share **[M1997-134]**.

### The market serves, it does not instruct

> "the market is there to serve you and not to instruct you. **That’s of enormous importance.** [...] The market doesn’t instruct us; the market is there to serve us. If it does something silly, we get a chance to do something because it’s doing something silly. We do it. But **it doesn’t tell us anything. It just tells us prices.** And if the price is out of line where the facts and reasoning lead you, then you — then action is called for." — **[M2006-077]**, 2006 annual meeting

The quotation carries no instruction about value **[M2006-077]**. To steer by it is to assume "that the stock market knows more than you do", and the remedy is to "formulate your ideas on price and value" and buy more when the price falls and funds allow **[M2003-046]**. The speakers do not try to pick bottoms: "Pricing is our game" **[M2009-101]**. The servant is useful only to an owner who is never forced to sell to it: "you don’t want to put yourself in a position where you have to sell" **[M2020-007]**, and an owner on borrowed money "could have been cleaned out" through falls in which nothing was wrong with the business **[M2020-022]**.

**The test:** is the price out of line with where the facts and reasoning lead **[M2006-077]**? Am I prepared to see it "go down 50 percent — or more — and be comfortable with it" **[M2020-022]**, and if a fall of 15% makes a difference to me, do I need "a somewhat different investment philosophy" **[M2025-025]**?

**Rules out:** momentum and charting, which treat the market as instructing **[M2006-077]**; dumping "a solid business with good long-term prospects" because others are frightened **[L2013-011]**.

### Margin of safety

> "if you have to actually do it on — with pencil and paper, **it’s too close to think about**. I mean, it ought to just kind of scream at you that you’ve got this **huge margin of safety**." — **[M1996-084]**, 1996 annual meeting

Buffett's picture is a truck that weighs nearly what the bridge is rated for: go down the road and find a bridge rated well above it **[M1996-085]**; the 2008 statement is "always leaving some extra room and things" **[M2008-001]**. The margin is set so wide that no calculation "to three decimal places" is needed, because to think one can pinpoint the value is "kidding yourself" **[M1995-115]**; they want the decision "obvious enough to us that it doesn’t require making a detailed calculation" **[M1995-032]**. The margin widens as understanding of the business falls and its volatility rises **[M1997-080]**. In arithmetic it is taken as "a big discount" from a present value at the risk-free rate, not as a higher rate **[M1997-126]**; that arithmetic belongs to the value question (Q7), and what the foundation carries is the attitude.

**The test:** if the decision needs pencil and paper, it is too close **[M1996-084]**.

**Rules out:** the close call that needs a spreadsheet to clear **[M1995-115]**, **[M1996-084]**.

### No macro forecast enters

> "I can’t recall a time when Charlie and I have looked at a business, either buying it in its entirety or buying pieces of it through the stock market. I just — **macro conclusions are — just never enter into the discussion.**" — **[M2000-094]**, 2000 annual meeting

The speakers hold no market opinion "because it wouldn’t be any good and it might interfere with the opinions we have that are good" **[M1994-035]**. The rule cuts both ways: no great business was passed over for external conditions, and nothing mediocre was bought because the world looked wonderful **[M2004-102]**, **[M2005-055]**. What counts instead is "the average profitability of the business over time and how strong its competitive mode is" (the transcript's word) **[M2015-016]**; Munger: "Microeconomics is what we do, and macroeconomics is what we put up with." **[M2016-053]**. Fear helps the buyer: the best purchases came "when apprehensions about some macro event were at a peak" **[L1994-004]**. Inflation enters only as a property of the business, its freedom to price without heavy added capital **[M2011-054]**, **[M2005-051]**, never as a forecast **[M2000-136]**. A currency holding is "a bet on how government now, and in the future, will behave" **[M2011-025]**.

**The test:** answer a forecast with "So what?" **[M2000-095]**; ask whether the decision would change if the next stretch of months were known, for a business held a lifetime **[M2015-016]**.

**Rules out:** sitting out a good purchase "because the world’s going to go to hell" **[M2005-055]**, **[L1994-004]**; buying the mediocre on a bright outlook **[M2004-102]**; currency-related investments unless "getting paid extremely well" for them **[M2011-025]**.

### Who is paid to tell you

> "you do not get **impartial advice** from Wall Street — (laughs) — when there’s (an) **enormous amount of fees possible from one action, and no fees applicable from another action**." — **[M2020-037]**, 2020 annual meeting

Owners as a group can earn no more than their businesses earn, and less by the "frictional" costs **[L2005-016]**; "the enemy of investment performance is activity" **[M2005-066]**. The information reaching the investor is unbalanced "because the money is in believing something different" **[M2001-052]**. Asset allocation advice is "just merchandising" sold to people who must first be convinced they have a problem **[M2004-046]**; the money manager's conflict is that asset gathering can pay more than asset managing **[M2010-054]**; the banker's projection of what he is selling always rises **[M2011-083]**; "fees too often lead to transactions" **[L2014-018]**; Munger names "fee-driven buying" **[M2021-028]**. The speakers trace most of this to "subconscious poor cognition", not malice **[M2010-072]**: "most good salespeople believe their own baloney" **[M2020-009]**. The rule is applied to Berkshire's own commentary when it has "a dog in this fight" **[L2009-015]**.

**The test:** who gains by persuading me I have a problem **[M2004-046]**? Is the speaker paid more for one answer than another **[M2020-037]**? Would the manager accept pay only for what he delivers "beyond what you can get yourself" **[M2017-060]**? "don’t ask the barber whether you need a haircut" **[M2011-083]**.

**Rules out:** fee-driven buying **[M2021-028]**, a seller's projection of what he sells **[M2011-083]**, and managers paid for gathering assets **[M2010-054]**.

### Temperament

> "**And the temperament is all important.** I mean, if you can’t control yourself, no matter what the intellect you bring to the process, you know, you’re going to have disasters. [...] It’s not a business that requires extraordinary intellect. **It does require extraordinary discipline.**" — **[M2004-035]**, 2004 annual meeting

Temperament is "the most important", but Munger adds that alone it "won’t do it": it needs "the right basic idea" and long curiosity **[M2004-115]**. A genius IQ is not needed and "can hurt"; what is needed is "an emotional stability", "an inner peace about your decisions", and the ability "to think for yourself" **[M2009-020]**. Overrating one's own ability is "a disaster" **[M2009-021]**; the ability "to detach yourself from the crowd" is "a quality you need" **[M2002-039]**; independent thinking and emotional stability are "vital to long-term investment success" **[L2006-013]**. Buffett calls sanity "the main requirement in this business" **[M2022-012]** and the game "easy" for one who can "control your emotions" **[M2015-041]**. The record is credited to doing "fewer dumb things than most people" **[M1999-109]**, and a loss is not chased where it was taken **[L1994-036]**.

**The test:** when others are fearful, do I get scared, and do I need someone else to tell me when to buy **[M2010-073]**? Does a fall in price change how I feel about what I own **[M2025-025]**?

**Rules out:** IQ or higher mathematics as the edge **[M2009-020]**, **[M2009-007]**; courage borrowed from a broker **[M2010-073]**.

### The index for the non-professional

> "under the conditions you name, I’d probably have it **all in a very low-cost index fund** [...] And because you postulated that **you’re not going to become a professional investor**, I would recognize the fact that I’m an amateur investor" — **[M2008-055]**, 2008 annual meeting

This is a fork before any question: the investor who will not be a professional should own a low-cost index and stop here **[M2008-055]**, **[M2011-039]**. Such an investor "must recognize your limitations and follow a course certain to work reasonably well" **[L2013-005]**; Buffett's own instruction for his trustee is the index plus short government bonds **[L2013-014]**. The reason is arithmetic: passive investors earn the average less low costs, active investors the average less high costs, so the passive group, the "know-nothings", must win **[L2007-018]**, **[M2016-035]**. The index is accumulated over time rather than bought all at once, with attention to cost **[M2004-059]**, **[M2002-010]**. For the one who does know what he is doing the rows turn the other way: wide diversification for the skilled is "madness" **[M2004-041]**, and two or three stocks suffice "if you know what you’re doing" **[M2019-080]**; but for the many Buffett does not "think most people are in a position to pick single stocks" **[M2020-009]**.

**The test:** will I become a professional, or stay an amateur **[M2008-055]**? Is the aim the largest result, or "peace of mind on something that cannot get a bad result" **[M2014-016]**?

**Rules out:** high-fee managers for the investor who cannot pick **[L2013-014]**, **[M2008-057]**.

### The analyst's habits

> "Darwin used to say that any time he got any evidence that flew in the face of his previous convictions, he had to **write it down in the first 30 minutes** or the mind was such that it would **reject contrary evidence to cherished beliefs**." — **[M1997-127]**, 1997 annual meeting

The rows carry the analyst's habits as rules of conduct, not as gates. Write contrary evidence down at once **[M1997-127]**; "destroy our previous ideas", since the worst anchor "is always your previous conclusion" **[M2016-054]**; earn the right to disagree by stating the other side's case better than its holder **[M2016-055]**, **[M2025-033]**; look in every business for "what’s wrong" and "what you’re missing" **[M2025-013]**. Scuttlebutt comes last and is aimed "to possibly reject your original hypothesis" **[M1998-144]**. Do one's own reading and checking: decisions are not made "based on what other people think" **[M1994-014]**, ratings are not used because "we don’t believe in outsourcing investment decisions" **[M2009-011]**, and projections are not consulted, with the projector's past projections asked for when they are offered **[M1995-050]**, **[M2003-065]**. Formulas are distrusted where the assumptions behind the symbols go unexamined **[L2008-018]** or the problem is twisted "so they fit the solution" **[M2012-083]**. Decisions are graded afterwards against what the alternatives did **[M1999-125]**, **[L1995-027]**, and other people's errors are studied as the cheaper teacher **[M2012-104]**.

**The test:** "What do I not know that I need to know?" **[M1999-129]**; what competitor do they fear most, and why **[M1998-145]**, **[M1999-130]**; "And then what?" to every economic assertion **[M1997-110]**, **[M2018-062]**; pick the extreme example and ask "what in hell is going on here" **[M1997-129]**; can I write the story of why it is worth this amount, easily **[M1999-131]**?

**Rules out:** a decision resting on projections **[M1995-050]**, ratings **[M2009-011]**, history-based models **[L2008-016]**, or the opinion poll **[M1994-014]**. A "what would prove me wrong" gate committed before buying: no instance found in the v5 ledger (a text sweep for "prove me/us wrong" and for a written sell rule found none) or in the four theme maps; the nearest rows are these habits, and the after-purchase triggers belong to Q11 **[M2009-040]**, **[M2016-081]**.

### What the foundations do not do

None of the foundations admits or rejects a business. The speakers call them the "basic ideas that underlie" the activity **[M1997-135]** and "lessons" about one's attitude toward stocks and markets **[M2008-001]**, and their verdict boxes, "in, out, and too hard" **[M2006-013]**, are filled by the questions that follow, not here. The margin of safety is an attitude here and an arithmetic only at the value question **[M1997-126]**. The index fork decides whether the questions are used at all, not how a business answers them **[M2008-055]**. A failed foundation is a defect in the analyst, to be repaired; it is not an OUT for the company.

### Tensions carried

- **M1999-082** vs **M1999-084**: the market "fairly efficient" at valuing businesses, against a method that buys when the price is below the value; Buffett holds both in one answer.
- **M2005-074** vs **M2005-054**: something intelligent can "very infrequently" be said about the market as a whole, against spending no time on what the market will do.
- **M1995-034** vs **L1994-004** (also **M1994-065**): wait when nothing makes sense, against never letting fear of unknowns defer the deployment of capital.
- **M2000-094**, **M2005-023** and **M2006-038** vs **L2004-012**: no decision on a macro view, beside a large currency commitment on a judgment about the dollar.
- **M2025-018** vs **L2023-007**: the yen borrowing called an incremental opportunity at the meeting, stated as financing policy in the letter.
- **M2005-051** vs **M2000-136**: inflation "always a factor" in the kind of business bought, against inflation never entering decision making.
- **M2017-024** vs **L2000-025** (also **M2017-063**, **M2023-009**): the huge margins of safety of the old days are gone, against acting when the price is "startlingly low".
- **M1995-050** vs **M1995-032**: never looked at a projection, yet value is a discounted stream of future cash.
- **M2003-065** vs **M2003-138**: no interest in anybody's projections, against having to "feel pretty good about our projections".
- **M2003-014** vs **M2003-015**: Buffett welcomes counterparties who price by formula, Munger calls the formula "a know-nothing value system".
- **M2004-041** vs **M2004-059**: wide diversification is madness for the skilled, yet the new investor should accumulate an index.
- **M2019-080** vs **M2020-009** and **M2021-003**: two or three stocks suffice, against the index for most people.
