## 1. The foundations

The foundation that bears hardest is **no macro forecast** **[M2000-094]**: about half of FY3/2026 profit came from Mineral & Metal Resources (¥253.6bn) and Energy (¥164.2bn) of ¥834.0bn (AR107 pp.69-70), and the company itself writes that "the condition of the market and the production volume for mineral resources and energy has become a significant variable in operating results" (AR107 p.66). Any reading of this name that rests on an iron ore, LNG or oil price is a macro forecast in disguise, and the run treats it as one. The **currency** row applies to a dollar-based holder: a yen earner held through a dollar ADR is partly "a bet on how government now, and in the future, will behave" **[M2011-025]**; the run works in yen. **Who is paid to tell you** **[M2011-083]**: the only document that claims a low-cost position for the iron ore assets is the company's own Integrated Report, and its chart is marked "(Illustrative purposes only)" (IR2026 PDF p.19). **The analyst's habits** bear directly: the operator holds this name, and the worst anchor "is always your previous conclusion" **[M2016-054]**; the run writes contrary evidence down as found **[M1997-127]**. The v5 ledger also carries the speakers' own remarks on the five Japanese trading houses **[M2023-031]**, **[L2023-008]**, **[M2025-018]**; decisions are not made "based on what other people think" **[M1994-014]** and "we don’t believe in outsourcing investment decisions" **[M2009-011]**, so those rows are used below only where the draft's own tests call for them, never as a verdict borrowed. **A share as a business** **[M1997-109]** and **margin of safety** **[M1996-084]** are carried to Q7.

## 2. The standing rule

Owning this does not by itself put the buyer at risk of ruin, provided it is held without borrowed money and without anything that can force a sale **[M2012-081]**, **[L2014-005]**, **[M2020-022]**; the run has no knowledge of how the operator's position is financed and takes no position on it.

## 3. The questions

### Q1. CAN I UNDERSTAND IT? Where its economics will be in five, ten or twenty years, inside the perimeter. STOP.

**The test as the draft states it.** Understanding is "a reasonable fix on about what the earning power and competitive position will look like in five or 10 years" **[M2012-065]**, **[M2000-037]**, asked by one who knows "where the perimeter is" **[M2010-083]**; the tests that bear are 2 (key variables and how predictable), 3 (do the past statements tell the future ones), 4 (important and knowable), 6 (the winner, not the industry), 9 (doubt), 11 (abroad) and 12 (a lender or a derivatives book).

**Applied to the filings.**
1. **What the business is.** A holding of 446 affiliated companies (278 subsidiaries, 168 equity-method investees) across seven segments (AR107 p.4). FY3/2026 profit of ¥834.0bn was made of share of profit of equity-method investees ¥447.4bn, dividend income ¥178.7bn, and gross profit ¥1,328.2bn less SG&A ¥902.1bn, net interest, gains and tax (AR107 p.67). Cash dividends received, including from equity-method investees, were ¥550.6bn (AR107 p.83; statement of cash flows p.214). Most of what the owner earns is therefore other companies' earnings, received as dividends or carried at equity.
2. **Key variables (test 2).** The filing names them: iron ore, where "a change of US$1 per ton ... is estimated to have an effect of ¥3.0 billion" on profit (AR107 p.69), equity production 63.8 Mt; oil, ¥0.9bn net per US$1/bbl (p.70); copper (Collahuasi, p.69); LNG prices and volumes (dividends from four LNG projects ¥69.2bn, p.70); and the yen against the Australian and US dollars (translation adjustments of +¥555.6bn in one year, p.83). To these the plan adds the returns on new capital: ¥1,380bn of investments and loans in FY3/2026 alone (p.78), including ¥723.8bn for the Rhodes Ridge iron ore project (p.85), whose "First ore [is] planned by 2030" (IR2026 PDF p.19), into an iron ore market where the filing expects China's steel output "to remain flat or in decline" (AR107 p.66). These are the variables on which the ten-year earning power turns, and none of them is predictable from where the analyst stands: "If something is not very predictable, forget it." **[M1998-044]**; "If something’s important but unknowable, forget it." **[M2006-076]**.
3. **Do the past statements tell the future ones (test 3)?** **[M2008-033]**. Five years: profit attributable ¥914.7bn, ¥1,130.6bn, ¥1,063.7bn, ¥900.3bn, ¥834.0bn (AR107 p.1); then Q1 FY3/27 up 53.4% to ¥294.1bn, with an ¥87.0bn valuation gain and a ¥30.8bn other loss on one US real estate business, and energy driven by transit restrictions in the Strait of Hormuz (Q1 FY3/27 pp.2-3). The cash series is steadier (own core operating cash flow ¥1,159bn, ¥1,206bn, ¥996bn, ¥1,027bn, ¥979bn; `arith.py`), which counts in the name's favour, but five years of a commodity-weighted portfolio is one partial cycle, and the statements show the level, not where it will be in ten years.
4. **A lender and a derivatives book inside the whole (test 12; "What cannot be judged from the outside").** JA Mitsui Leasing, an equity-method investee, bought receivables by factoring from First Brands Group, which "may have engaged in practices such as inflated billing, fabricated billing, or multiple assignments of considerable amounts of its receivables"; Mitsui's share of the loss was ¥60.4bn, plus ¥21.9bn in other comprehensive income, "additional gains or losses may arise", and Mitsui subscribed to the investee's capital increase (AR107 p.299). Current derivative assets and margin deposits rose by about ¥1,030.7bn and derivative and other financial liabilities by ¥1,152.8bn in one year (pp.79, 82); commodity VaR is ¥50.6bn at 10 days, 99% (p.253); financial guarantees outstanding ¥628.8bn (p.294). "You spot troubles in financial institutions late. It’s just the nature of the beast." **[M2001-066]**; "the only thing we understand is that we don't understand how much risk the institution is running." **[L2002-018]**; understanding each transaction is not understanding the whole **[M2002-094]**. Mitsui is not a bank, and these are not the bulk of it; the point is narrower: the run cannot see these books from the filing, and the one loss they produced this year was found by a bankruptcy, not by the accounts.
5. **The winner, not the industry (test 6), and doubt (test 9).** "there’s industries we know that may have a wonderful future, but we don’t have the faintest idea who the winners will be, so we don’t think about those, either." **[M2012-067]**. The v5 ledger's own row on this group says what level of understanding the speakers claimed: "They owned a whole bunch of businesses that we could understand as a group, although it didn’t mean we had deep understanding on any." **[M2023-031]**. The draft carries the basket as a reader's tension (**[M2002-060]** and **[M2010-085]** against **[M2002-092]**) and gives Q1 only one rule for the edge: "if you have doubts about something being into your circle of competence, it isn’t." **[M2002-092]**; "it’s better to be well within the circle than to be trying to tiptoe along the line." **[M2002-093]**. This run is of one house, not a basket, and the doubt is written down above in items 2 to 4.
6. **Abroad (test 11).** The governance system is legible: an English filing, an Audit & Supervisory Board structure, a majority of external members on the nomination and governance committees (AR107 pp.111, 115) **[M2000-141]**. This test passes; it is not what decides.

**What the draft would have me weigh on the other side, written down.** The product can be opaque if the economics are clear **[M2011-014]**; PetroChina was decided "off the figures" **[M2008-069]**; the precision asked is low **[M2015-086]**; and Q2's commodity rule presupposes that some commodity producers pass Q1. Against that, PetroChina was one producer of one commodity; Mitsui's ten-year earning power depends on several commodity prices, two exchange rates, the returns on about ¥1.4tn a year of new investment chosen from a ¥6tn pipeline (IR2026 PDF p.32), and affiliates whose books the run cannot read. The understanding available from the filings is of the parts, one by one, not of where the whole will stand in ten years.

**Verdict: TOO HARD.** The ten-year earning power turns on key variables the filing itself names and the analyst cannot predict **[M1998-044]**, **[M2006-076]**, inside a whole that includes a leasing and factoring book and a derivatives book that cannot be read from outside **[M2001-066]**, **[L2002-018]**; the doubt is documented, and doubt puts it outside **[M2002-092]**. This is not a judgment of quality: "It just means that we don’t know how to evaluate it." **[M2000-038]**; the box is "too hard" **[M2006-013]**, and "we just rule those out" **[M2008-085]**. **The run closes here.**

## 5. Q11 under the holdings draft (the operator holds the name), and Q12

The holdings draft starts from the default, "when in doubt, keep holding. But it’s no inviolable rule." **[M2009-040]**, and confirms first that the owner cannot be forced to sell **[M2020-007]**, **[M2020-022]** (not known to this run). The default belongs to "a business that is both understandable and durably wonderful" **[L1993-014]**; the purchase draft's Q1 above did not find this one understandable in its sense, and that bears on H6 below, not on the burden, which stays on the reason to sell.

**H1. What has moved: the business, or only its price?** STOP against selling on price alone. The price fell from the FY high of ¥6,674 (AR107 p.1) and from about ¥5,959 at March 31, 2026 (PER 20.47 x EPS ¥291.12, p.1) to ¥4,902. The business did not move by that much: FY3/2026 profit fell 7.4% and core cash 4.7%, and Q1 FY3/27 profit rose 53.4% (Q1 FY3/27 p.1). "the stock does not know that you own it." **[M1995-022]**; a fall is a reason to buy more if money is available, and otherwise "so what?" **[M1997-144]**; the loss is measured as an owner of the whole would measure it **[M2020-018]**. No silly price is in view at about 15 times forward earnings (company forecast ¥324.54 EPS for FY3/27, Q1 FY3/27 p.1) **[M2007-089]**. **Answer: price only; no action follows.**

**H2. Has the castle changed?** WEIGHING. Earnings allowing for industry conditions **[L2007-016]**: profit has fallen three years running from the FY3/2023 peak as iron ore, coal and LNG prices fell (AR107 pp.69-70); that is industry conditions, not a castle event. Threats graded **[M2016-081]**: the First Brands loss at JA Mitsui Leasing, ¥60.4bn against ¥834.0bn, is minor in the sense of **[M2012-051]** (earning power five years out not materially affected); the Strait of Hormuz cuts both ways for an LNG and oil holder and is not graded as life-threatening; China's steel outlook "flat or in decline" (AR107 p.66) against a ¥724bn new iron ore commitment is the slow kind that "can lull you to sleep" **[M2014-038]**, and the draft forbids treating it as no change and forbids selling on it alone **[M2016-080]**. **Answer: UNDECIDED, leaning no change in the castle; no threat graded above major.**

**H3. Has the management changed, or changed after being paid?** WEIGHING on ability. President and CEO Kenichi Hori signs both the FY3/2026 statements and the August 2026 notices (AR107 p.300; Notice 2026-08-04). Pay: directors' total ¥2,956m against profit ¥834bn; the cash bonus is a fixed share of profit and core operating cash flow with no charge for the capital used (AR107 pp.175-176, 181), which the rows call "a phony batting average" when the cost of capital is left out **[M1995-010]**; the performance-linked stock runs on ROE over three years (p.176), which carries the capital in it. Response to a problem **[M2017-005]**: on First Brands the company recognized the loss and put in more capital "as a shareholder" (p.299); that is support, not yet evidence of a cure. No loss of trust found **[L2022-001]**. **Answer: no change of management; no loss of trust; pay design weighs slightly against.**

**H4. Is the money kept still becoming more than a dollar?** WEIGHING, with a STOP on further money into a business that chews it up. From end-FY3/2022 to now, about ¥1,903bn was retained after dividends and buybacks (profit ¥3,928.6bn less dividends ¥1,016.5bn and net buybacks ¥1,008.9bn, FY3/2023 to FY3/2026; AR104, AR106, AR107 cash flow statements), about ¥637 a share. Market value per share rose from about ¥1,665 (PER 5.93 x EPS ¥280.81, AR107 p.1) to ¥4,902, about ¥3,237: the market leg of **[R1995-009]** passes by a wide margin, but much of it is re-rating, and the 2009 rewrite warns that the market leg alone misleads **[R2009-002]**. The earnings leg **[M2011-072]** does not pass over this span: EPS ¥360.91 in FY3/2023 to ¥291.12 in FY3/2026 while the retained capital grew; the span starts at a commodity peak, so this reads as a presumption to watch, not a finding **[M1998-110]**. Buybacks: ¥199.6bn in FY3/2026 at an average of about ¥4,869 (41,075,000 shares for ¥199,999,903,991; AR107 p.109) and a further ¥200bn authorized in August 2026; the notice states a number of shares, a sum and a period, and no price above which it will stop (Notice 2026-08-04), which is the form the rows read against **[L2016-002]**, **[L2011-003]**; whether the prices paid were below value is not established, because Q7 was not reached. Issuance is negligible (restricted-stock grants of about 0.5m shares a year, AR107 pp.103-104) **[L2014-012]**. The STOP **[L2023-010]**, **[M2020-026]**: not triggered for the whole; the capital increase into JA Mitsui Leasing is the one place to watch. **Answer: mixed; market leg passes, earnings leg fails over a peak-started span, buybacks carry no stated price; no STOP.**

**H5. Is there a plainly better use for this money?** WEIGHING. The run may not open the operator's portfolio and cannot name the least attractive holding **[M2007-104]**. The draft asks for "something you like immensely better" **[M1998-013]**, not a marginal swap, and a swap carries the two-decisions cost **[M1999-079]**. **Answer: UNDECIDED; nothing in this run supplies a better use.**

**H6. Was it a mistake to buy, now recognised?** WEIGHING on the finding. The holdings draft separates a misjudgment at entry from a change in the world **[M2012-031]**, **[M2016-065]**. Nothing in the filings shows the world changing against the purchase case. What this run found is that the v5 purchase draft's Q1, with its ten-year definition of understanding, does not admit this name for this analyst; that is a finding about the perimeter under a candidate framework, not a new fact about the business, and the holdings draft gives it no force of its own. Contrary evidence is written down here as the draft requires **[M1997-127]**, and if the operator comes to the same doubt, the rows press for the whole position and promptly **[M2020-020]**, **[L2008-003]**. **Answer: UNDECIDED; no mistake recognised on the filings; the Q1 finding is recorded as the open item for the owner.**

**Holdings summary.** No reason the rows name for selling is established; the default holds **[M2009-040]**. An add is a purchase on price and on the comparison (holdings draft section IV), and the purchase questions here closed at Q1 and, on the computation, would have closed again at Q7; so an add is not supported by this run. The holdings draft gives no named outcome (its section VI, item 2), and none is invented here.

**Q12, the newspaper test.** WEIGHING for a marketable stake **[M2008-011]**, **[M2021-012]**; none of the businesses the draft names (casinos, tobacco, loading schemes) is present. The facts an unfriendly reporter would use: a continuing interest in Sakhalin II under the Russia sanctions regime (carrying value ¥58.8bn, AR107 p.299) and metallurgical coal (p.69). **Answer: UNDECIDED; no named business; recorded.**

## 8. What in the draft was wrong or unclear

Q1 does not say how understanding is judged for a holding company: by the parts, by the whole, or, as the speakers say they bought this very group, "as a group, although it didn’t mean we had deep understanding on any" **[M2023-031]**; the draft carries the basket only as a reader's tension (**[M2002-060]**, **[M2010-085]** against **[M2002-092]**) and gives the run no rule, so I applied the one rule Q1 does state for the edge (doubt means outside) and wrote the doubt down. Second, Q1's test 2 ("how predictable" the key variables are, **[M1998-044]**) would stop most commodity producers, while Q2's commodity rule and its low-cost exception presuppose that some reach Q2; the draft does not say whether a commodity price is a "key variable" in Q1's sense or a matter for Q2's cost position, and the verdict at Q1 or Q2 turns on that choice. Third, Q3, Q4 and Q7 have no instruction for equity-method income: Q4's "regular pretax earnings" and Q7's "cash that can be taken out" diverge when a large share of profit (¥447bn) is booked at equity and only part (¥370bn) arrives as cash, and the draft does not say whether to count the investees' retained earnings (look-through) or only dividends received; I used dividends received, which is the cash, and said what it leaves out. Fourth, Q2's exception is written for "the low-cost operator" **[L2004-007]**, and the draft does not say whether a minority, non-operating partner in an operator's low-cost mine qualifies, nor whether the company's own illustrative cost-curve claim can establish it when the statutory filing is silent. Fifth, the protocol asks for an accession number, which a Japanese filer does not have on EDINET without an API key; a non-SEC identifier rule is missing. Finally, the v5 ledger holds rows that discuss this exact name, and neither the draft nor the protocol says whether a run may use the speakers' own remarks on the company under test; I used one only where Q1's own tests pointed to it and said so.
