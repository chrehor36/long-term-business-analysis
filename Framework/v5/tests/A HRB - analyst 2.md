### Q1 — CAN I UNDERSTAND IT? STOP. **Verdict: IN.**

**The test as the draft states it:** "a reasonable fix on about what the earning power and competitive position will look like in five or 10 years" **[M2012-065]**; identify "the key variables in that particular business, and evaluating how predictable they were" **[M1998-044]**; "do I understand enough about this business so that the financial statements will tell me the information that’s useful to me in making a judgment about what the future financial statements are going to look like" **[M2008-033]**.

**Applied to the filings.**
- *How it makes money.* One reported segment (10-K FY2026, Note 13, acc. 0000012659-26-000026). FY2026 revenue $3,945.4 million, of which U.S. assisted tax preparation $2,560.9 million, royalties $185.4 million, DIY $384.6 million, refund transfers $145.1 million, and the rest smaller (MD&A, Consolidated – Financial Results table). Costs are mostly field wages ($996.7 million), occupancy ($457.2 million) and marketing ($277.8 million). The business is seasonal: "we generally operate at a loss through the first two quarters of our fiscal year" (Item 1, Seasonality).
- *Key variables.* Two, as with Coca-Cola's two **[M1998-040]**: the number of returns prepared and the net average charge per return. The filing states them directly: assisted revenue rose 6.1% "due to a 4.0% increase in net average charge combined with a 2.0% increase in company-owned tax return volumes"; DIY revenue rose 0.2% "due to a 4.2% increase in paid net average charge" (MD&A, FY2026 compared to FY2025).
- *Do the past statements tell the future ones?* Over the span read, yes in direction: revenue, pretax income and cash flow move with volume and price, and the 10-K reports both every year. Net income from continuing operations $598.0, $609.5 and $736.3 million for FY2024 to FY2026, the last including a discrete tax benefit of $84.1 million (Statements of Operations; MD&A, income taxes).
- *Customers or technology?* The forecast that matters is a forecast of customer behaviour, whether people keep paying a person to prepare their return, which the rows treat as a consumer question, "what we think we can project out in terms of consumer behavior and threats to a business" **[M2023-030]**, **[M2017-019]**.

**The judgment.** The economics are plain and the key variables are named and reported: I understand how this makes money and what moves it **[M1998-044]**, **[M2008-033]**. What I cannot see is the second half of test 4, whether the volume variable is knowable ten years out against artificial intelligence and government filing (the 10-K's own words: "Technology advances quickly and in new and unexpected ways, and it is difficult to predict the manner in which these changes will impact the tax return preparation industry", Item 1A). The draft routes that question: "Rapid change is owned by Q2 ... Q1 points to it". I follow the routing and carry the doubt forward, with the note that **[M2002-092]** ("if you have doubts about something being into your circle of competence, it isn’t") pulls the other way, and that an analyst who placed the doubt here would close the file at Q1 in the same box. ## 8. What in the draft was wrong or unclear

Three things. First, **the boundary between Q1 and Q2 for a business whose economics are plain but whose future turns on a technology change.** Q1 says rapid change "is owned by Q2 ... Q1 points to it, and where it puts the forecast out of reach the box is TOO HARD", while Q1's own test 4 ("If something’s important but unknowable, forget it" **[M2006-076]**) and test 9 ("if you have doubts ... it isn’t" **[M2002-092]**) would close the file at Q1 on the same doubt; the box is the same either way, but the deciding question is not, and a reproducibility test that scores the deciding question will split on it. I followed the routing sentence and stopped at Q2. Second, **whether the hard sequence closes Q11 for a held name.** The protocol says a failing STOP marks later questions NOT REACHED, yet Q11 is asked "only after purchase" and the holdings draft's own section IV lets an add rest on the holding's answers instead of purchase Q1 to Q6; neither draft says what a holder does when the purchase draft puts a held name in TOO HARD, or whether an add on such a name is barred by the purchase STOP or permitted on Q7 and Q8 alone. I answered Q11 in full and left the add unresolved. Third, two smaller gaps: Q4 lists "EBITDA as earnings and managements that talk it **[M2002-026]**" among what it rules OUT and quotes a count of "about zero", yet the protocol confines Q4's STOP to confusion or suspicion, so a company that reconciles EBITDA cleanly to GAAP but pays its executives on it falls between the two (not reached here, but it would have been the next question); and the whitelisted `tools/run.py` prints v4 ids and a v4 numeric floor, which the protocol forbids the session to open in source form, so the tool leaks what the whitelist excludes. The brief's April 30 fiscal year was also stale (the filings say June 30 since FY2022); I used the filed year.
