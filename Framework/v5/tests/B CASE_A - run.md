## 3. The questions

### Q1. CAN I UNDERSTAND IT? STOP. **Verdict: TOO HARD.**

**The test as the draft states it.** Understanding is "a reasonable fix on about what the earning power and competitive position will look like in five or 10 years" **[M2012-065]**. For a financial institution the draft opens a narrow door: a bank can be understood when its two sides can be read, "very little risk on the asset side and very cheap money on the deposit side" **[M2002-022]**, the danger being "always on the asset side" **[M2011-022]**; and it closes the door where "the asset side cannot be read from the outside, as with derivatives 'on top of it' **[M2005-068]**, the file closes at Q1 in the 'too hard' box **[M2006-013]**."

**Applied to the case.**

1. **What it is** (DESC_A, from Item 1 of the filing of record): a bank holding company with financial holding company status, the third-largest in its home country by assets, designated a global systemically important bank, with one national bank holding roughly 89% of assets, three segments (community banking; wholesale banking; wealth and investment management), and subsidiaries in wholesale banking, securities brokerage and investment banking, mortgage-backed securities servicing and venture capital among many others. FY2016 (filed 2017-03-01): assets 1,930,115; liabilities 1,729,618; equity 199,581; net income 21,938; interest and dividend income 53,663.
2. **The asset side cannot be read from the case.** The case carries total assets only: no loan book by type, no credit quality, no charge-off history, no securities portfolio, no trading assets, no derivatives figure. The one test the door sets is the one test these files cannot run **[M2011-022]**. The case does not show derivatives, but it shows investment banking, wholesale banking and mortgage-backed securities servicing inside a bank that is, by its own designation, one of the systemically important banks of the world; that is the class the row names: "no one probably knows [...] even within a reasonable range — the exact condition of some of the biggest, you know, banks in the world" **[M2005-068]**; and of the derivatives footnotes of major banks, "the only thing we understand is that we don't understand how much risk the institution is running" **[L2002-018]**.
3. **The deposit side cannot be read either.** The case gives no deposit total, no deposit cost and no funding mix. What it does show is long-term debt rising from 125,354 (2011) to 255,077 (2016), while the description puts a total loss-absorbing capacity requirement on the company; whether the money comes "from a natural customer base" or "on a wholesale basis, and that money can run pretty fast" **[M2012-012]** is not answerable from these files.
4. **The crisis year is opaque in exactly the way the rows warn.** Net income fell from 8,057 (2007) to 2,655 (2008); operating cash flow was -4,788 in 2008; equity rose from 47,886 to 99,084 in the same year; diluted shares rose 55% from 2008 to 2010 and interest income 61% from 2008 to 2009. Something of very large scale happened in 2008 (an acquisition, a capital raise, or both); the case does not say what, nor what the asset side looked like under it. "You spot troubles in financial institutions late. It's just the nature of the beast." **[M2001-066]**; the real test is what the institution "achieves after operating for an extended period in a no-growth mode" **[L2004-009]**, and the case has no no-growth stretch: assets rose every year from 2010 to 2016, 1,258,128 to 1,930,115.
5. **A key variable is set by regulators, and the filing calls it unpredictable.** The description: a capital surcharge, enhanced leverage requirements taking effect within two years, loss-absorbing capacity requirements, stress tests that gate every distribution, and "further legislative and regulatory change could alter its operating environment in substantial and unpredictable ways." The rows: "You can change the math of banking, and the attractiveness of banking, totally, by capital requirements." **[M2016-027]**; "evaluating how predictable they were first [...] If something is not very predictable, forget it." **[M1998-044]**; "If something's important but unknowable, forget it." **[M2006-076]**. The filing itself also says technological advances "may diminish the importance of depository institutions in the transfer of funds", a threat to the cheap-money side the door depends on.
6. **The statements do not tell the future statements** **[M2008-033]**. Operating cash flow runs 58,540 (2012), 57,641 (2013), 17,529 (2014), 169 (2016); for a bank the series says nothing about owner cash. Return on average equity, 11% to 13% from 2011 to 2016 on assets about nine to ten times equity, is a geared return on a commodity, money, which the rows read for "how much of it is gearing" **[M2007-013]**; at that gearing 1% of assets is 19,301, about 10% of equity and about 0.9 times a year's net income.
7. **The whole, not the transactions.** "even though I could understand every individual transaction they did, I don't regard the whole enterprise, or the operation of it, necessarily as being within my circle of competence" **[M2002-094]**. And before "a firm buy decision on a bank" the rows ask for the culture of the institution, "and that's hard to do for 99 percent of the banks" **[M2008-090]**; the case carries nothing on culture.
8. **Doubt.** "if you have doubts about something being into your circle of competence, it isn't." **[M2002-092]**.

**What weighs the other way, recorded and not explained away.** The door is real: "There's no magic to it. You just have to stay away from doing something foolish." **[M1996-091]**; the speakers record misjudging banking the other way, "banking was a way better business than we figured out in advance" **[M2003-114]**; and a community-banking segment with offices in every state of its home country could be the "cheap money on the deposit side" **[M2002-022]**. None of this can be confirmed from the case, and the draft calls the door narrow, for "oddball" banks **[M2002-022]**, which the third-largest systemically important bank in its country is not.

**The verdict, and its two grounds kept apart.** TOO HARD **[M2006-013]**, "It doesn't mean it isn't a good buy [...] It just means that we don't know how to evaluate it." **[M2000-038]**. Ground (a), in the rows: the largest banks with wholesale and investment-banking books are named as the institutions whose condition no one knows within a reasonable range **[M2005-068]**, **[L2002-018]**, and a key variable is set by regulators the filing calls unpredictable **[M2016-027]**, **[M1998-044]**. Ground (b), in the case: the two sides the door asks to read are absent from the files. Ground (b) alone would be a verdict for documents not yet read, which the draft says has no box and would be a CONVENTION (section I); this run does not rely on (b) alone. Ground (a) is a judgment from the rows, and it carries the verdict.

**The documents that would test it** (none opened): the FY2016 10-K in full (MD&A: loans by type and geography, nonaccrual loans and net charge-offs by year, deposits by type and average cost, net interest margin; the derivatives note with notionals, fair values and collateral terms; the regulatory capital and leverage ratios; Item 1A and Item 3); the FY2008 to FY2010 10-Ks for the 2008 event and the asset side through the crisis; the 2017 proxy for pay and culture. The rows suggest that even with them the verdict would likely stand for a bank of this size **[M2005-068]**; that is a reading, not a finding.

## 8. What in the draft was wrong or unclear

The bank door and the bank exclusion give no way to decide between them for a large bank. Q1 says a bank is understood when its two sides can be read **[M2002-022]** and is TOO HARD when "the asset side cannot be read from the outside, as with derivatives" **[M2005-068]**, but it gives no test for when a bank with a large community deposit base and a wholesale and investment-banking book falls on one side or the other. Size is named only in passing ("some of the biggest [...] banks in the world"), the door's banks are called "oddball", and the culture precondition for "a firm buy decision on a bank" **[M2008-090]** sits in Q5's culture section although it reads as a Q1 condition, so where it binds is unclear. Second, and sharper in a sealed case: the draft has no box for "the documents that would decide this were not read" (section I says such a verdict would be a CONVENTION and supplies none), so TOO HARD carries two different meanings, unknowable in principle and unread here. I kept the two grounds apart and let the verdict rest on the first, the rows, not on the missing documents; a scorer should know that a run with the full 10-K might still differ on that judgment. Third, the protocol says a failed STOP closes the run while the instruction for this test says Q12 applies; I recorded Q12 after the close as not part of the box. Step 0's cover-page share count by class also cannot be met from the case, which gives a diluted weighted average.
