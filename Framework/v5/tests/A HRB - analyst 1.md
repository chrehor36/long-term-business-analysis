### Q1. CAN I UNDERSTAND IT? STOP. **Verdict: IN (narrowly).**

**The test as the draft states it:** "a reasonable fix on about what the earning power and competitive position will look like in five or 10 years" **[M2012-065]**; identify the key variables and how predictable they are **[M1998-044]**; do the past statements tell me the future ones **[M2008-033]**.

**Applied to the filings.**
- *How it makes money.* Of $3,945.4M FY2026 revenue, $2,560.9M is U.S. assisted preparation, $185.4M franchise royalties ("approximately 30% of gross tax return preparation and related service revenues"), $384.6M DIY, and the rest refund-linked products, the Emerald Card, International and Wave (10-K, MD&A revenue table; Item 1). 11.3 million U.S. assisted returns and 3.6 million paid DIY returns (10-K, Item 1). The business is seasonal: "we generally operate at a loss through the first two quarters of our fiscal year" (10-K, Item 1).
- *The key variables.* Assisted volume times net average charge, against field wages ($996.7M) and occupancy ($457.2M), both seasonal and largely variable (10-K, MD&A). In FY2026: "a 4.0% increase in net average charge combined with a 2.0% increase in company-owned tax return volumes", while "our total assisted tax return volume ... decreased 0.1%" (10-K, MD&A, FY2026 compared to FY2025). These two variables have been the business for decades; revenue was $2,893.8M in the year ended 2012-04-30 (XBRL, accession 0001574842-14-000031) and $3,945.4M in FY2026, about 2.2% a year compounded (arithmetic, CONVENTION: a compound rate between two filed figures, stated only to show the slope).
- *Do the past statements tell me the future ones?* For the business as it stands, yes: the statements show a stable price-times-volume engine with a stable cost structure **[M2008-033]**. The forecast is chiefly about customers (will people pay a human to prepare and sign their return), which the draft treats as the more forecastable kind **[M2017-019]**, **[M2023-030]**.
- *What I cannot see.* Whether that customer behaviour holds ten years out against artificial intelligence and free government filing. The draft assigns rapid change to Q2: "Rapid change is owned by Q2 ... Q1 points to it". I follow that assignment and carry the question forward.

**Verdict, in the draft's words:** IN. I understand how it makes money and its key variables; the one variable I cannot forecast is the change the draft sends to Q2. I record the doubt: "if you have doubts about something being into your circle of competence, it isn’t" **[M2002-092]** pulls toward closing here, and I let Q2 decide it under the draft's ownership rule (see section 8).

### Q2. WHY IS THE CASTLE STILL STANDING? STOP. **Verdict: TOO HARD. The run closes here.**

**The test as the draft states it:** "why is that castle still standing? And what’s going to keep it standing or cause it not to be standing five, 10, 20 years from now" **[M1995-038]**; the eleven tests; and "a castle whose future cannot be judged goes to TOO HARD" **[M2000-014]**, **[M2006-062]**.

**The tests, applied.**
1. *What keeps it standing.* Eleven million people pay for assisted preparation while free alternatives exist and are named in the filing: "U.S. federal, state, and foreign governmental authorities ... currently offer, or facilitate the offering of, tax return preparation and electronic filing options to taxpayers at no charge", and Free File runs under an agreement "currently set to expire in October 2029" (10-K, Item 1A). The reason the customer comes is a signed return, the "100% accuracy guarantee", audit representation and refund products (10-K, Item 1). The permanence of that reason is the open question.
2. *Without the lord.* Passes. The system of 6,802 company-owned and 1,814 franchise offices (10-K, MD&A) and a seasonal workforce of about 73,400 (10-K, Item 1) ran through a CEO change on January 1, 2026 (proxy, CEO transition) with revenue up 4.9%. It does not need a superstar **[M1996-037]**.
3. *The money test.* Mixed. Large, rich rivals sell both DIY and live-assisted products; HRB's paid DIY revenue rose 0.2% on a 4.2% rise in paid net average charge (10-K, MD&A), which implies fewer paid DIY returns (arithmetic, CONVENTION: revenue change divided by price change). In DIY the castle is being entered; in assisted it is not yet. "one competitor is frequently enough to ruin a business" **[M2012-108]**.
4. *Pricing.* Passes this year: price up 4.0% with company-owned volume up 2.0% **[M2000-031]**, **[M2005-019]**. No prayer session is visible **[M2005-020]**.
5. *Unit volume.* Weak. Total assisted volume fell 0.1% (10-K, MD&A); royalties fell 3.9% as franchises were bought in. "we want a lot more unit cases sold" **[M1999-054]** is not shown; a volume decline is not by itself a fail **[M2015-066]**.
8. *Over the low bid.* Passes on the evidence of paying customers beside free options **[M2017-009]**, **[M2008-076]**.
10. *Widening or narrowing.* No evidence of widening; DIY shows narrowing **[M1999-108]**, **[M2000-075]**.
11. *What could destroy, modify or reduce it, five to fifteen years out* **[M2000-014]**. The company itself answers in its risk factors: "advances in technology, including artificial intelligence and automation, may reduce the need for third-party tax preparation services or increase the ability of taxpayers to complete their returns without paid assistance"; "Technology advances quickly and in new and unexpected ways, and it is difficult to predict the manner in which these changes will impact the tax return preparation industry"; a new risk factor, "Artificial intelligence and emerging technologies may expose us to operational, regulatory, competitive, and reputational risks"; and on government, "in tax seasons 2024 and 2025, the IRS offered a limited free direct tax filing system, and although that program has been suspended, there can be no assurance that it (or similar programs) will not be reinstated or expanded" (10-K, Item 1A). The Gutenberg form of the test **[M2007-133]**, **[L2006-008]**: would anyone build a seasonal network of some 8,600 storefronts today against free filing and AI preparation? I cannot say yes.

**Why the box is TOO HARD and not OUT.** The castle is not shown to be open: it is standing, it prices, and its customers pay over free options, so OUT on the business is not supported. What I cannot judge is whether it stands in ten or twenty years, because the deciding force is a technology the company calls unpredictable. The draft's rows put that case in the third box: "if something comes in where there’s a technological component that’s of significance, or where we think the future technology could hurt the business as it presently exists ... it won’t make it through the filter" **[M1998-008]**; "it’s impossible for us to figure ... we don’t even think about it then" **[M2000-014]**; "when we see a moat that’s tenuous in any way ... We don’t know how to valuate that, and therefore we leave it alone" **[M2000-019]**; "industries where it’s very hard to evaluate moats ... those are the businesses of rapid change" **[M2001-069]**; "Our criterion of "enduring" causes us to rule out companies in industries prone to rapid and continuous change" **[L2007-005]**. The draft's own correction routes that verb to TOO HARD, not OUT. Price does not reopen it **[M2019-015]**, and TOO HARD "doesn’t mean it isn’t a good buy ... It just means that we don’t know how to evaluate it" **[M2000-038]**.

**Verdict, in the draft's words:** TOO HARD **[M2006-013]**. A STOP that returns TOO HARD closes the run.
