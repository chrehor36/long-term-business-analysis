**The analyst's habits**, because the most recent figures in the case (2018, a recovery year) invite the anchor the rows

test's design (the questions are used). No foundation admits or rejects the business.

Three things. **First, OUT against TOO HARD at Q2 is not decidable from the draft's text for a commodity producer whose
cost position is unknown.** The STOP paragraph says "a castle shown to be open is OUT; a castle whose future cannot be

rebuilt moat (its row **[L2007-005]**) sits in a bullet that was corrected to TOO HARD, while "the business whose price a
competitor sets" stays OUT, and a depleting producer is both. I returned OUT on the price-setter and money tests, which the
filing answers on its face, and named the documents that would test the exception; another analyst could defensibly
return TOO HARD, and the file closes at Q2 either way. **Second, the draft has no place for the speakers' own oil purchases.**
The rows say a commodity producer was bought "simply because it was very, very cheap in relation to earnings, in relation
to reserves" **[M2004-082]** and called "a very good business that’s selling at a very cheap price" **[M2005-023]**; under
this draft's Q2 such a business stops before Q7, and the only exception to "price does not reopen it" the draft carries is
newspapers **[L2012-010]**. Either the draft needs an asset-value route for the commodity producer (the oil-field formula
of **[M2002-079]** used at Q7 with the price unforecast), or it should record the PetroChina rows as a tension in section
