# CASE 1 — THE PERMANENT DESIGNATION FOR MARKETABLE SECURITIES WAS WITHDRAWN BY ITS AUTHOR IN 2016

## What the documents say today
`THE FRAMEWORK v4.md`, Q6 (the scope structure, corrected 2026-08-28 from Test D): *"a holding may be explicitly
designated PERMANENT, which exempts it from the overvaluation trigger only"*, resting on **[E2-39]**, the 1987 letter:
*"our insurance companies own three marketable common stocks that we would not sell even though they became far
overpriced [...] we view these investments exactly like our successful controlled businesses [...] we would, if
absolutely necessary, sell portions."* `THE HOLDINGS FRAMEWORK.md` carries it as the designation SECURITY or PERMANENT,
the H4 exemption ("PERMANENT holdings skip H4 **[E2-39]**") and the outcome row "H4 OUT, holding is PERMANENT: HOLD
unless absolutely necessary".

## What the corpus says
1. **The author withdrew it, in the 2016 letter, by name and with the reason:** *"It is true that we own some stocks that I
   have no intention of selling for as far as the eye can see (and we're talking 20/20 vision). But we have made no
   commitment that Berkshire will hold any of its marketable securities forever. [...] That principle covers controlled
   businesses, not marketable securities. This year I've added a final sentence to #11 to ensure that our owners
   understand that we regard any marketable security as available for sale, however unlikely such a sale now seems."*
   **[E5-64]**, 2016 letter.
2. **The Owner's Manual was amended the same year to say so**, in the reprint inside the 2016 report: *"We continue to
   avoid gin rummy behavior. [...] To clean up some confusion voiced in 2016, we emphasize that the comments here refer to
   businesses we control, not to marketable securities."* (v5 ledger, R2016-001; the ninth shelf class.)
3. **The 1987 passage the rule rests on is the one the author says caused the confusion.** [E2-39] remains true as
   history (three stocks were once treated as permanent) and false as a rule from 2016 on. The practice matches: the
   2020 airline exit and the 2024 Apple trim, both already cited at Q6 ([E2-40]'s crystallized-view trigger and
   section VI's open question 1), are sales of marketable securities once thought permanent.

## The reading the text supports
**There is no permanent class for marketable securities after 2016.** The never-sell principle (Owner's Manual #11,
[E2-32]) covers controlled businesses only. A holder who wishes to treat a stock as permanent may, as the author does
("no intention of selling for as far as the eye can see"), but the framework cannot exempt it from the overvaluation
condition on the corpus's authority, because the corpus withdrew that authority in so many words.

## The change, if approved
- `THE FRAMEWORK v4.md`, Q6: the PERMANENT sentence is replaced by: *"The 1987 letter treated three marketable stocks as
  permanent [E2-39]; the 2016 letter withdrew that treatment for all marketable securities and amended the Owner's
  Manual to say the never-sell principle covers controlled businesses only [E5-64]. No marketable holding is exempt from
  [E2-28]'s overvaluation condition on the corpus's authority. A holder who nonetheless designates a stock permanent does
  so as a CONVENTION of his own, confessed in writing, and it still yields to [E2-40]'s crystallized view."*
- `THE HOLDINGS FRAMEWORK.md`: the SECURITY or PERMANENT designation becomes a note of history; the H4 exemption and
  the "H4 OUT, holding is PERMANENT" outcome row are removed, with a dated note saying what stood and why; every holding
  is a SECURITY.
- Verdicts of record touched: **none.** No holding in `PORTFOLIO.md` carries a PERMANENT designation.

# CASE 2 — THE FIVE-YEAR RETENTION TEST AT H2 QUOTES A WORDING ITS AUTHOR CALLED IMPROPER IN 2009

## What the documents say today
`THE HOLDINGS FRAMEWORK.md`, H2, carries the retention test in its 1996 Owner's Manual form, **[E3-54]**: *"We test the
wisdom of retaining earnings by assessing whether retention, over time, delivers shareholders at least $1 of market value
for each $1 retained. [...] We will continue to apply it on a five-year rolling basis."*

## What the corpus says
The 2009 report's Owner's Manual reprint corrects it: *"I should have written the 'five-year rolling basis' sentence
differently, an error I didn't realize until I received a question about this subject at the 2009 annual meeting. When
the stock market has declined sharply over a five-year stretch, our market-price premium to book value has sometimes
shrunk. And when that happens, we fail the test as I improperly formulated it. [...] The five-year test should be: (1)
during the period did our book-value gain exceed the performance of the S&P; and (2) did our stock consistently sell at
a premium to book, meaning that every $1 of retained earnings was always worth more than $1? If these tests are met,
retaining earnings has made sense."* **[E5-65]**, 2009 report. The v5 read found the same passage (R2009-002) and the
2010 AM session recorded that the reprint dropped the old sentence (R1996-012 shows as not reprinted).

## The reading the text supports
The test's intent (a dollar kept must be worth more than a dollar) stands; its formulation as a market-value-to-retained
ratio over any five-year window was called improper by its author because a market decline fails it mechanically. The
corrected form has two legs: the business's own progress against the index over the period, and the market's valuation
of the whole consistently above the capital put in.

## The change, if approved
`THE HOLDINGS FRAMEWORK.md`, H2: the [E3-54] quotation stays as the test's origin, followed by [E5-65] as its corrected
form, and the review applies the two-leg test. Verdicts of record touched: the hold reads of 2026-08-28 (HRB, MITSY,
NCLTY) applied the 1996 form; a dated note in each says the corrected form would have been applied and whether it
changes the reading (none is expected to change: each passed or failed on other grounds). The v5 holdings draft already
carries the corrected form (its Q4 cites R2009-002 and R1995-009).
