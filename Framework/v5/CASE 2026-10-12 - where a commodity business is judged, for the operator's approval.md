# CASE 2026-10-12: where a commodity business is judged, for the operator's approval
*A PRIME RULE 5 case, written 2026-10-12 at the operator's "Go" on the recommendation to write it. Five blind v5 runs of
the Japanese trading houses, businesses of near-identical shape, placed the same question in four different places. The
remaining Japanese names wait behind the operator's sheet until this is decided; the overnight cycle runs the sheet
meanwhile.*

## Why this exists
Each trading house's deciding part is a mining business whose earnings move with a commodity price (coking coal, copper,
iron ore). The runs of 2026-10-12 answered "where is that judged?" four ways:

| run | where the commodity question was placed | box |
|---|---|---|
| MTSUY Mitsubishi | Q1: the ten-year earning power cannot be foreseen because the price cannot | TOO HARD (NATURE) at Q1 |
| MARUY Marubeni | Q2: the low-cost castle is tenuous because its durability is a price forecast | TOO HARD (NATURE) at Q2 |
| SSUMY Sumitomo | Q2: whether the mines are low-cost is knowable from the operators' reports, not read | TOO HARD (WORK) at Q2, pass written |
| ITOCY ITOCHU | Q1 and Q2 IN (lowest-cost iron ore on the operator's filing); the price level is Q7's width | OUT at Q7, on the floor |
| MITSY Mitsui | Q1 and Q2 IN (low-cost Pilbara ore); the price is Q7's width and cycle base | OUT at Q7, on price |

The ITOCY and MTSUY analysts each flagged the clash. The operator's sheet carries steel, oil services, mining and
utilities groups, every one of which meets this question.

## What the v5 rows say
**The price is not forecast, by anyone.** "We don’t think we can predict commodity prices." **[M2016-025]**; "there are very,
very few commodities that I’ve ever thought I was going to — would know the direction of their movement in the next six
months or a year." **[M2011-044]**; "we have no opinion on commodities. We — if we were in an oil stock, it’s because we think
it offers a lot of value at this price, but it does not mean that we think the price of oil is going up." **[M2007-129]**.

**And yet the business is bought, on its figures.** "if you can’t make a decision on something like PetroChina off the figures,
forget about going further, and that’s basically what we did." **[M2008-069]**; PetroChina "was bought simply because it was
very, very cheap in relation to earnings, in relation to reserves, in relation to daily oil production, and relation to
refining capacity" **[M2004-082]**; of Occidental, "I dont know what the price of oil is going to be next year. But I knew that
it was something to act on." **[M2024-054]**; the five trading houses were "understandable companies [...] that we could
understand as a group" **[M2023-031]**.

**What is measured is the cost position, not the price.** "If we owned a copper mining company in its entirety, we would
measure it, probably, more by cost of production than we would by whether copper was selling for $2.00 a pound or a dollar
a pound. [...] We do not want to pay for anything that is not under their control." **[M2006-004]**; "It’s like comparing a
copper producer whose costs are $2.50 a pound with a copper producer whose costs are $1 a pound. Those are two different
kinds of businesses. One is going to go broke at a buck-fifty a pound. And the other one’s going to still be doing fine.
[...] The figures are available." **[M2009-059]**; "commodity businesses have risk unless you’re the low-cost producer"
**[M1997-010]**; "when a company is selling a product with commodity-like economic characteristics, being the low-cost
producer is all-important" **[L2000-017]**.

**Where the price does decide the box.** "when you buy into an oil — a huge oil production company [...] It is a — it is a
investment that depends on the price of oil. [...] If you own oil, you should only own oil, if you expect these prices to go
up significantly." **[M2020-035]**: an investment whose outcome is the price alone is a bet on the price, which the speakers
do not make.

## What the corpus decides, and what it leaves open
**Decides** that a commodity business is not outside the circle for being one: it is understood from its figures
**[M2008-069]**, **[M2023-031]**; that its castle is its cost position, read from the filings **[M2006-004]**, **[M2009-059]**,
**[M1997-010]**; and that the price is not forecast but is a property of the investment's outcome **[M2020-035]**, which is
Q7's business (the range, the cycle base, the width). So MTSUY's Q1 stop confused "the price cannot be forecast" with "the
business cannot be understood": the speakers hold the first and deny the second. MARUY's Q2 stop asked the castle to
guarantee the price, which no castle does. ITOCY and MITSY read the rows as the rows read themselves.

**Leaves open** the case where the cost position cannot be read: a mining interest whose operator files nothing, or a
trading house that discloses no unit cost. SSUMY found that; its analyst called it WORK because the operators' reports exist.

## The change proposed
Add to Q2, after "A business of parts":

> **A commodity business** *(decided from the corpus; the rows are the rule, the placement is ours)*. A dependence
> on a commodity price is never by itself a STOP at Q1 or Q2: the speakers do not forecast the price **[M2016-025]**,
> **[M2011-044]** and buy the business off its figures all the same **[M2008-069]**, **[M2004-082]**. Q1 asks whether the
> business can be understood from its figures **[M2023-031]**; Q2 asks whether it is the low-cost producer, read from its own
> and its operators' filings **[M2006-004]**, **[M2009-059]**, **[M1997-010]**, and a high-cost producer in a commodity field
> is OUT (the list above). The price level enters only at Q7, as the cycle base and the width of the range, and an
> investment whose outcome is the price alone is a bet on the price, which Q7 closes OUT **[M2020-035]**. Where the cost
> position cannot be read because the operators' reports were not read, the file is TOO HARD (WORK); where no filing states
> it, TOO HARD (NATURE).

And, for the research pass, add to Part VII after "Facts for a later question":

> **When a research pass is run** *(CONVENTION, ours)*. A pass is run only where the COMPUTATION beneath the stop shows
> some case of the range earning the floor at the price; where none does, the pass is written and closed unrun with a dated
> note, since no answer to the question could make the name a purchase at that price **[M2003-149]**, and the file is
> re-looked under its alert like any OUT. Rationale: four passes were written in a week (COKE, GFF, SSUMY and earlier WSC)
> for names priced far above every case; a pass costs a run.

## What it would touch
`Framework/THE FRAMEWORK v5.md` Q2 (the paragraph), Part VII (the paragraph), section VI. The runs of 2026-10-12 bind as

## The operator is asked
1. Approve the commodity-business paragraph, or amend it.
2. Approve the research-pass rule, or refuse it.
3. Whether MTSUY and MARUY are re-run under the paragraph.

# DECIDED 2026-10-12
**The operator approved both paragraphs as written ("Go") and chose not to re-run MTSUY and MARUY**: both are priced above every case their runs computed. Each instead carries a re-look note in the register: re-run when its price falls below the top of its computed all-equity range (MTSUY ¥6,310, MARUY ¥4,391), with an alert at that level. Applied the same day to Q2, Part VII and section VI of `Framework/THE FRAMEWORK v5.md`; the five remaining Japanese names returned to the front of the order.
