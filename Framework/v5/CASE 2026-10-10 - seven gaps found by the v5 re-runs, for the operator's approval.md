# CASE 2026-10-10: seven gaps found by the v5 re-runs, for the operator's approval
*A PRIME RULE 5 case, written 2026-10-10 at the operator's instruction ("Write it, keep cycle running"). It collects the
gaps that the blind v5 runs of 2026-10-07 and 2026-10-10 (the re-run of every v4.1 name, batches 1 to 8, and AMETEK)
filled each in its own way. Nothing here changes a run of record; every run binds as written under the text in force
on its date. The overnight cycle keeps running under today's text while the operator decides.*

## Why this exists
The canonical brief tells every analyst: "Use only the conventions the text contains. If you need one it lacks, confess
your own as a CONVENTION of this run". Across 33 runs, the same seven needs were met by confessed conventions again and
again, and in four places the convention chosen decided the box or the alert. A rule written once removes that work
from every remaining run and makes the alerts comparable. Each section gives what the runs found, the rows, what the
corpus decides and leaves open, the change proposed, and what the operator is asked. Every fix proposed is a CONVENTION
(ours) unless a row decides it, and says so. No ledger row is added: every row cited is already in
`principle_ledger_v5.csv`.

## A. What "the midpoint of the range earns the floor" means

### What the runs found
Part VII's reporting convention defines the fair price as "the price at which the midpoint of the range earns the
floor". At least seven runs (ACLS, AVGO, BMI, CHD, KLAC, NDSN, MA, ORLY, JKHY and others) wrote down that the phrase is
not arithmetic and confessed a reading. Three readings occur: the average of the two end cases' cash streams valued at
ten percent (most runs); the cash stream whose value at the long rate is the midpoint, revalued at ten percent (JKHY);
the middle growth case valued at ten percent (ECL). They give different numbers for the same range.

### What the v5 rows say
The range is the speakers' instrument: "Using precise numbers is, in fact, foolish; working with a range of
possibilities is the better approach." **[L2000-024]**; "only when we employ a range of values, rather than some
pseudo-precise figure." **[L1999-027]**. For the price, the speakers name the bottom of the range, not its middle: "we
will buy the stock (or business) if it sells at a reasonable price in relation to the bottom boundary of our estimate."
**[L2013-012]**. The floor is "we don’t want to buy equities where our real expectancy is below 10 percent" **[M2003-149]**.

### What the corpus decides, and what it leaves open
Decides nothing about a midpoint: the fair price is a reporting convention of the operator's (Part VII, adopted
2026-10-06), not a rule of the speakers. Leaves open the arithmetic. Notes that the speakers' own reference point is the
bottom of the range **[L2013-012]**, which the operator may prefer.

### The change proposed
Add to Part VII, The owner's price:

> **How the fair price is computed** *(CONVENTION, ours, a definition of the operator's reporting figure)*. Each end case
> of the range is valued with its own cash stream discounted at ten percent instead of the long government rate; the fair
> price is the average of the two, per share, on the all-equity basis less net debt, with the equity-only figure beside.
> Rationale: it is the reading most runs chose, it needs no third growth case, and it moves with both ends of the range.

Alternative for the operator: **(a-ii)** the bottom end alone valued at ten percent, following **[L2013-012]**, which
gives a lower and more conservative alert price.

### The operator is asked
1. The arithmetic: the average of the ends (proposed), or the bottom end alone (a-ii).
2. Whether the alerts already armed are restated under the rule chosen (a re-computation from each run's own figures,
   recorded in an addendum), or left at their runs' figures.

## B. How far an absurd growth rate is cut back (Q7, specific (f))

### What the runs found
Specific (f) says a result "that traces to an absurdity is cut back" and gives no size. In BMI (18.9% shown), CMG (18%)
and EPAC (24%) the shown owner-cash growth, carried ten years, would have required cash earnings above any level the
business has produced per dollar of sales. Each analyst cut it to the business's own sales growth over the window or the
cycle, and each said the cut decided OUT against TOO HARD.

### What the v5 rows say
"if you trace out the mathematics of it, you bump into absurdities, then you better change expectations somewhat."
**[M1999-067]**; "what the farm produces is all you’re going to get from the farm." **[M1999-067]**; "15 percent per
annum compounded will do is grow way faster than the economy can grow, way faster than aggregate profits can grow, over a
long pull." **[M1997-096]**; "when the compound rate becomes higher than the discount rate, you get into infinite
numbers" **[M1997-095]**.

### What the corpus decides, and what it leaves open
Decides that the absurd rate is cut and that the cut follows what the business can produce. Leaves open the figure.

### The change proposed
Add to specific (f):

> Where the shown growth of owner cash, carried ten years, would require owner cash above the highest share of sales the
> business has earned in the filings read, the growth carried is the business's own sales growth over the same window
> (or between the same cycles), never above the discount rate; the shown rate and the cut are both written down
> *(CONVENTION, ours: the farm produces what it produces **[M1999-067]**; sales are what the farm produces)*.

### The operator is asked
Approve, amend (another measure, such as the growth of operating income), or refuse.

## C. A range wider than three to one whose top case still misses the floor

### What the runs found
Q7's range convention closes TOO HARD when the top of the range is more than about three times its bottom, and OUT when
the price is inside or just below a narrower range. MA (4.07 to one), CB (5.1 to one) and, uncut, BMI and CMG had wide
ranges where even the top case, at the price, returned less than the floor. The analysts closed OUT, each saying the
text does not say which test runs first.

### What the v5 rows say
On the floor: "there’s just a point at which we drop out of the game." **[M2003-149]**. On a wide range: "Usually, the
range must be so wide that no useful conclusion can be reached." **[L2000-025]**, and a wide range is not cured by a
bigger discount: "we don’t really try to compensate for that sort of thing by having some extra large margin of safety.
We really want to try to go on to something that we understand better." **[M2007-022]**.

### What the corpus decides, and what it leaves open
Decides that both end in not buying. Leaves open the label, which matters for the record and for the alert: OUT carries
a fair-price alert, TOO HARD does not.

### The change proposed
Add to the range convention:

> **The floor is tested first** *(CONVENTION, ours)*. Where no case in the range earns the floor at the price, the file
> closes OUT whatever the width, since the expected return at the price is below the minimum on every case the analyst
> could defend **[M2003-149]**. The width test closes TOO HARD only where some case in the range would clear the floor at
> the price, which is where "no useful conclusion can be reached" **[L2000-025]** matters to the decision.

### The operator is asked
Approve or refuse.

## D. A signed deal that has not closed

### What the runs found
ACLS (an all-stock merger awaiting China's regulator), CTAS (UniFirst, signed and approved, under FTC review) and EPAC
(SFE, signed, financing committed) each kept the deal out of the central figure and showed it beside, confessing the
choice. Q7's specific (c) covers deals inside the window and deals closed after it; it is silent on a signed, unclosed
deal.

### What the v5 rows say
"how sure are you that there are two in the bush, you know? How far away is the bush?" **[M2014-068]**: the rows value
what is sure, and an unclosed deal is less sure than a closed one. No row addresses deal timing directly.

### The change proposed
Add to specific (c):

> A deal signed but not closed by the price date is left out of the central figure and valued beside it as if closed, on
> the target's own filed record with the financing added to net debt; the box is decided on the central figure, and the
> run states what the deal would do to it *(CONVENTION, ours; the three runs that met it did this)*.

### The operator is asked
Approve or refuse.

## E. The serial acquirer whose central value is nil or negative

### What the runs found
ECL, EFX, AME and ESI produced a central range at or below zero, because specific (c)'s default pair deducts the
acquisition spending in every year, including the years after ten when no growth is credited. AME and NDSN named the
cause; each alert was then set on the organic pair instead, with a label saying so.

### What the v5 rows say
"they don’t get credit for gross cash flow, they get credit for whatever net cash is left every year." **[M1998-080]**
supports deducting the purchases. "growth can destroy value if it requires cash inputs in the early years of a project
or enterprise that exceed the discounted value of the cash that those assets can generate in later years" **[L2000-023]**
supports charging growth for what it costs. Neither says to charge purchases for growth that is not credited.

### What the corpus decides, and what it leaves open
Decides that purchases are a cost and growth must be paid for. Leaves open what happens after year ten, which is our
construction (the ten-year term is a confessed convention of Q7).

### The change proposed
Add to specific (c):

> After year ten, when growth stops, the acquisition spending stops with it: the business is valued at no growth on
> owner cash before purchases *(CONVENTION, ours: a business that buys no growth spends nothing to buy it; charging the
> spending forever while crediting growth for ten years values a growth the analyst has already refused)*. Where the
> central fair price is still not positive, the alert is set on the organic pair and labelled so.

### The operator is asked
Approve, or refuse (in which case the organic-pair alert practice is written into Part VII as it stands).

## F. Fast-changing technology at Q1: OUT or TOO HARD

### What the runs found
GRMN, KEYS and LRCX each noted that Q1's list of what it rules OUT includes "Businesses that live on continued
invention" (on **[M1999-075]**), while the routing sends a business whose ten-year economics cannot be foreseen because its
industry changes fast to TOO HARD (NATURE) (on **[M1998-008]**, **[M2001-069]**). Seven runs closed TOO HARD (NATURE) at Q1
on the routing; none used the OUT item.

### What the v5 rows say
"if they had never invented any more pharmaceuticals, it would be a terrible business." **[M1999-075]**; "if something comes
in where there’s a technological component that’s of significance [...] it won’t make it through the filter."
**[M1998-008]**; "there are many businesses — industries where it’s very hard to evaluate moats. There — those are the
businesses of rapid change." **[M2001-069]**; the insiders "would not want to put down on paper their predictions [...]
They would say, “That’s too hard.”" **[M2000-105]**.

### What the corpus decides, and what it leaves open
Decides that the reason is the inability to judge, which is the TOO HARD box **[M2001-069]**, **[M2000-105]**. The
pharmaceutical row says what such a business would be without invention; it is not a finding that the castle is open.

### The change proposed
Move the item "Businesses that live on continued invention" from Q1's "What it rules OUT" to the TOO HARD paragraph
beneath it, with a dated note keeping the old placement as the record.

### The operator is asked
Approve or refuse.

## G. A change for the better that only the next downturn can test

### What the runs found
Section C (adopted 2026-10-06) credits a change for the better "only on the evidence of a full cycle in which the price
behaviour held" and says "until then the file is TOO HARD (WORK) on that question". COKE (operating margin from about 6%
to about 13% since 2020) and GFF (Clopay's margin from about 15% to about 31% since 2022) closed TOO HARD (WORK) on it.
The GFF analyst found the defect: the work cannot answer the question, because the evidence the rule asks for is a
downturn that has not happened, so the research pass would end TOO HARD (NATURE) after spending its cost.

### What the v5 rows say
"We couldn't solve this problem, moreover, even if we were to spend years intensely studying those industries."
**[L1993-023]**; "if we can’t make a decision in five minutes, we can’t make it in five months." **[M2008-086]**; "you can
almost measure the strength of a business over time by the agony they go through in determining whether a price
increase can be sustained." **[M2005-020]**; "though the turn has not yet occurred, we believe you should view last year's
figures as reflecting a cyclical problem, not a secular one." **[L1995-022]**.

### What the corpus decides, and what it leaves open
Decides that study cannot cure a question only time can answer **[L1993-023]**, which is the NATURE cause. Leaves open
how the file is reopened when time answers.

### The change proposed
Replace "until then the file is TOO HARD (WORK) on that question, not IN" in section C's full-cycle sentence with:

> until then the file is TOO HARD (NATURE) on that question, with a dated re-look condition written in the box line: the
> first fiscal year in which the business's volume or its field's volume falls, read from the filings, after which the
> question is put again *(the cause is NATURE because only the downturn can answer it **[L1993-023]**; the re-look is
> ours)*.

The two open research passes (COKE, GFF) are then closed without being run, by a dated note in each run's register entry.

### The operator is asked
Approve or refuse; and whether the COKE and GFF passes are closed.
