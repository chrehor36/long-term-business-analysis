# SECTOR METHOD — owner earnings for insurers and float-bearing holding companies
**Written 2026-09-02, to unblock the MINI BERK track.** Evidence rows added first
(E5-46 … E5-50); this document is written against them, per PRIME RULE 6.

---

## THE QUESTION THIS PROJECT HAD NEVER FACED

Nine names sit in the operator's queue that the framework cannot currently run:
**L, WTM, MKL, BRK-B, BRK-A, HHH, GHC, BAM, BN.** The obstruction was stated in the run
queue on 2026-09-01 and it is real:

> *how is owner earnings computed for an insurer? Float is not operating cash flow,
> underwriting float arrives before the loss is paid, and (c) has no obvious meaning for a
> business whose principal asset is a securities portfolio.*

Three concrete failures follow, and each one has already bitten this project on a
non-insurer:

1. **Float inflates operating cash flow.** Premiums arrive before losses are paid, so
   growing an insurance book *looks* like cash generation. The UNH run of 2026-08-31
   already met this in a milder form and **stripped float growth from OCF** on the ground
   that claims-payable growth is borrowed money which reverses as membership contracts —
   *which it then did*.
2. **(c) has no referent.** A securities portfolio does not wear out. There is no
   maintenance capex that keeps unit volume and competitive position intact, because there
   are no units and no plant.
3. **The cash test inverts.** **[E2-61]** is explicit: *"Insurance is different: you can
   be broke but flush… insolvent insurers don't run out of cash until long after they have
   run out of net worth. In fact, these 'walking dead' often redouble their efforts to
   write business, accepting almost any price or risk, simply to keep the cash flowing
   in."* Cash inflow is the **symptom** of the failing insurer, not evidence of the
   healthy one.

**So the answer is not a better owner-earnings formula. The corpus does not compute one.**

## THE CORPUS'S OWN METHOD — two measurable components and one judgment

**[E5-37]**, 2013: *"different numbers are of different importance — or various numbers are
of different importance — depending on the kind of business."* The metric set is selected
by business type **first**. What follows is not an exception carved out for a favoured
name; it is the general rule applied.

### Component 1 — the investments, at market **[E5-46]**

> *"The first component of value is our investments: stocks, bonds and cash equivalents.
> At yearend these totaled $158 billion at market value. Insurance float … money we
> temporarily hold in our insurance operations that does not belong to us … funds $66
> billion of our investments. This float is "free" as long as insurance underwriting
> breaks even, meaning that the premiums we receive equal the losses and expenses we incur.
> Of course, underwriting results are volatile, swinging erratically between profits and
> losses. … If we do that, all of our investments … those funded both by float and by
> retained earnings … can be viewed as an element of value for Berkshire shareholders."*

**Float enters as the FUNDING of an asset, never as an earnings stream.** That single
move dissolves the whole difficulty. You do not capitalise float; you value what it bought,
and you charge for what it cost.

**Its cost is measurable and the corpus names the measure. [E3-69]**, 1990:

> *"what should the measure of an insurer's profitability be? Analysts and managers
> customarily look to the combined ratio … We believe a better measure, however, to be a
> comparison of underwriting loss to float developed. This loss/float ratio, like any
> statistic used in evaluating insurance results, is meaningless over short time periods:
> Quarterly underwriting figures and even annual ones are too heavily based on estimates to
> be much good. But when the ratio takes in a period of years, it gives a rough indication
> of the cost of funds generated by insurance operations. A low cost of funds signifies a
> good business; a high cost translates into a poor business."*

And **[E3-52]** supplies the leverage judgment: float and deferred taxes are *"liabilities
without covenants or due dates attached to them. In effect, they give us the benefit of
debt … but saddle us with none of its drawbacks."*

### Component 2 — earnings from everything that is not the portfolio **[E5-47]**

> *"Berkshire's second component of value is earnings that come from sources other than
> investments and insurance underwriting."*

Amended by the filer in 2015 to **include** underwriting income, with the reason published
**[E5-48]**. This is the two-business method the HOG run reached independently for a
manufacturer with a captive finance arm — **it is the corpus's own method, stated decades
earlier.**

### The rule that makes the two ADDABLE — and the trap it prevents **[E5-48]**

> *"We exclude in the second factor the dividends and interest from the investments we hold
> because including them would produce a double-counting of value. In arriving at our
> earnings figure, we deduct all corporate overhead, interest, depreciation, amortization
> and minority interests. Income taxes, though, are not deducted. That is, the earnings are
> pre-tax."*

**This is the single most important line in the method, and it is exactly the error an
unguarded run would make.** Investment income sits inside an insurer's reported operating
cash flow. Any run that computes an owner-earnings yield from OCF **and** then notes the
size of the portfolio has counted the portfolio twice. There is no windage that fixes that;
it is an arithmetic error.

Note also what the passage records: **the filer changing its own valuation method in
public, and saying so.** That is **[E2-67]**'s published-self-restatement standard applied
to a method rather than to reserves, and it is the candor benchmark against which the other
eight names in the Mini Berk queue get read.

### The third element — a judgment, and it can be negative **[E5-50]**

> *"There is a third, more subjective, element to an intrinsic value calculation that can
> be either positive or negative: the efficacy with which retained earnings will be deployed
> in the future. … Some companies will turn these retained dollars into fifty-cent pieces,
> others into two-dollar bills. This "what-will-they-do-with-the-money" factor must always
> be evaluated along with the "what-do-we-have-now" calculation … If a CEO can be expected
> to do this job well, the reinvestment prospects add to the company's current value; if the
> CEO's talents or motives are suspect, today's value must be discounted."*

**This is what stops the method from being a book-value exercise.** A pile of securities
plus operating earnings is worth its sum only if the retained dollars become two-dollar
bills. **[E3-54]** is the same idea as a measurement — $1 of market value per $1 retained,
five-year rolling — and it is available for every one of the nine names.

## THE METHOD, AS A RUN WOULD EXECUTE IT

**Nothing here overrides the six questions or their order.** Q1–Q4 are asked exactly as
written; this changes only what Q5 computes, and adds two Q4 substitutions the corpus
itself requires.

### Stage 0 — the artifact check, with two insurance-specific additions
The standing check that applies **[E4-46]**'s five-minute rule from the other side (cap
across **all** share classes, dividend regular vs special, boom-window test) **plus**:

- **(a) Share class by hand off the cover.** From this project's own record: BRK-A/BRK-B is
  the exact structure that produced a **$473M cap and a 4,683% yield** in the screen, and
  the LEVI / NKE / DKS / PINS mechanism has now been confirmed four separate times. There is
  no version of this run that starts from a cached share count.
- **(b) Is the company an insurer, a float-bearing holding company, or neither?** HHH and
  GHC are in this queue and may be neither. Say which, and take the ordinary method if so.

### Q5, in five steps

| step | what | authority |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | Investments — stocks, bonds, cash equivalents — **at market**, net of minority interests, excluding any held in a finance operation offset by borrowings | **[E5-46]** |
| **2** | The **cost of float**: multi-year underwriting result against float developed. **DIAGNOSTIC, NOT ADDITIVE — see the correction below** | **[E3-69]** |
| **3** | Pre-tax earnings of everything else **including underwriting income**, with dividends and interest from step 1 removed, after overhead, interest, D&A and minorities, **before tax** | **[E5-48]** |
| **4** | **The floor first, then the ranking** — a name whose honest pre-tax expectancy sits below ~10% is quit on, not ranked **[E4-28]**; what clears the floor ranks against the sovereign for the **earnings currency**, from the issuing authority, dated. *(Corrected 2026-09-02, found by the BRK run: this row carried "there is no hurdle; it ranks" — the pre-Test-D wording THE FRAMEWORK v4.md explicitly REWROTE on 2026-08-28 — and Berkshire is the exact case where the two disagree: above the bond on every construction, below the floor.)* | **[E4-28]**, framework Q5 |
| **5** | Apply the third element as a **stated** upward or downward judgment on the retention record, never silently | **[E5-50]**, **[E3-54]** |

**Step 2 is where the framework's own conservatism lives, and it must be spent once.**
**[E5-49]**: *"Over the past decade, annual underwriting income has averaged $1,434 per
share… You should recognize, however, that underwriting in any given year could well be
unprofitable, perhaps substantially so."* Together with **[E3-69]**'s *"meaningless over
short time periods"*, these two forbid a one-year insurer figure **in either direction** —
including a flattering one.

### Q4 — three substitutions the corpus requires

1. **Liquidity is read as reserve adequacy and net worth, never cash on hand [E2-61].**
   Cash inflow is the symptom of the walking dead. The **[E5-11]** strength-(2) test that
   four non-insurers have already failed on the kindness of strangers **[E5-39]** must be
   re-expressed here or it will return a false pass.
2. **Reserve development is the candor test [E2-67]**: *"judge whether we may have some
   systemic bias that should make you wary of our current and future figures."* Every US
   P&C filer publishes a ten-year loss-development triangle in Schedule P. **Read it, state
   the direction of the error, and say whether the filer names it.**
3. **Concentration is licensed by loss-absorption [E2-62]**: *"This concentration makes
   sense only because our insurance business is conducted from a position of exceptional
   financial strength. For almost all other insurers, a comparable degree of concentration
   (or anything close to it) would be totally inappropriate."* **This is an explicit warning
   against reading the eight smaller names as small Berkshires.** The method transfers; the
   license to concentrate does not.

## AMENDMENT 2026-09-02 — WHAT THE FIRST CONTACT WITH A FILER FOUND

**The WTM run was killed by a session limit before it reached a verdict, but it got far
enough to break three of this document's premises, and those survive on disk.** They are
folded in here rather than left in a run file, because a method whose gaps are recorded
only in the run that found them is a method that will repeat them. From this project's own
record.

### FINDING 1 & 2 — the word "float" is not in the filing, and the figure is not published
*(from this project's own record: the WTM run of 2026-09-02.)*

White Mountains' FY2025 10-K contains **"float" fifteen times, and all fifteen refer to
floating-rate debt** — *"Ark 2021 Subordinated Notes … floating rate"*, *"HG Global Senior
Notes … floating rate"*. **WTM never uses the word in [E5-46]'s sense and publishes no float
figure at all.**

**This document assumed a number that most filers do not report.** Berkshire publishes float
because Berkshire chose to; nothing requires it. So the method needs a construction recipe,
and the WTM run built one from the segment columns of the filed balance sheet:

> float = loss and LAE reserves + unearned premiums − reinsurance recoverables
>         − premiums receivable − deferred acquisition costs

**CONVENTION 4, confessed per PRIME RULE 3.** *Rationale: it is the liability the
policyholder has funded, less the assets already standing against it — which is what
[E5-46]'s "money we temporarily hold … that does not belong to us" describes. The
components are all separately disclosed, so another analyst can reproduce it in under two
minutes, which is the standard. It is not the only defensible construction and the run must
show its arithmetic rather than cite this line.*

### FINDING 3 — the method needs a materiality test, and this document had none
*(from this project's own record: the WTM run of 2026-09-02.)*

WTM's constructed float is **$1,831.1M against $8,323.9M of investments — 22.0%.**
Berkshire in [E5-46] at the same test is **$66bn against $158bn — 41.8%.** WTM's float ratio
is roughly **half** Berkshire's, and float is 34% of shareholders' equity.

From this project's own record, **the run's verdict on its own Stage 0(b) is the finding: *"WTM is a holding company that
owns a float-bearing business, not a float-bearing company."*** My brief had called it *"the
smallest and cleanest float-bearing name"* and the run recorded that as **half wrong** — it
is the smallest, and it is not clean.

**What this does to the method, in the run's words** — again this project's own record —**:** steps 1 and 3 carry essentially the
whole valuation, while step 2 prices a $1.8bn funding advantage inside a $5.0bn market cap —
**real, but a rounding item beside the portfolio, not the engine.** The method stays
runnable; **its centre of gravity moves from step 2 to step 3, and this document did not say
what to do when it does.** It does now:

> **Stage 0(b) must compute float ÷ investments and state it.** Where that ratio is well
> below Berkshire's, say so, and say plainly that step 2 is a minor term. **A low ratio is
> not a disqualification — it is a relocation of where the answer lives**, and a method that
> keeps insisting on the cost of float after measuring it at 22% is measuring the wrong
> thing.

### FINDING 8 — a gap in the FRAMEWORK, not just in this method, and it is left OPEN
*(from this project's own record: the WTM run of 2026-09-02.)*

Ark writes at Lloyd's and in Bermuda, so a material part of WTM's earnings is **GBP- and
EUR-denominated before translation**. The framework requires the sovereign **for the currency
the business earns in [E4-15, E3-32]**.

**It has no stated answer for one company earning in three.**

**This is recorded as an open question and NOT resolved here.** The count of deletions below
is this project's own record. Inventing a blending rule
would be exactly the kind of unsourced mechanism the v4 rebuild deleted forty-three of, and
PRIME RULE 5 requires a written case with quotes before a structural change. What the run
did instead — and what any run facing this should do until the question is settled — is
**state the exposure, use the reporting currency's sovereign, and disclose that the choice is
unresolved.** Naming a gap is a result; papering over it is not.

### And one thing that worked

From this project's own record: **the hand-read share count mattered again, at a smaller
scale than before.** The 10-K
reports 2,479,677 shares at 2025-12-31; the 10-Q cover of 2026-08-03 reports **2,386,721** —
down **3.7% in seven months** on buybacks. A cached count would have overstated the cap by
**$196M**. Not the LEVI or PINS order of magnitude, and still worth the two minutes.

## SECOND AMENDMENT 2026-09-02 — THE MKL RUN, WHICH FOUND AN ARITHMETIC DEFECT

From this project's own record: the Markel run adjudicated the method against a filer whose
segments were supposed to be the method pre-computed, and returned **nine findings**. Four
change the document.

### THE DEFECT — steps 2 and 3 double-counted underwriting profit
*(from this project's own record: the MKL run of 2026-09-02.)*

Trace it, because it was in the first draft and nobody would have caught it from the corpus
alone:

- **[E5-47]**, 2010, defines component 2 as *"earnings that come from sources other than
  investments and insurance underwriting"* — underwriting **excluded**.
- **[E5-48]**, 2015, records the filer amending exactly that: *"we are for the first time
  including insurance underwriting income in business earnings."*
- From this project's own record: step 3 said "pre-tax earnings of everything else," which
  **under the amendment includes underwriting**. Step 2 then priced the cost of float, **which is the underwriting result.**
  Anyone adding both counted it twice.

**THE RULING: step 2 is DIAGNOSTIC, not additive.** [E3-69] never adds the cost of float to
anything; its own conclusion is a judgment rather than a term — *"A low cost of funds
signifies a good business; a high cost translates into a poor business."* Compute it, report
it, let it inform the judgment. **Underwriting income enters once, in step 3.**

### STAGE 0(b) TESTED THE WRONG RATIO
*(from this project's own record: the MKL run of 2026-09-02.)*

From this project's own record: the first amendment made Stage 0(b) compute **float ÷
investments** and guard the low side,
because WTM came in at 22.0% against Berkshire's 41.8%. **Markel is 50.3% — above Berkshire —
and the low-side guard says nothing at all there.**

The MKL run's argument, recorded as **CONVENTION 5** because it is a refinement of the
corpus's reasoning rather than a quote: **the ratio that governs whether counting investments
gross at market is safe is investments ÷ EQUITY — Berkshire 0.45x against Markel 2.01x.**
*Rationale: [E5-46] licenses counting the whole portfolio as an element of value on an
explicit condition — that underwriting breaks even, so the float funding it is free. Where
investments are a small multiple of equity that condition carries little weight; where they
are twice equity, most of the portfolio is funded by liabilities and the valuation leans
almost entirely on a condition the same passage calls "volatile, swinging erratically."* The
ratio measures how much the answer depends on that condition. **Compute both ratios and state
what they imply. Neither is a threshold and neither disqualifies a name.**

### TWO UNSPECIFIED ITEMS IN STEP 1, ONE OF THEM LARGE

- From this project's own record: **gross versus net was never stated, and at Markel the
  ambiguity is worth $23.1bn against a $22.6bn market cap** — larger than the company. **Say which you use and why.**
- **Step 1 had no deferred-tax rule, though [E3-71] supplies one.** Munger valued the
  deferred-tax liability as the **advantage of an interest-free loan**, about $25 per Wesco
  share — *never at face and never at zero*. Use that treatment and show it.

### AND CONVENTION 4 SURVIVED ITS FIRST TEST

From this project's own record, the float construction recipe invented by the WTM run
**reproduces Markel's first-ever published float figure to within 1.1%.** It stays.

**Note what that test also exposed: Markel published no float figure at all in its FY2020–
FY2024 10-Ks.** The word first appears in FY2025. So **[E3-69]'s "period of years" is being
read off a table three months old**, and the run said so rather than treating five rows as
five years of evidence.

### Q2 — the warning that applies before any of this

**[E2-70]**, 1977: *"Insurance companies offer standardized policies which can be copied by
anyone. Their only products are promises. It is not difficult to be licensed, and rates are
an open book. There are no important advantages from trademarks, patents, location,
corporate longevity, raw material sources, etc.… there is no question that the nature of
the insurance business magnifies the effect which individual managers have on company
performance."*

**On the corpus's own account, an insurance underwriting operation is close to a commodity,
and the [E3-03] franchise question is therefore harder here than in retail, not easier.**
Ten consecutive watchlist names have closed at Q2. Nothing in this method makes Q2 easier,
and a run that reaches step 1 above without closing Q2 first has violated the hard sequence.

## WHAT IS CONVENTION HERE, AND WHY — confessed, per PRIME RULE 3

**CONVENTION 1 — "investments at market" is taken as the balance-sheet fair value the filer
reports.** The corpus states the component; it does not prescribe a source. *Rationale: the
filed statement is the primary document (operator rule 4), and using anything else would
substitute an estimate for a disclosure.* **Its limit is disclosed by the corpus itself
[E5-32]**: the filed statement is not bedrock — Salomon carried a daily invented plug for
twelve years, signed by its auditor.

**CONVENTION 2 — the cost of float is measured over the longest window the filer publishes,
and the window is stated.** *Rationale: [E3-69] requires "a period of years" and does not
name a number; naming one would be an invention. Stating the window lets the next reader
reproduce it in under two minutes, which is the standard.*

**CONVENTION 3 — where a company is part insurer and part operating business, the two are
valued separately and the reason is recorded.** *Rationale: this is [E5-47] plus the HOG
precedent, where a blended yield across a manufacturer and a finance book was shown to be
meaningless (consolidated OCF +$568.9M in FY2025 and −$59.7M in H1 2026).*

**No numeric threshold is introduced by this document.** There is no minimum float, no
maximum leverage, no combined-ratio line. **The 10:1 leverage ceiling was deleted in v4 for
exactly this reason** — the corpus says *twenty* to one and calls it *"a common ratio in
this industry"*, and its conclusion is about management quality rather than a ratio.

## WHAT THIS METHOD CANNOT DO — stated before it is used

- **It does not make an insurer's earnings predictable.** It makes the *components*
  measurable and leaves the judgment where the corpus leaves it.
- **It does not transfer the license to concentrate [E2-62].** Eight of the nine names lack
  Berkshire's loss-absorption, and the method must not be allowed to smuggle in its
  conclusions.
- **It cannot audit reserves.** Schedule P shows the filer's own history of error; it
  cannot show the error not yet made. Where the loss triangle is unobtainable or the filer
  is foreign, the honest verdict is **UNRESEARCHED** if a document would resolve it and
  **UNKNOWABLE** if none exists.
- **It does not resolve BAM/BN.** Those are asset managers with fee streams and
  consolidated funds, which is a *different* perimeter problem — closer to the DKS/HON
  class than to the insurer class. **They are removed from this method's scope and remain
  blocked**, and saying so is better than stretching a method to cover them.

## THE RUN ORDER THIS UNBLOCKS

From this project's own record, the Mini Berk queue holds nine names and this method
reaches seven of them: L (Loews), WTM (White Mountains), MKL (Markel), BRK-B, BRK-A, plus
HHH and GHC once Stage 0(b) establishes whether they are float-bearing at all. **BAM and BN
stay blocked** for the reason above.

**Read order, easiest method-test first:** **WTM → MKL → L → BRK-B**. White Mountains is the
smallest and cleanest float-bearing name; Markel is the closest structural analogue to the
two-component method and the best place to find out whether it survives contact with a filer
that is not Berkshire; Loews carries a non-insurance operating column that exercises
[E5-47] directly. **BRK-B is run last on purpose** — it is the name the method was derived
from, and testing a method first on the case that generated it is the error **[E4-26]** was
written to prevent.
