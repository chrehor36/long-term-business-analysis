## What it does

Black Hills delivers natural gas in six states and electricity in several western states, including Colorado and Wyoming. Gas utilities produced 53.2% of five-year operating profit and electric utilities 46.8%. Both operate under exclusive franchises with prices set by state regulators.

## Why it passed

- Its franchises are exclusive, so no rival can build a second set of gas mains or power lines in its territories.
- Its return on shareholders' equity averaged about 8.9% over ten years, above the 7.6% to 8.5% of three comparable utilities.
- Electric demand is rising: peak load in Wyoming was up 21% in 2025 on data-center customers, under a tariff that protects other customers.
- Gas customers grew about 1.0% a year.

## Why we are not buying at today's price

A regulator caps what this utility may earn, so the price is far above what that capped return justifies. The analysis values the shares at $52.96 to $63.44 against a price of $76.63, and a buyer at that price would earn about 4.1% to 5.7% a year on the cash the business can pay out.

## What to watch

- A signed merger with NorthWestern Energy would lower the analysis's value range to $37.34 to $54.99.
- Capital added from 2020 to 2025 earned only about 5% before tax, and the number of shares rose from 63.3M to 73.2M, so earnings per share grew just 1.6% a year.
- Debt is 55.1% of total capital, and a $1.8 billion gas generation project for a data-center customer is planned for 2027 to 2029.
