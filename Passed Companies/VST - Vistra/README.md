## What it does

Vistra sells electricity into wholesale power markets from 43,641 MW of plants (gas 62%, coal 20%, nuclear 15%, solar and batteries 3%). It also sells electricity to households and businesses, notably in Texas, where customers can switch suppliers. Its retail arm buys power at market prices.

## Why it passed

- Its retail business, the part that decides the verdict, has sold electricity in Texas's competitive market for over 20 years and keeps its customers through service and integrated supply.
- Owning its own generation hedges the retail book against power price swings.
- Retail customer counts rose by about 3%, 3% and 1% a year in 2020 to 2022 by meters.
- A federal nuclear production tax credit runs from 2024 to 2032, which supports the cash from its nuclear plants.

## Why we are not buying at today's price

At $141.73 a share the cash the business produces is only about 2.2% of the price, compared with 5.31% on a 30-year US Treasury bond. The analysis counts only the case with no growth, worth $26.02 a share, so the price is more than five times that value.

## What to watch

- Total debt is about $20.7 billion, and S&P rates it BBB-, the lowest investment-grade rating; $1.5 billion of junior notes were added in September 2026.
- In Winter Storm Uri in 2021, one weather event produced a large loss across its Texas generation, and collateral demands can drain cash quickly.
- It has bought back shares with borrowed money at prices far above the value range.
