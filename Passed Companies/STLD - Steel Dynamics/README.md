## What it does

Steel Dynamics makes steel in its mills, sells scrap metal and joists, and has built a large new mill and a recycled-aluminum rolling mill. Its profit depends on the gap between what steel sells for and what scrap and other inputs cost.

## Why it passed

- It is a low-cost producer, on a par with Nucor, which gives it a lasting edge over higher-cost rivals.
- Its pre-tax return on tangible capital averaged 28.7% from 2016 to 2025, ahead of Commercial Metals (16.4%) and Cleveland-Cliffs (10.2% over 2021-25).
- The accounts raised no suspicion, and no doubt about management's honesty was found.
- Management's ability was weighed in its favor.

## Why we are not buying at today's price

At $240.63 a share the price sits inside a narrow value range ($228.24 to $254.89), so it offers no margin below value. A buyer would earn only 3.4% to 5.9% a year in cash, below the roughly 10% the analysis requires, and the low end is below a 30-year US Treasury bond (5.6%).

## What to watch

- Its return on tangible capital fell to 13.1% in 2025 from 55-56% in 2021-22, so earnings swing with the steel price.
- It built two large mills (steel and aluminum) whose returns are not yet proven.
- It has net debt of about $3.6 billion, and its bonus pool pays out above a low return hurdle.
