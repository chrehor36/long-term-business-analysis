## What it does

Costco runs membership warehouse stores in which about 83 million paid members buy groceries and general goods at very low markups. It had 939 warehouses in late 2026 and plans up to 35 more. Members pay an annual fee, now $65 for the basic card, and Costco passes its cost advantage on in prices.

## Why it passed

- Its economics move slowly: paid members rose from 65.8 million (FY2022) to 81.0 million (FY2025), and about 90% renew.
- It earns 30% to 43% before tax on its tangible equity every year for ten years, with no net debt, because members and suppliers effectively fund its stock.
- Its gross margin stayed between 10.5% and 11.4% of sales for ten years, and its costs are below its rival clubs on their own filings.
- It raised its membership fee to $65 in 2024 with renewals held, and its share count has been flat at 438 to 443 million for ten years.

## Why we are not buying at today's price

At $935.68 a share against a value range of $203 to $453, the price is about twice the top of the range. A buyer would earn only about 1% to 3% a year on the cash the business produces, less than a 30-year US Treasury bond (5.66%).

## What to watch

- An open False Claims Act investigation, begun in January 2023, which the analysis recorded but found to raise no doubt about the filings.
- The renewal rate has slipped slightly (89.8% worldwide in FY2025), and online sales, now 7% of the total, are growing about 20% a year.
- It has bought back shares at prices well above what the analysis thinks they are worth.
