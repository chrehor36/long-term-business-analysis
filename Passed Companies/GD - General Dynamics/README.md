## What it does

General Dynamics has four parts: Marine Systems (32% of sales), which builds Navy nuclear submarines and destroyers; Aerospace (25%), which makes Gulfstream business jets; Combat Systems (17%), the sole maker of the Abrams tank and Stryker; and Technologies (26%), which does defense IT and communications.

## Why it passed

- It earned about 32% before tax on the net operating capital it uses, helped by $9.8 billion of customer advances.
- Electric Boat is the prime contractor and lead shipyard on all Navy nuclear-powered submarine programs, and Land Systems is the sole-source maker of the Abrams tank.
- Gulfstream deliveries rose from 136 in 2024 to 158 in 2025, with orders running 1.2 times deliveries.
- The Navy's long program of record, such as the 12-boat Columbia-class, gives it years of visible work.

## Why we are not buying at today's price

The analysis values the shares at $221 to $296 against a price of $331.72, above the top of the range. Even on the generous reading a buyer would earn about 9% a year before tax, below the roughly 10% the analysis requires.

## What to watch

- Its edge protects its place as a supplier rather than its price: segment margins sit inside its rivals' range, and the Marine margin is only 6.5% to 8.3%.
- Return falls to 9.4% when the $21.0 billion of goodwill from past purchases, mostly Technologies, is counted.
- Government revenue is 51% fixed-price, so cost overruns fall on the company.
