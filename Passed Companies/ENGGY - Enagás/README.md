## What it does

Enagás owns most of Spain's high-pressure gas transmission network and its LNG import terminals, and operates the system. It is paid by tariffs that the Spanish regulator sets. It also has stakes in investees abroad and a plan to spend on hydrogen.

## Why it passed

- It is a statutory monopoly: nobody can build a competing national gas grid.
- Gas transported rose 7.4% in 2025 to 372 TWh, and 4.2% more in the first quarter of 2026.
- Its return on the capital used was steady, about 8.0% in 2021 and 7.9% in 2025 before tax.
- Management passed the analysis's honesty checks, and the debt is mostly fixed-rate at an average cost of about 2.1%.

## Why we are not buying at today's price

A regulator caps what Enagás may earn, so the return stays modest. At EUR 16.81 a share the expected return is 8.0% a year if nothing grows and 5.5% if the business shrinks, short of the roughly 10% the analysis asks for. The analysis's own fair price is EUR 8.45, about half the current price.

## What to watch

- The regulator cut its pay in the 2021 to 2026 framework (indexation removed), and the 2027 to 2032 rules are not yet known.
- Net debt is EUR 2,475 million, 3.6 times adjusted profit, and the Teréga purchase would add EUR 573 million.
- Peru legal proceedings carry a possible liability of up to USD 242 million, and a quarter of the long-term plan is for hydrogen spending.
