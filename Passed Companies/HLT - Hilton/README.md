## What it does

Hilton licenses its brands and manages hotels for owners, earning fees based on a share of room revenue, under contracts of 20 to 30 years. Franchise fees were $2.78 billion and management fees $689M in 2025. It owns only 46 hotels, and its loyalty program is a major source of bookings.

## Why it passed

- Management and franchising produced 97% of five-year segment profit.
- Operating income was 54% of net tangible assets in 2025, at the top of its field.
- The system grew from 1,096,115 rooms in 2022 to 1,336,064 in 2025, with net unit growth of 6.7% in 2025 and a pipeline of 541,300 rooms.
- The fee per room kept just ahead of room prices over six years.

## Why we are not buying at today's price

The analysis values the shares at $56.50 to $147.79 against a price of $329.59. The best case a buyer could defend returns about 5.7% a year and the central top case 3.1%, against the 5.56% a 30-year US Treasury bond pays.

## What to watch

- Debt and finance leases are $13.4 billion, with $4.1 billion maturing in 2030.
- Buybacks at $256 to $327 a share were above the top of the analysis's value range and partly borrowed.
- Management features adjusted EBITDA in its reports and guidance rather than plain profit.
