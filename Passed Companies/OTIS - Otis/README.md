## What it does

Otis installs elevators and escalators, then maintains, repairs and modernizes them. It services a portfolio of about 2.5 million units in around 200 countries. Service is 65% of sales and 91% of segment operating profit, and new equipment is the other 35% of sales.

## Why it passed

- Service pricing was passed through every year of the recent inflation, and the service margin kept rising, from 21.8% in 2020 to 22.9% in 2022.
- Its portfolio of units under maintenance grew from over 2.0 million to about 2.5 million, at 4% or more a year for four years.
- The business needs little capital: about $548 million of tangible operating capital against $2,614 million of segment operating profit, with capital spending only 1.0% to 1.4% of sales.
- Customers fund its working capital, and returns on the service segment's assets held through the last full cycle.

## Why we are not buying at today's price

At $65.95 a share the cash the business produces is only about 4.2% of its value including debt, less than the 5.60% on a 30-year US Treasury bond. The analysis's value range is $41.24 to $44.13, so the price is well above the top of it.

## What to watch

- Independent service companies hold about half of all elevator units, which is the weak flank of its service business.
- Every average buyback price paid, $74.7 to $94.3, was above the analysis's value range, and total debt is $8.4 billion with negative shareholder equity.
- A 2007 EU cartel fine under its former parent, and open customer overcharge claims, are recorded.
