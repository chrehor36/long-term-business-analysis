## What it does

Andritz, an Austrian group, designs and builds large industrial plants and equipment for pulp and paper, metals, hydropower, environmental protection and animal feed. Pulp and paper is its biggest area, at about 55% of five years of operating profit. Once a mill is built, the customer keeps buying spare parts, rebuilds and service from the maker.

## Why it passed

- Service work on machines it has already installed made up 59% of 2025 revenue in its pulp and paper area, a steady stream that keeps coming after the big orders are done.
- It earned between roughly 41% and 60% a year before tax on the capital the business uses in each of the five years from 2021 to 2025.
- Customers pay in advance on large projects, so the business needs little of its own money, and the group holds more cash than debt (net cash of about EUR 593 million in mid-2026).
- Only a few large suppliers compete for complete pulp mills, and the analysis found no new entrant that had taken this business over many years.

## Why we are not buying at today's price

At EUR 81.40 a share the price sits inside the analysis's value range, and even the best case (cash growing 8.9% a year for ten years) earns only about 5.5% a year. That is well short of the roughly 10% a year the framework requires, and the analysis's fair price is EUR 35.02.

## What to watch

- Big orders are won by bidding and often carry fixed prices, so a badly priced project can cause large losses, as happened in 2013.
- Part of the cash it holds is customers' advance payments, which unwind if new orders fall.
- The filings mention asbestos injury claims; the analysis did not find their size, and it did not total the performance guarantees the company has issued.
