## What it does

Brookfield Infrastructure Corporation gives investors a share in Brookfield Infrastructure Partners, which owns part-interests in rail and ports, container leasing, pipelines, data centres and other infrastructure. Its largest part is transport, notably a Brazilian rail and port network.

## Why it passed

- A rival with money still cannot build a second Brazilian rail and port network, which gives the transport business a lasting edge.
- It raised rail tariffs about 7% a year in 2024, showing pricing power over customers.
- Its container-leasing business earned more than the only other US-listed container lessor in every overlapping year.
- The accounts and management passed the analysis's honesty checks.

## Why we are not buying at today's price

At $37.74 a unit the cash the business produces is only about 4.3% to 5.3% of its price, below the 5.43% on a 30-year US Treasury bond, and no case in the analysis's value range of $20.45 to $61.60 reaches the return the analysis requires.

## What to watch

- Net debt is $33,969 million, about 1.1 times the market value of the units, so little of the cash is left for owners.
- The Brazilian pipeline depends on a single customer for most of its revenue and moved to five-yearly tariff resets from 2026.
- Returns on the capital employed are modest, 6.6% to 7.2% a year before tax.
