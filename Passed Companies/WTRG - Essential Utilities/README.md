## What it does

Essential Utilities supplies water and wastewater service and, through Peoples, natural gas in Pennsylvania, Kentucky and other states. Regulated water is its deciding part, and regulated natural gas was 39% of the five-year total the analysis examined. State commissions set the rates, and it plans $8.7 billion of capital spending for 2026 to 2030.

## Why it passed

- A water franchise is a natural monopoly held under the state commissions, and the filings show it neither open to a funded attacker nor filling in.
- Its earning power is the rate base times a return set in public proceedings, so the ten-year economics can be foreseen in a general way.
- Its $8.7 billion capital plan for 2026 to 2030 is published, so the rate base growth is knowable.
- The analysis found no doubt about management's honesty: reports were clear, there was no restatement, and an adjustment cut the featured figure.

## Why we are not buying at today's price

A regulator caps what Essential Utilities may earn, so the price is far above what that capped return justifies. At $42.45 a share against a value range of $28.63 to $32.98, a buyer would earn only about 3.6% to 4.5% a year on the cash the business produces, less than a 30-year US Treasury bond (5.30%).

## What to watch

- Growth needs about a dollar of new capital for every dollar of growth, at a regulated return, so it needs a constant inflow of new money.
- It has paid dividends while selling new stock, and the stock's market return failed the analysis's five-year retention test.
- Peoples was bought in 2020 well above book value, and that premium earns nothing in rates; its gas business faces declining use per customer, and at least $450 million of PFAS-related capital is planned.
