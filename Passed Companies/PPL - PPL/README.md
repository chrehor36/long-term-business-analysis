## What it does

PPL owns four regulated utilities. In Kentucky, LG&E and KU generate and deliver electricity and gas to 1.4 million customers. In Pennsylvania, PPL Electric delivers power to 1.5 million customers. A Rhode Island utility delivers electricity and gas. Regulators set the prices.

## Why it passed

- The law grants each utility its service area and the regulators keep it, so customers have no other supplier.
- Earnings come from three states and four separate regulated companies, rather than a single utility.
- Insiders write down their forecast: a three-year capital plan of $17.35 billion for 2026 to 2028 with fully projected test years in Pennsylvania and Kentucky.
- A Kentucky tariff for large customers brings minimum payments and collateral from data-centre customers.

## Why we are not buying at today's price

A regulator caps what a utility may earn, so the price is far above what that capped return justifies. At $35.13 a share a buyer would earn about 2.8% to 4.2% a year, less than the 5.33% on a 30-year US Treasury bond, and the analysis's value range is only $18.20 to $18.40, about half the price.

## What to watch

- Long-term debt is $20.3 billion, and earnings cover interest only about 2.4 to 2.8 times.
- The capital plan of $17.35 billion over 2026 to 2028 is large against operating cash of $2.6 billion in 2025, funded by debt, hybrid securities and new shares.
- Return on equity was only about 5.1% over the period, below what regulators allow.
