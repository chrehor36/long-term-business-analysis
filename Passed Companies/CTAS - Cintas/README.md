## What it does

Cintas rents work uniforms to businesses, picks them up each week, cleans them and brings them back. It also supplies mats, restroom products, first-aid kits and fire-safety checks. It serves more than a million businesses over about 12,500 delivery routes, and no customer is more than 1% of sales.

## Why it passed

- Its route network is dense, so each extra customer on a route is cheap to serve. That makes it the low-cost operator.
- Its profit margin on uniforms rose from 17.9% to 24.1% over ten years, while both main rivals' margins collapsed (UniFirst 13.7% to 5.4%; Vestis 12.5% to 2.4%).
- It earns about 70% a year before tax on the capital the business uses.
- The accounts are clean and management is honest.

## Why we are not buying at today's price

Even if its cash grows 10.7% a year for ten years, a buyer at today's price would earn about 4.5% a year. That is less than a 30-year US Treasury bond pays (5.3%). The price assumes a long run of growth with no room for error.

## What to watch

- It is buying rival UniFirst for about $5.5 billion. The deal is under review by the FTC and has not closed.
- It has bought back its own shares at $179 to $200, above what we think they are worth.
- A $45 million legal settlement with public agencies over contract pricing (City of Laurel).
