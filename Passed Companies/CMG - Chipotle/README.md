## What it does

Chipotle owns and runs 4,186 restaurants (as of mid-2026) selling burritos, bowls, tacos and salads. It does not franchise in the US. Digital orders were 36.7% of food and beverage revenue in 2025.

## Why it passed

- The average restaurant took in $3.1 million in 2025 and cost about $1.5 million to build, with a restaurant margin of 25.4%.
- It earns about 34% a year before tax on the capital it uses, counting leased property, up from about 16% in 2019.
- It has had no borrowings at any year-end from 2016 to mid-2026, even through the 2015 food-safety outbreaks.
- Restaurant margins stayed within about a point of their peak after the long-serving chief executive left.

## Why we are not buying at today's price

The analysis values the shares at $18.00 to $31.52 against a price of $30.90, near the top of that range. A buyer at that price would expect about 3.2% to 5.7% a year, less than a 30-year US Treasury bond pays (5.64%). The price already assumes about 7.9% growth a year for ten years.

## What to watch

- Sales per restaurant fell in the first full year after the chief executive left: comparable sales were down 1.7% and transactions down 2.9%.
- A food-safety scare can hurt badly: after the 2015 outbreaks comparable sales fell 20.4% and operating income dropped from $763.6 million to $34.6 million.
- Its share buybacks name no price limit, so shares can be bought at prices above their value.
