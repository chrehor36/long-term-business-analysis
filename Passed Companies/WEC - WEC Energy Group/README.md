## What it does

WEC Energy Group delivers electricity and natural gas under regulated rates in four states, with Wisconsin as its largest. It also owns an interest in the American Transmission Company. A published plan calls for $33.4 billion of utility capital spending plus $4.1 billion for the transmission company from 2026 to 2030.

## Why it passed

- Its earnings are a regulated return on approved capital, so the next ten years are easy to picture.
- Its return on common equity runs 11% to 12%, though about 62% of its capital is debt.
- The accounts can be followed line by line, and no dishonesty was found.
- The analysis weighed management's ability in its favor.

## Why we are not buying at today's price

A regulator caps what the utility may earn, so the price is far above what that capped return justifies. At $111.12 a share the price is above the whole value range ($67.70 to $78.29), and the buyer would be paid less than a 30-year US Treasury bond (5.31%).

## What to watch

- The capital added since 2020 ($11.4 billion) earned only about 5.1% before tax, below a Treasury bond.
- Cash after capital spending was negative in every year from 2021 to 2025 (an average of minus $734 million), so the dividend and growth are paid from borrowing.
- Illinois regulators have paused pipe spending, and the bonus for 2025 was paid at its 200% maximum; plan-share purchases were made above the analysis's value range.
