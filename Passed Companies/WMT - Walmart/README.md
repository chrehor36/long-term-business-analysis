## What it does

Walmart sells groceries, general merchandise and health products through 4,611 Walmart stores and 601 Sam's Club warehouse clubs in the US, plus operations in Mexico, China, Canada and elsewhere (including Flipkart and PhonePe in India). Net sales were $706.4 billion in fiscal 2026, of which $483.0 billion came from Walmart U.S.

## Why it passed

- Its edge is being the cheapest nearby seller of essentials such as food, a reason customers have kept coming for decades.
- Before tax it earned 17% to 22% a year on the tangible capital it needs, every year from fiscal 2022 to 2026, and the analysis found this return did not come from a cyclical peak, a monopoly or debt.
- Suppliers finance $63 billion of its capital through payables, so sales of $706 billion rest on about $150 billion of tangible capital.
- Operating income was $29.8 billion in fiscal 2026, with Walmart U.S. alone earning $25.2 billion.

## Why we are not buying at today's price

At $105.07 a share the price is about four times the top of the analysis's value range of $21.36 to $26.66. The expected return is only about 1.5% to 2% a year before tax, against 5.66% on a 30-year US Treasury bond.

## What to watch

- Capital spending has more than doubled, from $10.3 billion in fiscal 2021 to $26.6 billion in 2026, while operating income rose from $22.5 billion to $29.8 billion, and the cash left for owners has grown slowly.
- The US store count and floor space did not grow, so the spending goes into automation, delivery and technology that must pay off.
- Its profit margin is only about 4.2%, so it works only while capital turns over quickly.
