## What it does

Marriott licenses its brands and manages hotels owned by others. It had 9,805 properties with about 1.78 million rooms at the end of 2025, and it owns or leases less than 1% of them. It earns franchise royalties of typically 4% to 7% of room revenue, plus management fees under agreements that often run 20 to 30 years.

## Why it passed

- Fee revenue was $5.4 billion in 2025, from franchise fees of $3.3 billion and management fees of $2.1 billion.
- Operating income of $4.1 billion in 2025 was about 50% of the tangible assets (and about 33% even after counting payments made to hotel owners as capital).
- Management contracts run 20 to 30 years and franchise terms 10 to 25 years, which locks in income.
- U.S. and Canada, 74% of segment profit, shows a strong brand and loyalty system.

## Why we are not buying at today's price

At $371.50 a share the price is far above the top of the analysis's value range of $90.30 to $199.82. The cash the business produces is only 1.87% to 2.60% of its price, against 5.56% on a 30-year US Treasury bond. The analysis's fair price is $45.91.

## What to watch

- Debt is $16.9 billion and shareholders' equity is negative because buybacks have exceeded earnings.
- The company reports adjusted profit figures that the analysis treats as flattering.
- Cash earned in a downturn can collapse: in 2020 operating income was $84 million against $445 million of interest.
