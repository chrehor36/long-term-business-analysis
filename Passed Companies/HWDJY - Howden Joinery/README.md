## What it does

Howden Joinery sells kitchens and joinery to small builders through 975 depots (893 in the UK), always in stock, with confidential local pricing and trade credit. About 38% of the cost of goods is made in its own factories. In June 2026 it also bought DIY Kitchens, an online seller to ordinary consumers.

## Why it passed

- Its operating margin was 14.7% in 2025, against -2.1% at Travis Perkins and 4.3% at Wickes, and it held between 12.6% and 19.2% for the decade.
- Gross margin stayed between 60.8% and 62.8% as it raised prices at the start of 2022, 2023 and 2026.
- Before tax it earned about 64% on the capital the business needed in 2019, with almost no goodwill, and had no borrowing for eleven years until 2026.
- The analysis found management honest and able.

## Why we are not buying at today's price

At 788p a share the price sits at the bottom of the analysis's value range of 780p to 1,044p, but no case in that range earns the roughly 10% a year the analysis requires. The analysis's fair price is 499p, and even the top of the range valued at that standard is only 567p.

## What to watch

- Margins have narrowed: operating margin averaged 16.6% in 2015 to 2019 against 14.7% in 2023 to 2025, and the cash the business produces fell from about 10% of sales to 5% to 6%.
- In June 2026 it took on its first borrowing in eleven years, a 240 million pound term loan for the DIY Kitchens deal.
- The buyback has no stated price limit, and has run above the bottom of the value range.
