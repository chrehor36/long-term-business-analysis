## What it does

NorthWestern Energy is a holding company over two regulated utilities serving about 850,300 customers. They supply electricity and natural gas in Montana, South Dakota and Nebraska, including Yellowstone National Park. In Montana, its electric business covers 440,700 customers across 73% of the state's land area.

## Why it passed

- It is a monopoly by law, with an allowed return printed in each regulatory order.
- Its rate base (the capital on which it may earn) is growing under a published plan, from an authorized $3,412 million at the end of 2022 to $5,142 million at the end of 2025.
- The earned return has sat below the allowance by a measurable margin for a decade, which makes it easy to predict.
- No doubt about honesty was found in the accounts or disclosures.

## Why we are not buying at today's price

A regulator caps what a utility may earn, so the price is far above what that capped return justifies. At $77.59 a share the cash left for owners is under 1% of the price on the company's own upkeep estimate and 3% on the kindest basis, against 5.33% for a 30-year US Treasury bond.

## What to watch

- It has signed a merger with Black Hills under which each share becomes 0.98 Black Hills shares, a price within 0.30% of the market price; the deal has not closed.
- Its return on equity fell from 10% in 2016 to 6.3% in 2025 while the allowed return stayed at about 9.65%, and Montana's commission settled every case of the decade below the request.
- Its dividend of $2.68 a share is paid from borrowing and share issues; the analysis weighed management's ability and pay against.
