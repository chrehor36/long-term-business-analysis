## What it does

Chesapeake Utilities delivers natural gas and electricity to homes and businesses in Delaware, Maryland and Florida, and runs gas pipelines. It also sells propane and other energy services. Regulated energy supplies 84% of its operating income.

## Why it passed

- It holds franchises with no direct rival for homes and businesses, so customers have no one else to buy from.
- Regulated energy operating income grew from $106.2 million in 2021 to $222.0 million in 2025.
- Regulators allow returns on equity of about 9.5% to 10.25% in most of its territories, and fuel costs are passed through to customers.
- The analysis found management honest.

## Why we are not buying at today's price

A regulator caps what a utility may earn, so the price is far above what that capped return justifies. At $132.18 a share the cash the business produces is only about 4% of its price, less than the 5.43% on a 30-year US Treasury bond, and the analysis's value range is only $33.47 to $47.46.

## What to watch

- Regulators often grant less than the company asks; Delaware asked $12.1 million and settled at $6.1 million.
- Debt is $1,687.7 million, with $550 to $600 million of spending planned for 2026 plus a share of a $1.2 billion Florida pipeline project, funded by debt and new shares.
- The capital added since 2021, including the $922.8 million Florida gas purchase, earned only about 5.7% a year after tax; hurricanes are a risk in Florida.
