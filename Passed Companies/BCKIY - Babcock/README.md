## What it does

Babcock is a British engineering group that builds and supports warships and submarines, trains military pilots, and maintains British Army vehicles. It owns the Devonport dockyard and runs the naval bases at Devonport and Clyde. Defence is 74% of its £5.2 billion of yearly revenue, and 70% of the revenue is in the UK.

## Why it passed

- It is described in its own filings as the sole through-life support provider for the UK's nuclear submarine fleet, and no other UK facility is licensed to do that refit work.
- A rival with billions could not build a licensed nuclear dock and the regulated workforce to run it within any useful time.
- Nuclear revenue roughly doubled, from £1.0 billion in 2022 to £2.1 billion in 2026.
- Its return on invested capital rose from 17.4% to 37.0% in three years, helped by customer advances and a smaller capital base.

## Why we are not buying at today's price

At 961.8p a share the price is near the top of the analysis's value range of 632p to 998p, and the expected return is only 4.0% to 6.1% a year. A 30-year UK government bond pays 5.86% with no business risk. The analysis's fair price is 427p.

## What to watch

- The UK government is nearly the only customer, and it can re-let contracts or lower the profit rate allowed on single-source work.
- The replacement submarine-support contract was unsigned at the price date, and part of the recent high return came from the best-margin year of the old one.
- A fixed-price frigate contract (Type 31) has already caused a large charge, and goodwill of about £1.3 billion was written off in 2020 and 2021 on earlier purchases.
- A £300 million bond fell due in October 2026 and a €550 million bond falls due in September 2027.
