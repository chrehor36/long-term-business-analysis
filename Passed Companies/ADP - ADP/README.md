## What it does

ADP runs payroll, payroll tax filing, HR, benefits and time tracking for over 1.1 million employer clients, paying more than 42 million workers. It also acts as a co-employer for about 762,000 worksite employees. It earns extra interest on the client money it holds between paying in and paying out.

## Why it passed

- Payroll and tax filing are tasks every employer must do correctly, so clients stay: it keeps 92.1% of its client revenue each year, up from 89.9% ten years ago.
- Moving to another provider takes six to nine months and a payroll mistake is costly, so clients rarely switch on price alone.
- It is connected directly to tens of thousands of government agencies, tax authorities and banks, a network rivals find hard to copy.
- It needs very little added capital to grow: about 65% a year after tax on the capital it uses, counting past purchases.

## Why we are not buying at today's price

The analysis puts the value at $156 to $246 a share, and the price was $260.26, above the top of that range. At that price a buyer would expect roughly 5.4% to 7.4% a year, about level with a 30-year US Treasury bond (5.66%), which carries far less risk.

## What to watch

- About a quarter of pre-tax earnings comes from interest on client funds, so falling interest rates would cut profit.
- Younger rivals (Paycom, Paylocity) are growing faster, and AI or cheap payroll software could make the service more of a commodity.
- It has bought back shares at prices above the bottom of the value range, and it borrows short-term to support the client funds.
