## What it does

Fastenal buys fasteners and other industrial and construction supplies, mostly made by others, and delivers them to customers from 15 distribution centers and 1,595 branches. Increasingly it installs vending machines and bins in customers' plants so the parts are at the point of use. About three-quarters of its customers are manufacturers, and no customer is 5% of sales.

## Why it passed

- Fifteen years of attack from large distributors and online sellers have not moved its operating margin, which stood at 21.0% in the second quarter of 2026.
- The largest rival in its peer list has twice its sales and has not reached Fastenal's operating margin in any year of the analysis's comparison.
- Customer sites spending $5,000 or more a month rose from 11,236 in 2016 to 19,066 in 2025, and their sales from $2,627 million to $7,311 million.
- It earns high and rising returns on its tangible capital without borrowing heavily, and pays out about 80% of net income as dividends.

## Why we are not buying at today's price

At $51.55 a share against a value range of $14.66 to $27.75, the price is well above the top. Even the growth case returns only about 3.2% a year at that price, less than a 30-year US Treasury bond (5.60%).

## What to watch

- The industry is large, fragmented and fiercely competitive, and its gross margin has fallen about five points in ten years even as the operating margin held.
- It bought back shares in 2026 at prices above the top of the analysis's value range.
- Its chief financial officer and its auditor both changed in 2025; the analysis recorded this without a stated cause.
