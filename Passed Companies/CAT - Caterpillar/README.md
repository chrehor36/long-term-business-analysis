## What it does

Caterpillar builds heavy machines for construction and mining, plus engines, turbines and locomotives. It sells them through about 150 independent dealers serving 190 countries, and those dealers also sell the parts and service that follow. Services revenue was about $24 billion in 2025, over a third of the machinery business's $64.0 billion of sales.

## Why it passed

- The machinery business earned about 48% a year before tax on the tangible capital it needs in 2025, and the five-year average cash it produced was 35% of that capital.
- Its dealer network, its installed base of machines and its control over the design of key components keep customers coming back for parts and service.
- In most cases selling and servicing Caterpillar products is the dealers' main business, so they have every reason to stay loyal.
- The accounts were clear enough to read, and management was judged able and honest.

## Why we are not buying at today's price

At $848.14 a share the price is above the top of the analysis's value range of $300 to $781. In the best case a buyer would earn about 5.3% a year after tax, less than a 30-year US Treasury bond pays (5.66%). The analysis's fair price is $281.63.

## What to watch

- The company has bought back its own shares at prices well above what the analysis thinks they are worth.
- Dealer agreements can be ended by either side on 90 days' notice.
- A tax dispute over a Swiss subsidiary was settled in 2022 without penalties.
- Machine demand rises and falls with the cycle, and a trough year in the base would lower every value figure.
