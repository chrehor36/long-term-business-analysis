## What it does

AMETEK makes advanced test and measurement instruments for process, aerospace, medical, research, power and industrial customers. It also makes engineered medical components, automation systems, thermal management systems and specialty metals, and runs aviation repair work. About 22,500 people work for it and 48.2% of sales are outside the US.

## Why it passed

- The business needs little capital: operating income before purchase-related amortization was about 100% of the capital it uses, on a pre-tax basis.
- Capital spending is small, about 1.8% of sales in 2025 ($130.2M), roughly equal to depreciation.
- The instrument group earned 71.6% of five-year segment operating income, and most of its sales come from process and analytical instruments.
- Cash from operations was $1.8 billion in 2025.

## Why we are not buying at today's price

The analysis puts the central value at -$5.56 to $31.35 a share, with the latest $5.0 billion purchase counted as debt and credited with nothing. Even if the company stopped buying other firms, the analysis values it at about $83 to $120 a share. A buyer at $248.58 would earn only about 1% to 3% a year, well below the 5.60% a 30-year US Treasury bond pays.

## What to watch

- A $5.0 billion all-cash purchase (Indicor Instrumentation) closed in August 2026, raising net debt from about $1.5 billion to about $6.5 billion; no earnings for it have been filed.
- The larger instrument group's sales fell 1% on an organic basis in 2025; its growth came from acquisitions and currency.
- Goodwill and other purchased intangibles are 70% of total assets, and buybacks at about $177 and $192 a share were above the analysis's value.
