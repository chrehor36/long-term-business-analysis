## What it does

ITOCHU is a Japanese trading company with many businesses. Its Metals & Minerals division, a low-cost iron-ore and coking-coal producer, is the part that carries the analysis. It also owns FamilyMart, the convenience store chain, since 2018.

## Why it passed

- Its iron-ore and coking-coal mines hold the lowest-cost position, which it kept through a full commodity cycle.
- Its own return on shareholders' equity ran from 21.8% in fiscal 2022 down to 14.6% in fiscal 2026.
- Its borrowing is moderate: net debt to equity fell from 0.54 to 0.46.
- The accounts raised no suspicion and no doubt about honesty was found.

## Why we are not buying at today's price

At ¥2,155 a share, the price is above the top of the analysis's value range (¥1,466 to ¥1,902). A buyer would earn about 3.1% to 3.8% a year in cash, below both the roughly 10% the analysis requires and the 4.136% a 30-year Japanese government bond pays.

## What to watch

- Cleaner steelmaking is a twenty-year threat to the iron-ore and coking-coal business, and its coal part is weak.
- The price of its raw materials swings, so the cash it produces is hard to pin down.
- It has net debt of about ¥3.6 trillion and a pending purchase of Dentsu Soken that the analysis valued as if closed; management's ability was weighed against.
