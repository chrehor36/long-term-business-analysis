## What it does

Asbury runs 171 franchised car dealerships (36 brands) and 39 collision centers in 15 states. It sells new and used cars, services them, and arranges loans and insurance-type products. It also has its own business that sells vehicle service contracts through its stores.

## Why it passed

- State franchise laws mean only a franchised dealer may sell a brand new, and they limit rivals from opening a same-brand store nearby.
- Parts and service plus finance and insurance make up 71% of its gross profit, and warranty and recall work must be done at a franchised dealer.
- On the tangible capital it needs, it earned 16% to 31% before tax every year from 2011 to 2019, and 27.9% in 2025.
- Even in the 2009 slump it still made $90.9 million from operations on $3.23 billion of revenue.

## Why we are not buying at today's price

At $165.28 a share, the price would earn about 8.2% a year in cash with no growth, below the roughly 10% the analysis requires. The cash it produces has not covered its acquisition spending, and the analysis's central value is far below the price.

## What to watch

- The automaker-granted franchise rights could be weakened by direct sales from electric-vehicle makers.
- It carries about $3.5 billion of debt plus $1.8 billion of vehicle floor-plan loans.
- The Federal Trade Commission has a complaint about add-on product sales and credit law at three Texas stores, still unresolved.
- Toyota and Lexus are 30% of new-vehicle revenue.
