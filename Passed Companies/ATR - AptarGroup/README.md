## What it does

AptarGroup makes dispensing parts: nasal-spray pumps, inhaler valves and injectable stoppers for drug makers, pumps for fragrance and skin care, and closures for food and drinks. Its drug-delivery business is the largest, at 77% of five years of segment profit. It has about five thousand customers.

## Why it passed

- Once a drug is approved with an Aptar device, switching the device means re-registering the drug, so customers rarely change.
- Its drug-delivery business earned a 25% to 31% profit margin on sales in each of the twelve years the analysis checked.
- The company as a whole earned about 20% to 24% a year before tax on the tangible capital it uses from 2021 to 2025.
- The money it added from 2019 to 2025 earned a decent return, about 10.6% a year before tax.

## Why we are not buying at today's price

At $120.25 a share the price is well above the analysis's value range of $69.80 to $76.99, and the expected return is only about 4% a year. That is less than a 30-year US Treasury bond pays (5.64%). The analysis's fair price is $28.37.

## What to watch

- Its beauty and closures businesses earn only ordinary returns and face price competition.
- Drug-delivery sales depend on its customers' drug launches succeeding.
- A Russian subsidiary (under 1% of assets) was put under temporary external administration in September 2026, and there are an antitrust suit and patent suits against it, with no loss estimate given.
