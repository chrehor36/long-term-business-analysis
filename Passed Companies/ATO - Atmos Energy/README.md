## What it does

Atmos Energy delivers natural gas to homes and businesses through about 3.4 million meters, mostly in Texas and neighboring states, under 1,010 city franchises. It also runs a large gas pipeline system in Texas. Regulators set the rates it may charge.

## Why it passed

- It faces no significant direct competition in its service areas, and a rival laying a second gas network would only earn the same regulated return on a doubled cost base.
- State regulators allow it a return on the money it invests, with returns on equity of 9.3% to 11.45% and most at 9.80%, and it can start recovering about 95% of its capital spending within six months.
- Its earnings have been steady: return on average equity stayed between 8.7% and 10.8% in nearly every year of the last ten.
- Its return on capital has edged up from 5.5% to 6.3% over five years, a hair above the 30-year US Treasury rate.

## Why we are not buying at today's price

A regulator caps what Atmos may earn, so the price is far above what that capped return justifies. The analysis values the shares at $25.68 to $95.67 against a price of $167.34, and a buyer at that price would expect only about 2% to 5% a year, less than a 30-year US Treasury bond (5.43%).

## What to watch

- It plans about $26 billion of capital spending over five years, so cash flow after that spending is negative and it keeps borrowing and selling new shares.
- Borrowings are $10.3 billion, about 40% of its capital, with another $700 million of notes issued in October 2026.
- Gas volume has been flat for seven years, so earnings growth comes from spending more, not from selling more gas.
