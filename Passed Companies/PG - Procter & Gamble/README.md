## What it does

Procter & Gamble makes daily-use products in eleven categories: fabric care, home care, baby care, feminine care, family care, hair care, personal care, skin care, grooming, oral care and personal health care. It sells them mainly through retailers.

## Why it passed

- Its brands, scale and low-cost position protect it in a field where products are close to commodities, and its margins stayed steady through a cost cycle.
- It earns about 75% to 139% before tax on the tangible capital it needs, helped by negative working capital of $11.1 billion (suppliers fund it).
- Even counting the $62.7 billion paid for goodwill and brands, mostly Gillette, it earns about 22% before tax.
- Competitors such as Unilever, Colgate, Kimberly-Clark, Clorox and Church & Dwight earn less on each dollar of sales.

## Why we are not buying at today's price

At $145.94 a share the price is 11% above the top of the analysis's value range ($104 to $131). A buyer would earn about 5% to 6.5% a year before tax, below the roughly 10% the analysis requires and no better than a 30-year US Treasury bond (5.66%).

## What to watch

- It has returned more cash than it produces: dividends of $10.2 billion and buybacks of $5.0 billion in fiscal 2026, with shares bought at prices above the analysis's value range.
- Sales volumes were flat for five years, a new chief executive is untried, and retailers and the shift in media are named threats.
- Management is paid on earnings per share, which rises with buybacks at any price.
