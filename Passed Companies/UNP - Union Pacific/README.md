## What it does

Union Pacific runs a 32,889-route-mile freight railroad across 23 western states and serves all six major Mexico gateways. It hauled $23.2 billion of freight in 2025, including grain, fertilizer, industrial goods and consumer goods. Its only major rail rival in the West is BNSF.

## Why it passed

- No one can lay a second 32,889-mile network into the ports, gateways and plants UNP reaches, and the rail rivals in its territory in 2025 are the same as in 2009.
- Its operating ratio (operating costs as a share of revenue, lower is better) improved from 67.8% in 2012 to under 60%, and it has raised prices through inflation.
- The analysis found management honest and able, and found no adverse integrity facts.
- The economics can be pictured ten years out because they follow carloads times price per car.

## Why we are not buying at today's price

The analysis values the shares at $120.64 to $162.31 against a price of $278.34, which is 1.7 times the top of the range. A buyer at that price would expect only about 2.6% to 4.5% a year, below a 30-year US Treasury bond (5.60%).

## What to watch

- It is merging with Norfolk Southern, which would add about $20 billion of new borrowing and Norfolk Southern's $15.5 billion of net debt; buybacks are paused for the deal.
- Over 2016 to 2025 it paid out about $70 billion in dividends and buybacks, about $19 billion more than the cash it earned after capital spending.
- Its buyback authorizations name a number of shares, never a price limit, so shares were bought above their value.
