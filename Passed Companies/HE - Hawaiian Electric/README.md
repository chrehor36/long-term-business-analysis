## What it does

Hawaiian Electric Industries is a holding company over three regulated electric utilities that serve about 95% of Hawaii's people on five separate island grids. The utilities produce about 99% of its revenue. It sold its bank in 2024 and plans to sell its other affiliated companies.

## Why it passed

- Its sales are protected by a legal franchise over isolated island grids, and Hawaii's legislature and regulator acted to keep it standing after a fire that destroyed most of its owners' equity.
- Rates are set by regulation with a mechanism that does not tie revenue to how much electricity is sold, plus pass-through of fuel costs.
- Its future is easy to foresee: flat volume and a rate base that grows only through grid hardening and the energy transition.
- The analysis found no sign of dishonesty in the documents it read.

## Why we are not buying at today's price

A regulator caps what Hawaiian Electric may earn, so the price is far above what that capped return justifies. At $8.75 a share, with the Maui wildfire settlement still owed, a buyer would earn about 5.4% a year, barely above a 30-year US Treasury bond (5.29%) and half the return the framework requires, against a value of $8.95 a share.

## What to watch

- It still owes $1.44 billion of Maui wildfire settlement installments and expects to pay them with a mix of debt, new shares and other securities.
- Securities and derivative lawsuits over the fire were settled by insurers without admission of fault, and Hawaii's liability caps (Act 258) are not yet in force.
- Its credit ratings have been below investment grade since August 2023, and one island utility earns only 4.54% against an authorized 9.5%.
