## What it does

Coca-Cola sells beverage concentrates and syrups to bottlers around the world, and also sells finished drinks through some bottlers it owns, US fountain syrup, Costa coffee shops and the fairlife milk brand. The finished-product side brings in more revenue but at lower profit margins.

## Why it passed

- Prices rose about 49% over five years (2021 to 2025) and volume still rose every year.
- Its main rival's own filings show the rival's US share falling eight points while Coca-Cola's held at 20%.
- Unit cases grew from 29.3 billion in 2016 to 33.8 billion, and the flagship Coca-Cola brand's share of worldwide volume rose from 45% to 47%.
- The business needs very little tangible capital: the pre-tax return on it rose from about 100% to over 300%.

## Why we are not buying at today's price

At $86.51 a share a buyer would earn about 3.6% to 4.6% a year, less than the 5.66% on a 30-year US Treasury bond. The analysis's value range is only $42.13 to $43.40, and its fair price is about $27.

## What to watch

- Its own US share is flat, and North America volume fell in 2025.
- Part of the price rise is inflationary pricing in countries such as Argentina, and some of its purchases outside the core drinks have failed.
- Dividends of $72.9 billion and buybacks of $16.9 billion have been paid out of cash from bottler sales and borrowing, with buybacks above the value range.
