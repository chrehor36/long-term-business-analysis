## What it does

Hilton Grand Vacations sells timeshare intervals and points under the Hilton brand, lends to most of its buyers (67% of 2025 sales were financed), and manages resorts for owners' associations for fees of 10% to 15% of costs. It also runs member clubs and rents unsold inventory.

## Why it passed

- It holds an exclusive Hilton licence for timeshare until 2051, which can be extended.
- Existing owners bought 74% of 2025 sales, up from 70% in 2023, so most sales come from customers it already has.
- Real estate sales and financing produced 59% of five-year segment profit, and the position against rivals has broadly held.
- Before tax it earned 23.7% on the tangible capital it needs in 2019, and 7.5% to 13.7% a year since.

## Why we are not buying at today's price

The analysis's range of $94.23 to $95.63 looks far above the $35.18 price only because it ignores $4.6 billion of corporate debt that takes the first claim on the cash. After that debt, the best case earns 9.12% a year, below the roughly 10% the analysis requires, and the analysis's fair price is $25.77, below the current price.

## What to watch

- Corporate debt is $4,876 million at about 5.7%, and it rose while the company bought back $600 million of shares.
- The Hilton licence can be ended if quality fails, and it costs 5% of gross revenue; licence fees rose from $138 million to $214 million between 2023 and 2025.
- Capital put in over the period earned only about 4% before tax, and the business depends on the credit market for funding its loans.
