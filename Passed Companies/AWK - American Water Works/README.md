## What it does

American Water Works supplies drinking water and treats wastewater in fourteen US states. Each service area is an exclusive franchise, because no one can lay a second set of pipes, and state regulators set the prices it may charge. No single state is more than a quarter of revenue.

## Why it passed

- No competitor can build a second pipe network, and the service certificate is exclusive, so its customers cannot go elsewhere.
- Its operating margin averaged 34.3% over ten years, close to the 35.7% of rival Essential Utilities and well above California Water's 16.8%.
- More states are moving toward rules that let utilities recover costs sooner, such as future test years (Missouri from July 2026) and infrastructure surcharges in nine states.
- The accounts are clean and no doubts about management's honesty were found.

## Why we are not buying at today's price

A regulator caps what a utility may earn, so the price is far above what that capped return justifies. At $141.10 a share a buyer would earn about 3.6% to 5.0% a year, less than the 5.30% on a 30-year US Treasury bond, and the analysis's value range is only $94.91 to $98.84.

## What to watch

- Its prices and returns are political: regulators decide how much of its costs it may recover.
- It is merging with Essential Utilities (stock for stock, bringing a gas utility), with an $835 million termination fee payable by American Water in some circumstances.
- Total debt is about $16 billion, and spending on pipes and plants is large, continuous and largely compulsory.
