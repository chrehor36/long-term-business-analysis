## What it does

Philip Morris International sells cigarettes, led by Marlboro, in about 170 markets outside the US. It also sells smoke-free products such as IQOS heated tobacco, ZYN nicotine pouches and VEEV e-vapor in 106 markets. 2025 net revenue was $40.6 billion, of which $16.9 billion was smoke-free.

## Why it passed

- It can raise prices without losing customers: in 2025 price increases added $1.5 billion of revenue while cigarette volume fell only 1.5%, and in one 2026 quarter it took 10.0% in pricing with share unchanged.
- Marlboro is asked for by name, and its share of international cigarettes rose from 9.8% to 10.7% over two years.
- Its return on the net tangible capital it uses is about 107% a year before tax, because the business needs little capital.
- Operating income rose from $11.4 billion in 2018 to $14.9 billion in 2025.

## Why we are not buying at today's price

The analysis values the shares at $97.72 to $106.25 against a price of $189.53, far above the range. A buyer at that price would expect about 3% to 7.5% a year, below the roughly 10% the analysis requires and below a 30-year US Treasury bond (5.66%) at the low end.

## What to watch

- Cigarette volume keeps falling (about -3% guided), so the business depends on smoke-free products taking over, and rivals are attacking nicotine pouches.
- It has $48.8 billion of debt, and what it reinvested beyond dividends was borrowed: $22.5 billion of acquisitions and rights in 2018 to 2025 against debt up $17.1 billion.
- The US business of Marlboro, held by Altria, is shrinking (smokeable volume down 10.0% in 2025), a sign of what can happen to the brand.
