## What it does

Jack Henry sells and hosts the main record-keeping system (deposits, loans, accounting) for community and regional banks and credit unions. It adds related products and payment processing, and sells payments services to about 5,600 other clients. Hosted clients are typically on six-year contracts with per-account fees and minimum payments.

## Why it passed

- Over 900 banks and over 700 credit unions use its core systems, and changing a bank's main record-keeping system is costly and risky, so clients rarely leave.
- Its core business earned a 60.3% margin on segment sales in fiscal 2026, up from 54.3% in 2018.
- Contracted future revenue was $8.44 billion in mid-2026, against $4.64 billion in 2021.
- It earned about 41% a year after tax on the tangible capital it uses in fiscal 2026, and carries almost no debt ($40 million).

## Why we are not buying at today's price

At $146.07 a share the price is above the top of the analysis's value range of $67.98 to $129.58. The cash the business produces is about 3.2% of its value at that price before any growth, against 5.64% on a 30-year US Treasury bond. The analysis's fair price is $52.52.

## What to watch

- It has used its credit line to fund share buybacks while keeping cash near zero, and the buybacks have been made at prices above the analysis's value range.
- It reports having had cybersecurity incidents in the past, and federal banking regulators examine its hosted services.
- Its payments segment has a lower margin (48.8%) than the core business and has slipped since 2018.
