## What it does

Church & Dwight sells household and personal-care products, including baking soda, laundry and cleaning products, cat litter, TROJAN condoms, dry shampoo, mouthwash and water flossers. Its US consumer business supplies 85% of its operating profit, and it also sells internationally and makes sodium bicarbonate for industry and animal feed.

## Why it passed

- Many of its brands are number one in their category by dollar share, such as TROJAN condoms, NAIR, ORAJEL, BATISTE dry shampoo, THERABREATH, HERO and WATERPIK.
- It earned about 40% a year before tax on its tangible assets, averaged over ten years, because it needs little plant.
- Its US consumer gross margin was 46.7% in 2025 and has stayed between 43% and 49% for ten years.
- Capital spending ran only 1.2% to 3.8% of sales, so almost all profit is left for owners.

## Why we are not buying at today's price

At $96.54 a share a buyer would earn about 3.6% to 4.7% a year, less than the 5.64% on a 30-year US Treasury bond. The analysis's value range is $56.49 to $65.75, so the price is well above it.

## What to watch

- Walmart is about 23% of sales and the top four customers about 44%, with orders placed one at a time rather than under long contracts.
- Six of its seven main brands were bought, and the analysis found three failed purchases in the decade; buybacks have been at prices above its value range.
- The WATERPIK trade name carries a value of $644.7 million, 117% of its book value, so it could be written down.
