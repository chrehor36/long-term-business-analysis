## What it does

Ameren sells electricity and natural gas to homes and businesses in Missouri and Illinois, and owns high-voltage transmission lines. Regulators in those states and at the federal level set the return it may earn on the plant it builds. It plans $30.5 to $33.1 billion of capital spending from 2026 to 2030.

## Why it passed

- Its economics are easy to foresee: the allowed returns are written in rate orders (for example 8.72% on equity at Ameren Illinois electric distribution and 10.48% for federal transmission).
- The company publishes its plant-building plan five years ahead, so the growth in its earning base is knowable.
- The return on the equity slice of its capital has been 10.1% to 11.4% in every year from 2018.
- The analysis found no wildfire-type liability in its filings and judged management honest.

## Why we are not buying at today's price

A regulator caps what Ameren may earn, so the price is far above what that capped return justifies. At $106.08 a share against a value range of $18.84 to $36.89, a buyer would earn only about 2.9% to 3.9% a year on the cash the business produces, less than a 30-year US Treasury bond (5.43%).

## What to watch

- Debt is 60% of its capital (52% in 2016), and it must raise new debt and sell new shares every year to fund the building plan.
- Without leverage the business earns roughly the long bond rate on its capital (about 6% a year).
- Coal still supplied 56.5% of Ameren Missouri's energy in 2025, and a plant was retired early after a court found Clean Air Act violations.
