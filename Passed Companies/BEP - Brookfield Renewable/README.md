## What it does

Brookfield Renewable owns about 47,200 MW of power plants, mainly hydroelectric dams in North and South America, plus wind, solar and storage. About 90% of its 2026 output is sold under contracts with 13 years remaining on average. It also buys, builds and sells projects, often through funds run by its parent Brookfield.

## Why it passed

- Hydro is its largest part (43% of five-year earnings before depreciation), and dams cannot be copied, run without star managers, and need little money to stay in place.
- Hydro output has been steady, with long-term-average generation of 19,726 GWh in 2021 and 20,420 GWh in 2025, and a dam lasts up to 115 years.
- Revenue per MWh stayed between $71 and $74, and the contracts are inflation-linked.
- The analysis found no sign of dishonesty in management.

## Why we are not buying at today's price

The business uses far more cash than it produces. After the cost of keeping and growing the fleet, the cash left for owners was negative on every case the filings allow, so the analysis found no value range and no positive fair price at $28.40 a unit.

## What to watch

- Its reported profit comes mostly from selling assets (gains of $852 million in 2025); recurring earnings fell from $633 million to $482 million between 2021 and 2025.
- Net income attributable to unitholders was negative in every year from 2021 to 2025, and interest costs rose from $539 million to $969 million.
- Fees to its manager scale with size rather than value per unit, and the 2026 Aypa acquisition had not closed.
