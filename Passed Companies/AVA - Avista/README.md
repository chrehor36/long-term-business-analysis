## What it does

Avista sells electricity and natural gas to homes and businesses in Washington, Idaho, Oregon and Montana, and runs the electric utility in Juneau, Alaska. State regulators set the returns it may earn on its plant. The main utility earned 93% of the company's net income over 2021 to 2025.

## Why it passed

- Its future is easy to foresee: allowed returns are set in public rate cases years ahead (Washington 9.8%, Idaho 9.6%, Oregon 9.5%).
- Its plant base grew about 4.9% a year, from $5.2 billion in 2021 to $6.3 billion in 2025.
- Its customers and electricity load are rising, and its franchise cannot be attacked by competitors.
- A 2020 wildfire lawsuit settled for $27 million, of which the company paid $21 million, all insured.

## Why we are not buying at today's price

A regulator caps what Avista may earn, so the price is far above what that capped return justifies. At $36.67 a share against a value range of $3.29 to $9.97, a buyer would earn only about 3% to 5% a year on the cash the business produces, less than a 30-year US Treasury bond (5.43%).

## What to watch

- It has earned 6.5% to 7.3% on its equity, below its allowed returns and below neighbouring utilities, and its added capital earned only about 6%.
- Wildfire liability is the event that could most damage it; Idaho and Washington passed wildfire laws in 2025.
- It sells new shares and borrows every year to fund its building, and its pay plan rewards earnings per share with no charge for capital.
