## What it does

Kadant makes doctor blades, rotary joints, syphons and stock-preparation and recycling equipment for paper, packaging and other mills, plus debarkers, conveyor idlers and balers. Replacement parts and consumables were a record 71% of its $1,052.2 million of 2025 revenue, and the rest was capital equipment. A doctor blade lasts from eight hours to two months.

## Why it passed

- Customers keep buying worn critical parts even when they cut capital spending, and prices held through every volume fall in the filings.
- Its markets are small and specialized, and its parts business earns high returns on the tangible capital it needs.
- Parts and consumables, the steadier part of the business, grew to 71% of revenue in 2025.
- It kept its cash for acquisitions paid in cash, with no share issuance and no buyback above value.

## Why we are not buying at today's price

At $264.15 a share the price is above the whole value range of $162 to $224. A buyer would earn about 3.6% to 4.7% a year on the cash the business produces, below a 30-year US Treasury bond (5.43%).

## What to watch

- Organic revenue growth fell from 15% (FY2021) to a decline of 4% (FY2025); most of the reported growth was bought through acquisitions, which earn decent but not high returns.
- Threats the analysis carried: mill closures, bundling by machine builders, Chinese competitors, and the graphic paper grades in structural decline (about 4% of revenue).
- It features adjusted profit figures and redefined adjusted earnings per share, and its pay is based on adjusted EBITDA.
