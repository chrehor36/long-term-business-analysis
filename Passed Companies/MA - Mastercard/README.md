## What it does

Mastercard runs a four-party card network that connects banks, merchants and card holders. It does not issue cards or lend money. It earns fees on the dollar volume of payments, and sells value-added services such as security and data, which were 41% of 2025 net revenue.

## Why it passed

- The network is accepted almost everywhere, and everyone uses it because everyone else is already on it. Discover had a bank's balance sheet behind it and still handled only $402.5 billion against Mastercard's roughly $9.8 trillion in 2024.
- Its operating profit was $18.9 billion in 2025, about 100% of the tangible capital it uses, which is small.
- Even in 2020, its worst year, revenue fell only 9% and operating margin stayed at 52.8%.
- Its fee per dollar of payments has held near 0.18%, and it has raised prices without losing business.

## Why we are not buying at today's price

The analysis puts the value at $196.74 to $801.66 a share, a wide range, against a price of $589.14. At that price the cash it produces is only about 2% of the price, and the expected return is about 2% to 7% a year, below the roughly 10% the analysis requires and, at the low end, well below a 30-year US Treasury bond (5.60%).

## What to watch

- Competition-law cases, including a European Commission ruling on interchange fees, are recorded as an integrity concern.
- To keep banks on its network it pays growing incentives, and the capitalized cost of these payments is $10.4 billion.
- In the first half of 2026 it spent $10.5 billion on buybacks and dividends against $6.8 billion of operating cash, funded by $5.6 billion of new borrowing.
