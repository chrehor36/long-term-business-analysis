## What it does

Tractor Supply runs about 2,460 stores in towns and rural communities, plus around 200 Petsense pet stores. It sells livestock and farm supplies (27% of sales), pet food and supplies (24%), seasonal and recreation goods (24%), truck, tool and hardware (15%) and clothing and gifts (10%). Owned and exclusive brands are about 30% of sales.

## Why it passed

- Its stores are the local scale in small towns, selling heavy needs-based goods, such as feed and fencing, that general retailers do not carry in depth and that are costly to ship.
- Operating margin has stayed at 9% to 10% for ten years, while gross margin rose from 34.3% to 36.4% as customer transactions grew.
- It earned 39% a year before tax on the tangible capital it needs, and about 21% even counting its store leases as capital.
- An antitrust finding concluded that its closest competitors are other farm stores.

## Why we are not buying at today's price

At $30.80 a share the price sits inside the analysis's value range of $19.00 to $42.65, and a buyer would earn 3.5% to 7.5% a year, against 5.63% on a 30-year US Treasury bond and the roughly 10% the analysis requires. The analysis's own fair price is about $16.

## What to watch

- In pet products, the second largest category, rivals are crossing the moat, and the company is cutting prices to defend its position in 2026.
- Over five years it spent $3.0 billion on buybacks out of $5.4 billion of profit, at prices above value and partly with borrowed money.
- It leases about 97% of its stores, with lease liabilities of $4.1 billion.
