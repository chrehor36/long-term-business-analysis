## What it does

After spinning off its aerospace business and doing a 1-for-2 reverse split in June 2026, Honeywell is mainly an automation company. It makes fire detection, building controls, energy management and access control systems, plus plant control systems for industrial sites. It also holds a stake in the quantum-computing company Quantinuum.

## Why it passed

- Its largest part, Building Automation, raised its profit margin from 21.2% in 2020 to 26.5% in 2025, with organic sales up 7% to 9% a quarter.
- The systems are installed in buildings and serviced for their lives through a network of installers, so customers come back because of the installed base, not one person.
- Plant control (Process Automation) earns a margin of about 24%.
- It needs little physical capital: capital spending was only 1.8% to 2.4% of sales in 2023 to 2025.

## Why we are not buying at today's price

The analysis values the shares at $87 to $163 against a price of $214.14, above the top of the range. A buyer at that price would expect only about 2.3% to 4.2% a year, below a 30-year US Treasury bond (5.66%).

## What to watch

- The new company has only a short pro forma record, so its true earning power is reconstructed from combined and recast figures rather than filed statements.
- Debt is about $20.9 billion against $11.4 billion of cash, before a $1.75 billion Johnson Matthey purchase in July 2026.
- The old company's buybacks named no price, and it used $10.7 billion of debt-financed acquisitions in the period.
