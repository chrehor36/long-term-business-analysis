## What it does

Unitil owns five electric and gas distribution utilities and one gas pipeline, regulated by three state commissions and the federal energy regulator. Gas is about two-thirds of its operating profit and electricity about one-third. The cost of the energy it buys is passed through to customers, and in June 2026 it bought water companies that have not yet reported earnings.

## Why it passed

- Regulators set its revenue so that it recovers its costs and earns a return on the utility assets it builds.
- Gas operating profit grew from $45.7 million in 2021 to $75.4 million in 2025, and electric from $26.7 million to $31.3 million.
- Swings in the price of purchased energy do not affect its earnings, and sales in Massachusetts and New Hampshire are decoupled from volume.
- Net income rose from $32.2 million to $50.2 million from 2020 to 2025, earning about 8.2% after tax on the equity added.

## Why we are not buying at today's price

A regulator caps what this utility may earn, so the price is far above what that capped return justifies. At $55.15 a share the price is above the analysis's value range of $24.57 to $49.60, and even the best case earns only about 4.8% a year, against 5.33% on a 30-year US Treasury bond.

## What to watch

- Debt was $956 million in mid-2026, about 60% of debt plus equity, and pre-tax earnings covered interest only about 2.5 times in 2025.
- Regulators in Massachusetts and Maine are tightening on gas, and the federal transmission return was cut to 9.57%.
- The newly bought water companies have no filed earnings yet, and a term loan matures on 2026-10-31.
