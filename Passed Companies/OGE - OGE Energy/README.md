## What it does

OGE Energy owns OG&E, a vertically integrated electric company that generates, transmits and delivers power to about 913,000 customers in Oklahoma and western Arkansas. State regulators set its rates: 88% of 2025 electric revenue comes under the Oklahoma Corporation Commission, 7% under Arkansas, and 5% under the federal regulator FERC.

## Why it passed

- Nobody builds a second electric grid alongside OG&E's 30,000-square-mile network (net plant $12.7 billion), and customers have no cheaper supplier for their home's wires.
- Regulators grant it a set return on the money it invests, about 9.5% on equity in recent cases.
- It earned 6.5% on its total capital in 2025, and about 6.7% on the capital it added since 2020.
- Its earnings can be pictured ten years out, because they follow the capital it is allowed to invest.

## Why we are not buying at today's price

A regulator caps what OGE may earn, so the price is far above what that capped return justifies. The analysis values the shares at about $27 against a price of $48.41, nearly 80% above, and the cash left for owners is only about 3% of the price, against 5.33% on a 30-year US Treasury bond.

## What to watch

- Regulators often grant much less than asked: it asked for $332.5 million in Oklahoma in 2023 and was granted $126.7 million.
- Spending on the grid and plants ran at 2.1 times depreciation in 2021 to 2025, and the 2026 to 2030 plan is $7.3 billion, so cash left for owners was negative every year and the gap was filled with new debt and shares.
- Rating agencies' outlooks on its debt are negative, and rural electric cooperatives contest its right to serve large new loads.
