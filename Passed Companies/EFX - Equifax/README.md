## What it does

Equifax sells data and checks to lenders, employers and governments. Its largest business, Workforce Solutions, verifies employment and income using payroll records from over 4 million organizations. It also runs the U.S. credit bureau and credit bureaus in more than a dozen other countries.

## Why it passed

- Workforce Solutions grew revenue from $826.8 million to $2,582.3 million with an operating margin never below 40% in ten years.
- Its employment database holds about 209 million active and 813 million total records, fed by over 4 million organizations that are generally not paid to contribute.
- Its margins held through a change of chief executive and through new entrants.
- No dishonesty was found in management or the accounts.

## Why we are not buying at today's price

At $142.40 a share, the price sits near the top of the analysis's best-case range of $70.62 to $147.98, which assumes the company stops buying other companies and grows 7% a year on its own. On the analysis's standard assumptions the value is nil. At the price a buyer would earn about 3.5% a year with no growth, less than a 30-year US Treasury bond (5.64%).

## What to watch

- From 2021 to 2025 it spent almost all of its cash flow ($743.8 million a year) on buying other businesses, which have earned low returns.
- The U.S. credit-bureau business has seen its margin roughly halve (42.7% in 2017 to 22.9% in 2025) while TransUnion's rose, and a supplier, FICO, has raised its prices.
- The analysis could not decide whether management can rebuild its systems and grow Workforce Solutions enough to justify the price.
