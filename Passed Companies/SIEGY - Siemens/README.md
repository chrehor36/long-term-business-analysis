## What it does

Siemens runs factory automation and industrial software (Digital Industries), electrical grid and building technology (Smart Infrastructure), and a majority stake of 67% to 79% in medical-technology maker Healthineers. Over FY2021 to FY2025, these three parts produced 34%, 29% and 28% of its industrial segment profit. The US-traded shares are depositary shares, each representing half of one Siemens ordinary share.

## Why it passed

- The largest part's position has stood for a full decade against a named rival's own filings.
- Its operations earn high returns on the capital they need and reinvest little to grow.
- Smart Infrastructure's segment profit rose from 1,743 million euros in FY2021 to 4,506 million in FY2025.
- No doubt about the honesty of present management arose; legacy proceedings from 1992 to 2011 are recorded.

## Why we are not buying at today's price

The analysis's central value range is EUR 127.03 to 237.70 per US-traded share (about USD 142 to 266, at an exchange rate it flagged as approximate), against a price of USD 153.36. The price does not clear the framework's bar: the best case earns about 6% a year, and no growth about 3.6%, against about 10% required. The 30-year euro rate is 3.78%.

## What to watch

- Digital Industries' segment profit fell from 4,947 million euros in FY2023 to 2,643 million in FY2025, and the analysis flagged the narrowing and an AI threat to its software half.
- It has made serial acquisitions at full prices (such as Altair and Dotmatics in 2025) that have not yet shown a decent return, and its 2026 buyback was above the bottom of the value range.
- It features an adjusted profit figure and gives earnings guidance.
