## What it does

Sherwin-Williams makes paint and coatings and sells most of the architectural paint itself, through 4,853 company-operated stores in the US, Canada and the Caribbean. These mainly serve painting contractors and do-it-yourself homeowners. It also sells brands such as Valspar, Minwax and Krylon through home centres and dealers, and industrial coatings worldwide.

## Why it passed

- Its paint stores made $3.06 billion of pre-tax income on $13.6 billion of sales in 2025, a 22.5% margin, and the store margin has widened from 13% to 22%.
- A competitor, PPG, ran paint stores that earned only 1% to 4% and sold them at a loss, which shows how hard the model is to copy.
- Its prices rose in every year the analysis read, which indicates pricing power.
- Before tax it earned very high returns on the tangible capital it needs: 71% in 2018, for example (84% before amortizing acquired intangibles).

## Why we are not buying at today's price

At $318.46 a share the price is above the top of the analysis's value range of $140 to $225. The expected return at that price is about 3% to 5% a year before tax, below the 5.66% a 30-year US Treasury bond pays, and the price already assumes about 10% a year cash growth.

## What to watch

- It has bought back shares at prices above the analysis's value range, and partly with borrowed money in 2026.
- Its company reports lean on EBITDA and adjusted earnings, which the analysis treats as flattering.
- Old lead-pigment litigation is named among its risks, and $1.5 billion of long-term debt was due within a year.
