## What it does

NiSource delivers gas to about 2.4 million customers in five states through Columbia Operations. NIPSCO supplies gas (about 0.9 million customers) and electricity (about 0.5 million) in northern Indiana. A new unit is building power generation for data-centre customers, including an Amazon contract and a later Alphabet contract.

## Why it passed

- Its gas and electric networks are franchises, so customers have no other supplier.
- Operating income rose by $828.4 million from 2021 to 2025, about 7.7% a year before tax on the extra capital invested.
- Two large data-centre customers have signed contracts, which adds new electricity demand.
- Credit ratings are investment grade (BBB+ at S&P, Baa2 at Moody's, BBB at Fitch), all stable.

## Why we are not buying at today's price

A regulator caps what a utility may earn, so the price is far above what that capped return justifies. At $43.69 a share the cash the business produces is only 3.5% of the price, less than the 5.29% on a 30-year US Treasury bond, and the analysis's value range is only $29.20 to $32.83.

## What to watch

- Debt is $17.4 billion, and it issued $750 million more of junior notes in August 2026; spending averages 3.6 times depreciation.
- A catastrophic gas incident in 2018 has already cost about $1 billion, and Indiana is investigating its returns and cost trackers.
- It has repeatedly sold new shares to fund growth while paying a dividend.
