## What it does

Home Depot runs 2,359 home-improvement stores in the US, Canada and Mexico, selling to do-it-yourself homeowners, homeowners who hire help, and professional contractors. Online orders are 15.9% of sales. It also owns a trade-distribution group (roofing, building products, landscape, pool and HVAC supplies) with over 1,340 locations.

## Why it passed

- Its stores sold about $619 per square foot against $440 for Lowe's, so its fixed costs are spread over about 40% more sales.
- Its gross margin (33.3%) is almost the same as Lowe's (33.5%), so the cost advantage shows up in lower running costs rather than higher prices.
- The store business earned about 45.8% a year before tax on the tangible capital it needs in the latest year.
- The stores make up 98% of operating income, so the business is easy to understand.

## Why we are not buying at today's price

At $281.15 a share the price is above the analysis's value range of $199.08 to $249.02. The expected return at that price is 3.9% to 5.5% a year, below a 30-year US Treasury bond (5.66%), which pays more with no business risk.

## What to watch

- It has $48.8 billion of senior notes and relies partly on short-term commercial paper that it assumes will keep rolling over.
- Its acquisitions in trade distribution earn little so far ($316 million of operating income on $12.7 billion of sales).
- Its recent cash flow was helped by about $730 million of tariff refunds that will not repeat.
