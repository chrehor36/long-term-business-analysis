## What it does

AXIA Energia is the largest electricity generator in Brazil, with 43,872 MW of capacity, much of it hydro. It also owns high-voltage transmission lines whose revenue is set by the regulator and indexed to inflation.

## Why it passed

- The existing transmission network cannot be crossed by a rival with money, and the regulator sets the return it earns.
- Transmission revenue is regulated and inflation-indexed, and the hydro fleet produces a known volume, so ten-year earnings can be pictured within a range.
- The cash statements are clear and no dishonesty was found in management's record.
- Its related-party contracts were reviewed and not found to show wrongdoing.

## Why we are not buying at today's price

A regulator caps what its transmission business may earn, so the price is far above what that capped return justifies. At R$ 59.49 a share the expected return is only 3.6% to 5.1% a year in the central case, below both the 12.87% Brazilian government yield and the roughly 10% the analysis requires.

## What to watch

- The regulator is narrowing allowed returns, and its return on book equity averaged only 6.0% over 2021 to 2025.
- The price of power from its hydro plants cannot be forecast, only bounded.
- Value in US dollars depends on the Brazilian real, and Brazil's own interest rates are very high.
