## What it does

Southwest Gas Holdings delivers natural gas to about 2.28 million customers: 1,224,000 in Arizona, 849,000 in Nevada and 208,000 in California. Over 99% are homes and small businesses. Since it deconsolidated its construction subsidiary Centuri in August 2025, it is solely a gas distribution utility whose rates are set by state commissions.

## Why it passed

- State commissions grant the service territories, and a second gas network under the streets of Las Vegas, Phoenix and Tucson would only recover the same regulated return.
- Operating income has risen every year from $318.6 million in 2021 to $482.4 million in 2025.
- Arizona and Nevada laws protect natural gas: Arizona bars municipal gas bans and Nevada's SB 281 reinforces gas's role.
- Regulators allow it a return on its investment: 9.5% in Nevada, 9.84% in Arizona and 11.16% in California.

## Why we are not buying at today's price

A regulator caps what Southwest Gas may earn, and it has actually earned less than its allowed return every year (6.0% to 8.5% on equity over 2018 to 2025). So the price is far above what that capped return justifies. The analysis values the shares at $63.70 against a price of $92.02, and a buyer would expect only about 3.6% to 4.1% a year, below a 30-year US Treasury bond (5.31%).

## What to watch

- Regulators grant less than it asks: Arizona asked $125.6 million and was granted $80.2 million, and Nevada asked $74.0 million and got $59.1 million.
- California policy (AB 1207) nudges customers toward electric utilities, a long-run threat to that part of the business.
- It has about $3.4 billion of long-term debt, with $1.5 billion maturing between 2026 and 2030, and it sold $955.8 million of new shares in recent years.
