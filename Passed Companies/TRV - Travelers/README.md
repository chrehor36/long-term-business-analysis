## What it does

Travelers sells property and casualty insurance through three segments: Business Insurance, Bond and Specialty Insurance, and Personal Insurance. It collects premiums up front and invests them until claims are paid, holding about $81 billion of such float in mid-2026. It has no life-insurance or other non-insurance business.

## Why it passed

- It made an underwriting profit in every one of ten years, so the float cost less than nothing: an average of -3.06% from 2021 to 2025, against a 30-year US Treasury rate of 3.69%.
- That held through catastrophe years of $2.99 billion to $3.69 billion in 2023 to 2025.
- Its return on equity has been in the mid-teens over five years, earned with an underwriting profit on top of investment income.
- No sign of dishonesty was found, the accounts and reserve tables are clear, and capital not needed is returned to owners.

## Why we are not buying at today's price

At $360.70 a share, the price is at the top of the analysis's value range of $151 to $366. At that price a buyer would earn about 4% a year before growth, below the 5.66% of a 30-year US Treasury bond and far below the roughly 10% the framework requires.

## What to watch

- It is not a structural moat: Travelers is neither the low-cost operator nor the best underwriter against its rivals, and trails Chubb, Berkley and Hartford's commercial business.
- Reserve releases have run high while the company buys back its own stock, and the buybacks name no price and were made above the bottom of the value range.
- Pay depends on a return-on-equity target and adjusted figures, and the 2024 and 2025 accident years have yet to mature as booked.
