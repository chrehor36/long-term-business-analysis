## What it does

Nordson makes equipment, components and spare parts that dispense and control adhesives, coatings, polymers and fluids, along with test and inspection equipment for electronics and single-use medical components. It sells mainly direct in more than 35 countries, 67% of its sales are outside the United States, and no customer is 10% of sales. Its largest part is industrial adhesive dispensing for packaging and product assembly.

## Why it passed

- The deciding part, industrial adhesive dispensing, kept its position across a seventeen-year segment record that includes the 2009 recession and the inflation of 2021 to 2023.
- Its rivals also earn high returns, so this is not an ordinary field, and the threats the filings name (Asian copies, a well-funded rival) are not shown to be filling the moat.
- The core business needs little capital: capital spending was $34.6 million to $64.4 million a year against depreciation of $49.1 million to $71.3 million.
- The accounts are clean, bad quarters are reported as bad, and guidance is cut when missed.

## Why we are not buying at today's price

At $328.17 a share against a value range of $151.16 to $248.42, the price is above the top. A buyer would earn less than the framework requires, and the 30-year US Treasury pays 5.60%. The price needs the acquired growth to come free.

## What to watch

- Most of its growth was bought: organic sales changed +10.8% in FY2022, then -1.4%, -2.5% and -2.5%, under 1% a year compounded.
- The capital added through acquisitions (about 957 million euros for ARAG and $790 million for Atrion) earns about 8% after tax, far below the core's return.
- Its buybacks name no price and were made above the analysis's value range, and the filings do not say how much of sales comes from parts and consumables.
