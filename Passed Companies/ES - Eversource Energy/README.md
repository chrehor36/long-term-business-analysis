## What it does

Eversource delivers electricity and gas through regulated utilities in Connecticut, Massachusetts and New Hampshire, including about 1.32 million customers at Connecticut Light & Power. It owns the transmission lines and local wires and earns a regulator-allowed return on them. The cost of the power it supplies is passed through without a profit.

## Why it passed

- No competitor can take over its existing transmission and distribution wires, so the franchise stands.
- Its earnings are written into regulatory orders, so their shape ten years out can be foreseen.
- Allowed returns are stated in the filings, for example 9.5% on 50% equity at its New Hampshire electric utility.
- No dishonesty was found in the filings.

## Why we are not buying at today's price

A regulator caps what Eversource may earn, so even at a price below the analysis's value range ($71.73 to $81.97 against $67.84), the return is only about 6.45% to 5.52% a year, not the roughly 10% the analysis requires. The 30-year US Treasury bond pays 5.29%, so the extra payment for the risk is thin.

## What to watch

- The regulatory climate is the main risk, and a federal ruling on the allowed return on transmission cut earnings.
- It recorded an offshore wind liability and has about $28 billion of net debt.
- The state denied an earlier sale of its water business, which closed later; management's ability was weighed against.
