## What it does

DTE Energy runs DTE Electric and DTE Gas, regulated utilities serving customers in Michigan, plus smaller businesses such as an energy trading unit and a metallurgical coke plant. Prices are set by the Michigan regulator in rate cases.

## Why it passed

- Its distribution franchise cannot be duplicated by a rival, and Michigan caps customers' ability to buy from other suppliers at 10% of retail sales.
- The allowed return on equity has held steady at 9.9% for the electric utility and 9.8% for the gas utility.
- Its 2026 to 2030 plan is $30 billion at the electric utility and $4.5 billion at the gas utility, which grows the capital base it earns on.
- Two data-center customers (1.4 GW and 1.0 GW) have minimum charges, termination fees and parent credit support.

## Why we are not buying at today's price

A regulator caps what this utility may earn, so the price is far above what that capped return justifies. The analysis values the shares at $114.06 to $123.78 against a price of $129.73, and a buyer at that price would earn about 4.8% to 5.2% a year, below the 5.43% a 30-year US Treasury bond pays.

## What to watch

- Debt is 69.4% of capital, and 2026 capital spending of about $6.8 billion exceeds cash from operations of about $3.9 billion, so the gap must be borrowed or raised from shareholders.
- The regulator granted $242M and a 9.9% return in the last electric rate case, against the $574M and 10.75% requested.
- Its coke plant faces federal clean-air violation notices, and coal still supplies most of its own electric generation until 2032.
