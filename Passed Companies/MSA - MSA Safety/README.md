## What it does

MSA Safety makes gas and flame detectors, breathing apparatus for fire services, and protective gear such as hard hats. Its 2025 sales were about $1.9 billion. Its products are certified to safety standards, and the Americas region carries about 80% of its segment profit.

## Why it passed

- Customers buy certified life-safety devices and a fire department standardizes on one breathing-apparatus platform and trains on it, so they do not switch on price.
- It earns about 53% a year before tax on the operating capital it needs, a figure that has stayed between 41% and 53% for nearly a decade.
- Operating margin rose from 14.3% in 2016 to 19.8% in 2025, with gross margin at 46.5%.
- Rivals with deep pockets did not win: Honeywell wrote down and sold its protective equipment business, and 3M bought a business instead of building one.

## Why we are not buying at today's price

The analysis values the shares at $84.50 to $137.29 against a price of $180.71, above the top of the range. A buyer at that price would expect about 4.5% a year, or 3.7% with half the growth, less than a 30-year US Treasury bond (5.60%).

## What to watch

- It still has product-liability claims from past cumulative-trauma cases, about 21,372 claims at mid-2026 with no reserve set aside, and defense costs are expensed as they arise.
- Net debt is $954.7 million after buying Autronica, about 2.0 times adjusted earnings before interest, tax and depreciation.
- The International segment earns only a 15.2% margin against 28.9% in the Americas.
