## What it does

Hyatt licenses its hotel brands and manages hotels for owners, collecting fees as a share of hotel revenue under long contracts, along with a reservation and loyalty system. It has sold most of the hotels it used to own. The system had 373,000 rooms at the end of 2025, up from 223,000 in 2019.

## Why it passed

- Its fee system needs little of its own capital, and the room count grew about 8.9% a year from 2019 to 2025, faster than Marriott (4.3%) or Hilton (5.6%).
- Fees are paid as a share of hotel revenue under long contracts, so revenue is steadier than the hotel business itself.
- Gross fees almost doubled from $608 million in 2019 to $1,198 million in 2025, though part of that came from acquisitions.
- It has a pipeline of about 148,000 more rooms.

## Why we are not buying at today's price

The price was $162.92. The analysis's central value range is about -$3 to $40 a share, and even a more generous version tops out at $125. A buyer at that price would earn only about 2% a year on the cash produced, against 5.56% on a 30-year US Treasury bond.

## What to watch

- It spent $5.1 billion on acquisitions in five years (mostly offset by hotel sales), yet cash left for owners did not rise.
- It pays 12.8% of gross fees as incentive payments to win and keep hotel contracts, double what Marriott and Hilton pay.
- The cash left for owners is only about 7% of gross fees, against 44% at Marriott and 51% at Hilton, and online platforms and short-term rentals are a named threat.
