## What it does

Dominion Energy sells electricity under exclusive franchises in Virginia and South Carolina, and also sells natural gas. Dominion Energy Virginia produced 73% of its segment net income over 2021 to 2025. Regulators set the return it may earn on the plant it builds, and its Virginia plan calls for $55.8 billion of capital spending over 2026 to 2030.

## Why it passed

- Its earning power is an allowed return on a filed capital plan, so it can be foreseen: the Virginia regulator authorized a 9.80% return on equity from 2025.
- Its territory is an exclusive retail franchise that competitors cannot enter.
- The analysis found no deception in its filings, a pay plan that fell when relative results fell, and a board that bargained with two bidders.
- The business is easy to understand: the same territory, a larger plant base, and a return set by commissions.

## Why we are not buying at today's price

A regulator caps what Dominion may earn, so the price is far above what that capped return justifies. At $63.56 a share against a value range of $26.14 to $38.39, even the best case earns only about 3.3% a year on the cash the business produces, less than a 30-year US Treasury bond (5.43%).

## What to watch

- It has signed a deal to be combined with NextEra Energy; on that deal a Dominion share is worth about the same as on its own (the analysis's no-growth figure was $37.81 against $38.39).
- Debt rose from $35.1 billion to $48.9 billion (2015 to 2025) and is 62.7% of its capital; pre-tax earnings covered interest only 2.8 times in 2025.
- In Virginia a review showed it earned 7.77% against an authorized 9.70%, and it has repeatedly asked owners for new equity, issuing shares at $55 to $61 after buying back at about $79.
