## What it does

Visa runs VisaNet, a network that approves, clears and settles card payments between banks and sellers in more than 200 countries and territories. It does not issue cards or lend money, so it takes no credit risk. It earns fees on payment volume, on each transaction, on cross-border payments, and on added services.

## Why it passed

- It needs almost no tangible capital, and each added dollar of capital has earned more than its own amount every year.
- A rival with money and its own cards carries only about 3% of Visa's volume, and the customers who sue Visa keep accepting its cards as it grows.
- It has raised its prices over the period the analysis read, and its volume record is long and steady.
- No sign of dishonesty toward owners was found in the reports or proxy.

## Why we are not buying at today's price

At $369.71 a share, the price is inside the value range of $167 to $377 and 2% below the top. A buyer would earn about 2.6% to 5.8% a year after tax, below a 30-year US Treasury bond's 5.66% in most cases and well below the roughly 10% the framework requires. The price implies the cash growing about 10% a year for ten years just to match the bond.

## What to watch

- The US Department of Justice sued Visa in September 2024 for monopolizing debit network services.
- Regulators cap the fees on its network in the EU, Australia, New Zealand and parts of Latin America, and European claims were filed in 2026; growth beyond volume has come from price, which is contested.
- It has bought back shares near the top of the value range with no stated price, and it features adjusted figures that leave out recurring costs.
