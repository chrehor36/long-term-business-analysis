## What it does

McDonald's franchises about 45,000 restaurants, of which 2,039 it runs itself. It owns or leases the land and buildings and collects rent and a royalty on sales: franchised revenues were $16.5 billion in 2025, of which rents were $10.4 billion and royalties $6.0 billion. Systemwide sales were $139.4 billion.

## Why it passed

- Its cash is a share of what 45,000 restaurants sell, fixed by franchise agreements that usually run 20 years, with $31.5 billion of future minimum rents already contracted.
- Its sites and drive-thrus cannot be copied with money alone, and the brand travels abroad.
- It earns a high return on the capital it needs, and the increment still earns about 23% before tax.
- No doubt about the honesty of management was raised by the documents read.

## Why we are not buying at today's price

At $233.04 a share against a value range of $164 to $195, the price is above the top. A buyer would earn about 4.0% to 4.8% a year after tax on the cash the business produces, less than a 30-year US Treasury bond (5.66%). The analysis says the business is not the reason; the price is.

## What to watch

- Guest counts in the US are falling, and a rival reported an 8.5% quarter.
- Capital spending is up 43% in two years and it announced $8.5 billion of support for franchisees, which has kept the cash the owners get flat for four years.
- It has bought back shares, partly with borrowed money, at prices above any value the analysis can support, with no stated price discipline.
