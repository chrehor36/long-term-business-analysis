## What it does

Evergy owns three regulated electric utilities that generate, transmit and deliver power to about 1.7 million customers in Kansas and Missouri. Revenue was $5.96 billion in 2025, with homes at 37%, businesses at 33% and industry at 11%. State regulators set retail prices, and fuel costs are passed through to customers.

## Why it passed

- The company does not compete for customers in its territories, and in exchange its terms of service are regulated by the states.
- No one can build a second distribution grid into its territory, and its $27.4 billion of power plant and wires could not be duplicated.
- Its return on the capital it uses was around 7.5% in 2021, a steady regulated return.
- Fuel and purchased-power cost swings are almost entirely passed on to customers.

## Why we are not buying at today's price

A regulator caps what this utility may earn, so the price is far above what that capped return justifies. At $84.95 a share the price is above the top of the analysis's value range of $41.25 to $65.33, and the best case earns about 4.07% a year, against 5.29% on a 30-year US Treasury bond.

## What to watch

- Every price rise is a contested case: its Kansas request of $196 million ended at $128 million, and a Missouri decision is due January 2027.
- Debt was $16.5 billion in mid-2026, about $1.8 billion of long-term debt falls due in 2027, and pre-tax earnings covered interest only about 2.5 times in 2025.
- Wildfire insurance may not be enough, and Missouri or Kansas could pass retail-choice laws that would force write-offs.
