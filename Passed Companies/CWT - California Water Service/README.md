## What it does

California Water Service pumps or buys water and pipes it to about 562,000 connections. 91.2% of its revenue comes from its California districts, with small operations in Washington, New Mexico, Hawaii and Texas. The California Public Utilities Commission sets the prices it may charge.

## Why it passed

- State law bars any other private water company from entering its territory unless service there is found to be deficient.
- The pipes in the street cannot practically be duplicated, and no substitute for piped water is named in its annual report.
- The regulator has allowed a steady 10.27% return on equity, with 53.4% equity in the capital structure, unchanged since 2023.
- It plans $580 to $640 million of investment in 2026, which grows the base on which it earns its allowed return.

## Why we are not buying at today's price

A regulator caps what this utility may earn, so the price is far above what that capped return justifies. At $47.82 a share the price is about double the analysis's value range of $22.60 to $27.66, and even the best case earns only 4.19% a year, against 5.30% on a 30-year US Treasury bond.

## What to watch

- Spending on pipes ($517 million in 2025) is far above depreciation and above what the business generates, so it keeps borrowing; debt was about 3.1 times its earnings before interest and tax over interest in 2025.
- California holds utilities strictly liable for damage in some cases, even without fault, and land-movement lawsuits are pending.
- A public agency could take over the system by eminent domain for just compensation, though only one small case was pending.
