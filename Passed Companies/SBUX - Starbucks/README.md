## What it does

Starbucks runs and licenses coffeehouses, mainly in North America (about 70% of its profit over five years), and also internationally. It sells packaged coffee and ready-to-drink products through a long-term licence with Nestle. It sold its China operations to a partner in March 2026.

## Why it passed

- North America is 70% of segment profit and has recovered from each downturn: its margin fell by half and returned when customer traffic came back.
- Its operating income was 62% to 79% of net store property in FY2021 to FY2024, a high return on the physical capital it needs.
- The Nestle licence brings in steady profit at margins of 44% to 52% with little capital.
- Customers pay in advance through stored-value cards, so working capital is negative.

## Why we are not buying at today's price

The analysis puts the central value at $45.46 to $99.49 a share against a price of $90.75, in the upper half of the range. The expected return at that price is about 6.0% in the growth case, which does not reach the roughly 10% the analysis requires, and the cash produced is only 3.16% of the price against 5.60% on a 30-year US Treasury bond.

## What to watch

- Operating income fell from $5.4 billion in FY2024 to $2.9 billion in FY2025, and the return on net store property dropped from 79% to 35%.
- In China a rival, Luckin Coffee, grew to 30,888 stores, and Starbucks sold its China business.
- Shareholder equity is negative (-$7.7 billion) after buybacks that named no price limit; it has $13.3 billion of borrowings and about $12.4 billion of lease obligations.
