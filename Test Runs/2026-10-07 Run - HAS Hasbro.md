## Q1 — CAN I UNDERSTAND IT? Where its economics will be in ten years, inside my perimeter. STOP.
**The test.** Understanding is "a reasonable fix on about what the earning power and competitive position will look like in five or 10 years" **[M2012-065]**. Hasbro reports three segments with different economics (10-K FY2025 Note 22): Wizards of the Coast and Digital Gaming (Magic: The Gathering, Dungeons & Dragons, digital and licensed games), Consumer Products (toys and board games sold to retailers, plus out-licensing), and Entertainment (Peppa Pig and other family content, after the eOne film and TV sale). Separate parts, because "lumping them together [...] impedes analysis" **[L2008-005]**; by Q1's holding-company paragraph, as extended on 2026-10-06, a part matters when it decides under Q2's business-of-parts rule (more than half of five-year operating profit, or else the largest).

**Which part matters.** Segment operating profit 2021 to 2025 (USD millions; FY2023 10-K Note 21 for 2021 to 2023, FY2025 10-K Note 22 for 2024 and 2025): Wizards 547.0, 538.3, 525.7, 632.0, 1,006.8 = 3,249.8; Consumer Products 401.4, 217.3, (64.7), 115.3, (942.6) = (273.3), or 748.6 before the 2025 goodwill impairment of $1,021.9M; Entertainment (91.8), 22.7, (1,911.5), (1.6), 0.4; Corporate and Other (661.4); company total 333.3. Wizards carries more than half of five-year operating profit on any reading, before or after the impairments. **Wizards is the deciding part; Consumer Products and Entertainment do not decide** (CONVENTION of Q2, the business of parts, applied as written).

**The deciding part's key variables, and whether they are foreseeable.**
- *What it is.* Wizards' 2025 revenue was $2,186.9M: Tabletop Gaming $1,686.6M, Digital and Licensed Gaming $500.3M (10-K FY2025 Item 7). Magic alone was $1,720.1M (Q4 2025 release, `0000046080-26-000006`), about four fifths of the segment. Magic is a trading card game first sold in 1993; the company states that it "grew for its fourth consecutive year and has now grown in 12 of the last 13 years" through 2021 (Q4 2021 release, `0000046080-22-000012`); the FY2018 10-K records the one decline, in 2017. Then: 2022 $1,065.2M (+7%), 2023 $1,085.8M (+2%), 2024 $1,078.6M (-1%), 2025 $1,720.1M (+59%), and tabletop revenue up 32% again in the first half of 2026 ($989.0M against $750.1M; 10-Q).
- *Variable 1, the players' continued engagement and spend.* This is consumer behaviour, the kind of forecast the speakers place inside the circle: "we know what we think we can project out in terms of consumer behavior and threats to a business" **[M2023-030]**; of Apple, "much more of a consumer products business, in terms of [...] analyzing moats around it, and consumer behavior" **[M2017-019]**. Thirty-three years of a paper game surviving the arrival of digital gaming is evidence that the behaviour is stable in kind.
- *Variable 2, the set cadence, the supply decisions and the price.* Set by Wizards itself; not disclosed in units or prices in any filing read. The pending class action is about exactly this (statements about Magic's growth and a "segmentation" strategy; 10-Q Part II Item 1).
- *Variable 3, borrowed brands.* The 2025 record came from "Universes Beyond sets such as Final Fantasy, Avatar: The Last Airbender, and Marvel's Spider-Man", for which royalties are paid; Wizards' royalty line went from $42.3M (2024) to $116.2M (2025) (10-K FY2025 Note 22).
- *Variable 4, digital.* Internal AAA studios (EXODUS and WARLOCK, expected 2027), licensed mobile games (MONOPOLY GO! $168.0M of 2025 revenue) and Baldur's Gate 3. The filer itself says "the pace of change in product offerings and consumer tastes in electronics and digital gaming areas is potentially even greater than for our other products" (10-K FY2025 Item 1A); titles for 2028 and later were cancelled and $56.4M impaired in Q2 2026 (10-Q). This is technology and hit-driven; it is about a quarter of the deciding part's revenue and is not its engine.

**Routing.** The deciding part is not a business "that must deal with fast-moving technology" **[L1993-023]** in its core: its engine is a paper game and a hobby-store and collector community. The fast-moving piece (internal digital games) is a minority inside the part, and the filer is now cutting it. The toy business, where the speakers "can't determine whether we are dealing with a "pet rock" or a "Barbie."" **[L1993-023]**, is real in Consumer Products, but Consumer Products does not decide. Would the insiders write the forecast down? The test asks whether they "would not want to put down on paper their predictions" **[M2000-105]**; Hasbro's management did put five-year plans and margin targets on paper in 2022 (Q3 2022 release `0000046080-22-000119`, "our plan to drive accelerated growth and profit over the next five years"), which is a habit weighed at Q4, but it is also evidence that the insiders do not regard the forecast as unwritable.

**How far off could I be.** "we also have some model in our mind of how far off we can be" **[M2011-084]**. Far, on the level: Wizards' operating profit moved 59% in one year (2024 to 2025) on licensed sets. Not far, on the kind: a high-margin franchise game whose competitive position in ten years is very likely to be among the leading collectible card games. The doubt is about the level of earning power, which Q7's range carries; it is not a doubt that the economics are of a kind I can understand. I record it plainly against the perimeter row, "if you have doubts about something being into your circle of competence, it isn’t" **[M2002-092]**: the doubt I hold is about how much, not about what, and the run carries it to Q2's castle tests and to Q7.

- **VERDICT: IN** (the deciding part, Wizards, is understood as a consumer franchise with a 33-year record whose key variables are consumer behaviour and the company's own supply decisions, **[M2012-065]**, **[M2023-030]**, **[M2017-019]**; the fast-moving digital piece and the fad-prone toy business do not decide). Doubts carried forward: the level of Magic's earning power, the borrowed brands, the supply decisions under litigation.

**The castle tests on the deciding part (Wizards, which is mostly Magic):**
1. *The key factors and their permanence* **[M1995-038]**. Magic is an owned rules system with a card pool built since 1993, played in hobby stores and collected; the filer names its dependence on "specialty hobby stores" and on its direct channels (Secret Lair, Magic: The Gathering Arena, D&D Beyond) (10-K FY2025 Items 1 and 1A). The evidence of permanence is the record: one down year (2017) in 2009 to 2021 by the company's own count, flat 2022 to 2024, a record 2025, and a further rise in the first half of 2026. Leadership alone is not the answer the test wants, since "Leadership alone provides no certainties" **[L1996-031]**; the answer here is the owned game and its community, which a rival cannot buy.
2. *Without the lord.* The filer says Magic and D&D "depend on a limited number of highly skilled creative and technical personnel" (Item 1A). That is a design staff, not a superstar of the kind the row excludes, "if a business requires a superstar to produce great results, the business itself cannot be deemed great" **[L2007-006]**; and the franchise has run under several Wizards presidents (the current one leaves on 2026-09-01, 8-K `0000046080-26-000047`). Passes, with the staff noted.
3. *The money test* **[M2011-015]**. Could a rival with money take it? The filings name no rival, and the other collectible-card-game publishers are not SEC filers (Japanese listed companies and private firms); their figures could not be read. What the filings do show is the defender's result: Magic grew 59% in 2025 and tabletop grew 32% in the first half of 2026. The test cannot be run on the attacker's side from the record available; it is answered only by the defender's results.
4. *Pricing power and the agony* **[M2005-020]**. No set prices or unit volumes are disclosed in any filing read. The margin held between 36.1% and 46.4% through 2021 to 2023, the years of general price inflation. The agony is visible once: Q3 2022, Wizards' adjusted operating profit fell 31% on "lower revenue as well as higher allowances, promotional activity and costs associated with higher inventory levels" (release `0000046080-22-000119`), and it recovered.
5. *Unit volume and share of mind* **[M1997-099]**, **[M1999-054]**. Not measurable from the filings: revenue by brand is disclosed from 2022 only, units never. The rows measure the castle "by unit cases sold"; this run cannot, and says so.
6. *The low-cost position.* Not the castle here; a brand and game business.
7. *The brand in the customer's mind* **[M2015-038]**. Magic is owned and "the brand has to stand for something in the consumer’s mind". Against it: the record year was carried by other owners' brands (Final Fantasy, Spider-Man, Avatar, Lord of the Rings), with Wizards' royalties up from $42.3M to $116.2M in a year (10-K FY2025 Note 22). Some of the 2025 castle is borrowed.
8. *Would the customer choose it over the low bid.* A Magic player cannot buy a cheaper Magic; the substitute is another game, which the filings do not measure.
9. *Ask the competitors.* Not possible from filings.
10. *Widening or narrowing* **[M1999-108]**, **[L2005-010]**. On the filed results the moat widened: revenue nearly tripled from 2019 to 2025 at a steady margin. Against it, the class action alleges that statements attributing Magic's growth to a consumer-driven "segmentation" strategy were false or misleading (10-Q Part II Item 1); unproven, and the company "intends to vigorously defend". If true, part of the growth was product proliferation sold to the same players, which is harvesting rather than widening. The run cannot decide it from filings; it is carried to Q5 as an integrity fact and to Q7 as a reason for a wide range.
11. *What could destroy, modify or reduce it* **[M2000-014]**. Oversupply that erodes what collectors hold; a rival game; digital substitution (the filer's own Arena exists and paper still grew); loss of key licences. None is shown happening on the filed evidence.
