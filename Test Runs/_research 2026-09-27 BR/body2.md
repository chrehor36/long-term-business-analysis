## Q2 — IS IT A FRANCHISE? **[E3-03]**

*Every Broadridge figure below is read from its filed 10-Ks for fiscal 2007 to 2026 (accessions in Step 0), or from SEC companyfacts checked against those statements. Peer figures are each peer's own SEC companyfacts (10-K annual facts, newest filed vintage). Scripts and outputs, all in `Test Runs/_research 2026-09-27 BR/`: `xb.py` (`xb_out.txt`), `peers/row.py` (copied from the NGVC run, construction unchanged; `peers/<TICKER>_row.txt`), `peers/summary_out.txt`, `riskfactors.txt`, `mdna_a.txt`.*

**The hypotheses I formed while reading, both stated so they can be attacked [E4-26].** *(a) The one company almost every US broker and bank has appointed, by power of attorney, to reach the beneficial owners of their clients' shares: an unduplicated network carrying over 1 billion equity proxy positions a year, earning 49-137% pre-tax on its tangible operating capital in every year since fiscal 2011, growing with the number of positions (+12% in fiscal 2026) and needing little capital to do it. Customers have no close substitute and the returns demonstrate it [E3-43].* *(b) The price of that one service is written in the exchange's rules and changes only when a committee reviews it and the SEC approves; the other roughly 70% of the fee revenue (fund data, issuer services, customer statements, trade processing and wealth platforms) is sold in markets the filer calls "highly competitive", against clients' own departments, with price concessions at renewal as a stated historical rate. The high return belongs to the regime on the first part and does not exist on the evidence of pricing for the second.* The file does not get to pick. Each is tested on the filings below.

**Where the money is, by product line (fiscal 2026 10-K, Note 3, *"Regulatory | $ | 1,434.9 | $ | 1,280.6 | $ | 1,195.6"*, 2026/2025/2024).** Fee revenue (recurring plus event-driven) **$5,226.1M**, of which ICS Regulatory recurring **$1,434.9M (27.5%)**, ICS event-driven $348.1M (6.7%; proxy contests, mutual-fund proxies, corporate actions), Data-Driven Fund Solutions, Issuer ($294.8M) and Customer Communications ($752.8M) together $1,527.2M (29.2%), and GTO **$1,916.0M (36.7%)** (Capital markets $1,184.2M, Wealth and investment management $731.8M). Distribution revenue of **$2,250.6M** is mostly postage passed through at cost. **Segment pre-tax earnings are not split by product line**; ICS earned $1,103.5M (19.8% of its revenue) and GTO $297.8M (15.5%).

**[E3-03], criterion by criterion.** *"An economic franchise arises from a product or service that: (1) is needed or desired; (2) is thought by its customers to have no close substitute and; (3) is not subject to price regulation."*
- **(1) Needed or desired [x]: passes.** Proxy delivery and vote collection are required by SEC rules for every listed company holding a meeting; trade processing is required by every broker that trades.
- **(3) Not subject to price regulation: FAILS for the core, in the filer's own words, in every 10-K on the record.**
  - Fiscal 2026, Item 1, Regulation: *"SEC rules require public companies to reimburse banks and broker-dealers for the expense of distributing certain stockholder communications to beneficial owners of securities held in street name, and those reimbursement rates, as well as fees that can be charged for proxy services are set by the NYSE."* Item 1, Regulatory Solutions: *"The reimbursement rates are set forth in the rules of self-regulatory organizations (“SROs”), including the New York Stock Exchange (“NYSE”). We bill public companies for the proxy services performed, collect the fees, and remit to the Nominee its portion of the fees. In addition, the NYSE rules establish fees for certain services provided by intermediaries, such as Broadridge, in the proxy process."* Traced year by year in the twenty downloaded 10-Ks: the reimbursement rates are stated to be set in the SRO rules in **every 10-K from fiscal 2007 to 2026**; the NYSE's fees for intermediaries *"such as Broadridge"* are named from **fiscal 2012** (*"the NYSE rules establish fees specifically for the services provided by intermediaries in the proxy process such as Broadridge"*); the plainest wording, *"set by the NYSE"*, from **fiscal 2023**. Before 2012 the filer described its fee as a share of the regulated reimbursement: fiscal 2008, *"We bill public companies on behalf of the nominees, collect the fee and remit to the nominee any difference between the fee that the nominee is entitled to collect and the amount that the nominee has agreed to pay us for our services."*
  - **How the price moves when it moves: by regulatory process, not by the seller.** Fiscal 2011: *"In September 2010, the NYSE formed the Proxy Fee Advisory Committee made up of issuers, broker-dealers and investors to review the NYSE proxy distribution fees."* Fiscal 2012: *"In May 2012, the Proxy Fee Advisory Committee issued its recommendations on the fees paid by issuers for the distribution of proxy materials to beneficial shareholders."* Fiscal 2013: *"The proposed changes in fees are subject to SEC approval."* Fiscal 2014: *"In fiscal year 2014, the SEC approved the proxy distribution fee changes proposed by the NYSE in May 2012. The fee changes went into effect for shareholder meetings with record dates on or after January 1, 2014, and we have implemented the approved changes."* Three and a half years from review to effect, decided by a committee of the payers and the regulator. That is price regulation in the form [E3-03] excludes; it is not a company that *"regularly price[s] its product or service aggressively"* **[E3-43]**.
  - **The regime is live, and it can move demand as well as price.** Fiscal 2026 Item 1A: *"Changes in laws and regulations could require changes in the services we provide, the manner in which we provide our services, and the fees we charge for our services, or they could result in a reduction or elimination of the demand for our services"*; and on the SEC's proposal of 2026-07-16: *"If the rule is adopted as proposed, the volume of physically delivered communications we process would decrease. While the volume of electronic delivery of communications we process is expected to increase, our recurring revenue growth and distribution revenues will decrease."*
- **(2) Thought by its customers to have no close substitute: holds for the regulated core; FAILS for the rest, in the filer's words and on its pricing record.**
  - **The core.** The filer describes a *"unique, central, and integral role in ensuring that the beneficial proxy process occurs without issue"* and names no rival in beneficial proxy distribution in twenty 10-Ks (every competitor paragraph lists categories: *"independent proxy distribution service providers, transfer agents, proxy advisory firms, proxy solicitation firms, firms that process proxy votes, and financial printers"*). I record criterion 2 as met for this line; it does not rescue the line, because criterion 3 fails on it.
  - **The rest, the filer's words, fiscal 2026:** *"We operate in a highly competitive industry."* *"Within our Global Technology and Operations business, our capital markets solutions compete with in-house operations as well as vendors that provide trade processing, back-office record keeping, and sell-side order and execution management systems."* *"We also face competition from numerous firms in the compiling, printing and electronic distribution of statements, bills and other customer communications."* Item 1A: *"Our competitors may be able to respond more quickly to new or changing opportunities, technologies, and client requirements and may be able to undertake more extensive promotional activities, offer more attractive terms to clients and adopt more aggressive pricing policies than we will be able to offer or adopt."*
  - **The rest, the pricing record.** Fiscal 2009 10-K: *"In connection with the recent renewal and extension of some contracts with our Securities Processing Solutions clients, the increased pricing pressure has resulted in a higher than historical average rate of price concessions. These contract renewal price concessions are estimated to be approximately 5% of our annual Securities Processing Solutions segment’s revenues, compared with the historical average rate of 3% of the annual Securities Processing Solutions segment’s revenues."* Fiscal 2010: segment revenues *"a decrease of $23.0 million, or 4%"*, *"driven by the fiscal year 2009 carryover impact of client losses and price concessions"*. Fiscal 2015 (GTO): *"lower internal growth (-1 pt) due to contract renewals"*; fiscal 2016: *"internal growth ... partially offset by contract renewals (2 pts)"*; fiscal 2017: *"partially offset by contract renewals (1 pt)"*. A business whose own filings carry a **standing rate of price given back at renewal** fails **[E2-44]**'s first characteristic (*"an ability to increase prices rather easily ... without fear of significant loss of either market share or unit volume"*) on the filer's words.

- **Returns, read against [E3-46]:** the tangible return is high in every year (49-137% pre-tax), which is the strongest single fact for hypothesis (a) and is weighed below. **It is not, by itself, [E3-43]'s demonstration**, because [E3-43] joins the return to the pricing (*"regularly price ... aggressively and thereby to earn"*): on the core the price is the exchange's, and on the rest the filings show concessions. A high return on little tangible capital earned by an agent inside an administered fee schedule is the case **[E2-59]** describes: prices *"administered in some manner and thereby insulated at least partially from normal market forces"*, *"legally through government intervention"*. The framework's reading of that row governs (Section III, Q2; its emphasis dropped): *"Regulation caps a franchise ([E3-03] criterion 3) and floors a commodity business; neither creates the class."*
- **Return including goodwill, the acquirer's return:** 32.2% (2016) to **11.9% (2022)**, 19.4% (2026): the $4,378.2M of cash paid for businesses in fiscal 2017-2026 (Itiviti $2,603.6M in fiscal 2021) earns far less than the base. That is a Q3 matter (the Pro-Am effect, [E2-56]) and is carried there if the file reaches it.
- **Units, read against [E4-55]:** the physical series the filer publishes is positions and trades: *"Equity positions | 16 | % | 16 | %"*, *"Equity revenue positions | 12 | % | 12 | %"*, *"Mutual fund / ETF positions | 6 | % | 7 | %"*, *"Internal Trade Growth | 15 | % | 13 | %"* (fiscal 2026/2025). **Regulatory recurring revenue rose 12% with equity revenue positions up 12%**: the growth is volume at the administered price, which is [E2-44]'s second characteristic (volume with little capital) met and the first (price) not tested, because the seller cannot set it.

- **Peers taken: seven.** Not measurable from any SEC filing: **the beneficial-proxy rival or rivals the filer's category list implies** (none named in twenty 10-Ks), Computershare (transfer agency; ASX), Equiniti/EQ, ISS (Deutsche Börse), ION and InvestCloud (private), and the clients' in-house departments, which the filer names as its main competitor in trade processing. **The verdict below rests on the filer's own words about its price (criterion 3) and its own renewal-concession record (criterion 2), not on relative position, so the class is not held PROVISIONAL** (the MRK, MDT, KVUE, CHD and NGVC precedent).
- **What the row shows.** Broadridge's tangible return is in the same band as the processors and the financial printer (62-75% against DFIN 70-87%, SS&C 86-88%, Fiserv 66-74%): **high tangible returns on little tangible capital are a feature of this whole class of outsourced financial processing, including Donnelley Financial, whose sales have fallen 2.7% a year for nine years.** A high tangible return in this industry does not by itself mark the company that has a franchise. Broadridge's return including goodwill (15.7-19.4%) sits above SS&C, Fiserv and FIS and below SEI, DFIN and FactSet. **The row's limit [E3-61]:** it shows position, not conduct.
- **The low-cost door [E2-58]:** not claimed by the filer and not shown by the row; the door is in any case the commodity business's door, and the core here is administered, not commodity.

**The remaining Q2 tests.**
- **[E4-04], the durability question, in the ruling's form.** *"does a lapse in spending destroy the structure, or merely narrow it — and does the spending defend the same advantage, or buy its replacement?"* For the regulated core the structure is maintained by the rule, not by spending. For GTO the filer's own description is of a field under *"rapid technological and regulatory changes"* with *"risk of disintermediation due to emerging technologies, fintech start-ups and new market entrants"*, and the company buys capability (CQG *"has expanded our execution management, algorithmic trading and market data capabilities"*; Itiviti; SIS). **The perimeter close (UNKNOWABLE at Q2) was considered and is refused in writing**: it applies to *"A name that passes [E3-03] and whose durability cannot be judged from filings"*, and this name does not pass [E3-03]; its core fails criterion 3 on the filer's words and its unregulated lines fail criterion 2 on the filer's words and record. **So [E4-04] is not the ground of the verdict.**
- **[E4-36], which cause of success.** The record comes from a position granted by a rule set (the nominees' mandates inside the SEC's reimbursement scheme and the NYSE's fee schedule), plus volume growth in positions (equity positions +16% a year in fiscal 2025 and 2026, partly fractional shares that earn nothing: *"Equity revenue position growth excludes small or fractional equity positions for which the Company does not recognize revenue"*). Neither the rule nor the volume is ownable by the company in the sense [E4-36]'s owned causes are.
- **[E2-45], the attacker's test.** For the core, an attacker needs the nominees' mandates, which are contracts; the filer has held them through twenty years of filings, and I record that the attack has not succeeded. For the rest, the filer answers it: clients can do the work in-house, and competitors *"adopt more aggressive pricing policies than we will be able to offer or adopt"*.
- **[E2-53], dominance.** The dominant-newspaper class sets its own price (*"Good or bad, it will prosper"*); the dominant proxy agent does not: its price is the exchange's. Dominance is recorded for the core and does not carry the class.
- **[E4-23], key-person dependence.** None found; the core runs on contracts and rules (*"we play a unique, central, and integral role"*), not on a person.
- **Untapped pricing power [E3-33] / [E5-28]:** the core is the one line where near-monopoly could be claimed; **the untapped power is not the manager's to use**. [E3-33] asks for a business *"where any manager could raise the return enormously just by raising prices"*; here a raise requires an exchange rule filing and SEC approval, and the last one took from September 2010 to January 2014.
- **[E4-32], direction.** Mixed and not the ground: the tangible return fell from 136.5% (fiscal 2016) to 49.3% (2022) and recovered to 73-75% (2025-2026); ICS pre-tax margin fell to 19.8% from 20.6% in fiscal 2026 while GTO rose to 15.5% from 11.3%. The regulator's July 2026 proposal points the core's volume down (*"our recurring revenue growth and distribution revenues will decrease"*).

**THE STRONGEST EVIDENCE AGAINST THE VERDICT BELOW, hunted hardest [E4-26, E3-41, E4-51].** The verdict below is OUT, so the case for IN is set out first, as its holders would state it:
1. **The returns are franchise returns.** 49-137% pre-tax on tangible operating capital in every fiscal year from 2011 to 2026, 12-32% even with every acquisition's goodwill counted, operating margin on fee revenue near 25%, and cash from operations growing from $277.2M (fiscal 2009) to $1,345.6M (fiscal 2026). [E3-43] says the three conditions *"will be demonstrated by"* high returns; here they are.
2. **The core has no close substitute and never lost it.** Twenty 10-Ks name no rival in beneficial proxy distribution; the filer processes *"over 1 billion equity proxy positions"* a year; the attacker cannot get the nominees' mandates.
3. **The regulation is a floor, not a cap, and it has not bitten.** The exchange's schedule sets what issuers must pay; the 2014 change was a revision, not a cut on the record read here; the fee schedule protects the agent from price competition, and the growth since has come at 12% a year in positions with little capital. Munger's and Buffett's own purchases include regulated businesses.
4. **Most of the remaining revenue is recurring under multi-year contracts** with *"high client satisfaction"*, and closed sales rose to $305.1M in fiscal 2026 from $287.9M.

**Answered.** (1) is the strongest fact in the file and is why this run does not call the business ordinary: the returns are real and have lasted. But [E3-03] defines the franchise by three conditions, and [E3-43] names the demonstration as pricing *"regularly ... aggressively"* **and** the returns it earns; the filings show that the core's price is set in *"the rules of self-regulatory organizations"* and moved once in the record read, by a committee of the payers and the SEC, and that the unregulated lines give price back at renewal at a stated *"historical average rate of 3%"* of segment revenue. High tangible returns are also shared, in the row, by a financial printer whose sales have fallen for nine years (DFIN) and by processors carrying 85-89% goodwill. (2) is accepted for the core, and criterion 2 is recorded as met there; it does not rescue the core, because criterion 3 fails on it. (3) is the reading [E2-59] exists to refuse: administered prices *"insulated at least partially from normal market forces"* are the escape of a business that would otherwise be priced by the market, and the framework's text is explicit that regulation *"floors a commodity business"* and caps a franchise and *"neither creates the class"*; the moat belongs to the regime, and the regime is visibly in motion (the 2010 Concept Release, the 2010-2014 fee review, the July 2026 Regulation E-Delivery proposal, and the 10-K's own sentence that such a change *"could result in a reduction or elimination of the demand for our services"*). Buffett's regulated purchases are not bought as franchises in [E3-03]'s sense, and that row is the only one this file may use for the class. (4) Recurring contracts are real; the concession record is the evidence of what happens to price when they renew. **[E3-47]** (a wrongly closed file inside the circle of competence is the costliest error) was weighed against OUT in writing; it is why the reversal conditions below reopen the file on filed documents, and why the alternative readings are recorded rather than hidden.

**The alternative reading, recorded for the operator [E4-51].** A reader could hold that criterion 3 fails only on the **Regulatory** product line (27.5% of fee revenue, profit share not disclosed) and that the file should then turn on the remaining lines' criterion 2, which the record above answers on the filer's words (competition from in-house operations and vendors; renewal concessions). Both routes end at OUT on the business. A second reader could close at **UNKNOWABLE** on the perimeter ([E4-04] as a competence limit, GTO being technology). That is refused because the ruling of 2026-09-20 applies it only to a name that passes [E3-03], and this name's failure is on the filed words, not on an inability to judge durability.
