## Q1 — CAN I UNDERSTAND HOW THIS MAKES MONEY? **[E3-31]**
- **Unit economics in my own words, no management language.** Most shares of US public companies are held through brokers and banks, not in the owners' names. When a company holds a shareholder meeting, the SEC's rules oblige it to pay those brokers and banks to forward its proxy materials to the real owners and to collect their votes, at rates written into the stock exchanges' rules. Almost every broker and bank has handed that job to Broadridge by contract and power of attorney (*"A large number of Nominees have contracted out the processes of distributing proxy materials and tabulating voting instructions to us"*). So Broadridge bills the company at the exchange's rates, keeps its own fee, pays the broker's share on, and passes the postage through at cost (distribution revenue, $2,250.6M of $7,476.8M in fiscal 2026, driven by *"the postage rate increases of approximately $123 million"*). It does the same for mutual-fund reports and prospectuses, and it sells the adjacent work to the same parties: filing and printing for issuers, transfer agency, statements and bills for banks and other firms, fund data. The second business (GTO) rents banks and brokers the systems that record, clear and settle their trades and run their wealth accounts, on multi-year contracts priced per trade or account, and competes with the clients' own departments and with other vendors. Of fiscal 2026 segment pre-tax earnings, ICS earned **$1,103.5M** and GTO **$297.8M**.
- **The scarce input this business controls.** The contracts and powers of attorney from the nominees that make it the one party able to reach most street-name owners at once, and the database of those owners' delivery preferences (*"maintenance of a rules engine and database that contains the delivery method preferences of our clients’ customers"*); in GTO, the installed base of clients whose books and records sit on its platforms (the costs of moving a client onto them are capitalised: *"approximately 9% of our total assets as of June 30, 2026, with one client representing a large portion of this amount"*).
- **Will the fundamentals look broadly the same in ten years?** The proxy mechanism is written into SEC and exchange rules and has run on the same design for the whole filed record (fiscal 2007 to 2026). What can change it is a regulator, not a customer: the 10-K names the SEC's July 2026 *"Regulation E-Delivery"* proposal (if adopted, *"the volume of physically delivered communications we process would decrease"*) and the rise of tokenized securities (demand for *"investor communications, proxy distribution, and transfer agency services ... could be altered, reduced or eliminated"*). GTO is software sold into an industry the filer describes as having *"rapid technological and regulatory changes"*. Whether the position endures is Q2's question; the mechanism is understandable now.
- **Relatively simple and stable in character [E3-31]?** The ICS core is: a fee per position per meeting, set by rule, collected by an agent that holds the nominees' mandates. GTO is less simple (trade-processing software across *"more than 100 markets"*, blockchain repo, AI), and it is **26% of revenue and 21% of segment pre-tax earnings**; the question whether its durability can be judged from filings is carried to Q2 as the [E4-04] question, as the MRK and MDT runs carried theirs.
