Tightness in supply usually does not last long."* A contract manufacturer sells capacity and execution to equipment makers that own the product; the question is whether those customers regard Sanmina's service as having no close substitute, and if not, whether [E2-58]'s one exception (*"a cost advantage that is both wide and sustainable"*) is shown.

### THE STRONGEST EVIDENCE AGAINST THIS VERDICT, stated as its holders would state it [E4-51, E4-26, E3-47]
The verdict below is OUT, so the case for IN is set out first, at full strength.
1. *"The margin has widened for seven years. Gross margin went from 6.5% (FY2018) to 8.8% (FY2025) and 10.5% in the quarter to June 2026; operating margin from 1.7% to 4.4%; and it held in FY2024 when sales fell 15.3%. A manufacturer that widens its margin through a revenue decline is pricing, not taking what it is given. [E4-32]: direction outranks existence."* **Answer:** accepted as the direction, and it is the best fact in the file. But the filer gives the causes every year and none is price: FY2016, *"primarily attributable to improved operational efficiencies"*; FY2023, IMS *"increased operating efficiencies from higher volume"* and CPS *"improved operating efficiencies and a favorable mix of products"*; FY2024, CPS up *"primarily due to significant losses recognized on certain fixed-price customer contracts in 2023 compared to 2024"*; FY2025, CPS *"primarily due to improved operating efficiencies partially offset by unfavorable product mix"*; the 2026 quarter, *"The increase in gross margin is driven by the ZT Acquisition and product mix."* And the whole industry widened in the same years (row below): Flex 5.5% → 9.2%, Jabil 7.6% → 8.9%, Benchmark 8.2% → 10.2%, Celestica 8.8% (2022) → 12.1%. A rise nearly every filer shared is the tide, not Sanmina's position; Sanmina's FY2025 gross margin of 8.8% is below every filed EMS peer in the row except Kimball.
2. *"The returns are respectable and rising: 18.5% pre-tax on net capital in FY2025, 21-23% in FY2022-FY2023, on a business whose customers and suppliers fund much of the inventory. [E3-46]'s number is met well enough."* **Answer:** accepted as the number: a fourteen-year mean of 15.1% pre-tax, with 7.2% in FY2018 and 9.5% in FY2012, is an adequate return, not the high rate [E3-43] ties to aggressive pricing, and the high years are the peak-volume years (FY2022-FY2023, net sales $7.9-8.9bn). [E3-43] joins the return to the pricing; with the pricing half absent, a respectable return is what a well-run *"business"* earns, which is the other half of the same passage.
3. *"Switching costs are real in the markets Sanmina chose: a qualified medical or defense production line cannot be moved in a quarter; SCI holds 'facility security clearance'; the CPS products (Viking memory and storage, SCI's defense products, circuit boards) carry 12.4-14.7% gross margins; supply agreements run three to five years; ten customers are half of sales because they stay."* **Answer:** accepted that switching is slow in the regulated niches, and the CPS margin shows it. Three limits, all in the filer's words: the agreements *"generally do not obligate the customer to purchase minimum quantities"* and *"include provisions for cost reduction objectives during the term of the agreement, which can have the effect of reducing revenue and profitability"* (the price is contracted to fall, not rise); the customer can leave the industry altogether (*"We also face competition from current and potential OEM customers who may elect to manufacture their own products internally"*); and CPS is about a fifth of revenue while IMS, the core, earned 7.5-7.7% gross in FY2023-FY2025. A slow exit is a cost to the customer, not a price Sanmina has shown it can charge.
4. *"ZT Systems changed the company: rack-scale AI infrastructure for hyperscalers is capacity few can supply, the earnout was marked up from $111M to $183M in two quarters on 'a change in financial forecasts', and the June quarter's IMS gross margin was 10.2% against 7.5% a year earlier. This is the moat that has not yet shown up in the annual numbers [E3-33]."* **Answer:** accepted as the fact of the quarter. What it is, in the corpus's own taxonomy, is [E3-43]'s second route for *"a business"*: *"if supply of its product or service is tight. Tightness in supply usually does not last long."* It is also [E2-58]'s cycle stated in advance (*"the rebound to prosperity frequently produces a pervasive enthusiasm for expansion that, within a few years, again creates over-capacity"*), and it is [E4-36]'s *"Catching and riding some sort of big wave"*, whose advantage *"lives in the wave, not the surfer"* ([E3-51]; the framework's gloss). Celestica, a filed peer on the same wave, shows 12.1% gross in 2025; the wave is shared. [E3-33] is scoped by [E5-28] to *"a monopoly or a near monopoly"*, and a filer that names six global competitors is not claiming one.
5. *"Sanmina is a low-cost operator, which [E3-43] names as the other class that earns exceptional profits, and [E2-58] admits a few producers with a cost advantage. The filer says so: 'maintaining low costs is the cornerstone of our success and growth'."* **Answer:** the filer does say so (FY2023 10-K). But [E2-58]'s exception requires the advantage to be *"both wide and sustainable"*, and the filed margins do not show width: Sanmina's ten-year mean operating margin is 3.7%, inside a peer band of 2.8-4.6% (row below), below Plexus and Celestica, and its own risk factor concedes the opposite of a cost lead: *"We may not be able to offer prices as low as some of our competitors for any number of reasons, including the willingness of competitors to provide EMS services at prices we are unable or unwilling to offer."* **The GWW operator question** (whether *"A few producers"* admits a two-firm low-cost tier) is written both ways and does not bear: under either reading, a producer must sit in the low-cost tier by filed figures, and Sanmina sits in the middle of the band, not at its edge.

None of the five shows Sanmina raising a price. The first and the fourth are the strongest facts against this verdict and are carried into the reversal condition.

### THE READING
- **The filer states [E2-58]'s equation in its own risk factors, in the same words, for sixteen years.** FY2010: *"The EMS industry is highly competitive and the industry has been experiencing a surplus of manufacturing capacity"*. FY2016, FY2023 and FY2025, word for word: *"The EMS industry is highly competitive and the industry has experienced a surplus of manufacturing capacity."* and *"In addition, due to the extremely price sensitive nature of our industry, business that we do win or maintain may have lower margins than our historical or target margins."* [E2-58]: *"persistent over-capacity without administered prices (or costs) equals poor profitability."* No administered price exists here ([E2-59]; government contracts are cost-audited, which caps rather than floors).
- **The price runs one way by contract.** *"In some circumstances, our supply agreements with customers include provisions for cost reduction objectives during the term of the agreement, which can have the effect of reducing revenue and profitability from these arrangements."* (FY2016, FY2023, FY2025; FY2010 in shorter form). Risk factors add *"intense competition among our customers and their competitors, leading to reductions in prices for their products and increases in pricing pressure placed on us"* and *"competition in the EMS industry and pricing pressures from OEMs due to greater focus on cost reduction"*.
- **No price increase by Sanmina is recorded in any gross-margin explanation read** (FY2010, FY2016, FY2023, FY2024, FY2025 10-K MD&As and the 10-Q to 2026-06-27; `pricing_search.txt`). A search recorded here, not an absence claim beyond it. The one mention of pricing as a lever (*"we are proactively handling cost impacts through a combination of well-calibrated pricing actions and targeted cost-saving measures"*, FY2023) is a cost pass-through, and the filer says who bears pass-through costs: *"Although our customers are generally liable for tariffs and price increases we pay for components and finished products, our gross margins could be impacted if we are unable to fully recover these costs."* Recovering a cost is not [E2-44]'s *"ability to increase prices rather easily (even when product demand is flat and capacity is not fully utilized)"*.
- **[E2-44] (1) in a falling year: FY2024, net sales −15.3%.** Gross margin rose 8.3% → 8.5%, explained by the absence of the FY2023 fixed-price contract losses, and operating margin fell 5.1% → 4.4%. No price is named.
- **[E3-62], the second step, is on the filer's face.** The strategy is capex for efficiency (*"We continue to invest in factory automation, process improvements, robotics and AI to further enhance our manufacturing efficiency"*) and the product is sold to customers who write cost reductions into the contract; Munger's question, *"how much is going to stay home and how much is just going to flow through to the customer"*, is answered by the contract clause. Jabil's filing says the same of the industry: *"Our business is highly competitive, and our manufacturing processes are generally not subject to significant proprietary protection."*

**[E3-03] criterion by criterion.**
- **(1) needed or desired: passes.** $8.1bn of FY2025 sales, and outsourcing is a durable practice among equipment makers.
- **(2) thought by its customers to have no close substitute: fails, in the filer's words and on the filed record.** The filer names the substitutes: *"Our competitors include major global EMS providers, including Benchmark Electronics, Inc., Celestica, Inc., Flex Ltd., Hon Hai Precision Industry Co., Ltd. (Foxconn), Jabil Inc. and Plexus Corp., as well as other companies that have a regional, product, service or industry-specific focus."*, plus the customer's own factory. It names the basis: *"Competition is based on a number of factors, including end markets served, price and quality."* And the margins it has earned for seventeen years sit inside the band of the six it names.
- **(3) not subject to price regulation: passes**, with the qualification that the defense business works under rules *"that govern price negotiations, cost accounting standards"*.
- **The commodity end [E2-58]: it is the class, and the exception is not shown.** No wide cost advantage in the row; the filer concedes competitors may undercut it.
