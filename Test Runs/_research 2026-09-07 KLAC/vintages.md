## 1. THE DOWNTURN TEST — FY2024, and what actually happened

**KLA has had exactly ONE year of revenue decline in the last decade: FY2024, down 6.5%.** This is
the memory/WFE trough (calendar 2023 into 2024). What the filings show about that year:

| | FY2023 | **FY2024** | change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Total revenue $M | 10,496.1 | **9,812.2** | **-6.5%** |
| Product revenue $M | 8,379.0 | **7,482.7** | **-10.7%** |
| **Service revenue $M** | 2,117.0 | **2,329.6** | **+10.0%** |
| Service % of revenue | 20.2% | **23.7%** | +3.5pp |
| **Gross margin** | 59.8% | **60.0%** | **+0.2pp** |
| Operating margin | 38.1% | 34.1% | -4.0pp |
| Net margin | 32.3% | 28.1% | -4.2pp |
| Operating cash flow $M | (see below) | 3,308.6 | |

**Three findings, and they are the heart of the case:**

**(a) Service revenue GREW 10% while product revenue FELL 10.7%.** The annuity is genuinely
counter-cyclical to the equipment cycle — the installed base keeps running and keeps needing
service even when nobody is buying new tools. This is the strongest single piece of evidence in the
whole file for the durability of the service stream.

**(b) Gross margin ROSE in the down year**, from 59.8% to 60.0%. Management's own bridge from the
FY2024 10-K, verbatim:

> "Fiscal Year Ended June 30, 2023 | 59.8%
> Revenue volume of products and services | (1.1)%
> Mix of products and services sold | 0.6%
> Manufacturing labor, overhead and efficiencies | (0.3)%
> Other service and manufacturing costs | 1.0%
> Fiscal Year Ended June 30, 2024 | 60.0%"

> "The increase in our gross margin from 59.8% to 60.0% during the fiscal year ended June 30, 2024
> is primarily attributable to a decrease in other service and manufacturing costs and **a more
> profitable mix of products and services sold**, partially offset by a lower revenue volume of
> products and services sold."

Note the mix effect was **+0.6pp** — a bigger revenue share of service was margin-accretive.
Management says so in its own bridge. This is the only place in KLA's filings that even indirectly
concedes service carries a favourable margin, and it is a directional statement, not a number.
**Service gross margin is still not disclosed** — see the FY2026 extract, section 2.3.

**(c) Operating margin fell 4 points but stayed above 34%.** The trough operating margin of the
last decade, in the worst revenue year, was **30.3% (FY2020)** and in the only revenue-decline year
was **34.1% (FY2024)**. Note the FY2024 figure is depressed by the $289.5M Display goodwill and
intangible impairment; excluding it, FY2024 operating margin was **37.1%**, i.e. barely down on
FY2023's 38.1% on 6.5% lower revenue.

**The through-cycle margin floor, stated plainly: KLA has not printed a gross margin below 57.8%
or an operating margin below 30.3% in ten years, and both of those troughs came from the Orbotech
acquisition (dilutive mix plus intangible amortization), not from the semiconductor cycle.**

## 2. THE FY2019/FY2020 EPISODE — the margin decline was an ACQUISITION, not the cycle

The task assumed calendar 2019 was the last real WFE downturn. **It was — but KLA's fiscal-year
revenue never declined through it** (FY2019 +13.2%, FY2020 +27.1%), because KLA acquired Orbotech
(closed February 2019) and the acquired revenue masked the cyclical dip.

The gross margin DID fall hard, from 64.1% to 57.9% in two years. Management's own bridge from the
FY2020 10-K attributes it, verbatim:

> "Fiscal year ended June 30, 2018 | 64.1%
> Revenue volume of products and services | (1.0)%
> Mix of products and services sold | 0.7%
> Manufacturing labor, overhead and efficiencies | (1.6)%
> Other service and manufacturing costs | (0.5)%
> **Impact from acquisition of Orbotech | (2.6)%**
> Fiscal year ended June 30, 2019 | 59.1%
> Revenue volume of products and services | 1.5%
> Mix of products and services sold | (0.8)%
> Manufacturing labor, overhead and efficiencies | 0.5%
> **Intangible Amortization | (1.6)%**
> Other service and manufacturing costs | (0.8)%
> Fiscal year ended June 30, 2020 | 57.9%"

**Read the volume lines: (1.0)% in FY2019 and +1.5% in FY2020.** The cycle cost KLA about one point
of gross margin at most. The other five points were **Orbotech dilution (-2.6pp) and purchased
intangible amortization (-1.6pp)** — self-inflicted, one-time, and since worked off. The core
process-control business never lost its margin to the cycle.

**This is the correct reading and it cuts both ways.** It means (i) the 57.8% trough is not a
cyclical floor and the true cyclical floor is higher and untested, and (ii) KLA's one big
diversifying acquisition destroyed roughly 6 points of gross margin and later required $289.5M
(FY2024) plus $239.1M (FY2025) of goodwill and intangible impairments — **$528.6 million written
off against the Orbotech-derived Display and PCB businesses.** The record on capital allocation
outside the core is poor, and the filings say so in their own impairment notes.

**SERVICE REVENUE HAS NEVER DECLINED IN ANY YEAR OF THIS TEN-YEAR SERIES.** Compound annual growth
FY2017-FY2026 is **+16.7%**; over the nine years service grew 4.03x while product grew 3.87x.
The minimum annual growth rate was **+10.0%, and it occurred in FY2024, the one revenue-decline
year.** The FY2019 jump of +34.3% includes acquired Orbotech service.

Note the *share* is not trending up — it oscillates between 20% and 26% because product revenue is
the volatile numerator's competitor. **Service share is a cycle indicator, not a mix trend: it
peaks when product revenue troughs (25.8% in FY2019, 23.7% in FY2024) and bottoms at the cycle peak
(20.2% in FY2023).** Anyone reading rising service share as structural improvement is misreading a
cyclical artifact.

### Service gross profit across the vintages — the precise finding

**SERVICE GROSS PROFIT IS NOT DISCLOSED IN ANY VINTAGE. But the search is not empty, and the
distinction matters, so here it is exactly.**

I searched all six downloaded 10-Ks for "cost of service", "costs of service", "cost of product",
"costs of product", "service gross", "product gross" and "gross profit". Hit counts:
FY2019: 1 · FY2020: 0 · FY2023: 17 · FY2024: 14 · FY2025: 0 · FY2026: 0.

Every one of those hits was inspected. **None is a product-versus-service split.** They are:

**(a) FY2023 and FY2024 — "SEGMENT gross profit", a per-segment measure.** These two vintages used
segment gross profit as the CODM measure, verbatim (FY2024 10-K, Note on segment reporting):

> "The CODM assesses the performance of each operating segment and allocates resources to those
> segments based on total revenues and **segment gross profit** and does not evaluate the segments
> using discrete asset information. Segment gross profit excludes corporate allocations and effects
> of changes in foreign currency exchange rates, amortization of intangible assets, amortization of
> inventory fair value adjustments, and transaction costs associated with our acquisitions related
> to costs of revenues."

This is a real and useful disclosure that the FY2026 vintage no longer presents in the same form, so
it is recorded here (In thousands, from the FY2024 10-K):

| | FY2024 | FY2023 | FY2022 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Semiconductor Process Control — Revenues | 8,733,556 | 9,324,190 | 7,924,822 |
| Semiconductor Process Control — **Segment gross profit** | **5,629,302** | **5,957,573** | **5,167,679** |
| *implied segment gross margin* | *64.5%* | *63.9%* | *65.2%* |
| Specialty Semiconductor Process — Revenues | 528,701 | 543,398 | 456,579 |
| Specialty Semiconductor Process — Segment gross profit | 282,910 | 281,942 | 242,520 |
| *implied segment gross margin* | *53.5%* | *51.9%* | *53.1%* |
| PCB and Component Inspection — Revenues | 552,491 | 631,604 | 832,176 |
| PCB and Component Inspection — Segment gross profit | 158,960 | 221,251 | 378,964 |
| *implied segment gross margin* | *28.8%* | *35.0%* | *45.5%* |
| **Total segment gross profit** | **6,071,172** | **6,460,766** | **5,789,163** |

*(The implied margins are my arithmetic. Note these segment margins are struck before intangible
amortization and so run above the consolidated figure.)* **This is a SEGMENT split, not a
product/service split** — and Services are explicitly sold across multiple segments, so it cannot
be reworked into a service margin.

**(b) FY2019 — one narrative mention of "product gross margins", with no number.** Verbatim:
"...is primarily attributable to the acquisition of Orbotech, **which historically had lower
product gross margins**, an increase in service and manufacturing costs, and a lower revenue volume
of pr[oducts]..." This says Orbotech's product margins were lower than KLA's. It gives no figure
and no service comparison.

**The substantive conclusion stands and is now precisely stated: in ten years of filings KLA has
never split cost of revenues between product and service, and has never disclosed a service gross
profit, service gross margin or cost of service revenue. The absence is structural, consistent
across every vintage, and cannot be closed from primary filings.** The FY2020 and FY2019 filings do
disclose a "Gross margin percentage" line, but in total only.

  future Commerce rules and regulations."

**The China story, correctly told.** China went from 12% of revenue in FY2017 to a 43% spike in
FY2024 and has since settled at 30%. The FY2024 spike was an anomaly of the denominator: China
dollars grew 46% that year (2,867 to 4,197) *while total revenue fell 6.5%* — Chinese customers
were stockpiling legacy-node process-control tools ahead of tightening export controls, and that
purchasing carried KLA through the downturn.

**The dangerous inference to avoid.** China's percentage falling from 43% to 30% looks like
de-risking. **It is not.** In dollars, China revenue has been essentially flat for three years:
$4,196.7M then $4,042.6M then $4,048.4M. The share fell only because Taiwan and Korea grew. **KLA's
absolute China exposure is unchanged at roughly $4 billion a year, or 30% of revenue and — since
this is high-margin equipment revenue — probably more than 30% of profit.** The FY2026 MD&A says as
much, verbatim: "Revenue in China was comparable to the prior fiscal year, as continued investments
in legacy-node technologies by domestic semiconductor companies were largely offset by export
control restrictions affecting certain advanced technology transactions."

**Also note the FY2024 comparison problem for through-cycle work:** the one revenue-decline year is
also the year China was 43% of sales. The downturn resilience shown in section 1 was partly bought
by a one-off Chinese pre-buy that cannot repeat. **Do not treat FY2024 as a clean cyclical stress
test.** That is the single most important caveat in this file.

## 5. GROSS MARGIN THROUGH THE LAST TWO DOWNTURNS — the answer to the question asked

| FY | Gross margin | Revenue YoY | What was happening |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2017 | 63.0% | +16.6% | pre-Orbotech, pure process control |
| 2018 | **64.2%** | +16.0% | **peak margin of the decade** |
| 2019 | 59.1% | +13.2% | Orbotech closed Feb 2019: -2.6pp acquisition mix |
| 2020 | **57.8%** | +27.1% | **trough margin of the decade**: -1.6pp intangible amortization |
| 2021 | 59.9% | +19.2% | recovering |
| 2022 | 61.0% | +33.1% | cycle peak approaching |
| 2023 | 59.8% | +13.9% | -2.1pp from "other service and manufacturing costs" |
| **2024** | **60.0%** | **-6.5%** | **margin ROSE in the down year** |
| 2025 | 60.9% | +23.9% | recovery |
| 2026 | 61.3% | +11.7% | +0.5pp of it pure operating leverage |

**Ten-year mean gross margin: 60.3%. Ten-year range: 57.8% to 64.2% — a 6.4-point band across two
cycles, an acquisition, and a trade war.** Excluding the two Orbotech-distorted years (FY2019,
FY2020), the range narrows to **59.8% to 64.2%**, and the last four years sit in a 1.5-point band
(59.8%-61.3%).

**This is the central quantitative fact about KLA and it is what a franchise looks like in the
accounts.** Gross margin did not break in the one year revenue fell; it went up. But see the China
caveat in section 4 before treating that as proven.

**Operating margin, same view:** ten-year mean **35.5%**, range **30.3% (FY2020) to 41.7%
(FY2026)**. Adjusted for the FY2024 impairment, the FY2024 trough is 37.1%. The FY2020 trough of
30.3% was acquisition-driven.

| 2025 | 1,360.3 | 11.2% | 1,029.7 | 8.5% |

**In FY2024, the down year, R&D fell only 1.4% (1,296.7 to 1,279.0) while revenue fell 6.5% — so
R&D intensity ROSE from 12.4% to 13.0%.** KLA did not cut research into the downturn. That matches
the Item 1 claim, verbatim from the FY2026 10-K: "**Even during down cycles in the semiconductor
industry, we have remained committed to significant engineering efforts toward both product
improvement and new product development to enhance our competitive position.**" The FY2024 numbers
substantiate that claim rather than merely asserting it. SG&A likewise fell only 1.7%.

The declining R&D and SG&A *ratios* since FY2019 are operating leverage on a fixed cost base, and
they are the mechanism behind the operating margin rising from 30.4% to 41.7%. **They also mean the
downside is geared: a 20% revenue decline against a $2.66bn fixed R&D-plus-SG&A base would cost
roughly 5-6 points of operating margin before any gross margin effect.**

## 8. THE ONE-PARAGRAPH SUMMARY

Over ten fiscal years KLA grew revenue 3.9x, from $3.48bn to $13.58bn, at a 16.3% CAGR, and held
gross margin in a 57.8%-64.2% band whose low point was caused by an acquisition rather than the
cycle. Service revenue grew every single year without exception, at a 16.7% CAGR, and its worst
year (+10.0%) was the company's only revenue-decline year. In that year, FY2024, gross margin rose.
Operating margin has never been below 30.3% and is now 41.7%. Against that, three facts must be
held in view: the only downturn observation is contaminated by a 43%-of-revenue Chinese pre-buy;
China exposure is flat at roughly $4bn a year and is only falling as a percentage; and the one
large diversifying acquisition cost roughly six points of gross margin and $528.6 million of
subsequent impairments. **The franchise evidence is strong but rests on a single, impure downturn
observation. That is the honest state of the record.**
