### The company-selected measure — VERBATIM

> "Free cash flow margin equals our free cash flow divided by total revenues. Free cash flow
> equals net cash provided by operating activities less capital expenditures. Free cash flow
> margin over the applicable three-year period relative to our industry peer group for that
> same three-year period is used in determining the level at which the performance criteria
> under our annual PRSUs are satisfied, between 0%-200% of the target shares granted for the
> periods presented."
> — DEF 14A 2025-09-23, Pay Versus Performance footnote (6)

The proxy reconciles the measure openly (p.72):

| $M | FY2021 | FY2022 | FY2023 | FY2024 | FY2025 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Net cash provided by operating activities | 2,185.0 | 3,312.7 | 3,669.8 | 3,308.6 | 4,081.9 |
| Less capital expenditures | (231.6) | (307.3) | (341.6) | (277.4) | (335.3) |
| Free cash flow | 1,953.4 | 3,005.4 | 3,328.2 | 3,031.2 | 3,746.6 |
| Total revenues | 6,918.7 | 9,211.9 | 10,496.1 | 9,812.2 | 12,156.2 |
| Free cash flow margin | 28.2% | 32.6% | 31.7% | 30.9% | 30.8% |

**Assessment (preliminary).** The company-selected measure is a *cash* measure, not EPS and
not the stock price. That is materially better than the framework's worst case. It is a
**margin**, i.e. a ratio, which cannot be improved by shrinking the denominator without also
shrinking the business, and it is measured **relative to an industry peer group over three
years**, which strips out the cycle. Free cash flow margin is the closest thing on a proxy
statement to an owner-earnings-shaped metric. NOTE the qualifier: FCF here is
CFO less capex, i.e. **total** capex, not maintenance capex, and it is before the
working-capital judgment the framework requires. It is not owner earnings. It is directionally
honest.

## 1b. DEF 14A — THE INCENTIVE PLAN METRICS, VERBATIM

This is the section that decides whether the pay design fires a framework flag. KLA runs
**three** incentive vehicles. Note first what is ABSENT: **there is no stock-option column in
the Summary Compensation Table at all.** KLA grants no options to NEOs. There is **no
relative-TSR-linked award** — the string "relative TSR" does not appear in the proxy. Neither
the share price nor total shareholder return is a performance *metric* anywhere in the plan;
the stock price only enters as the currency in which awards are denominated.

### (i) Annual cash bonus — "Operating Margin Dollar" + a balanced scorecard

> "Our annual Executive Incentive Plan (our 'Bonus Plan') is intended to motivate our senior
> executives, including our NEOs, to achieve short-term corporate objectives by providing a
> competitive cash bonus, which is earned based upon the achievement of pre-determined Company
> performance goals, relating to Operating Margin Dollar (as defined below), the Company's
> 'balanced scorecard' objectives (as described below) and individual performance."

> "We used Operating Margin Dollar achievement as a key performance metric in our calendar year
> 2024 Bonus Plan because we believe that it reflects several important competitive and business
> elements, such as product acceptance, market share and cost discipline, and is therefore a
> very good barometer of our overall performance. For our calendar year 2024 Bonus Plan,
> 'Operating Margin Dollar' represents our total revenues less total costs of revenues, research
> and development expenses and selling, general and administrative expenses, other than expenses
> related to acquisitions, goodwill impairment…"

The Bonus Plan runs on a **calendar year**, not the fiscal year. NEOs participated in the 2024
Bonus Plan from 2024-07-01 to 2024-12-31 and the 2025 Bonus Plan from 2025-01-01 to 2025-06-30.
FY2025 bonuses therefore reflect **calendar 2024** performance.

**The 2024 grid, actually disclosed with numbers** — threshold $1.179bn, target $3.930bn,
maximum $5.502bn of Operating Margin Dollars. Actual achievement **$4.404bn, ~12% above
target**; balanced scorecard rating **"4+"**; funding **144% of target** before individual
multipliers. Individual multipliers range 80%-120%.

The balanced scorecard, with weights and the board's own scoring, is printed in full:

| Objective | Metric | Weight | Score |
|---|---|---|---|
| Financial - top line | Revenue | 20% | 4 |
| Financial - product differentiation | Gross margin | 15% | 3 |
| Financial - productivity | Operating margin | 15% | 4 |
| Quality of result (3 rows) | Share of WFE; process control share; key product roadmap milestones | 40% | 4+ |
| Human capital | Turnover, engagement, hiring, inclusion | 10% | 4+ |

**This is unusually candid disclosure.** The company printed the target it had to hit, the
number it actually hit, the full payout grid, and a self-score that includes a **3 ("primarily
meets expectations") on gross margin** with the stated reason "slightly less than our internal
plan." A management team gaming the disclosure does not print a below-plan self-score.

### (ii) Annual PRSUs — Relative Free Cash Flow Margin

> "The NEOs' fiscal year 2025 PRSUs are earned based on the Company's Relative Free Cash Flow
> Margin (as defined below). 'Relative Free Cash Flow Margin' means our cumulative free cash
> flow (cash flow provided by operations, less capital expenditures), divided by cumulative
> revenues, relative to the cumulative free cash flow margin for our industry peer group
> companies for the three years ending June 30, 2027."

> "We believe that the Relative Free Cash Flow Margin metric is a key measure of our long-term
> performance and stockholder value creation. Our ability to generate cash from operations is
> essential to fund the expansive research and development efforts that are instrumental to our
> long-term success, as well as our efforts to return cash to stockholders. The relative nature
> of the metric ensures that our performance must compare favorably to our industry peer group
> companies for PRSUs to be earned."

Payout curve: below 30th percentile = 0; 30th percentile (threshold) = 25%; **55th percentile
(target) = 100%**; 80th percentile or above (maximum) = 200%. Linear interpolation between.

FY2025 changes disclosed openly: maximum raised **from 150% to 200%** of target, and the
threshold for maximum raised from the 75th to the 80th percentile, and vesting moved to 100% at
three years. Raising the cap from 150% to 200% is a **pay-opportunity increase**; the company
paired it with a harder maximum hurdle and disclosed both in one sentence.

Settled example, FY2022 PRSUs (three years to 2024-06-30): earned at the **82nd percentile → 150%
payout** (the then-cap). So the plan does pay out high, and it did so on a genuinely relative
cash measure.

### (iii) The one-off EPS Awards — the flag

> "The Complementary EPS-based PRSU awards were special grants made only in fiscal year 2023. In
> fiscal year 2025, the first tranche of the EPS Awards … was earned based on our non-GAAP
> diluted earnings per share ('EPS') for the two-year performance period ended June 30, 2024. In
> August 2024 … Tranche 1 of the EPS Awards had been earned at 131% of the target number of PRSUs
> subject to such tranche based on cumulative EPS achieved of $49.11."

Tranche 1 grid (pre-split): threshold cumulative EPS $40.10 (50% payout), target $47.18 (100%),
maximum $56.62 (250%); actual **$49.11 → 131%**.

> "EPS is a non-GAAP financial measure and is disclosed in our earnings releases each quarter.
> EPS excludes the following measures included in GAAP earnings per diluted share attributable
> to KLA: (i) acquisition-related charges; (ii) restructuring, severance and other charges;
> (iii) charges associated with redemption of debt; (iv) goodwill and intangible impairment;
> (v) income tax effects of non-GAAP adjustments; and (vi) discrete tax items…"

**THE FLAG, stated plainly.** A metric that is (a) **per share**, (b) **non-GAAP**, and
(c) **explicitly excludes goodwill and intangible impairment** is the classic construction the
framework distrusts. It rewards the numerator for excluding the cost of a bad acquisition and
rewards the denominator for buying back stock. KLA retired 3.0-3.2% of its shares annually over
the performance window (section 2), which mechanically lifted cumulative EPS toward the $47.18
target without a dollar of extra earnings.

**Three mitigations, all verifiable:**
1. It was a **one-time grant made only in fiscal year 2023** ("special grants made only in fiscal
   year 2023"). It is not the recurring plan. The recurring PRSU is the cash-margin metric.
2. The exclusion list is **closed and enumerated** — six named items, not an open "and other
   items management deems non-recurring." It also carries the discipline clause "in each case,
   with such adjustments to occur in a manner materially consistent with our past practice or as
   a result of new laws and regulations."
3. The company's own **"What We Don't Do"** table commits to metric separation:
   > "Different metrics in short- and long-term incentive plans: The metrics used for our annual
   > cash bonus program (i.e., Operating Margin Dollar achievement and corporate balanced
   > scorecard assessment) are different from those used for our PRSUs (i.e., Relative Free Cash
   > Flow Margin (as defined below) and non-GAAP EPS (as defined below))."

**Net reading: the pay design does NOT fire the framework's primary flag.** The recurring
long-term metric is relative free cash flow margin; the recurring annual metric is an absolute
operating-profit dollar figure with a disclosed grid; there are no options and no TSR metric.
The EPS Award is a real but bounded and non-recurring exception, now largely worked through.

### 2c. What the record says

**Shares outstanding are falling, and have fallen every year since FY2019.**
155,995 (FY2016) → 130,698 (FY2026) = **−16.2% over ten years, ≈ −1.75%/yr compounded.** From the
FY2019 peak of 159,475 the decline is **−18.0% in seven years, ≈ −2.8%/yr**. Net issuance under
employee stock plans runs roughly 0.5-0.7 million shares/yr pre-split (FY2024 707k, FY2025 604k
equivalent, FY2026 500k equivalent) — comfortably swamped by repurchases. **The buyback is real
retirement, not option-dilution offset.** This is the single strongest capital-allocation fact in
the file.

**The FY2019 anomaly is Orbotech.** Shares rose 156,048 → 159,475 in FY2019 despite $1,095.2M of
buybacks, because KLA issued stock as part of the Orbotech consideration. Worth stating plainly:
**KLA's largest-ever acquisition was the only event in eleven years that increased the share
count.**

**Buyback price discipline — the honest verdict is MIXED, leaning poor.** Implied average prices
paid (pre-split, cash or equity-statement value ÷ shares):

The pattern is **pro-cyclical**: the smallest buyback in the series ($25.0M, FY2017) came at
~$103 and the largest ($3.97bn cash / $4.86bn booked, FY2022) came at ~$413 near the cycle peak,

was cheapest and borrowed to buy most near a top. Spending rises monotonically with price

## 4. METRIC-SWITCHING CHECK

The test: did KLA change which number it leads with after a deterioration? The deterioration to
test against is **FY2024** — revenue fell $10.50bn → $9.81bn (−6.6%) and net income fell $3.39bn
→ $2.76bn (−18.5%). If a framing switch was ever going to happen, it would be in the FY2024
release.

### 4a. EBITDA — the answer is a flat no

**"EBITDA" appears ZERO times** in the FY2019, FY2023 and FY2026 10-Ks, and **ZERO times** in the
FY2019 Q4, FY2023 Q4, FY2024 Q4 and FY2026 Q4 earnings releases. Neither does "adjusted EBITDA."
Searched on the raw filed HTML, not a summary. **KLA does not promote EBITDA in any form.**

Equally notable: **"free cash flow" and the acronym "GAAP" appear ZERO times in the 10-Ks
themselves** (FY2026 has two incidental uses). The 10-K uses only "accounting principles generally
accepted in the United States." **KLA's 10-K MD&A is entirely GAAP with no non-GAAP overlay at
all.** The non-GAAP presentation lives exclusively in the earnings releases, where Reg G governs
and a reconciliation is mandatory. That is the correct place for it.

### 4b. The lead metric has not moved, across seven years and a down year

| Release | Opening sentence leads with | Table order | Bullet structure |
|---|---|---|---|
| FY2019 Q4 (2019-08-05, acc. 0000319201-19-000026) | "KLA reported **GAAP** net income … and **GAAP** earnings per diluted share … on revenues of" | GAAP Results, then Non-GAAP Results | no bullets |
| FY2023 Q4 (2023-07-27, acc. 0000319201-23-000027) | "KLA reported **GAAP** net income … and **GAAP** diluted earnings per share … on total revenues of" | GAAP, then Non-GAAP | revenue vs guidance; GAAP **and** non-GAAP EPS vs guidance; CFO **and** FCF; capital returns |
| **FY2024 Q4 (2024-07-24, acc. 0000319201-24-000018) — the down year** | "KLA reported **GAAP** net income … and **GAAP** diluted earnings per share … on total revenues of" | GAAP, then Non-GAAP | identical four bullets |
| FY2026 Q4 (2026-07-28, acc. 0000319201-26-000024) | "KLA reported **GAAP** net income … and **GAAP** diluted EPS … on total revenues of" | GAAP, then Non-GAAP | identical four bullets, plus a split-adjustment bullet |

**Verdict: NO metric switch.** GAAP leads the headline sentence and the GAAP table precedes the
non-GAAP table in every release checked, including the down year. The bullet template is
character-for-character stable from FY2023 through FY2026 and carries **both** GAAP and non-GAAP
EPS in the same bullet — the company does not print the non-GAAP number without the GAAP number
beside it. Free cash flow was **added** to the bullets between FY2019 and FY2023, and added
alongside operating cash flow with an explicit definition; it displaced nothing.

### 4c. The non-GAAP gap is shrinking, which is the honest direction

| Quarter | GAAP diluted EPS | Non-GAAP diluted EPS | Gap |
|---|---|---|---|
| Q4 FY2019 | $1.35 | $1.78 | **+31.9%** |
| Q4 FY2023 | $4.97 | $5.40 | +8.7% |
| Q4 FY2024 | $6.18 | $6.60 | +6.8% |
| Q4 FY2026 (post-split) | $1.04 | $1.05 | **+1.0%** |

The FY2019 gap of nearly a third is the Orbotech purchase accounting — amortisation of acquired
intangibles — running through the P&L. As those intangibles amortise off, the gap collapses to
**one percent**. A company leaning on non-GAAP to flatter a story does not let the adjustment
shrink to nothing; KLA's non-GAAP number is now within a cent of its GAAP number. **The two
framings have converged, which removes most of the room to switch between them.**

### 4d. The framing tells that ARE present

Two, both minor, both recorded rather than dismissed:

1. **Performance is framed against KLA's own guidance, not against the prior year.** The FY2024
   release bullets say revenue was "at the upper end of the guidance range" and EPS "above the
   midpoints of the respective guidance ranges." Nowhere in the highlights or the CEO quote does
   the release state that full-year revenue fell 6.6% and net income fell 18.5%. The CEO quote
   reads: *"KLA's June quarter results exceeded expectations, including revenue, gross margin and
   EPS, which were all above their respective guidance midpoints, demonstrating the enduring power
   and differentiation of the KLA portfolio."* Beating a self-set midpoint in a down year is
   presented as the achievement. **This is guidance-anchored framing** and it connects directly to
   the [E3-48] finding in section 5: a management that always beats its own midpoint can always
   report a beat, whatever the business did.
2. **The non-GAAP exclusion list is stable but self-serving on impairment.** Footnote (c) of the
   FY2026 Q4 release, verbatim:
   > "Impairment of goodwill and purchased intangible assets in the twelve months ended June 30,
   > 2025 included non-cash expense recognized as a result of the company's testing for goodwill
   > impairment and long-lived assets impairment, which resulted from **the continued
   > deterioration of the long-term forecast for our PCB business**. Management believes that it
   > is appropriate to exclude these impairment charges as they are not indicative of ongoing
   > operating results and therefore limit comparability."

   Excluding an impairment as "not indicative of ongoing operating results" is the standard move
   and it writes off the cost of a past acquisition. **But note what the same company does in the
   adjoining footnote (a), which works against itself:**
   > "…management believes that it is important for investors to understand that such intangible
   > assets were recorded as part of purchase price accounting arising from acquisitions, and such
   > amortization of intangible assets related to past acquisitions **will recur in future periods
   > until such intangible assets have been fully amortized**. Investors should note that **the use
   > of these intangible assets contributed to our revenues** earned during the periods presented
   > and are expected to contribute to our future period revenues as well."

   That is a company telling readers, in its own release, that the item it is excluding is
   recurring and that it earns revenue. Most issuers do not write that sentence. **Record it on
   the honesty side of the ledger.**

**Corroboration for section 3 from this source:** the PCB business acquired with Orbotech
deteriorated far enough to force a goodwill and long-lived-asset impairment in FY2025, disclosed
here in the company's own words. Separately, footnote (b) of the FY2024 Q4 release records **"a
gain on the sale of Orbograph, Ltd. ('Orbograph'), which was sold in the first quarter of fiscal

### 3e. The Q3 reading on Orbotech

**KLA bought a display business inside a $3.26bn acquisition in 2019, exited display manufacturing
entirely by December 2024, wrote off ~41% of the acquired goodwill, and has never in eight annual
reports connected that outcome back to the March 2018 announcement case.** The word "synergies"
vanished from the 10-K in FY2022, the year the PCB/Display segment peaked and began to fall.

Two things must be held together here and not collapsed into one:

1. **This is the norm, not an aggravating fact.** Almost no US issuer publishes an acquisition
   post-mortem. Scoring KLA badly *relative to peers* on this would be wrong.
2. **It is still a silence, not a post-mortem, and the framework asks for the post-mortem.** The
   only honest signal available on this deal was *compelled by GAAP impairment testing*, not
   volunteered by management. Under Q3 the correct entry is: **the impairments were disclosed
   promptly, specifically, with the reporting unit and the cause named — but the strategic case
   was never marked to reality in the shareholder's own words.**

The mitigating fact of substance: **the impairment disclosures themselves are specific and
unflattering** ("significant deterioration," "continued deterioration," a named decision to exit a
business bought six years earlier). That is not a company hiding a bad deal. It is a company
reporting the wreckage line by line without ever saying "we were wrong in 2018."

## 8. SUMMARY — WHAT THE Q3 EVIDENCE SAYS

### On the honesty side of the ledger

1. **Cash taxes paid EXCEED the book tax provision by $908m over nine years**, in eight of nine
   years. The [E4-30] flag does not fire; it fires in reverse. On the tax authorities' view, the
   reported earnings are at least as real as stated. **This is the strongest single honesty
   datum in the file.**
2. **EBITDA appears zero times in any 10-K or earnings release checked.** No adjusted EBITDA. The
   10-K MD&A is entirely GAAP with no non-GAAP overlay at all.
3. **GAAP leads every earnings release**, including the FY2024 down year — headline sentence and
   table order both. No metric switch across FY2019, FY2023, FY2024, FY2026.
4. **The non-GAAP-to-GAAP EPS gap has collapsed from +31.9% (Q4 FY2019) to +1.0% (Q4 FY2026).**
5. **The proxy discloses things that work against management**: a below-plan self-score of 3 on
   gross margin; the full bonus payout grid with target and actual; and an explicit statement that
   the SCT overstates the shares granted by 8% because of the 30-day-average pricing convention.
6. **The 2006 board used the words "intentional" and "falsification of Company records"** about its
   own former executives. Then it fired the CEO.
7. **Eighteen years clean**: no restatement, no material weakness, no non-reliance 8-K, no NT
   filing, no named litigation — through a $3.3bn acquisition and a tripling of revenue.
8. **Impairments were disclosed promptly and specifically**, with the reporting unit and the cause
   named ("significant deterioration," "continued deterioration," a named decision to exit
   Display).
9. **No options granted. No tax gross-ups. Perquisites 0.2% of CEO pay. No hedging or pledging.
   Double-trigger only. No related-party self-dealing.**

### On the other side

1. **INSIDER OWNERSHIP IS 0.0915%.** All sixteen directors and executive officers hold 120,462
   shares of 131,684,530. The CEO of twenty years holds 0.026%. **These are very highly paid
   employees, not owners.** This is the clearest Q3 negative in the file.

6. **THE EPS AWARD FIRES THE PAY FLAG** — non-GAAP, per-share, and explicitly excluding goodwill
