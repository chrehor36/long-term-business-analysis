### The finding
- **The registrant says it sells commodities** (*"Many of the products sold by us are commodities, and
  competition among manufacturers is based largely on price"*, Item 1A), so [E3-03] clause (2) fails and the
  run went to [E2-58]'s one exception, a cost advantage wide and sustainable enough to "consistently do well".
  A twenty-one-year segment series was built from ten of Eagle's 10-Ks (FY2006-FY2026).
- **Cement** (half of segment earnings) never lost money through 2008-13 (trough 15.2% in FY2013; 24-28% since
  FY2015) inside a supply-tight, permit-protected, freight-sheltered structure. **Summit's cement segment, the
  like-for-like filed peer in the same region, earned 26.2% against Eagle's 26.0% on the same basis.** The
  economics are the structure's.
- **Wallboard** (40%) has a real, wide relative cost advantage over the only filers (USG, CBPX: 15-20 points
  in 2014-18; break-even while USG lost money in the glut). **It still earned 0.4-2.9% for four straight
  years (FY2009-12)** as net price fell 43%; the recovery came with industry-wide January 2012 and 2013 price
  increases at unchanged share, the conduct the Domestic Wallboard Antitrust Litigation alleged was collusive
  (settled for about $45.1M with a denial of wrongdoing; the DOJ closed its investigation in August 2018; no
  finding made by the run). Industry utilization now 78%, price and volume both down in FY2026, and Eagle
  adding 25% at Duke: [E2-58]'s *"nothing fails like success"* in the registrant's own capex plan.
