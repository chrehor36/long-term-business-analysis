## GATE 2 — MOAT
Franchise test [E3-03], from UPS's own 10-K: **"We offer a broad array of
transportation and logistics services and compete with many local,
regional, national and international logistics providers as well as
national postal services. We believe our strategy, network and
competitive strengths position us well to compete in the marketplace."**
— a real, if generic, network-scale claim, followed by an explicit
cross-reference to its own risk factors: **"Our industry continues to
rapidly evolve. We expect to continue to face significant competition,
which could materially adversely affect us."**
- **No close substitute**: **real, structural** — UPS's ground-delivery
  density network (routes, sorting hubs, drivers) is a genuine, hard-to-
  replicate physical infrastructure asset, similar in kind to FedEx's air
  network (not independently compared against FedEx's own filing this
  pass). This is a real scale moat, not a brand-based one.
- The company's own risk-factor cross-reference to "significant
  competition" is a real, disclosed concern, not boilerplate dismissed by
  this run — Amazon's build-out of its own delivery network (general,
  widely known market fact, not independently confirmed in this filing) is
  a material, ongoing threat to exactly this kind of scale moat.
**Class: NARROW** (real network-density advantage, increasingly contested),
**Direction: NARROWING** given the well-known Amazon-logistics threat.
**VERDICT: PASS with NARROW/NARROWING classification.**
