## Q2 — WHY IS THE CASTLE STILL STANDING, and what keeps it standing ten to twenty years? STOP.
The question: "why is that castle still standing? And what’s going to keep it standing or cause it not to be standing five, 10, 20 years from now." **[M1995-038]**. The deciding part is Texas (Q1's table). A commodity business is judged here by whether it is the low-cost producer, read from its own and its operators' filings **[M2006-004]**, **[M2009-059]**, a high-cost producer in a commodity field being OUT **[M1997-010]** (Q2, the commodity-business paragraph of 2026-10-12).

**Is the deciding part a commodity business?** On the filer's own words, yes at both ends. Generation: power sold into ERCOT, an energy-only market, at a price the market sets. Retail: the household chooses on "price, incentive, customer service, brand" (FY2025 10-K, Item 1; the FY2016 and FY2018 10-Ks put "price" first in the same list); business customers, 39.3 TWh of the 78.1 TWh of Texas load in 2025, contract for one to five years through channels that include "brokers" (Item 1), which is a bid. The commodity mark in the rows is the customer's indifference, "most insureds don't care from whom they buy" **[L2004-003]**; the filer says there is "considerable competition for customers" and "a high degree of fragmentation" (Item 1, Competition). Electricity delivered by the same wires is the same product whichever retailer bills it.

**The castle tests, each with its filing fact.**
1. *The attacker with money* **[M2011-015]**. A Texas retailer needs a PUCT certificate and a hedge book; the filer counts "both large and small competitors". Entry is open; what an entrant cannot buy quickly is NRG's customer list and plants. The money test is not passed on the evidence: one well-capitalised rival, Vistra's TXU Energy, grew its Texas customer count by about 1% to 3% a year in 2018 to 2022 by its own 10-Ks (`competitors.md`), while NRG's Texas home count fell from 3,024 thousand to 2,860 thousand (2021 to 2025). The rows warn that "one competitor is frequently enough to ruin a business" **[M2012-108]**; ruin is not shown here, a slow loss of share is.
2. *Pricing power and the agony before a rise* **[M2005-020]**. The filer reports no retail price, unit margin or price premium in the 10-Ks read for FY2019 to FY2025 (no instance found, by a search of the FY2019, FY2021 and FY2025 10-Ks for "margin per MWh"). The one retailer read that reports a unit margin, Via Renewables, shows how it moves: electricity margin $38.40/MWh in Q1 2025, $13.89/MWh in Q1 2026 (`competitors.md`, its 10-Q). Maryland's SB 1 capped residential retail prices at a trailing utility average from 2025 (FY2025 10-K, Item 1): in that state the legislature, not the retailer, sets the ceiling.
3. *Unit volume and share of mind* **[M1997-099]**. Texas home load 35.1 TWh (2016), 38.5 TWh (2020), 42.4 TWh (2021, with Direct Energy), 38.8 TWh (2025); Texas home ending customers 2,102 thousand (2016), 2,451 thousand (2020), 3,024 thousand (2021), 2,860 thousand (2025) (10-K operational statistics). Since the Direct Energy step, volume and customers have both fallen.
4. *The low-cost position* **[M2018-043]**, **[L2004-007]**. Generation: Texas average net heat rate 11,087 Btu/kWh, capacity factor 34.8%, 4,202 MW coal of 9,271 MW (FY2025 10-K, Item 1); NRG's own former West combined-cycle plant ran at 7,443. The Texas fleet is mid-merit coal and older gas, not the plants at the low end of the supply curve. Supply: NRG generated 28.7 TWh in Texas against 78.1 TWh of Texas load, so about two-thirds of Texas supply is bought at the market price any retailer pays. Retail cost: no filing read states a cost to serve, an acquisition cost per customer or a churn rate for NRG or for Vistra (no instance found: NRG 10-Ks FY2016, FY2019, FY2021, FY2025 searched for "churn", "attrition", "retention rate", the only hit being the auditor's note on the Direct Energy customer-attrition assumption; Vistra's 10-Ks give net meter-count changes only, `competitors.md`). The small retailers that do report churn lose 4% to 7% of their customers a month (Genie, Via; `competitors.md`).
5. *The brand, and the low bid* **[M2017-009]**. Reliant has kept about two to three million Texas homes for a decade, which is evidence of an inertia worth something; but the filer does not say what those customers pay over the low bid, and the count is falling.
6. *Ask the competitors* **[M1999-130]**. Vistra's 10-Ks: "Competitive retail activity in ERCOT has resulted in retail customer churn" (`competitors.md`); no Vistra filing read names NRG as the rival it fears.
7. *Widening or narrowing* **[M1999-108]**. On the counts, narrowing in Texas since 2021, while the company buys breadth (Vivint in 2023, the LS Power fleet in 2026) rather than widening the Texas moat.
8. *What could destroy, modify or reduce it, five to fifteen years out* **[M2000-014]**. Three things the filings name and none forecasts: (i) weather in an energy-only market: Uri cost NRG a gross $1,069M in a week in 2021, and NRG was made whole for $689M of it only by a law (FY2021 10-K, Item 7); retailers without that cushion failed (Just Energy sought creditor protection on 2021-03-09, `competitors.md`); (ii) the market's rules: the PUCT shelved the Performance Credit Mechanism in December 2024, adopted a firming requirement in December 2025, replaced the operating-reserve demand curve on 2025-12-05, and the legislature passed SB 6 on large loads and created the Texas Energy Fund (FY2025 10-K, Item 1), so the rules that set scarcity prices, which is where a Texas generator-retailer earns or loses its year, are rewritten every session; (iii) retail choice itself, which legislatures can narrow, as Maryland did. The filer writes no ten-year forecast of any of the three.
