## Q2 — WHY IS THE CASTLE STILL STANDING, and what keeps it standing ten to twenty years? STOP.
The question: "why is that castle still standing? And what’s going to keep it standing or cause it not to be standing five, 10, 20 years from now. What are the key factors? And how permanent are they?" **[M1995-038]**

- **The castle questions (what keeps it standing, how permanent).** The castle is a legal one. "There is no competition for electric distribution service within Virginia Power’s service territory in Virginia and North Carolina and no such competition is currently permitted"; for DESC, "There is no competition for electric distribution or generation service within DESC’s retail electric service territory in South Carolina" (FY2025 10-K, Item 1, Competition). The key factor is the certificate and the statute, and its permanence is the legislature's and the commissions' conduct, not the company's skill. The same filing names where the wall is thin: "Virginia Power has an exclusive franchise to serve retail electric customers in Virginia. However, Virginia’s Retail Access Statutes allow certain electric generation customers exceptions to this franchise. As market conditions change, Virginia Power’s customers may further pursue exceptions and Virginia Power’s exclusive franchise may erode." (Item 1A); and transmission is open to "competition from non-incumbent PJM transmission owners" under Order 1000 (Item 1).
- **Would it stand without the lord?** Yes in the sense the speakers mean **[M1996-037]**: the franchise does not need a superstar. But the owners' results have depended heavily on management's large bets, which went wrong twice in the span: the Atlantic Coast Pipeline loss of $2,405M in 2020 (FY2021 10-K cash-flow statement, "Loss from investment in Atlantic Coast Pipeline") and CVOW charges "for costs not expected to be recovered from customers" (FY2025 10-K, Item 7).
- **The money test.** "if I had a hundred million dollars and I wanted to go in and take on See’s Candy, could I do it?" **[M2011-015]**: no sum lets an attacker string a second distribution grid in Virginia; the law forbids it. The attackers that exist are the large customer leaving under the retail-access exceptions, the non-incumbent transmission builder, and rooftop or on-site generation (Item 1A names "customer–level distributed energy resources"); none has taken material share in the span (electricity delivered rose 5% in 2024 and 6% in 2025, Item 7).
- **Pricing power and the agony before a rise** **[M2005-020]**. The price is set by the commission, so every rise is a hearing. In the 2025 Biennial Review Virginia Power asked $822M from January 2026 and $345M more from 2027; the commission granted $566M and $210M and an ROE of 9.80% (Note 13). In 2023 the legislature itself set the ROE (9.70%) and from 2025 sends 85% of earnings up to 150 basis points above the authorized ROE, and 100% above that, back to customers (Item 1). This is not a moat's pricing power; the speakers say of the regulated field that the "threat of reregulation [...] has a tempering effect on their pricing power" **[M2007-112]**.
- **Unit volume.** Electricity delivered by Dominion Energy Virginia: 89.9, 94.5 and 100.2 million MWh in 2023, 2024 and 2025; distribution customer accounts 2,752, 2,782 and 2,809 thousand (Item 7). Data centers were 26% and 28% of Virginia Power's sales in 2024 and 2025, "primarily in Loudoun County" (Item 1).
- **The low-cost position, the brand, the low bid.** Not the tests that decide here: the retail customer cannot take the low bid, because there is no other bid, except the large customers the retail-access statutes let out.
- **Ask the competitors.** The 10-K names no SEC-filing rival for the deciding part (its utility territories admit none; Millstone's rivals are "other generating assets bidding to operate within the RTO", unnamed). See the competitor row below.
- **Widening or narrowing** **[M1999-108]**. Mixed, and on the evidence narrowing at the edges. Narrowing: the 2023 Virginia act took away the CCRO offsets and raised the share of over-earnings credited to customers (Item 1); the earned ROE in the 2023-24 test period was 7.77% against 9.70% (Note 13); Moody's outlook Negative (Item 7). In South Carolina the castle Dominion bought had already been breached: SCANA's abandonment of the NND nuclear project left DESC, "As a condition to the SCANA Merger Approval Order", to "provide refunds and restitution of $2.0 billion over 20 years with capital support from Dominion Energy" (FY2019 10-K), and the NND capital-cost rider "will continue to decline over the 20-year period" (FY2025 10-K, Item 1). Widening: the rate base grows with load, and from January 2027 high-load customers must sign "a 14-year contract, collateral over that period and demand minimums" (Item 1).

The regulatory climate, which the speakers name as the error of their own utility investing: "I did not anticipate or even consider the adverse developments in regulatory returns" **[L2023-011]**; a catastrophe at a single concentrated utility, "A catastrophic event can render a single utility insolvent" **[L2005-019]** (four reactors in Virginia, one in South Carolina, two at Millstone; coal ash at "19 different locations, including 12 at Virginia Power", Item 1A); and a fall in the data-center load that the capital plan is built for.
