**Keyence spends 2.4% to 3.0% of sales on R&D and earns 51%. Cognex spends 13% to 17% and
earns 16%.** Cognex spends roughly five times as much of each sales dollar on R&D as Keyence
does.

**But in absolute money the gap runs the other way, and this is the mechanism that matters.**
Keyence's own annual report prints a US dollar translation column alongside the yen (converted
at ¥160 = US$1, "the approximate exchange rate on March 19, 2026", the filing's own stated
convenience rate). Its FY2025 R&D appears there as **$205,249 thousand**. Cognex's FY2025
research, development and engineering expense was **$138,970 thousand**, per its FY2025 10-K
MD&A. So:

> **Keyence outspends Cognex on R&D by roughly 48% in absolute dollars while spending a fifth
> as much of each sales dollar, because its revenue base is roughly seven times larger.**

Both figures are as printed in the two companies' own filings; no conversion of my own is
involved. This is the most concrete mechanism in the file for why the margin gap might persist
rather than close: Cognex cannot out-invest Keyence by spending a larger percentage, because
Keyence's percentage is applied to a far larger base. Note the qualifier the convenience rate
carries: it is a single-date translation printed for reader convenience, not an average rate
for the year, so treat the 48% as an order of magnitude and not a precise ratio.

### B6a. THE LIST WAS NOT A ONE-OFF. It ran for at least three years before it was pulled.

I checked two vintages earlier than FY2019 to see whether the FY2019 naming was an anomaly.
It was not, it was the end of a practice.

**FY2017 10-K** (accession 0000851205-18-000004, `cgnx-20171231x10xk.htm`), verbatim:

> "Key competitors in geographies worldwide include Keyence Corporation, Sick AG, and Omron
> Corporation."

**FY2018 10-K** (accession 0000851205-19-000003, `cgnx-20181231x10xk.htm`), verbatim:

> "Key competitors in geographies worldwide include Keyence Corporation, Sick AG, Datalogic,
> and Omron Corporation."

So the sequence is: FY2017 three names, **Datalogic added for FY2018**, FY2019 unchanged at
four names, **FY2020 all four deleted**. Keyence is named first in every vintage in which any
name appears. Cognex named its rivals for at least four consecutive annual reports and then
stopped.

**The reading.** Over seven vintages, the disclosure moved in one direction: from four named
rivals plus a candid admission that Cognex could not size its own market, to no names at all
and a bare claim of leadership. Meanwhile the *risk factor* went the other way and got more
specific about the actual threat, which by FY2025 is AI and open source lowering the barrier
to entry, not the named incumbents. The FY2019 list is therefore the last time Cognex told
an investor, in a filing, who it thinks it is up against. That list is the spine of this
competitor row, and it is why Keyence is the crucial name.

**Caution against over-reading.** Deleting a competitor list is a common and legally
unremarkable edit. The SEC does not require naming competitors, and Item 1 lists get
trimmed for many reasons. I have not found any statement by Cognex explaining the removal,
and I did not look for one outside the 10-Ks. What the change does establish is that the
FY2019 list is Cognex's own, in its own words, and that no later filing contradicts it.

## SECTION C: KEYENCE CORPORATION (6861.T), THE CRUCIAL COMPARABLE

### C0. THE DISCLOSURE LIMIT, STATED FIRST

Keyence is **NOT an SEC registrant**. It files no 10-K, no 20-F and no 6-K. There is no
SEC XBRL companyfacts endpoint for it and no EDGAR CIK. Everything below comes from
Keyence's own **English-language Annual Report PDFs**, which contain the audited
consolidated financial statements with an unqualified opinion from **Deloitte Touche
Tohmatsu LLC**, prepared under **Japanese GAAP**, not IFRS and not US GAAP. From the
FY2025 annual report, Note 1, verbatim:

> "The accompanying consolidated financial statements have been prepared in accordance with
> the provisions set forth in the Japanese Financial Instruments and Exchange Act and its
> related accounting regulations and in accordance with accounting principles generally
> accepted in Japan ("Japanese GAAP"), which are different in certain respects as to the
> application and disclosure requirements of International Financial Reporting Standards."

The full annual securities report (the *yuho*) is filed in Japanese only. I did not read it.
The English annual report is the audited financial statements and is sufficient for revenue,
cost of sales, operating income, R&D and equity, which is everything this row needs.

### C1. FISCAL YEAR END: MARCH 20, NOT MARCH 31

Stated on every statement page: **"YEAR ENDED MARCH 20, 2026"**. Keyence uses a 20th-of-March
year end, not the March 31 used by most Japanese filers. It has not changed across the
period examined: the FY2019 annual report says "YEAR ENDED MARCH 20, 2019" and the FY2026
annual report says "YEAR ENDED MARCH 20, 2026".

**Labelling.** Keyence's own results materials label the year ended March 20, 2026 as
**"FY2025"** (document title: "KEYENCE CORPORATION FY2025 Financial Results Materials
April 24, 2026"). Its Annual Report PDF for the same year is titled "ANNUAL REPORT 2026".
So one year carries two names. I use **Keyence's own FY label** below, because it puts
Keyence FY2019 (year ended March 20, 2020, which is ~78% inside calendar 2019) alongside
Cognex FY2019 (calendar 2019). That is the closest honest alignment available. It is not
exact, and the mismatch matters most at turning points: Keyence FY2019 already carries about
three months of the COVID quarter that Cognex records in FY2020.

**Two derivations, both flagged.**
1. **Gross profit is DERIVED, not disclosed.** Keyence's income statement has no gross profit
   line. It runs: NET SALES, then a "COSTS AND EXPENSES" block containing Cost of Sales,
   Selling general and administrative, and Research and development, then Total costs and
   expenses, then OPERATING INCOME. I computed gross profit as net sales minus cost of sales.
   This is arithmetic on two disclosed figures, not an estimate, but it is my line and not
   Keyence's.
2. **ROE is net income over END-of-year total equity**, the same **CONVENTION** used for
   Cognex above, so the two are comparable to each other. Keyence does not print an ROE.

on its own line INSIDE total costs and expenses, parallel to SG&A. Cognex also reports R&D
separately below gross profit. So R&D is excluded from cost of sales in both, and the gross
margins are on the same footing in that respect. Keyence's R&D accounting policy, verbatim:

> "Research and Development Costs - Research and development costs are charged to income as
> incurred."

No capitalization. Same treatment as Cognex.

## SECTION E: WHY COGNEX'S GROSS MARGIN FELL. COGNEX'S OWN ANSWER.

This matters because the 7.6-point gross margin decline is the single most important fact in

**FY2024 10-K** (the largest single-year drop, 71.8% to 68.4%):

> "Gross margin decreased to 68% in 2024 compared to 72% in 2023. The decrease was due to a
> less favorable revenue mix in 2024 that included higher logistics revenue and products with
> relatively lower gross margins from the Moritex acquisition that closed in the fourth
> quarter of 2023, as well as the amortization of Moritex acquired technologies. **Lower
> average selling prices due to pricing pressures** also contributed to the lower margin in
> 2024."

**FY2025 10-K:**

> "Gross margin decreased to 67% in 2025 compared to 68% in 2024. The decrease was primarily
> due to a $13 million charge recorded in the fourth quarter of 2025 for excess and obsolete
> inventory following a comprehensive strategic product portfolio review under our new
> leadership team. As part of this strategic review, the Company is reducing focus on certain
> legacy products, which increased the risk of excess and obsolete inventory. Less favorable

its largest named competitor is not visibly under the same pressure.

## SECTION F: METHOD, AND WHAT "SAME-METRIC, SAME-WINDOW" COST

This row deliberately does NOT normalise everything into one comparable number, because doing
so would require estimates the filings do not support. Instead every deviation is named.

**Metrics that ARE the same across Cognex, Keyence, Zebra and Basler:**
- Revenue.
- Gross margin, in the sense of (revenue minus cost of sales) / revenue, with R&D excluded
  from cost of sales in all four. Keyence's is derived, Cognex's, Zebra's and Basler's are
  disclosed lines.
- Operating margin, fully loaded, at the company level.
- R&D expensed as incurred, as a percent of revenue. **Exception: Basler capitalises part of
  its development cost, so its P&L R&D charge is not on the same basis; both its gross and
  expensed figures are carried.**

**Metrics that are NOT the same and must not be put in one column:**
- Zebra's AVA segment operating margin (excludes share-based compensation, intangible
  amortization, restructuring and acquisition costs).
- Omron's operating income (struck BEFORE restructuring expense, which Omron reports below the
  operating line; a US filer would run it through operating expenses).
- Emerson's Test & Measurement segment EBIT (carries purchase-accounting amortization that
  never existed in NI's own P&L).
- Teledyne's Digital Imaging segment (a components business plus thermal, X-ray and space
  imaging, not a factory machine-vision business).

**ROE is a CONVENTION applied uniformly by me:** net income divided by END-of-year book
equity. Omron is the exception and is noted in its section: Omron discloses its own ROE on an
average-equity basis, so its column is the company's figure, not mine, and is not strictly
comparable to the others.

**Currency.** No figure is translated. Keyence is in yen, Basler and Datalogic in euro,
Cognex, Zebra, NI, Emerson and Teledyne in dollars, Omron in yen. Margins and ratios are
currency-invariant, which is why this row is built on margins rather than on absolute size.
Any cross-currency revenue comparison in this file would be a new number and is not made.

**Fiscal-year alignment.** Cognex, Zebra, Basler, Datalogic, NI and Teledyne are on calendar
or near-calendar years. Keyence ends March 20 and Omron ends March 31, so both are offset by
roughly one quarter. Emerson ends September 30. The offsets are stated in each section and
are NOT adjusted away.

## SECTION H: THE TWO QUESTIONS THE ROW WAS BUILT TO ANSWER

### H1. How does Keyence's operating margin compare to Cognex's across the same years?

**Keyence earns between 2.5 and 4.1 times Cognex's operating margin, in every single year,
and the gap has WIDENED since 2022.**

| Year | Cognex OM % | Keyence OM % | Gap in points | Keyence as a multiple |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2019 | 19.7 | 50.3 | 30.6 | 2.6x |
| 2020 | 21.0 | 51.4 | 30.4 | 2.4x |
| 2021 | 30.4 | 55.4 | 25.0 | 1.8x |
| 2022 | 24.5 | 54.1 | 29.6 | 2.2x |
| 2023 | 15.6 | 51.2 | 35.6 | 3.3x |
| 2024 | 12.6 | 51.9 | **39.3** | **4.1x** |
| 2025 | 16.3 | 51.0 | 34.7 | 3.1x |

Three observations that the table makes and I did not assume:

1. **Keyence's margin is stable and Cognex's is not.** Over seven years Keyence ranged from
   50.3% to 55.4%, a spread of 5.1 points. Cognex ranged from 12.6% to 30.4%, a spread of 17.8
   points, three and a half times as wide. Keyence's WORST year in the period (50.3%, FY2019)
   is 20 points better than Cognex's BEST year (30.4%, 2021).
2. **The narrowest gap was 2021, the boom.** Cognex closed to 1.8x at the top of the automation
   cycle. That is the year a bull case would point to. It has not been repeated, and the gap
   in 2024 was the widest of the seven years.
3. **Keyence did not need the boom.** Keyence's operating margin in FY2020 (51.4%, a year in
   which its sales FELL from 551,843 to 538,134) was higher than in FY2019. It held above 50%
   through a revenue decline. Cognex's fell from 30.4% to 12.6% on a revenue decline of similar
   proportion. That is the clearest single piece of evidence in this file about the relative
   durability of the two franchises.

**The honest qualifier, restated.** Keyence's 51% is a whole-company number and Keyence
discloses no vision-segment margin (Section C5). It is NOT established that Keyence earns 51%
on machine vision specifically. What is established is that the company Cognex named first
among its competitors earns roughly three times Cognex's return on sales, consistently, and
that no filing from either company contradicts this.

  inventory correction → 47.4 by 2025. Basler explains the 2025 recovery verbatim as coming

**The conclusion the row supports.** The 2022 to 2025 window contained a genuine industry-wide
shock, visible in Basler's 6-point collapse and Zebra's 2022 trough and Omron's FY2023
collapse to a 4.2% operating margin. **Every one of those companies has recovered its gross
margin. Cognex has not.** Cognex's gross margin in 2025 is its lowest in the period and is
still falling. That is the fact a Q2 franchise verdict has to survive.

### H3. THE MORITEX COUNTER-ARGUMENT, AND HOW FAR IT ACTUALLY GOES

The obvious defence of Cognex's gross margin decline is that it bought a lower-margin
business. That defence is real but it is **bounded**, and the bound can be computed from
Cognex's own disclosures.

**What Cognex discloses.** FY2024 10-K, verbatim:

> "Revenue from the acquisition of Moritex that closed in the fourth quarter of 2023
> represented approximately 8% of total revenue in 2024 and 1% of total revenue in 2023."

> "Excluding Moritex, revenue increased by 1% in 2024 over the prior year due to higher
> revenue from customers in the logistics and semiconductor industries, partially offset by
> lower revenue from customers in the automotive industry and softness across our broader
> factory automation business."

And on the acquisition itself, FY2024 10-K, verbatim:

> "In the fourth quarter of 2023, we acquired Moritex Corporation, a global provider of
> premium optical components based in Japan. With an enterprise value of approximately $270
> million, this was Cognex's largest acquisition to-date."

**The bound (labelled arithmetic, NOT an estimate of Moritex's real margin).** Cognex's gross
margin fell from 71.8% in 2023 to 68.4% in 2024, a drop of 3.4 points, in the year Moritex went
from 1% to 8% of revenue. If the entire drop were mix and the non-Moritex business had held
71.8%, then Moritex's own gross margin would have to have been about **29%** to produce the
observed blend (0.92 × 71.8 + 0.08 × x = 68.4). Cognex does **NOT** disclose Moritex's gross
margin, so 29% is not a finding about Moritex. It is the answer to a different question: *how
low would Moritex have to be for mix alone to explain the whole decline?* A ~29% gross margin
would be very low even for an optical components manufacturer, which suggests mix alone does
**not** explain all of it. Cognex's own text agrees, since it lists three causes and not one:
mix, "the amortization of Moritex acquired technologies", and "lower average selling prices
due to pricing pressures".

**And Moritex does not explain 2025 at all.** Moritex was in the base for both 2024 and 2025,
yet gross margin fell a further 1.5 points to 66.9%. Cognex attributes that year to an
inventory write-down, industry mix and tariffs (Section E).

**Verdict on this sub-question: UNRESEARCHED, resolvable.** The document that would settle the
split is a disclosure of Moritex standalone gross margin, or a Cognex ex-Moritex gross margin
series. Neither appears in the 10-Ks I read. Cognex gives ex-Moritex REVENUE growth but not
ex-Moritex GROSS MARGIN. Before Q2 is decided, the remaining places to look are the FY2024 and
FY2025 earnings call transcripts and the Q4 2023 8-K on the acquisition. **Do not close Q2
either way until that is done.**

## SECTION M: THE EUROPEAN PEERS' VERDICT ON THE INDUSTRY SHOCK

This is the most analytically important thing the two European pure-plays contribute, and it
sharpens Section H2.

**Gross margin, 2022 to 2025:**

| | FY2022 | FY2023 | FY2024 | FY2025 | 2022 → 2025 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Basler | 48.4 | 42.2 | 45.1 *restated* | 47.4 | **−1.0 pt** |
| Datalogic *as first reported* | 41.9 | 41.8 | 42.1 | 42.8 | **+0.9 pt** |
| Datalogic *on restated FY2022 base* | 40.1 | 41.8 | 42.1 | 42.8 | **+2.7 pts** |
| **Cognex** | **71.8** | **71.8** | **68.4** | **66.9** | **−4.9 pts** |

**EBIT / operating margin, 2022 to 2025:**

| | FY2022 | FY2023 | FY2024 | FY2025 | 2022 → 2025 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Basler | 10.6 | −10.8 | −5.5 | 7.9 | **−2.7 pts** |

| **Cognex** | **24.5** | **15.6** | **12.6** | **16.3** | **−8.2 pts** |

**The finding.** The European peers **did not lose gross margin** after the automation boom.
They lost **operating** margin, and the disclosed arithmetic says why: they held R&D roughly
constant in absolute euros through a revenue decline of 17% (Basler, €272.2m to €224.5m) to
24% (Datalogic, €654.6m to €500.1m). Datalogic's R&D ratio went from 9.4% to 13.2% of revenue

erosion. Neither company attributes its margin path to price: Basler cites cost reduction,
regional mix and capacity utilisation; Datalogic cites "improved productivity" and mix.

**Why this matters for Cognex.** It splits the industry shock into two effects and tells you
which one Cognex has. Everyone in the row suffered the operating-deleverage effect. **Only
Cognex also lost gross margin.** Basler's gross margin fell 6.2 points in one year and won back
5.2 of them; Cognex's has fallen every year since 2020 and has won back none. Whatever is
happening at the gross line at Cognex is not the industry cycle, because the industry cycle is
visible in these two companies and it did not do this to their gross margins.

is sales less **cost of materials only**. It does not deduct manufacturing labour, manufacturing
overhead, depreciation on production assets, or any of the other conversion costs that sit
inside Cognex's, Keyence's, Zebra's and Basler's cost of sales. The 70.5% and Cognex's 66.9%
are different measurements of different things, and comparing them would make a 3.0%-EBIT-margin
sensor manufacturer look more profitable at the gross line than Cognex.

**Verdict: a Cognex-comparable gross margin for SICK is UNKNOWABLE from the published
statements**, because the cost-of-sales split is never disclosed under this format. This is the
single most dangerous number in the whole competitor row and it is recorded here so nobody
picks it up later.

**Machine vision revenue: UNAVAILABLE.** Obstacle: SICK reports by field of business (factory
automation, logistics automation, process automation), not by product. Closest disclosed figure
is factory automation sales of EUR 1,185 million in 2023.

**What SICK adds.** A business roughly twice Cognex's revenue earning a **3.0% EBIT margin in
2025** while spending 12.9% of sales on R&D, and shedding 2,000 employees in one year. It is
the clearest evidence in the row that a sensor and code-reading hardware business without a
software attach earns very little, and it is the low end of the range Cognex's named
competitors occupy.

## SECTION Q: WHAT THIS ROW SETTLES, AND WHAT IT DOES NOT

**Settled, from filings:**

1. **Cognex named Keyence first among its competitors in four consecutive 10-Ks (FY2017 to
   FY2019) and has named nobody since FY2020.** The naming is Cognex's own and is independently
   corroborated by Zebra, which names Cognex, Keyence and SICK in both its FY2022 and FY2025
   10-Ks.
2. **Keyence's operating margin is 2.4x to 4.1x Cognex's in every one of the seven years, and
   the gap was at its widest in 2024.** Keyence's worst year beats Cognex's best year by 20
   points.
3. **Keyence's margin is stable through revenue declines and Cognex's is not.** Keyence held
   above 50% in a year its sales fell; Cognex fell from 30.4% to 12.6% on a comparable decline.
4. **The 2022 to 2025 industry shock was real and was not Cognex-specific.** It is visible in
   Basler (EBIT from +13.2% to −10.8%), Datalogic (EBIT 6.3% to 0.6%), Omron IAB (18.0% to
   5.4%) and SICK (EBIT margin to 3.0%).
5. **But the shock hit the OPERATING line, not the gross line, everywhere except Cognex.**
   Basler, Datalogic, Zebra, Omron and Keyence all have flat or higher gross margins in 2025
   than 2022. Cognex is down 4.9 points over that window and 7.6 points from its 2020 peak, and
   is the only monotonic decline in the set.
6. **Nobody in the industry discloses machine-vision revenue separately.** Not Keyence, not
   Omron, not Zebra, not Datalogic, not Teledyne, not SICK, not Advantech, not Hikvision. The
   competitor row is therefore necessarily built on company-level and segment-level economics,
   and every attempt to size the machine-vision market from filings fails. Cognex said as much
   itself until FY2022: "reliable estimates of the machine vision market and the number and
   relative size of competitors are not readily available."

**NOT settled, and the row should not be used as if it were:**

1. **It is NOT established that Keyence earns 51% on machine vision specifically.** That is a
   whole-company number covering sensors, measuring instruments, laser markers, microscopes,
   3D printers and PLCs as well as vision. Section C5.
2. **It is NOT established how much of Cognex's gross margin decline is Moritex mix versus
   pricing power.** Section H3 bounds it but does not resolve it, and the resolving document
   was not read. **This blocks a clean Q2 verdict.**
3. **Cross-company operating margins are not all the same metric.** Omron's is struck before
   restructuring, Zebra's AVA excludes share-based compensation and amortization, Emerson's
   carries purchase accounting, Basler capitalises R&D. Section F lists every deviation.
4. **No conclusion about Q2 is drawn here.** This file is evidence for the competitor row, which
   is one input to Q2. Under the operator protocol, computation is not clearance, and this
   document contains no entry language and no valuation.
