Research date: 2026-09-07. Status: COMPLETE. Fourteen annual report PDFs read (seven per
company, FY2019 to FY2025). Every number below carries a document, a fiscal year and a URL.
No aggregator was used as a source for any figure.

## Registrant status (both companies)

**NEITHER COMPANY IS AN SEC REGISTRANT.** Basler AG is listed on the Frankfurt Stock
Exchange (Prime Standard, ISIN DE0005102008, ticker BSL). Datalogic S.p.A. is listed on
Borsa Italiana (Euronext Milan / STAR segment, ISIN IT0004135213, ticker DAL). Neither
files a 10-K, 10-Q, 20-F or 6-K; neither appears in SEC EDGAR or SEC XBRL financial
statement data sets.

What this limits, concretely:
- No SEC full-text search, no EDGAR accession numbers, no SEC XBRL company-facts API.
- Disclosure follows **IFRS as adopted by the EU** plus local rules: German HGB /
  WpHG / Bundesanzeiger publication for Basler; Italian Consob / Borsa Italiana STAR
  rules for Datalogic.
- Consequence for the framework's operator rule 4 ("the filing gets read"): the primary
  document is the company's own annual report PDF, published on its IR site and filed to
  the German Bundesanzeiger / the Italian Consob storage mechanism (eMarket STORAGE).
  Those PDFs are the equivalent of the 10-K here.
- Line-item comparability with Cognex is NOT automatic. See the format note under Basler.

# COMPANY 1: BASLER AG (BSL.DE, Ahrensburg, Germany)

## Format note: gross margin IS directly disclosed (the brief's caveat does not bind)

Basler does **not** use the nature-of-expense (Gesamtkostenverfahren) format. Its
consolidated income statement uses the **cost-of-sales method (Umsatzkostenverfahren)**
across the whole 2019-2025 window, so **gross profit is a disclosed line item**, labelled
"Gross profit on sales", and a "Gross profit margin" percentage appears in the Key Figures
table on page 2 of every annual report. This is unusual for a German mid-cap and is
convenient here: the line is directly comparable in construction to Cognex's gross margin.

Two structural caveats before comparing to Cognex:
1. Basler inserts a **"Currency earnings"** line between Sales revenues and Cost of sales.
   Gross profit on sales = Sales revenues + Currency earnings − Cost of sales. Cognex has
   no such line, so Basler's gross margin carries an FX effect that Cognex's does not.
   (2025: currency earnings were −€2,767k; 2024: +€1,211k.)
2. **FY2024 was restated** in the FY2025 report under IAS 8 and IAS 1. Both versions are
   shown below. Do not mix them.

# COMPANY 2: DATALOGIC S.p.A. (DAL.MI, Bologna, Italy)

## Format notes, and one naming trap you must not fall into

1. **Gross profit IS disclosed**, in the "Group reclassified income results" table of the
   Report on Operations, under the label **"Gross Operating Margin"** (= Revenue minus Cost
   of goods sold). The percentage of revenue is printed alongside every line.
2. **THE TRAP.** Datalogic's own glossary of alternative performance measures uses the
   phrase "Gross Operating Margin" a *second* time, as a synonym for **EBITDA**. Verbatim,
   from the FY2025 Annual Report: *"EBITDA (Earnings Before Interest, Taxes, Depreciation
   and Amortisation) or Gross Operating Margin: profit/(loss) for the year from continuing
   operations before depreciation and amortisation of tangible and intangible fixed assets
   and rights of use, financials (including foreign exchange income and expense) and income
   tax."* The same document therefore uses one label for two different numbers. In the
   reclassified P&L it means gross profit (42.8% of sales in FY2025); in the glossary and
   the KPI dashboard it means EBITDA (10.6% of sales in FY2025). **Everything I report
   below as "Gross profit" is the revenue-minus-COGS line, not EBITDA.** Any figure lifted
   from a third-party database labelled "gross operating margin" for Datalogic should be
   checked against this.
3. **Datalogic headlines Adjusted EBITDA**, not gross margin and not EBIT. The KPI page of
   every recent annual report leads with "Adjusted EBITDA" in euro and as % of revenue.
4. **Three restatement events break the series.** Comparability across the window is the
   main obstacle here, not availability:
   - **FY2019 → FY2020 report**: revenue restated from €612.5m down to €585.8m and the
     industry split reallocated heavily (discontinued operations / reallocation).
   - **FY2022 → FY2023 report**: IAS 1 reclassification of cost items **from operating
     costs into cost of goods sold**. This moved the FY2022 gross margin from 41.9% to
     40.1% with no change in revenue or EBIT. **The FY2022 gross margin you use determines
     whether Datalogic's gross margin looks like it fell or rose into 2025.** See the
     direction note at the end.
   - **FY2023 → FY2024 report**: IFRS 5 restatement following the sale of Informatics
     Holdings, Inc. (results moved to discontinued operations), plus an IAS 1
     reclassification of certain commercial costs as a reduction of revenue. FY2023 revenue
     restated from €536.6m to €518.7m.

# THE QUESTION THE ANALYSIS TURNS ON: MARGIN DIRECTION, 2022 TO 2025

The brief asks whether the industry's margins compressed after the 2021-22 automation boom.
Filing-sourced answer, both companies, 2022 to 2025:

## Gross margin

| | FY2022 | FY2023 | FY2024 | FY2025 | 2022 → 2025 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Basler** | 48.4 % | 42.2 % | 45.7 % (45.1 % restated) | 47.4 % | **−1.0 pp** |
| **Datalogic** (as first reported each year) | 41.9 % | 41.8 % | 42.1 % | 42.8 % | **+0.9 pp** |
| **Datalogic** (on the FY2023 restated FY2022 base) | 40.1 % | 41.8 % | 42.1 % | 42.8 % | **+2.7 pp** |

**Gross margin did NOT compress on a 2022-to-2025 basis at either company.** It collapsed
in the middle of the window and then recovered. Basler's dropped 6.2 points in a single
year (2022 to 2023) and has since won back 5.2 of them, ending 1.0 point below 2022.
Datalogic's is flat-to-up on any base you choose. The shape is a V, not a step down.

Basler's own stated mechanism for the 2025 recovery is cost reduction, regional mix and
capacity utilisation, partly offset by FX, price-sensitive large projects and tariffs.
Datalogic's stated mechanism for FY2024 and FY2025 is *"improved productivity"* plus, in
FY2025, *"a favourable mix"*. Neither company attributes the recovery to price.

## EBIT margin

| | FY2022 | FY2023 | FY2024 | FY2025 | 2022 → 2025 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Basler** (computed EBIT / sales) | 10.6 % | −10.8 % | −5.3 % (−5.5 % restated) | 7.9 % | **−2.7 pp** |
| **Datalogic** EBIT (disclosed) | 6.3 % | 1.8 % (1.2 % restated) | 0.6 % | 2.0 % | **−4.3 pp** |
| **Datalogic** Adjusted EBIT (disclosed) | 7.5 % | 3.1 % (2.6 % restated) | 2.2 % | 4.2 % | **−3.3 pp** |
| **Datalogic** Adjusted EBITDA (disclosed) | 12.3 % | 9.2 % (8.9 % restated) | 9.0 % | 10.6 % | **−1.7 pp** |

**EBIT margin DID compress, at both companies, and by more than gross margin did.** This is
the finding that matters. The compression is not in the cost of the product; it is below
the gross line.

The arithmetic of why, in the companies' own disclosed numbers:
- **Basler** held gross margin roughly flat 2022 to 2025 (−1.0 pp) while its **R&D ratio
  moved from 12.3 % to 12.8 %** and revenue fell 17 % from the 2022 peak (€272.2m to
  €224.5m). Operating leverage, not price, took the EBIT margin from 10.6 % to 7.9 %. It
  peaked at 13.2 % in 2021.
- **Datalogic** *improved* gross margin 2022 to 2025 while its **R&D ratio went from 9.4 %
  to 13.2 %**, a 3.8-point increase, on revenue that fell 24 % from the 2022 peak (€654.6m
  to €500.1m). Almost the entire EBIT-margin loss is explained by R&D held roughly constant
  in euros (€61.5m to €65.9m) while the revenue base shrank by a quarter.

So the honest one-line statement for the competitor row is: **the European machine-vision
peers did not lose gross margin after the automation boom, they lost operating margin, and
they lost it because they kept spending on R&D through a revenue decline of 17 % to 24 %
from the 2022 peak.** Both are still spending: Basler committed publicly to a 13 % EBIT
margin on €275m of sales by 2028, and Datalogic's R&D ratio is at a seven-year high.

## Peak-to-trough, for scale

| | Peak revenue | Trough revenue | FY2025 revenue | FY2025 vs peak |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Basler | €272.2m (2022) | €183.7m (2024) | €224.5m | −17.5 % |
| Datalogic | €654.6m (2022) | €493.8m (2024) | €500.1m | −23.6 % |

Both bottomed in FY2024 and both grew in FY2025. Basler's FY2025 recovery (+22 % revenue,
EBIT from −€10.1m to +€17.7m) is far sharper than Datalogic's (+1.3 % revenue, EBIT from
€2.9m to €9.9m).

## Method statement

Fourteen PDFs were downloaded directly from the two companies' own IR domains
(`assets-ctf.baslerweb.com` and `www.datalogic.com`), the full text extracted locally, and
every figure read off the income statement, the notes, or the company's own Key Figures /
Highlights tables. Each fiscal year from 2019 to 2024 was cross-checked against at least two
separate annual reports for both companies, because each report carries multi-year
comparatives. Every disagreement between reports is carried above as two rows rather than
reconciled away. **No aggregator (Macrotrends, StockAnalysis, MarketScreener, Yahoo) was
used as a source for any number in this file.**
