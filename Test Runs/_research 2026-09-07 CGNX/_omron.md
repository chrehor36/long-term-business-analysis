## 0. REGISTRANT STATUS AND WHAT IT LIMITS

**OMRON Corporation is NOT an SEC registrant.** It is listed on the Tokyo Stock Exchange under
securities code 6645. It files no 10-K, no 20-F and no 6-K. There is no EDGAR accession number
for any figure in this file. Consequences, stated plainly:

- **No SEC-audited English annual report exists.** Every English document used here is a
  company-prepared translation of a Japanese original, and each says so on its face. The
  standing caveat, verbatim from the results releases: *"This document has been translated from
  the Japanese original as a guide to non-Japanese investors and contains forward-looking
  statements that are based on managements' estimates, assumptions and projections at the time
  of publication."*
- **No EDGAR XBRL.** There is no `companyfacts` / `frames` cross-check of the kind available for
  a US filer such as Cognex. Omron files XBRL with the TSE and EDINET, in Japanese.
- **The statutory filing is Japanese.** The binding legal document is the
  `yuka shoken hokokusho` (Annual Securities Report). The English *"Summary of Consolidated
  Financial Results"* (kessan tanshin) is a summary release, not the statutory filing. Omron
  itself points this out, verbatim in the Fact Book: *"Please refer to English reference
  translation of Annual Securities Report for the latest audited figures."*
- **Operator rule 4 compliance.** Nine primary PDFs were downloaded and read (seven annual
  kessan tanshin, the Financial Fact Book 2026, and the Integrated Report 2024 IAB chapter). No
  figure was screened from tagged data. **Every company-level figure below appears in at least
  two independently published Omron documents and they agree** (see section 6).

### What the segment shape says for a Cognex comparison

IAB ran a **15%–18% operating margin from FY2019 through FY2022**, collapsed to **5.5% in
FY2023**, and has recovered only to **10.4% in FY2025** even though FY2025 sales (409,478) are
within 16% of the FY2022 record (485,738). Two readings follow. First, this is a cyclical
capital-goods business whose trough margin is roughly a third of its peak. Second, IAB at its
best earned mid-to-high-teens operating margins on a sales base roughly ten times Cognex's,
which is a different economic animal from a focused vision-and-ID pure play. The margin gap
between the two, whatever it is, has to be attributed before it is treated as evidence of moat.

## 4. ACCOUNTING STANDARD AND COMPARABILITY

### 4a. The standard, and why the brief's premise is INVERTED

**Correction to the task brief.** The brief anticipated that Omron "reports under IFRS from a
certain year," with earlier years on US GAAP or JGAAP. The record runs the other way for the
window requested.

**All seven fiscal years FY2019 through FY2025 are reported under U.S. GAAP.** The standard is
named in the title of every one of the seven annual releases, verbatim: **"(U.S. GAAP)"**. The
Fact Book states it for the whole eleven-year table, verbatim: *"The financial statements are
prepared in accordance with U.S. GAAP standards."* The FY2025 segment note states it again,
verbatim: *"The segment information is presented in accordance with accounting principles
generally accepted in the United States of America."* and cites the US standard by number,
verbatim: *"ASC 280, \"Segment Reporting,\" establishes the disclosure of information about
operating segments in financial statements."*

**IFRS begins AFTER the window.** Omron's first IFRS reporting is the fiscal year beginning
1 April 2026 (FY2026, ending 31 March 2027). Evidence, verbatim from the title page:
*"Consolidated Financial Results for the First Quarter of the Fiscal Year Ending March 31, 2027
(Under IFRS)"*
(https://www.omron.com/global/en/assets/file/ir/irlib/20260805_financial_results.pdf, 5 Aug 2026).
The last U.S. GAAP annual is the year ended 31 March 2026.

**Net effect for the CGNX row: there is NO accounting-standard break inside FY2019–FY2025.** The
seven-year series sits on one basis, and it is the same basis Cognex reports on, which makes the
gross-margin and operating-margin comparison unusually clean. Two caveats survive: this is a
Japanese company's application of US GAAP, not audited by a PCAOB-registered auditor for SEC
purposes; and a constant standard does not mean constant *segments* (see 4b).

Each release answers the standing question "(a) Changes in accounting policy accompanying
revision of accounting standards, etc.". Answers by document, verbatim: FY2019 **"Yes"** ·
FY2020 **"No"** · FY2021 **"Yes"** · FY2022 **"No"** · FY2023 **"No"** · FY2024 **"No"** ·
FY2025 **"No"**. The two "Yes" years are ordinary new-pronouncement adoptions, not a change of
framework. **Which specific pronouncements were adopted in FY2019 and FY2021 is UNRESEARCHED**;
the document that would resolve it is the corresponding Annual Securities Report.

### 4b. Five real comparability breaks, none of them a standard change

**Break 1, FY2019: AEC classified as discontinued operations.** Verbatim: *"With the April 16,
2019, determination to transfer the Automotive Electronic Components Business (AEC), OMRON has
classified this business unit as discontinued operations."* The Fact Book confirms the reach,
verbatim: *"In connection with the classification of AEC, transferred on October 31, 2019, as a
discontinued operation, information figures from FY2018 have been restated to reflect this
change."* **Effect: FY2018 onward is on the ex-AEC basis, so FY2019–FY2023 are internally
consistent. Any Omron figure published for FY2017 or earlier is not comparable to this table.**
FY2019 net income of 74,895 nonetheless **includes** the disposal gain (see section 2).

**Break 2, FY2020: segment count cut from five to four.** Verbatim: *"Due to the transfer of the
Environmental Solutions Business under the SSB and the closure of the Backlights Business, the
Company revised business classifications at the beginning of the fiscal year ended March 2021,
reclassifying operations under Other Businesses to the SSB or to Eliminations & Corporate, and
disclosing results for four segments (IAB, EMC, SSB, and HCB)."* **IAB was not touched**: the
FY2019 IAB figures 352,762 / 53,595 are identical in the 2020 and 2021 releases and in the Fact
Book. The IAB row is unaffected.

**Break 3, FY2022: businesses moved OUT of IAB into DMB. This one HITS the IAB row.** Verbatim
from the FY2022 release: *"Due to a revision of business management classifications, certain
businesses of the IAB have been transferred to the DMB beginning with the fiscal year ended March
2023. Accordingly, the Company reclassified results for the fiscal year ended March 2022 under
this new categorization for presentation herein."* And from the Fact Book, which states the
restatement reach precisely, verbatim: *"From FY2022, as a result of a segment reclassification,
figures for certain business that had been included in IAB have been reclassified into DMB.
FY2020-2021 figures have been restated to reflect this."* **Effect: FY2020 and FY2021 IAB were
restated (FY2020 external sales 346,446 → 335,621, operating income 58,793 → 57,182; FY2021
432,637 → 418,107 and 78,103 → 76,257). FY2019 was NOT restated.** The FY2020–FY2025 IAB series
in section 3 is comparable; FY2019 is not strictly comparable to it.

**Break 4, FY2025: DMB classified as a discontinued operation. This is the LARGEST break, and it
hits the company totals.** Verbatim from the FY2025 segment note: *"In connection with the
decision to transfer the DMB, the Company classified DMB as a discontinued operation and removed
the business from the segment information for the consolidated fiscal years ended March 2025 and
2026."* And on the face of the summary, verbatim: *"Due to the classification of DMB (Devices &
Module Solutions Business) as a discontinued operation during the current fiscal year..."* with
the note *"Accordingly, we have reclassified the previous year's results for net sales, gross
profit margin, operating income, and income from continuing operations before income..."*

FY2024 as first reported versus as restated:

| FY2024 (YE 31 Mar 2025) | As first reported (May 2025) | As restated (May 2026 / Fact Book 2026) | Delta |
|---|---|---|---|
| Net sales | 801,753 | 715,379 | −86,374 |
| Gross profit | 357,142 | 330,287 | −26,855 |
| Gross margin | 44.5% | 46.2% | +1.7 pt |
| Operating income | 54,038 | 53,446 | −592 |
| Operating margin | 6.7% | 7.5% | +0.8 pt |
| R&D expense | 44,339 | 39,960 | −4,379 |
| R&D % of sales | 5.5% | 5.6% | +0.1 pt |

**Comparing FY2025's 767,351 against FY2023's 818,761 or FY2022's 876,082 is WRONG.** The
earlier figures include DMB; the later do not. On a like-for-like continuing-operations basis
FY2025 sales grew 7.3% over FY2024 (767,351 vs 715,379). **Omron has NOT published a restated
continuing-operations series back beyond FY2024**, and its own Fact Book carries the
discontinuity un-bridged, stating verbatim: *"In connection with the classification of DMB as a
discontinued operation, information figures for FY2024 have been restated to reflect this
change."* Only FY2024. **Building a restated FY2019–FY2023 continuing-operations series is
UNRESEARCHED**; the document that would resolve it, if any, is the FY2026 Annual Securities
Report. It may simply not exist, in which case it is UNKNOWABLE.

Note also that DMB's removal *raises* reported margins: the discontinued components business
carried lower gross margin than the group, so FY2024 gross margin improves by 1.7 points and
operating margin by 0.8 points purely from the reclassification. **A reader comparing FY2025's
45.7% gross margin to FY2022's 45.0% is comparing two different companies.**

**Break 5, FY2023: DSB created; FY2025: Omron Digital moved.** Verbatim: *"The DSB is a new
segment established in the third quarter of the fiscal year ending March 31, 2024, and
disclosures related to this segment began in the same period."* DSB grew sharply on the JMDC
consolidation (17,370 → 42,738 → 51,151 external sales, FY2023 → FY2025). And verbatim: *"Since
FY2025, the financial results of Omron Digital Co., Ltd., which had been included under SSB, will
be included under Eliminations & Corporate. As a result, we have reclassified segment information
results for FY2024."* Neither touches IAB.

### 4c. Not a break, but read it before using FY2024–FY2025 operating margin

FY2023's operating income collapse to 34,342 and the FY2024–FY2025 recovery are struck around
Structural Reform Program NEXT2025. FY2024 income before taxes carries, verbatim: *"one-time
expenses of JPY22.0 billion incurred to optimize the number and capacity of our workforce related
to Structural Reform Program NEXT2025"*, plus *"a write-down of goodwill of JPY11.7 billion
related to the Data Solution Business and a JPY12.3 billion in gain on valuation of investment
securities."*

**Critical presentational point: restructuring expenses of 27,781 (FY2024) and 23,795 (FY2025 as
restated) and 2,617 (FY2025) sit BELOW the operating income line, not in it.** Omron's operating
income is struck before restructuring. Against a US filer that runs restructuring through
operating expenses, Omron's FY2024–FY2025 operating margin is flattered by roughly 3.3 points in
FY2024. **Anyone putting Omron's 7.5%–7.8% operating margin next to Cognex's must decide whether
to deduct it.** Deducting it (my arithmetic, from disclosed figures): FY2024 restated becomes
(53,446 − 23,795) / 715,379 = **4.1%**, not 7.5%; FY2024 as first reported becomes
(54,038 − 27,781) / 801,753 = **3.3%**, not 6.7%; FY2025 becomes
(59,935 − 2,617) / 767,351 = **7.5%**, not 7.8%. The distortion is large in FY2024 and small in
FY2025, so the apparent FY2024-to-FY2025 margin recovery of 0.3 points is really about
3.4 points once restructuring is charged against the year it belongs to.

### 5c. What is NOT disclosed, and the obstacle

**Machine vision / code reader revenue: NOT DISCLOSED.** Obstacle: Omron's finest published
revenue grain is the operating segment. The FY2025 segment note states the basis verbatim: *"The
CODM uses segment income or loss to allocate resources to each segment and to assess comparison
between plans and actual results in evaluating business performance of segments."* Segment income
is the finest measure the chief operating decision maker uses, so ASC 280 requires nothing below
it. The closest Omron comes is the FY2023 product split quoted in section 3 (**"40% Input"**),
which bundles vision with every other sensing device. **Applying the framework's four-verdict
test: I cannot name a document Omron publishes that would resolve vision-line revenue.** Verdict:
**UNKNOWABLE from Omron's disclosure**, not merely unresearched. It could only be approached
through third-party market research, which is not a primary source and is not used here.

**Vision-line gross margin, R&D, headcount, or unit volumes: NOT DISCLOSED.** Same obstacle,
same verdict.

**IAB R&D for years other than FY2023: UNRESEARCHED.** Obstacle: the IAB R&D figure appears only
in the Integrated Report's per-segment "INPUT / OUTPUT / OUTCOME" panel, one year at a time. The
resolving documents are named and exist: the Integrated Report 2020 through 2026 IAB chapters.
Only the 2024 edition was retrieved. The 2025 and 2026 editions are **not** at the `ar24e`-style
URL pattern (probed, 404), and omron.com's IR index pages are JavaScript-rendered single-page
shells that return only navigation chrome to a plain HTTP fetch, so the chunk URLs could not be
enumerated from the site. Resolvable with a headless browser or a targeted search.

   restatement is flagged in section 4, and every disagreement traced to a restatement Omron
   itself documents.
2. **Fact Book against releases.** The Fact Book 2026 eleven-year table was checked line by line
   against the seven releases. **Net sales, cost of sales, gross profit, SG&A, R&D, operating
   income, net income attributable and ROE all match exactly for all seven years**, on the

## 8. THE ROW, IN ONE LINE

Omron IAB is a **JPY 409.5 billion** (FY2025) industrial automation segment that names
**"industrial camera/code reader devices"** and **"inspection systems"** among seven product
families, has never sized that line and never will, earns **15%–18% operating margin at the top
of the cycle and 5.5% at the bottom**, currently sits at **10.4%**, and is run as a
breadth-and-application-engineering business on the company's own account: *"the most extensive
product lineup in the industry."* Seven clean U.S. GAAP years are available, with one large
discontinued-operations break between FY2023 and FY2024 that must be bridged before any
multi-year growth or margin comparison against Cognex is made.
