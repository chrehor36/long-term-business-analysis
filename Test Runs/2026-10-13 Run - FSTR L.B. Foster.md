  filing says an attacker could not take the business; the filing says attackers arrive. "If the answer had been yes, we

  **[M2012-106]**.
- **Pricing power**, read by "the agony they go through in determining whether a price increase can be sustained" **[M2005-020]**. Through the 2021 to 2022 inflation the costs came first
  and the price later: consolidated gross margin fell from 19.1% in 2020 to 16.8% in 2021, "primarily impacted by decreased

## WHAT IN THE FRAMEWORK WAS WRONG OR UNCLEAR
(1) **The business of parts meets a segment that is itself several businesses.** The deciding part is Rail, but Rail
combines a distributor of mill rail, an engineered friction-management niche with its own patents, and a shrinking UK
technology unit, and the filer reports profit only for the whole segment. The parts CONVENTION reads reported parts, so
the run judged Rail as a whole and recorded Friction Management as the one candidate castle it could not isolate. The text
does not say whether an unreported business unit that might carry a castle should be credited, or whether the file should go TOO HARD
(WORK) until a filing breaks the unit out. The run held that the deciding part is the reported segment. (2) **(b) and the
serial-acquirer CONVENTION together produce a one-point range.** When owner cash changes sign, (b) makes the range the
no-growth case alone, and the serial-acquirer rule asks for "organic growth as the filer reports it", which this filer does
not report as a series. Together they left a single case, so the fair price is that case at ten percent and Part VII's
"two ends averaged" has nothing to average. The run said so and showed the alternatives beside it. (3) **Is the cycle base set
by owner cash or by the business?** (a) defines the cycle on owner cash, but this company's owner cash in 2021 and 2022
was driven by a warranty settlement and by working capital around acquisitions, not by its markets. The choice of 2018 to
2023 (peak to peak, dropping the 2017 tax-refund year) is the analyst's reading; the literal window is shown beside. (4)
**Whose tax: what "runs out" means.** The NOL never expires but is limited to 80% of taxable income. The convention says "runs out
inside ten years", and the run read "runs out" as "is used up", which adds about $1.89 a share at the long rate; read as
"expires", it would add nothing. (5) **The brief's competitor rule when the 10-K names no rival.** No SEC
filer reports FSTR's product mix. Koppers' railroad segment was the nearest, and from 2020 its only segment profit measure
is adjusted EBITDA, so the run used adjusted EBITDA less segment depreciation, a measure the rows themselves distrust
as earnings. It was used only as a margin comparison, and it flatters the rival.
