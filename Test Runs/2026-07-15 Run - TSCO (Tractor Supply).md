## GATE 2 — MOAT
Franchise test [E3-03], from Tractor Supply's own 10-K: **"We operate in a
competitive retail industry. We believe the principal competitive factors
include location of stores, fulfillment options, price, quality of
merchandise, in-stock inventory consistency, merchandise assortment and
presentation, product knowledge, and customer service. We compete with
general merchandise retailers, home center retailers, pet retailers,
specialty and discount retailers, independently owned retail farm and
ranch stores, numerous privately-held regional farm store chains and farm
cooperatives, as well as internet-based retailers. However, we believe we
successfully differentiate ourselves from many of these retailers by
focusing on our specialized market..."**
- **No close substitute**: **real, narrow niche differentiation** — the
  company explicitly claims (its own words) successful differentiation via
  specialization in the rural/farm-lifestyle niche, a real, less pure-
  price-competitive category than general merchandise (bulky farm supplies,
  live-animal-adjacent products, and localized in-stock availability are
  genuinely harder for pure e-commerce to displace than typical retail
  categories).
**Class: NARROW** (specialized niche positioning, partially insulated from
Amazon-style substitution), **Direction: STABLE**.
**VERDICT: PASS with NARROW classification.**

## GATE 4 — FINANCIAL QUALITY (full OE formula, P44 MIXED band)
Convention P44's MIXED band (50%) applied — store fleet plus distribution
centers is a moderately capital-intensive retail model:

| FY | Net income | D&A | Total capex | Maint. capex (50%) | **OE** |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2022 | $1,088.7M | $343.1M | $773.4M | $386.7M | **$1,045.1M** |
| 2023 | $1,107.2M | $393.0M | $753.9M | $376.9M | **$1,123.3M** |
| 2024 | $1,101.2M | $447.2M | $784.0M | $392.0M | **$1,156.4M** |
| 2025 | $1,096.1M | $494.0M | $894.8M | $447.4M | **$1,142.7M** |

**LOWEST VERIFIED TIER: $1,045.1M (FY2022).** Notably, reported NI has been
essentially flat ($1,088.7M to $1,096.1M) across all four years despite
rising capex — a mature, saturated-footprint retailer funding continued
store growth/remodels from stable, not growing, underlying earnings.
Fortress (non-financial survival track): Total assets $10,933.7M, equity
$2,581.3M (FY2025) — implied liabilities ~$8.35B against ~$1.05-1.16B OE,
a meaningful multiple, though this run notes (without independently
confirming the exact composition this pass) that retail balance sheets
commonly carry large **capitalized operating-lease liabilities** under
current accounting rules, which read as "leverage" in a raw assets/equity
ratio without being distress-risk debt in the way a term loan would be —
flagged as a real interpretive caveat, not resolved with certainty.
**VERDICT: PASS**, with the leverage figure explicitly flagged as likely
lease-inflated rather than pure financial debt, pending confirmation.
