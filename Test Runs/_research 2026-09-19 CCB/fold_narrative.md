**THE FINDING, and it is a method finding as much as a company finding: a sponsor bank's moat
cannot be evidenced from the sponsor's own filings, and this run went and got the customers'
instead.** Coastal rents its charter to 28 financial-technology firms (CCBX: 48.1% of loans at
2025-12-31, 52.9% at 2026-06-30, 67% of 2025 consolidated pre-tax income). EDGAR full-text search
for `"Coastal Community Bank"` across **all** filers returned 113 hits since 2025-01-01, of which 45
are Coastal's own and the rest identify partners Coastal never names: **Dave Inc. (20), Prosper
Marketplace and Prosper Funding (21), Upbound Group (5), TriplePulse (3)**. Then:
- **Dave Inc.'s FY2025 10-K:** *"our partnerships with Evolve and Coastal, **our two bank
  partners**."* Coastal's largest identifiable partner is deliberately dual-sourced to a competing
  sponsor bank.
- **Prosper Marketplace's FY2025 10-K:** personal loans at **WebBank**, only the credit card at
  Coastal — and an amendment that *"**reduces the program fee percentage that the Company pays to
  Coastal by 0.75%**"*. The customer negotiated Coastal's price down, in writing, in its own filing.
- Coastal's own 10-K concedes it: a partner *"could terminate a relationship with us for many
  reasons, **including being able to obtain better terms from another provider**."*
**[E3-03] criterion 2 fails on the record, not on inference**, and the one metric that measures what
Coastal actually sells has fallen three years running on the filer's own non-GAAP table: **net BaaS
loan income per dollar of average CCBX loans 9.71% → 9.09% → 8.41%**, with the partner's share of
CCBX interest income up from **40.4% to 47.0%**. [E4-32] reads that DOWN. Class **NONE**.

**THE COMPETITOR ROW INVERTED THE BULL CASE, which is the third time a row has done that in this
tree (after HOG and CHE).** Eleven filed peers. Coastal's headline 7.14% NIM is second-highest in
the row and it is the accounting — the filer's own reconciliation cuts the CCBX margin to **3.50%**.
**And on the price of BaaS deposits Coastal is the worst in the row: 3.52% for CCBX deposits against
Pathward's 0.09% and The Bancorp's 2.06%** (TBBK, with 95% of average deposits from fintech, says

primarily earn fee income"*). **The classic banking-as-a-service prize is free deposits, and Coastal
is not getting it.** Its cheap money (1.56%) belongs to the community bank — the half that is not
growing.

**THE PRIOR THAT SURVIVED, and it is the strongest fact against this run's conclusion.** The brief
asked for *"any regulatory agreement or consent order."* There is none: **zero hits for "consent
order" in six annual reports, FY2020–FY2025**, and both Company and Bank *"well capitalized."*
Against the row that is a real relative advantage, and it is **[E3-02]** vindicated — Coastal did
not *"mindlessly imitate the behavior of their peers"*, and the peers were caught: **Green Dot**, a
Federal Reserve consent order *"including a $44 million civil money penalty"*; **Customers

the name ([E2-37]), and it is a permission re-earned at every examination, not a property ([E4-04]).

**WHAT THE FRAMEWORK NEEDS IN ORDER TO RUN A BANK — four gaps, for the operator, because four more
banks are queued (ACNB, SOFI, JPM, TFC).**
1. **Q2 has no passable form for a bank, and the corpus says so itself.** **[E3-43]** classes a bank
   as *"a business, unlike a franchise"* — which is *why* **[E3-29]** raises Q3 to gate weight — and
   **[E3-03]** criterion 2 requires *no close substitute*, which no deposit-taker in a metropolitan
   market has ever had. Read literally, **every bank stops at Q2**, and the corpus bought banks.
   **This run did not use that reasoning**; it closed Q2 on Coastal's own and its counterparties'
   filed facts. But the general question is structural and needs a ruling (PRIME RULE 5). The two
   candidates: (a) Q2 for a bank is a *local-deposit-franchise* test, which Coastal's community-bank
   half passes; (b) **[E3-29]** creates an explicit exception class in which Q2 returns NARROW and
   the decision moves wholly to Q3 and Q5. **(b) has the better textual support and the worse
   discipline.**
2. **The evidence ladder stops too soon for a regulated filer.** The **FFIEC Call Report** (the only
   public data on the private sponsor banks that are Coastal's real competitors — Cross River,
   WebBank, Evolve, Column, Lead, Sutton, Celtic, Thread) and the **Federal Reserve / state banking
   department enforcement registers** (the only place a supervisory action becomes public) are both
   outside the six rungs. Their absence turned the industry's most important competitor comparison
   into a stated limit and the supervisory record into an absence claim reading *"no instance found
   in SEC filings."*
3. **Owner earnings needs a declared form for balance-sheet businesses, as float got one.** The
   CONVENTION above should be adopted with a ledger row or refused.
4. **A ruling on whether `COMPUTATION — NOT A CLEARANCE` may carry a floor verdict.** This run found
   a second, independent, sufficient reason not to buy and recorded it as a note beside the Q2 FAIL
   rather than as the fail line.

**TOOLING FINDING, and it is a live trap for any future financials screen:** the screening layer
builds owner earnings from **operating cash flow**, which for a bank measures growth, not earnings.
**CCB 2025: operating cash flow $254.6M against net income $47.0M — 5.4×**, because $6.69 billion of
loans ran through loans-held-for-sale. **Any screen that priced a bank that way priced it at roughly
five times its earnings.** The 2026-09-01 triage dropped financials as a class, which accidentally
avoided it; if financials are ever screened, the construction must change first. *(No screen output
for CCB exists on disk to check against — the 2026-09-01 triage priced it with a financial flag and
then dropped it, and the pricing script is still not committed, which is the HBB finding of
