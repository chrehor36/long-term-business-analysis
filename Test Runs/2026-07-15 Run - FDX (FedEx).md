## GATE 1 — CIRCLE OF COMPETENCE
Air and ground package delivery/freight logistics. Same network-
infrastructure economics as UPS (run separately). **VERDICT: PASS.**

## GATE 2 — MOAT
Franchise test [E3-03], from FedEx's own 10-K: **"...the U.S. domestic and
international package and the U.S. domestic freight and international
freight markets are both highly competitive and sensitive to price and
service, especially in periods of little or no macroeconomic growth. The
ability to compete effectively depends upon price, frequency, reliability,
capacity and speed of scheduled service... Competitors within the U.S.
include other package delivery concerns, principally United Parcel
Service, Inc. ('UPS'), passenger air[lines]..."** — FedEx names UPS as its
principal competitor directly, the mirror image of UPS's own filing
(run separately).
- **No close substitute**: **real, structural** — FedEx pioneered the
  overnight air-express model and maintains a large, hard-to-replicate air
  cargo network, a genuine infrastructure moat similar in kind to UPS's
  ground-density advantage, though concentrated more in air/express than
  UPS's ground strength.
**Class: NARROW** (real air/ground network infrastructure), **Direction:
NARROWING** — same Amazon-in-house-logistics threat already documented at
UPS applies equally here (general knowledge, not independently quantified
against FedEx-specific volume data this pass).
**VERDICT: PASS with NARROW/NARROWING classification**, same reasoning
basis as UPS.

## GATE 6 — VALUATION — FAILS ON THE FULL OE FORMULA
**6-B Statute** (lowest tier, $3,231.5M): yield = 3,231.5 / 74,840 =
**4.32%** vs. hurdle **5.10%** → **FAILS.**

**This is a genuine, not cherry-picked, divergence from the mechanical
screen**, which showed FDX passing at 5.11% using the NI+D&A proxy (no
capex deduction). Once FedEx's real, heavy maintenance capex is deducted
via the full OE formula — exactly the correction this framework's Gate 4
discipline exists to make — the resulting yield drops below the hurdle.
This is the same pattern already observed elsewhere in this project (per
this project's own prior finding that "7 of 13 REITs cleared the bare
screen but failed Book One once real OE replaced the NI+D&A proxy") now
appearing in the S&P 500 batch for the first time today.
**VERDICT: WAIT.** Not a Gate 2-5 elimination — the business itself passed
every qualitative gate — but the price, relative to FedEx's real owner
earnings (not the inflated screen proxy), does not clear even the bare
comparison hurdle. No entry language applies.
