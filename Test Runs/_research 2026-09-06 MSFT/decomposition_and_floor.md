## FINDING 1 — THE [E5-20] TEST IS ANSWERED BY ARITHMETIC, NOT BY ASSERTION
Capex ÷ D&A ran at **0.93x–1.26x for the six years FY2015–FY2020** — the [E3-44] default
case, where *"the depreciation charge is not inappropriate … to use as a proxy for required
capital expenditures."* It then went **1.76x → 1.65x → 2.03x → 2.12x → 2.19x → 3.01x.**
Microsoft is now spending **three dollars of capital for every dollar of depreciation it
charges.** [E5-20]'s railroad benchmark — *"merely spending their depreciation expense will
not keep them in the same place … the true maintenance capex … is higher than 60 percent"*
of total capex — puts the railroads' maintenance share above 60%; **depreciation is 33% of
Microsoft's FY2026 capex.** The D&A end of the band is INVALID here **by measurement**, and
the run did not have to assert it.

**The VZ shape confirmed, not assumed:** D&A reflects a fleet bought at FY2020–23 prices and
sizes; the replacement bill is being written at FY2026 prices and sizes. And the CVX
inversion — the opposite risk the brief named, where purchase accounting inflates
depreciation above true renewal — **does not apply**: MSFT's goodwill and intangibles are
large but its PP&E is overwhelmingly self-constructed, and the direction of the capex/D&A
ratio is the wrong way round for that inversion.

## FINDING 2 — THE MARGIN LEVER HAS REVERSED AT THE GROSS LINE. [E2-63]
The eleven-year operating-income growth decomposes as
**revenue ×3.546 × operating margin ×2.412 = OI ×8.548** (FY2015→FY2026), i.e. **+21.5%/yr
of operating income, of which +12.2%/yr was revenue and the rest was margin.**
The margin half is arithmetically near-spent and is now going backwards where it matters:
- **Gross margin has FALLEN three consecutive years: 69.8% (FY2024) → 68.8% → 67.9%.**
  That is the capital-intensive leg diluting the annuity's margin, in the filed numbers.
- Operating margin still rises only because operating expenses are growing slower than
  revenue (R&D 10.7%, S&M 8.0%, G&A 2.4% of revenue in FY2026). **Even at zero operating
  expense the operating margin caps at the gross margin, 67.9%** — a further ×1.45 and then
  nothing, ever. [E2-63]: *"most operating businesses are also capped unless more capital is
  continuously invested."*
- So the second decade cannot repeat the first decade's arithmetic. Growth must come from
  **volume**, and volume in the growing leg costs 35 cents of capital per dollar of revenue.

## FINDING 3 — [E2-44](2) FAILS, AND IT IS THE SAME FACT
*"grow dollar volume with only minor additional investment of capital."* Capex has gone
**6.4% → 34.9% of revenue in eleven years.** Whatever else this is, it is not capital-light
growth. ([E2-44](1) — pricing power with flat demand — is tested separately at Q2.)

## FINDING 4 — THE INCREMENTAL RETURN LOOKS SPECTACULAR, AND [E2-56] SAYS DO NOT TRUST IT
FY2023–FY2026: capex $253,083M against D&A $102,786M = **$150,297M of net new capital**,
against operating income up **$71,854M** (FY2022 $83,383M → FY2026 $155,237M).
**= 47.8% pre-tax incremental return**, far above [E5-40]'s ~12% "quite satisfactory."
On the five-year frame (FY2022–26 capex $276,969M less D&A $117,246M = $159,723M net new;
OI FY2021 $69,916M → FY2026 $155,237M, +$85,321M) it is **53.4% pre-tax.**

**This is the strongest fact in the file for the bull case and it must be stated at full
strength [E4-51]. It is also exactly the number [E2-56] warns is camouflage:** *"Their
marvelous core businesses … camouflage repeated failures in capital allocation elsewhere"* —
judge retention **segment by segment, incrementally, never on the blended return.** The
numerator here contains the annuity's own growth, which consumed almost none of the
denominator. The consolidated 47.8% is arithmetic about two businesses averaged together.
**Whether it survives the segment split is the decisive question of this run** and is
answered at Q2/Q4 from the segment footnote, not here.

**THE SINGLE MOST ARRESTING STATEMENT IN THE FILE:** the ~10% floor at this price requires
**~$371,055M of owner earnings.** Microsoft's FY2026 **total revenue was $331,839M.**
**To clear the floor at today's price, Microsoft would have to earn more in owner earnings
than it currently earns in revenue.**

## THE DISCONFIRMING FACT, STATED AT FULL STRENGTH [E4-51], [E4-26]
Microsoft's filed record **exceeds its required rate on every window.** Operating income
compounded **+21.5%/yr** for eleven years; revenue **+12.2%/yr**; owner earnings at the D&A
end **+18.5%/yr** over ten years (27,463 → 131,996). The floor needs 6.4–8.5% perpetual and
the company has delivered two to three times that. This is the third name in queue history
(after CTAS and GRMN) whose record exceeds the rate its price requires.
It fails anyway, and the reason is **[E4-44]**: *"the value of an asset … cannot over the
long term grow faster than its earnings do"* — combined with Finding 2, the historic rate was
half margin and the margin half is spent and reversing; and **[E4-35]**'s base rate, which
makes sustained double-digit growth a fewer-than-one-in-twenty event **among the best
businesses in the world**, and asks the floor case to carry the burden of proof in writing
against those odds.
