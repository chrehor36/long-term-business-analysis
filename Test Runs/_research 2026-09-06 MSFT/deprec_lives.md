# 5. RETIREMENTS, IMPAIRMENTS, WRITE-OFFS, AND OBSERVED VS ACCOUNTING LIFE

## 5A. The only PP&E impairment quantified in any of the eleven filings

FY2020 10-K, Note 7 (repeated as the comparative in the FY2021 10-K), verbatim:
> "During fiscal year 2020, we recorded an impairment charge of $ 186 million to Property and
> Equipment, primarily to leasehold improvements, due to the closing of our Microsoft Store
> physical locations."

This is **retail leasehold improvements, not server or datacentre equipment.**

## 5B. The FY2023 "Q2 charge": hardware portfolio, amount not separately stated

FY2023 10-K, MD&A, verbatim:
> "On January 18, 2023, we announced decisions we made to align our cost structure with our
> revenue and customer demand, prioritize our investments in strategic areas, and consolidate
> office space. As a result, we recorded a $1.2 billion charge in the second quarter of fiscal
> year 2023 ("Q2 charge"), which included employee severance expenses of $800 million,
> impairment charges resulting from changes to our hardware portfolio, and costs related to
> lease consolidation activities."

Only the $800 million severance component is broken out. **The hardware-portfolio impairment
and the lease-consolidation cost are not separately quantified: not found in filings.** The
$1.2 billion total less $800 million severance leaves $400 million shared between the hardware
impairment and lease consolidation, split undisclosed. "Hardware portfolio" is not defined as
server or datacentre equipment.

## 5C. FY2026: XBOX impairment, amount not stated

FY2026 10-K, MD&A, verbatim:
> "Research and development expenses increased $3.1 billion or 9% driven by continued
> investments in compute capacity, AI talent, and data to support product development that
> benefits the entire portfolio, as well as impairment and other related expenses in our XBOX
> business."

**No dollar amount is given for the XBOX impairment: not found in filings.** It is a gaming
charge, not server or datacentre equipment.

## 5D. ASSET RETIREMENTS OF SERVER / DATACENTRE EQUIPMENT: **NOT FOUND IN FILINGS**

There is **no disclosure in any of the eleven 10-Ks of retirements, disposals, scrappage, or
write-offs of server, network, or datacentre equipment.** The PP&E footnote presents only gross
cost, accumulated depreciation and net; it contains **no rollforward** (no additions,
retirements, or disposals lines). The gross cost of a retired asset and its accumulated
depreciation are therefore never visible.

## 5E. OBSERVED SERVICE LIFE VERSUS ACCOUNTING LIFE: **NOT FOUND IN FILINGS**

**Microsoft never discloses the actual observed service life of its servers, nor any retirement
or failure-rate data, nor any back-test of the accounting life against realised experience.**
No 10-K FY2016-FY2026 contains such a statement.

The **only** rationale ever given for either extension is the single sentence in the FY2022 and
FY2023 10-Ks:
> "Due to investments in software that increased efficiencies in how we operate our server and
> network equipment, as well as advances in technology, we determined we should increase the
> estimated useful lives of both server and network equipment from four years to six years."

The FY2021 extension (change #1) is given **no rationale at all**. The FY2020 and FY2021 10-Ks
state only that "we completed an assessment" and what the conclusion was. No description of the
assessment's method, evidence, or inputs is provided in any filing.

Note also that the two changes are, on their face, in tension: the July 2020 assessment
concluded the right server life was four years; the July 2022 assessment, two years later,
concluded it was six. **No filing reconciles the two conclusions or explains what the 2020
assessment missed.** The FY2022 10-K acknowledges only the bare fact of the earlier change:
"We had previously increased the estimated useful lives of both server and network equipment in
July 2020."

# 6. CAPITALIZED SOFTWARE / DEVELOPMENT COSTS

Microsoft's filings treat this in **two separate places** that must not be conflated.

## 6A. INTERNAL-USE SOFTWARE: capitalized into PP&E

Internal-use software is **capitalized** and sits inside the property and equipment balance. It
appears in the Note 1 useful-life sentence in all eleven years, and from FY2026 is named in
the PP&E line item caption itself ("Servers, network equipment, **and software**"). Its life:

- FY2016-FY2022: "computer software developed or acquired for internal use, three to seven years"
- FY2023-FY2024: "computer software developed or acquired for internal use, three years"
- FY2025-FY2026: "software developed or acquired for internal use, three years"

**Microsoft never states a capitalization policy for internal-use software**: no threshold, no
description of which costs qualify, no annual capitalized amount, no accumulated balance. The
amount of internal-use software inside the "Computer equipment and software" / "Servers,
network equipment, and software" line is **never separately disclosed in any of the eleven
filings: not found in filings.** Only the depreciable life is given.

## 6B. SOFTWARE TO BE SOLD TO CUSTOMERS: expensed until technological feasibility

This is the policy that is stated verbatim, in the Critical Accounting Policies section.

**FY2016 through FY2025 (unchanged for a decade)**, e.g. FY2024 10-K:
> "Costs incurred internally in researching and developing a computer software product are
> charged to expense until technological feasibility has been established for the product. Once
> technological feasibility is established, software costs are capitalized until the product is
> available for general release to customers. Judgment is required in determining when
> technological feasibility of a product is established. We have determined that technological
> feasibility for our software products is reached after all high-risk development issues have
> been resolved through coding and testing. Generally, this occurs shortly before the products
> are released to production. The amortization of these costs is included in cost of revenue
> over the estimated life of the products."

(FY2016 and FY2017 read "all software costs are capitalized"; from FY2019 the word "all" is
dropped. FY2016 reads "released to manufacturing" where later years read "released to
production.")

**FY2026 10-K: the scope is narrowed, verbatim:**
> "Costs incurred internally in researching and developing **a software product to be marketed
> or sold to external users** are charged to expense until technological feasibility has been
> established for the product. Once technological feasibility is established, software costs
> are capitalized until the product is available for general release to customers. ..."

**The FY2026 change from "a computer software product" to "a software product to be marketed or
sold to external users" is a substantive narrowing of the policy's stated scope.** It is not
flagged as a change anywhere in the filing, and no effect is quantified: **not found in
filings.**

Because the practical effect is that technological feasibility is reached "shortly before the
products are released to production", the amount capitalized under this policy has always been
small. The last disclosed amounts:

| FY | Amortization of capitalized software |
|---|---|
| 2014 | $200 million |
| 2015 | $79 million |
| 2016 | $69 million |
| 2017 | $55 million |
| 2018 | $54 million |
| 2019 onward | **no longer disclosed: not found in filings** |

FY2018 10-K, intangible assets note, verbatim:
> "Intangible assets amortization expense was $2.2 billion, $1.7 billion, and $978 million for
> fiscal years 2018, 2017, and 2016, respectively. Amortization of capitalized software was $54
> million, $55 million, and $69 million for fiscal years 2018, 2017, and 2016, respectively."

**The "Amortization of capitalized software" disclosure stops after the FY2018 10-K.** No 10-K
from FY2019 onward quantifies capitalized software of any kind.

## 6C. TWO MORE FY2026 PRESENTATION CHANGES

**(i) Cost of revenue no longer mentions capitalized software.**

FY2024 10-K, Note 1, verbatim:
> "Cost of revenue includes: manufacturing and distribution costs for products sold and programs
> licensed; ... costs associated with the delivery of consulting services; **and the amortization
> of capitalized software development costs. Capitalized software development costs are
> amortized over the estimated lives of the products.**"

FY2026 10-K, Note 1, verbatim (the two sentences are gone):
> "Cost of revenue includes: costs incurred to support and maintain cloud-based and other online
> products and services, including datacenter costs and royalties; operating costs related to
> product support service centers and product distribution centers; manufacturing and
> distribution costs for products sold and programs licensed; traffic acquisition costs to
> drive traffic to our websites and to acquire online advertising space; and costs associated
> with the delivery of consulting services."

**(ii) R&D expense now explicitly absorbs infrastructure depreciation.**

FY2024 10-K, MD&A, verbatim:
> "Research and development expenses include payroll, employee benefits, stock-based
> compensation expense, and other headcount-related expenses associated with product
> development. Research and development expenses also include third-party development and
> programming costs and the amortization of purchased software code and services content."

FY2026 10-K, MD&A, verbatim:
> "Research and development expenses include payroll, stock-based compensation expense, employee
> benefits, and other headcount-related expenses associated with product development. Research
> and development expenses also include **technology development costs, including AI training
> and other infrastructure costs**, third-party development and programming costs, and **the
> depreciation and amortization of assets used to conduct research and development**."

This means that from FY2026 an undisclosed portion of the $34.3 billion depreciation charge is
classified in R&D rather than cost of revenue. **The split is not quantified: not found in
filings.** Note that the FASB standard requiring disaggregation of depreciation by expense
caption is disclosed as **not yet adopted**:

FY2026 10-K, Note 1, "Recent Accounting Guidance Not Yet Adopted", verbatim:
> "In November 2024, the FASB issued a new standard to expand disclosures about income statement
> expenses. The guidance requires disaggregation of certain costs and expenses included in each
> relevant expense caption on our consolidated income statements in a separate note to the
> financial statements at each interim and annual reporting period, including amounts of
> purchases of inventory, employee compensation, **depreciation**, and intangible asset
> amortization. The standard will be effective for us beginning with our annual reporting for
> fiscal year 2028 and interim periods thereafter, with early adoption permitted."
