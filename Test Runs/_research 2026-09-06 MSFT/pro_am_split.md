# MSFT — [E2-56], the Pro-Am test. Does the 47.8% incremental return survive the segment split?
*"Their marvelous core businesses … camouflage repeated failures in capital allocation
elsewhere"* — judge retention **segment by segment, incrementally, never on the blended
return.* All figures from the FY2025/FY2026 10-K segment footnote (post-recast, so
comparable). $M.

## THE SPLIT
| | FY2024 (recast) | FY2026 | Δ revenue | Δ operating income | margin FY2024 → FY2026 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Productivity & Business Processes | 106,820 / OI 59,661 | 139,996 / OI 83,879 | +33,176 | **+24,218** | 55.9% → **59.9%** ▲ |
| Intelligent Cloud | 87,464 / OI 37,813 | 137,791 / OI 56,972 | +50,327 | **+19,159** | 43.2% → **41.3%** ▼ |
| More Personal Computing | 50,838 / OI 11,959 | 54,052 / OI 14,386 | +3,214 | +2,427 | 23.5% → 26.6% ▲ |
| **Total** | 245,122 / 109,433 | 331,839 / 155,237 | +86,717 | **+45,804** | 44.6% → 46.8% |
*(reconciles exactly: 24,218 + 19,159 + 2,427 = 45,804)*

## THE ANSWER: NO, IT DOES NOT SURVIVE
Capital spent FY2025+FY2026: capex **$180,499M**, D&A **$67,967M**, net new capital
**$112,532M** — and essentially all of it went to datacentres, i.e. to **Intelligent Cloud.**

| | operating income added | net new capital | incremental pre-tax return |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Consolidated (the camouflaged number)** | +45,804 | 112,532 | **40.7%** |
| **Intelligent Cloud — the leg that spent the money** | +19,159 | 112,532 | **17.0%** |
| **Productivity & Business Processes — the leg that spent nothing** | **+24,218** | ~0 | **not measurable; it is free** |

**The annuity added MORE operating income than the capital sink did — $24.2bn versus
$19.2bn — while consuming essentially none of the $112.5bn.** The consolidated incremental
return is two businesses averaged: one earning an unmeasurably high return on no capital, one
earning about 17% pre-tax on $112.5bn. [E2-56] is not a caution here; it is a description.

Longer frame, same answer: FY2023 (recast) IC OI 28,411 → FY2026 56,972 = **+28,561** against
FY2024-26 net new capital of **$136,051M** (capex 224,976 − D&A 88,925) = **21.0% pre-tax.**

## AND THE DEPRECIATION-LIFE QUESTION LANDS ON EXACTLY THIS NUMBER
Nearly all of the life-extension benefit sits in Intelligent Cloud's cost of revenue.
Removing my scaled FY2026 run-rate estimate (~$20bn, disclosed as my computation) from IC's
operating income gives IC OI ≈ $36,972M, a **26.8% margin instead of 41.3%**, and an
incremental return of **(36,972 − 28,411) / 136,051 = 6.3% pre-tax.**

**So the honest incremental return on the AI-datacentre capital is somewhere between 6% and
21% pre-tax, and which end depends entirely on whether a server lasts three years or six.**
Against [E5-40]'s *"quite satisfactory"* ~12% on retained capital, the top of that range
passes comfortably and the bottom fails. **The width is the finding [E4-25]** — and it is the
same width, arriving from a completely independent direction, as the owner-earnings band.

## THE CORROBORATING FILED SERIES — IC's GROSS MARGIN IS COLLAPSING
The FY2025 vintage is the first to disclose segment cost of revenue. Implied Intelligent
Cloud **gross** margin: **FY2024 66.1% → FY2025 62.2% → FY2026 58.0%.**
**IC cost of revenue grew 44.1% in FY2026 against segment revenue growth of 29.7%.**
That is the AI infrastructure arriving in the segment's own cost line, in the filed numbers,
three years running — and it is the single cleanest piece of evidence that the marginal
dollar of datacentre capital earns less than the average dollar already deployed.
Consolidated gross margin tells the same story: **69.8% → 68.8% → 67.9%.**

## WHAT THIS DOES TO THE BRIEF'S HYPOTHESIS (a)
Hypothesis (a) was: *the capex is genuinely growth capital funding a franchise extension, so
current owner earnings are understated and the floor is closer than it looks.*
**Half of it is true and the half that is true belongs to the other segment.** The franchise
extension is real and it is happening in Productivity, where it costs nothing. The capital is
being spent in Intelligent Cloud, where the return is 6–21% and the gross margin is falling.
**The two facts the hypothesis needs — high incremental return AND the capital — are in
different segments.**
