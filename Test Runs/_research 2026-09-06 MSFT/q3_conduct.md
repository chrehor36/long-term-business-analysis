**11-year mean ROE 33.1%. 11-year mean ROTE 59.5%.** Both computed on year-end equity, not
average equity; on average equity the ROE figures run roughly 2–4 points higher in the
growth years.

**Readings.**

1. **ROE clears the [E2-01] bar in every one of eleven years, with a floor of 20.0%** — and
   that floor year (FY2018) is depressed by a one-off: the TCJA net charge. Underlying FY2018
   ROE is closer to 30%. There is no year in this window in which the business earned a poor
   return on its equity.

2. **The tangible denominator matters, and it matters in the direction that flatters.**
   Goodwill and intangibles rose from $21.6B (FY2016) to $138.3B (FY2026), driven by LinkedIn
   (FY2017: goodwill +$17.3B), Nuance (FY2022: +$17.8B), and Activision (FY2024: +$51.3B).
   ROTE exceeds ROE by 9 to 39 percentage points throughout. **The operating business earns
   extraordinary returns on tangible capital; the reported ROE is dragged down by the price
   paid for acquisitions.** For a franchise test this is the right way round — the underlying
   economics are better than the headline.

3. **But the trend in both series is DOWN since FY2022, and this is the finding that matters.**
   ROE peaked at 43.7% in FY2022 and has fallen to 30.2%; ROTE peaked at 82.9% and has fallen
   to 44.0% — **roughly halved in four years.** The cause is visible in the table: **equity is
   compounding faster than earnings.** Equity grew from $166.5B to $442.4B (+166%) while net
   income grew from $72.7B to $133.7B (+84%). Retained earnings are being accumulated on the
   balance sheet — capital is being *retained*, not returned, and the incremental capital is
   going into AI/datacenter capex. **The Q4 survival leg and the Q5 valuation leg must test
   the return on that incremental capital directly**, because the marginal ROE implied here is
   far below the average: incremental net income of $61.0B on incremental equity of $275.8B is
   an incremental return of about **22%** — still good, but well under the 43.7% the company
   was earning when the build-out began.

*(Disclosed ETR is the XBRL-tagged `EffectiveIncomeTaxRateContinuingOperations`. The FY2026
MD&A states it in rounded form: "Our effective tax rate for fiscal years 2026 and 2025 was 19%
and 18%, respectively." Consistent.)*

**Reading — a real flag, and the filing resolves it. The resolution binds Q4.**

**For eight consecutive years FY2019–FY2025, MSFT paid MORE cash tax than its disclosed
effective rate**, by 3.5 to 9.0 percentage points. That is the *good* direction and the
opposite of the [E4-30] warning pattern: book tax expense was consistently more favourable
than the cash actually written to governments, which means reported earnings were, if
anything, conservatively stated on the tax line.

**FY2026 breaks the pattern hard.** Cash taxes paid **fell in absolute dollars** from
$28.7B to $21.2B — a **26% decline** — in a year when **pretax income rose 34%**, from
$123.6B to $165.9B. The cash tax rate collapsed from 23.2% to **12.8%**, its lowest in the
window, and for the first time since FY2018 the cash rate fell *below* the disclosed ETR
(19.4%). **Cash taxes fell as a share of pretax income by 10.4 percentage points in one
year.** On FY2025's cash rate, FY2026 would have paid roughly $38.5B; the shortfall is on
the order of **$17B of cash retained in the business that the prior-year relationship would
have sent to tax authorities.**

### The filing DOES explain it — in the deferred tax table, and the answer is timing

*(Correction recorded in-run. My first pass through the MD&A found no narrative explanation and
I provisionally marked this UNRESEARCHED. That was wrong: the FY2026 Note 11 deferred-tax table
contains the mechanism, and the new ASU 2023-09 disaggregation confirms it. The finding stands
but the verdict changes from "unexplained" to "explained, and it reverses.")*

**First, the new disaggregation.** FY2026 is the first year MSFT reports cash taxes by
jurisdiction, under ASU 2023-09, verbatim:

> "Income taxes paid, net of refunds, pursuant to the disclosure requirements of ASU 2023-09 in
> fiscal year 2026 were as follows: (In millions) … **U.S. federal $6,246 · U.S. state and local
> $2,917 · Foreign Ireland $6,495 · Other $5,530 · Income taxes paid, net of refunds $21,188.**
> Income taxes paid, net of refunds, were $28.7 billion, and $23.4 billion in fiscal years 2025
> and 2024, respectively."

**Only $6,246 million of U.S. federal cash tax was paid on $103.6 billion of U.S. pretax income
— a U.S. federal cash rate of 6.0%.** Microsoft paid **more cash tax to Ireland ($6,495M) than
to the U.S. federal government ($6,246M)**, on U.S. pretax income 1.7× the size of all foreign
pretax income combined.

**Second, the mechanism.** The Note 11 deferred income tax liability table, as filed ($MM):

| Deferred income tax liabilities | FY2026 | FY2025 | Change |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Book/tax basis differences in investments and debt | (2,972) | 0 | (2,972) |
| **Leasing assets** | **(21,474)** | **(12,696)** | **(8,778)** |
| **Depreciation** | **(17,675)** | **(5,699)** | **(11,976)** |
| Deferred tax on foreign earnings | (396) | (1,148) | +752 |
| Other | (152) | (127) | (25) |
| **Total deferred income tax liabilities** | **(42,669)** | **(19,670)** | **(22,999)** |

| | FY2026 | FY2025 | Change |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Deferred income tax assets, net of valuation allowance | 54,729 | 45,943 | +8,786 |
| **Net deferred income tax assets** | **12,060** | **26,273** | **(14,213)** |

**There it is.** Deferred tax liabilities on **depreciation tripled**, from $5,699M to
$17,675M (+$11,976M), and on **leasing assets grew $8,778M**. Net deferred income tax assets
**fell $14.2 billion in a single year.** That $14.2B swing is almost exactly the scale of the
cash-tax shortfall computed above.

**Conclusion: the FY2026 cash tax benefit is accelerated tax depreciation on the AI datacenter
and finance-lease build-out. It is a TIMING difference, not a permanent rate reduction, and it
REVERSES.** As those assets depreciate for book purposes in later years without further tax
deductions, cash taxes must rise back toward — and eventually above — the book rate.

**The consequence for Q4 is direct and binding: owner earnings must NOT be credited with the
FY2026 cash-tax underpayment.** The ~$14B of deferred benefit is a loan from the Treasury
against future cash taxes, not earnings. Using FY2026 cash taxes as a run-rate would overstate
owner earnings by roughly $10–14 billion a year. The disclosed effective rate of **19.4%** is
the better basis for a normalised owner-earnings tax charge than the 12.8% cash rate.

Note also that the capitalised R&D deferred tax asset **fell** from $16,891M to $15,305M — the
§174 capitalisation regime is unwinding, which works in the opposite direction and slightly
offsets the depreciation benefit.

**What remains genuinely UNRESEARCHED:** whether the depreciation surge reflects a change in
tax law (100% bonus depreciation) or simply the volume of the build. The filing names no new
legislation — **"tax legislation" 0 hits, "bonus depreciation" 0, "Section 174" 0, "OBBBA" 0**
— so the statutory driver is not establishable from these documents. **NOT FOUND IN FILINGS.**
The nameable resolving documents are the FY2027 Q1/Q2 10-Qs and the FY2027 10-K, which will
show whether the low cash rate persists (law change) or reverts (build-volume timing).

**Unrecognized tax benefits are also compounding**, per the FY2026 Note 11 rollforward:
ending unrecognized tax benefits **$25,831M (FY2026), $24,729M (FY2025), $22,760M (FY2024)** —
up 13% in two years. See section 9.

**TCJA transition tax:** the FY2026 10-K contains **ZERO occurrences of "transition tax"** —
the 8-year installment obligation has run off and is no longer discussed. The schedule and
remaining amounts must be read from the FY2018–FY2021 vintages; see section 3b below.

   three years the count is **flat to +0.03%**, despite $41.7B of program repurchases.
   **The buyback is not shrinking the share count; it is absorbing stock-based compensation
   dilution.** Roughly 30–36MM shares a year are being bought back and roughly the same number
   is being issued to employees. The owner's per-share claim is not growing from repurchase.

3. **Total shareholder return as a share of earnings has collapsed from 79% to 32%.** In
   FY2020 MSFT returned 79 cents of every dollar earned; in FY2026, 32 cents. The retained
   two-thirds is the AI capex build. **This is the same finding as the ROE decline in section 2,
   seen from the cash side.** Whether that retention is value-creating is a Q4/Q5 question and
   this leg does not answer it — but the framework's test on retained earnings ("has each
   dollar retained created at least a dollar of market value") must be run explicitly, because
   management has quietly shifted from a return-of-capital posture to a reinvestment posture
   without, in the filings swept here, ever framing the change as a capital-allocation decision.

### The IRS transfer-pricing dispute — the big one

**Became public in a filing:** the NOPAs were received **September 26, 2023**, inside FY2024.
The FY2023 10-K (filed 2023-07-27) predates receipt. **First 10-K disclosure: FY2024, filed

**What is accrued versus not accrued — and the accrual IS quantified.**

**Reading — this materially softens the flag.** Microsoft carries roughly **$35.2 billion** on

the carry is of the same order as the claim. **This is not an unreserved exposure.** The company
is accruing roughly $1.4B a year of interest against it and has been building the reserve
steadily for years. The honest statement is: *the exposure is large and unresolved, but it is
substantially provided for* — and a full loss would still hurt, because penalties are not
included and the reserve covers all uncertain positions globally, not the NOPAs alone.

**Readings.** (1) **$28.9 billion plus penalties and interest is 22% of FY2026 net income and
6.5% of total stockholders' equity** — this is the largest single quantified contingency in the
filings and it is unresolved after three years. (2) **The dispute is persistent and expanding
in scope**: the 2004–2013 years are in NOPAs and the **2014–2017 years remain under audit**, so
the same transfer-pricing issue is live across **fourteen tax years**. (3) Unrecognized tax
benefits **grew $3.1B in two years** and accrued interest grew **$2.6B**, consistent with
continued provisioning against the exposure. (4) **What is still not disclosed** is how much of
the $25.8B relates to the IRS matter specifically rather than to uncertain positions in other
jurisdictions. **UNRESEARCHED, resolvable:** the FY2026 Note 11 disclosure of reasonably
possible changes to unrecognized tax benefits within the next 12 months, and any Tax Court
docket once the administrative appeal concludes.

> includes uncertain tax positions related to transfer pricing issues that **remain unresolved

> audits, resolution of the remaining matters could have a material impact on the Company's
> financial statements.** Conclusions on recognizing and measuring uncertain tax positions involve
> significant estimates and management judgment…"

An independent auditor naming this as the critical audit matter is corroboration that the

a fine" is disclosed without the number.

**Total accrued legal liabilities of $553 million** against a company earning $133.7 billion is
**0.4% of net income** — de minimis. The contingency note is short and the numbers are small.

## SUMMARY OF FLAGS RAISED BY THIS LEG

**Clean passes:** EBITDA zero across eleven 10-Ks and the proxy · restructuring zero since FY2021
· no 10-K/A ever filed · single share class, one vote · dividend not funded by issuance, by an
order of magnitude · no earnings guidance in the 10-K · auditor non-audit fees 6.5% of total ·
ROE above 20% in all eleven years · goodwill impaired honestly in FY2012 and FY2015 when it was
warranted.

**Flags to carry forward:**

1. **FY2026 cash taxes fell 26% in dollars while pretax income rose 34%** (underlying cash rate
   ~10.1% ex-transition-tax; U.S. federal cash rate just **6.0%**, and MSFT paid more cash tax
   to Ireland than to the U.S. federal government). **Explained by the filing's deferred-tax
   table: accelerated depreciation and leasing on the AI build swung net deferred tax assets
   down $14.2B. It is TIMING and it reverses — owner earnings must be taxed at ~19.4%, not
   12.8%.**
2. **ROE down from 43.7% to 30.2% and ROTE from 82.9% to 44.0% since FY2022**; incremental ROE on
   the last four years' retained capital is ~22%, half the prior average.
3. **Share count flat for three years** despite $41.7B of program repurchase — the buyback funds
   SBC dilution, not per-share growth. Shareholder return fell from 79% to 32% of earnings.
4. **The bonus metric is a third revenue definition, $7.46B above GAAP**, and 70% of PSA weight
   is cloud **revenue growth** with **no return-on-capital or margin metric anywhere.**
5. **MSFT trailed its own chosen peer index in 3 of 5 disclosed 402(v) years** (255 v 276 in
   FY2025) while CEO "compensation actually paid" ran $93M–$171M.
6. **Activision: $75.4B for a business the company's own pro forma shows adding $172M — 0.2% —

7. **$28.9B IRS transfer-pricing NOPA unresolved since 2023**, spanning fourteen tax years —

   $9.4B accrued interest). Auditor's critical audit matter.
8. **$329.1B of not-yet-commenced datacenter leases off balance sheet.**
9. **Insider ownership effectively nil** (CEO 0.012%); Chairman and CEO roles combined.
10. **New FY2026 non-GAAP "adjusted net income"** excluding OpenAI marks — currently applied
