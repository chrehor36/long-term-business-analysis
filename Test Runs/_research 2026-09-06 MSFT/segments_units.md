# A. SEGMENT OPERATING MARGIN ACROSS THE DECADE

## A.0 — There were FOUR recast events, not one

The assignment named the FY2025 recast. The sweep found four. Each is dated to its vintage
and quoted verbatim.

### Recast 1 — FY2016 10-K: the three-segment structure is CREATED

> "In June 2015, we announced a change in organizational structure to align to our strategic
> direction as a productivity and platform company. During the first quarter of fiscal year
> 2016, our chief operating decision maker requested changes in the information that he
> regularly reviews for purposes of allocating resources and assessing performance. As a
> result, beginning in fiscal year 2016, we report our financial performance based on our new
> segments, Productivity and Business Processes, Intelligent Cloud, and More Personal
> Computing, and analyze operating income as the measure of segment profitability. We have
> recast certain prior period amounts to conform to the way we internally manage and monitor
> segment performance."
> — FY2016 10-K, Note 22 Segment Information and Geographic Data

**The three segments do not exist before FY2016.** FY2016 is the first year of the structure.
FY2014 and FY2015 appear in the FY2016 10-K only as recast comparatives.

### Recast 2 — FY2017 10-K: an unexplained re-allocation of FY2016

The FY2017 10-K restates FY2016 segment operating income without a stated reason in the
segment note. Totals are unchanged; the allocation between segments moved.

|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| FY2016 10-K | 12,461 | 9,358 | 6,142 | (7,779) | 20,182 |
| FY2017 10-K | 12,418 | 9,315 | 6,202 | (7,753) | 20,182 |

MPC FY2016 revenue also moved: 40,460 (FY2016 vintage) → 40,434 (FY2017 vintage). Total
revenue unchanged at 85,320.

### Recast 3 — FY2018 10-K: ASC 606 full retrospective. THE BIG ONE.

> "We elected to early adopt the standard effective July 1, 2017, using the full retrospective
> method, which required us to restate each prior reporting period presented… The most
> significant impact of the standard relates to our accounting for software license revenue.
> Specifically, for Windows 10, we recognize revenue predominantly at the time of billing and
> delivery rather than ratably over the life of the related device."
> — FY2018 10-K, Note 1 Accounting Policies

The auditor's report confirms it:

> "the Company has changed its method of accounting for revenue from contracts with customers
> and for accounting for leases in fiscal year 2018 due to the adoption of the new revenue
> standard and new lease standard, respectively. The Company adopted the new revenue standard
> using the full retrospective approach"
> — FY2018 10-K, Report of Independent Registered Public Accounting Firm (Deloitte & Touche LLP)

This is why the pre-FY2018 vintages carry a large negative **Corporate and Other** line: the
Windows 10 revenue deferral. FY2016 10-K note (a): *"Revenue reconciling amounts and other for
fiscal year 2016 primarily consisted of a net $6.6 billion of revenue deferrals related to
sales of Windows 10."* Under ASC 606 that deferral is reversed and Corporate and Other goes to
zero.

**FY2016 total revenue: $85,320M as originally filed → $91,154M as restated. A $5.8bn
difference for the same year.** Total operating income $20,182M → $26,078M.

# B. THE [E4-55] QUESTION: WHAT PHYSICAL OR UNIT SERIES IS ACTUALLY FILED?

## B.0 — The sweep that was run

**Corpus:** all eleven 10-K vintages FY2016-FY2026 (documents and accession numbers in the table
at the top), full text of the primary EDGAR document, HTML stripped.

**Search terms, run case-insensitively against every vintage:** remaining performance obligation;
commercial bookings; bookings; Office 365 Commercial seat; Microsoft 365 Commercial seat; paid
seat; seats; seat growth; installed base; subscriber; LinkedIn member; Game Pass; monthly active
user; MAU; daily active user; Windows OEM; commercial cloud; Microsoft Cloud; Azure; Xbox; units;
devices sold; console; consoles sold; "We sold N million"; price increase; pricing change; higher
price; revenue per user; annualized revenue run rate; Copilot.

**Headline result.** Microsoft's Key Performance Indicators are, with two exceptions, defined in
the filing itself as *growth rates* — not levels. The FY2020-FY2026 Metrics tables name them
verbatim as "Office 365 Commercial **seat growth**", "Azure and other cloud services revenue
**growth**", "LinkedIn revenue **growth**", "Windows OEM revenue **growth**". Only "Microsoft 365
Consumer subscribers" and "Commercial remaining performance obligation" are defined as levels —
**and Microsoft 365 Consumer subscribers was withdrawn in FY2026.**

## B.1 — FILED: Remaining performance obligation (RPO)

**First appearance: the FY2018 10-K** (filed 2018-08-03), as a consequence of ASC 606 adoption.
It does not exist in the FY2016 or FY2017 vintages — the concept is created by the standard.

Called "contracted not recognized revenue" in FY2018-FY2019; renamed to the ASC 606 term from
FY2020, when "Commercial remaining performance obligation" also enters the MD&A Metrics table.

| FY | Total RPO | Commercial RPO | % expected in next 12 months | Vintage |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2018 | $73 billion | not split | **60%** | FY2018 10-K |
| 2019 | $91 billion | not split | **50%** | FY2019 10-K |
| 2020 | $111 billion | $107 billion | **50%** | FY2020 10-K |
| 2021 | $146 billion | $141 billion | **50%** | FY2021 10-K |
| 2022 | $193 billion | $189 billion | **45%** | FY2022 10-K |
| 2023 | $229 billion | $224 billion | **45%** | FY2023 10-K |
| 2024 | $275 billion | $269 billion | **45%** | FY2024 10-K |
| 2025 | $375 billion | $368 billion | **40%** | FY2025 10-K |
| 2026 | **$684 billion** | **$678 billion** | **30%** | FY2026 10-K |

Verbatim, the two ends of the series:

> "Contracted not recognized revenue was $73 billion as of June 30, 2018, of which we expect to
> recognize approximately 60% of the revenue over the next 12 months and the remainder
> thereafter." — FY2018 10-K, Note 15

> "Revenue allocated to remaining performance obligations, which includes unearned revenue and
> amounts expected to be invoiced and recognized as revenue in future periods, was $684 billion
> as of June 30, 2026. Revenue allocated to remaining performance obligations related to the
> commercial portion of revenue was $678 billion as of June 30, 2026, **with a weighted average
> duration of approximately 2.3 years.** We expect to recognize approximately 30% of both our
> total company remaining performance obligation revenue and commercial remaining performance
> obligation revenue over the next 12 months and the remainder thereafter."
> — FY2026 10-K, Note 12

**Two things to read here, and they point in opposite directions.**

1. The **near-term recognition rate has halved over eight years: 60% to 30%.** The RPO is
   lengthening. FY2019 explains why, and it is the candid version:
   > "Many customers are committing to our products and services for longer contract terms, which
   > is increasing the percentage of contracted revenue that will be recognized beyond the next 12
   > months." — FY2019 10-K, Note 14
   That sentence appears in FY2019 and **is not repeated in any later vintage** even as the
   percentage keeps falling.
2. **FY2026 RPO grew 84% while revenue grew 18%.** Commercial RPO went $368bn to $678bn, +$310bn
   in one year, against total FY2026 revenue of $331.8bn. The 12-month recognition rate dropped
   40% to 30% simultaneously. A dollar backlog growing 4.7x faster than revenue, whose near-term
   share is falling, reflects a duration change as well as a demand change. The FY2026 vintage is
   the **first ever to disclose a weighted average duration** (2.3 years) — a genuine and material
   addition to disclosure, and the number needed to interpret the rest.

**Caution required by the filing's own language.** RPO is a *dollar* series, not a unit series. It
is contracted revenue, and it carries the price increases inside it. FY2026 Critical Accounting
Estimates:
> "Estimating the total consideration and revenue that will be allocated to remaining performance
> obligation can involve significant judgments, including identifying and assessing variable
> consideration and potential renegotiation of commitments."

## B.5 — AZURE: NO ABSOLUTE REVENUE NUMBER IS EVER FILED. GROWTH RATE ONLY.

**ABSENCE FINDING. This is the largest single hole in the filings.**

Azure is the asset the market values Microsoft on. **In eleven consecutive 10-Ks, Microsoft has
never filed an Azure revenue figure in dollars.** Only a year-over-year growth percentage.

Sweep run: for every occurrence of `Azure` in all eleven vintages, a plus/minus 160-character
window was tested for any dollar amount, and every window containing one was inspected by hand.
**In every case the dollar figure belongs to a different subject** — total Server products and
cloud services revenue, total commercial/Microsoft Cloud revenue, a gross-margin point change, or
an operating-income movement — never to Azure itself. There is no sentence of the form "Azure
revenue was $X" in any vintage.

The filed series, verbatim from each vintage:

| FY | Filed Azure disclosure | Vintage |
|---|---|---|
| 2016 | "Microsoft Azure ("Azure") revenue grew **113%**, with usage of Azure compute and Azure SQL database more than doubling year-over-year" | FY2016 |
| 2017 | "Azure revenue growth of **99%**" | FY2017 |
| 2018 | "Azure revenue growth of **91%**, due to higher infrastructure-as-a-service and platform-as-a-service consumption-based and per user-based services" | FY2018 |
| 2019 | "Azure revenue grew **72%**" (FY2019; FY2018 shown as 91% in the same document) | FY2019 |
| 2020 | "Azure revenue grew **56%**, due to growth in our consumption-based services" | FY2020 |
| 2021 | "Azure revenue grew **50%** due to growth in our consumption-based services" | FY2021 |
| 2022 | "Azure and other cloud services revenue grew **45%** driven by growth in our consumption-based services" | FY2022 |
| 2023 | "Azure and other cloud services revenue grew **29%** driven by growth in our consumption-based services" | FY2023 |
| 2024 | "Azure and other cloud services revenue grew **30%** driven by growth in our consumption-based services" | FY2024 |
| 2025 | "Azure and other cloud services revenue grew **34%** driven by demand for our portfolio of services" | FY2025 |
| 2026 | "Azure and other cloud services revenue grew **41%** driven by demand for services across the platform with continued growth across all workloads" | FY2026 |

**Three compounding problems with this series, all visible inside the filings:**

1. **No base.** A growth rate with no level is uncheckable. You cannot compute Azure's dollar
   revenue, its margin, or its share of Intelligent Cloud from any 10-K.
2. **The definition changed in FY2022** — "Azure" became "Azure **and other cloud services**", and
   Nuance was folded in. FY2022 10-K, verbatim:
   > "In the third quarter of fiscal year 2022, we completed our acquisition of Nuance. Nuance is
   > included in all commercial metrics and our Server products and cloud services revenue growth
   > metric. **Azure and other cloud services revenue includes Nuance cloud services**, and Server
   > products revenue includes Nuance on-premises offerings."

## C.2 — The withdrawals and definition changes, dated

### FY2017 vintage — FIVE unit metrics withdrawn at once, WITHOUT explanation

The FY2016 10-K MD&A highlights carried a dense set of unit disclosures. **None of them appears in
the FY2017 10-K, and no vintage since. No reason is given in any filing.**

Withdrawn in the FY2017 vintage:
- Xbox Live monthly active users (49 million, FY2016)
- Windows 10 installed devices (more than 350 million, FY2016)
- Enterprise Mobility customers (over 33,000, FY2016)
- Xbox consoles sold (12.1 million FY2015 / 11.7 million FY2014, filed in FY2016)
- Dynamics CRM Online seat additions ("more than doubled year-over-year", FY2016)

**This is the single largest disclosure contraction in the decade, and it is silent.** Note the
context: FY2016 was the year More Personal Computing revenue fell from $43.2bn to $40.5bn and MPC
operating margin sat at 15.2%, the lowest of any segment-year in the table. The Xbox unit series
was withdrawn in the vintage immediately after console volume declined — the FY2016 filing itself
says Xbox hardware revenue *"decreased 16%, mainly due to lower prices of Xbox One consoles sold
and a decline in Xbox 360 console volume."* **A unit series withdrawn the year after the units
turned down is the [E2-49] flag, not the candour case.**

### FY2018 vintage — run rate replaced by actual revenue. THE CANDOUR CASE.

Commercial cloud "annualized revenue run rate" (an extrapolated twelve-times-last-month number) is
dropped and replaced with actual annual commercial cloud revenue ($23.2 billion). Lumia unit
disclosure ends with the exit of the phone business. RPO is added as required by ASC 606, and the
ASC 606 restatement is fully explained in Note 1 and confirmed by the auditor. **Disclosure quality
improved in this vintage.**

### FY2020 vintage — the Metrics section is created. THE SECOND CANDOUR CASE.

FY2020 is the first 10-K with a formal "Metrics" section defining every KPI, with a stated rationale:
> "We use metrics in assessing the performance of our business and to make informed decisions
> regarding the allocation of resources. We disclose metrics to enable investors to evaluate
> progress against our ambitions, provide transparency into performance trends, and reflect the
> continued evolution of our products and services."

This is a real improvement in auditability — it is what makes the rest of this section possible.
**But note what the same section does: it converts most KPIs into named growth rates.** The
definitions describe counts ("The number of Office 365 Commercial seats at end of period") while
the metric itself is titled "seat growth". The transparency added is definitional, not numerical.

Also in this vintage: "Enterprise Services revenue growth" is dropped from the metrics list after
FY2020.

### FY2022 vintage — announced ahead, with reasons. MIXED.

> "In the first quarter of fiscal year 2022, we made updates to the presentation and method of
> calculation for certain metrics, most notably changes to incorporate all current and anticipated
> revenue streams within our Office Consumer and Server products and cloud services metrics and
> changes to align with how we manage our Windows OEM and Search and news advertising businesses.
> None of these changes had a material impact on previously reported amounts in our MD&A."
> — FY2022 10-K, Item 7

Changes in this vintage:
- **Windows OEM Pro and Windows OEM non-Pro merged into a single "Windows OEM revenue growth."**
  A loss of granularity: the commercial-vs-consumer split of the Windows OEM business is no longer
  visible. Announced, with a reason given, but the granularity is gone.
- "Commercial cloud" renamed "Microsoft Cloud".
- **Azure metric redefined to "Azure and other cloud services", absorbing Nuance** (see B.5). No
  restated prior-year growth rate is provided, so the FY2021 50% and FY2022 45% are not
  like-for-like.
- "Search advertising revenue, excluding TAC" becomes "Search and news advertising".

### FY2023 vintage — Surface revenue metric dropped, silently

"Surface revenue" is a named metric in FY2020, FY2021 and FY2022. **It is absent from FY2023
onward, with no announcement.** Devices revenue in the product table fell from $7,306M (FY2022, as
recast) to $5,521M (FY2023) — a 24% decline — in the same vintage the metric disappeared.

### FY2024 vintage — the M365 Consumer subscriber definition is EXPANDED; LinkedIn members withdrawn

Announced, verbatim:
> "In the first quarter of fiscal year 2024, we made updates to the presentation and method of
> calculation for certain metrics, revising our Microsoft Cloud revenue metric to include revenue
> growth and **expanding our Microsoft 365 Consumer subscribers metric to include Microsoft 365
> Basic subscribers**, aligning with how we manage our business." — FY2024 10-K, Item 7

And the definition in the same vintage's metrics table becomes:
> "Microsoft 365 Consumer subscribers — The number of Microsoft 365 Consumer **and Copilot Pro**
> subscribers at end of period"

**Read the sequence.** Subscriber growth had decelerated 23%, 22%, 15%, 12% across FY2020-FY2023.
In FY2024 the metric's population was widened to include Microsoft 365 Basic and Copilot Pro
subscribers, and the reported figure jumped from 67 million to 82 million (+22% on the count, but
reported as "+10%" growth). **The change was announced with a reason, which is the candour half.
The effect was to add subscribers to a decelerating count, which is the flag half.** No restated
prior-year count on the new basis is filed, so the true underlying FY2024 growth cannot be computed
from the filings.

Simultaneously and **without any announcement**, the LinkedIn member count ("over 950 million
members", FY2023) disappears from Item 1 and has not returned.

### FY2025 vintage — the segment recast, plus the LinkedIn wording change

The Aug-2024 segment recomposition (Part A, Recast 5) is announced and explained, and prior periods
are restated in the filing — **this is done correctly, and the FY2025 10-K adds segment cost of
revenue and operating expenses for the first time.** Genuine improvement.

Two quieter changes in the same vintage:
- "Office 365 Commercial seat growth" is renamed "Microsoft 365 Commercial seat growth". The
  underlying population changes with the segment recomposition. **No restated prior-year seat growth
  is filed.**
- The LinkedIn growth driver is reworded. FY2024 and earlier: *"Growth will depend on our ability to
  **increase the number of LinkedIn members** and our ability to continue offering services…"*
  FY2025 and FY2026: *"Growth will depend on our ability to **increase LinkedIn member engagement on
  the platform** and our ability to continue offering insight and AI-enabled services…"* **The
  stated driver moves from a countable quantity to an uncountable one, one vintage after the count
  itself was withdrawn.**

### FY2026 vintage — Microsoft 365 Consumer subscribers REMOVED

Announced in the filing, verbatim:
> "In the first quarter of fiscal year 2026, we made updates to our metrics to align with how we
> manage and monitor certain businesses. **As part of these updates, Microsoft 365 Consumer
> subscribers was removed as a metric.**" — FY2026 10-K, Item 7, Metrics

**The removal is disclosed — Microsoft says plainly that it removed it, which is more than it did
for the FY2017 withdrawals or LinkedIn members.** But the stated reason is "to align with how we
manage and monitor certain businesses," which explains nothing about why a count that had been filed
for ten consecutive years is no longer useful.

**The sequence is the finding.** Growth on this metric ran 23%, 22%, 15%, 12%, 10% (on a widened
definition), 8% — and the metric was removed in the vintage after it printed 8%. The FY2026 MD&A
still contains the growth number (7%), so the deceleration continued through the year of removal.
**The level is gone; only the rate remains.**

Also in FY2026: "Windows OEM and Devices revenue growth" merges two previously separate lines
(FY2025 introduced this); "Search and news advertising" is renamed back to "Search advertising".

## C.3 — What the evidence shows

**Withdrawals with no reason given (the [E2-49] flag):** Xbox Live MAU, Windows 10 device count,
Enterprise Mobility customers, Xbox console units, Dynamics CRM seat additions — all FY2017.
Surface revenue — FY2023. LinkedIn members — FY2024. Xbox Game Pass members — FY2021.

**Changes announced ahead with reasons (the candour case):** the ASC 606 restatement (FY2018); the
creation of the Metrics section (FY2020); the Q1-FY2022 metric updates; the Q1-FY2024 metric
updates; the Aug-2024 segment recast (FY2025); the FY2026 removal of M365 Consumer subscribers; the
FY2026 addition of RPO weighted average duration.

**Changes announced but which nonetheless destroy comparability:** the FY2022 Windows OEM Pro/non-Pro
merge; the FY2022 Azure/Nuance redefinition; the FY2024 M365 Consumer subscriber population
expansion; the FY2025 seat metric rename. In each case a reason was given and **no restated
prior-period figure was filed**, so the series cannot be joined across the break from the filings
alone.

**The direction of travel is one-way.** Across eleven vintages, unit and count disclosures were
removed and never reinstated; the additions were dollar and percentage disclosures. The one
unambiguous gain in checkable substance is FY2025's segment cost of revenue and operating expenses,
and FY2026's RPO duration.

# D. [E2-44] PRICING

## D.1 — The sweep

Terms run against all eleven vintages: `price increase`, `pricing change`, `pricing increase`,
`pricing action`, `higher pric`, `increase in pric`, `price change`, `price adjustment`, `revenue
per user`.

**Result: across a decade of 10-Ks, Microsoft names an actual price increase exactly ONCE.**

## D.2 — The one explicit price increase: FY2025 consumer Microsoft 365

The single most important pricing sentence in the eleven filings, verbatim and complete:

> "Microsoft 365 Consumer products and cloud services revenue increased $756 million or 11%.
> Microsoft 365 Consumer cloud revenue grew 11% driven by **Microsoft 365 Consumer subscriber
> growth of 8% to 89.0 million**, as well as growth in revenue per user **from the price increase
> announced in January 2025**."
> — FY2025 10-K, Item 7, Productivity and Business Processes

**This is the rare case where the filing gives the full decomposition.** Revenue +11% = subscriber
count +8% (to a stated level of 89.0 million) + revenue per user, attributed by name to the January
2025 price increase. **Volume and price are both stated, and the volume is a level, not a rate.**

**Did seats hold through the increase?** On this evidence, yes: the count grew 8%, from 82 million
to 89.0 million, in the fiscal year containing the increase. That is deceleration from the prior
year's 10% but not attrition. **The filing does not state a churn, cancellation, or downgrade rate,
and no vintage ever does.**

**But note what happened next.** The FY2026 vintage removed the subscriber count metric. FY2026
files only:
> "Microsoft 365 Consumer cloud revenue grew 28% driven by growth in revenue per user and Microsoft
> 365 Consumer subscriber growth of 7%." — FY2026 10-K, Item 7

Revenue +28% against subscriber growth of +7% implies roughly 20 points of revenue-per-user
expansion in FY2026 — the largest price/mix contribution in the series — **and it is the first year
in a decade in which the subscriber level behind that percentage is not filed.** The filing does not
state the FY2026 subscriber count, so the 7% cannot be anchored. The price increase and the
withdrawal of the unit count fall in consecutive vintages.

## D.3 — Windows OEM pricing: the standing risk-factor language, all eleven vintages

The only other recurring pricing statement is boilerplate, and it is a *risk* disclosure rather than
a *pricing action* disclosure. It appears essentially unchanged in **all eleven vintages**,
FY2016 through FY2026:

> "**Pricing changes and promotions**, pricing variation that occurs when the mix of devices
> manufactured shifts from local and regional system builders to large multinational OEMs, and
> different pricing of Windows versions licensed…"

FY2017 adds a related line, not repeated in later vintages:
> "pricing changes and promotions, mix of computing devices, and the customer mix among large
> enterprises, small and medium businesses, and educational institutions"

**No Windows OEM price, price change, or average selling price is ever filed in any vintage.**

## D.4 — Xbox pricing: direction only, never an amount

Every Xbox pricing statement in the decade is qualitative and describes *hardware*, not services:

| FY | Verbatim | Vintage |
|---|---|---|
| 2016 | "Xbox hardware revenue decreased 16%, mainly due to **lower prices** of Xbox One consoles sold and a decline in Xbox 360 console volume" | FY2016 |
| 2017 | "Xbox hardware revenue decreased 21%, mainly due to **lower prices of consoles sold** and a decline in volume of consoles sold" | FY2017 |
| 2020 | "declined 31%, primarily due to a decrease in **volume and price** of consoles sold" | FY2020 |
| 2021 | "**higher price of consoles sold** due to the Xbox Series X\|S launches" | FY2021 |

**No Xbox price level, price change amount, or console ASP is filed in any vintage.** The
FY2016/FY2017 statements are notable for pairing price and volume — after the unit series was
withdrawn (Part C), even that pairing disappears.

## D.5 — Azure pricing and Microsoft 365 Commercial pricing: NOT FOUND

**ABSENCE FINDING.** Across all eleven vintages:
- **No Azure price, price change, price increase, discount, or rate-card statement is ever filed.**
  The FY2016-FY2026 Azure disclosure is a growth percentage attributed to "consumption-based
  services" or "demand", never to price. There is no statement anywhere in the decade about whether
  Azure's growth is price or volume.
- **No Microsoft 365 / Office 365 COMMERCIAL price increase is ever named**, despite the commercial
  business being roughly ten times the consumer business by revenue. The January 2025 increase named
  in FY2025 is the *consumer* one.

## D.6 — How MD&A attributes revenue: price, volume, or mix?

This is the heart of the [E2-44] question. The filings use a consistent formula, and it is worth
seeing what it does and does not say.

**The standing formulation, Microsoft 365 Commercial, essentially unchanged FY2018-FY2026:**
> "Microsoft 365 Commercial revenue is mainly affected by a combination of **continued installed
> base growth and average revenue per user expansion**, as well as the continued shift from Office
> licensed on-premises to Microsoft 365." — FY2025 10-K, Item 1

This names two drivers — a volume driver (installed base) and a price/mix driver (revenue per
user) — **and quantifies neither.** Neither the installed base nor the average revenue per user is
ever filed as a number in any vintage.

**The annual attribution, FY2021-FY2026, always a pair of percentages or a percentage and a
qualitative phrase:**

| FY | Verbatim attribution for Office/Microsoft 365 Commercial cloud | Volume stated? | Price stated? |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2021 | "driven by **seat growth of 17%** and **higher revenue per user**" | rate only | qualitative |
| 2022 | "driven by **seat growth of 14%**, with continued momentum in small and medium business and frontline worker offerings, as well as **growth in revenue per user**" | rate only | qualitative |
| 2023 | "**seat growth of 11%**, driven by small and medium business and frontline worker offerings, as well as **growth in revenue per user**" | rate only | qualitative |
| 2024 | "**seat growth of 7%**, driven by small and medium business and frontline worker offerings, as well as **growth in revenue per user**" | rate only | qualitative |
| 2025 | "**seat growth of 6%** driven by small and medium businesses and frontline worker offerings, as well as **growth in revenue per user**" | rate only | qualitative |
| 2026 | "**seats grew 6%** driven by small and medium businesses and frontline worker offerings" + separately, revenue per user growth "driven by **Microsoft 365 Copilot and Microsoft 365 E5**" | rate only | qualitative, attributed to mix |

**The answer to the question as asked: the attribution is stated as volume AND price, in that
order, with the volume given as a growth rate and the price given as a phrase.** In no vintage is
the price contribution quantified, and in no vintage is the seat level filed. The gap between
revenue growth and seat growth is where price and mix live, and it is widening:

- FY2026 Microsoft 365 Commercial cloud revenue grew **17%** against seats +**6%** — roughly 11
  points from revenue per user.
- FY2026 Microsoft 365 Consumer cloud revenue grew **28%** against subscribers +**7%** — roughly 20
  points from revenue per user, in the first year the subscriber level was withheld.

FY2026 attributes the commercial half of that expansion to product mix, by name:
> "revenue per user driven by **Microsoft 365 Copilot and Microsoft 365 E5**" — FY2026 10-K, Item 7

That is a mix statement (customers moving to higher-priced SKUs), not a list-price statement.
**Microsoft distinguishes mix from price in the consumer segment (FY2025: "the price increase
announced in January 2025") but never does so for the commercial segment.**

## D.7 — What is NOT stated about pricing, anywhere in eleven vintages

- No churn, retention, renewal, or cancellation rate for any subscription business.
- No average selling price or average revenue per user, as a level, for any product.
- No list price for any product.
- No statement of the size of any price increase, including the January 2025 one — the filing names
  that it happened, not what it was.
- No statement of whether commercial seats held through any price change (no commercial price change
  is named at all).
- No price/volume/mix bridge for any segment or product line.

**The single exception is FY2025's consumer disclosure**, which names the increase, gives the
subscriber level (89.0 million), and gives the growth rate (8%) — enough to see that volume held.
That disclosure has no counterpart in any other year or any other product, **and the unit series
that made it interpretable was removed in the following vintage.**

Every operating margin in Part A was computed from the two filed figures on the same row of the
same vintage. No margin is computed across vintages.

## E.2 — What this research does NOT establish

- **Nothing here is a valuation, and no gate is cleared by it.** This is Q1/Q2 evidence
  gathering: it describes how the business reports itself and what it declines to report.
- **The eight withdrawn unit series cannot be reconstructed from the 10-Ks.** Where a level was
  never filed (seats, Azure dollars, OEM units), no arithmetic on the filings recovers it.
- **Series broken by definition changes cannot be joined from the filings alone.** In each of the
  four definition changes identified in Part C, no restated prior-period figure was filed.
- **Quarterly filings, earnings releases, and the 8-K exhibits were not searched.** The absence
  findings in Part B are absences *from the 10-K*, which is what was asked. "Commercial bookings"
  and Azure dollar revenue may exist in other Microsoft disclosures; they are not in the annual
  report. That distinction is stated, not blurred.
- **The FY2016 vintage's own comparatives (FY2014, FY2015) were read but not tabulated**, since
  the three-segment structure begins in FY2016 and those years exist only as recast comparatives.

## E.3 — The one number to carry forward, and its caveat

If a single series is taken from this work it should be **commercial RPO with its 12-month
recognition percentage and, from FY2026, its weighted average duration** — because it is the only
level Microsoft still files that is forward-looking, and because the three figures must be read
together. $678 billion at 30%-within-12-months and 2.3 years duration is a materially different
object from $269 billion at 45% and unstated duration, and reading the headline alone would miss
that. It remains a **dollar** series: it contains price, and no unit denominator is filed against it.
