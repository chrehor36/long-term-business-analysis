## WHY THIS DOES *NOT* CHANGE OWNER EARNINGS — AND WHY THAT IS THE POINT
**The capex end of owner earnings is structurally immune to the life question.** Depreciation
is added back inside operating cash flow and actual cash capex is subtracted. A life
extension moves reported EPS and it moves the D&A end of the band; **it cannot move
OCF − SBC − capex by one dollar.** This is precisely why the corpus insists on the
construction it does, and it is the cleanest demonstration of [E4-29]'s point the queue has
recorded: *"Trumpeting EBITDA … implies that depreciation is not truly an expense … That's
nonsense"*, and [E5-41]'s *"reverse float"* — the money is spent first and the expense
recorded later, and here management twice decided to record it even later still.

**What the life question DOES decide is (c) itself** — because the life IS the replacement
cycle, and (c) is the replacement bill.

## THE ACCRUAL ADJUSTMENT — capital committed exceeds capital paid
Unpaid PP&E in accounts payable rose from $6.9bn to **$26.7bn** in FY2026, a **3.9x jump the
filing does not explain**. Cash capex was $115,948M; **capital actually incurred in FY2026
was ~$135.7bn.** On that basis FY2026 owner earnings at the capex end are **$34,782M**
(OCF 182,935 − SBC 12,405 − accrued capex 135,748) — a **0.94% yield.**

**Hypothesis (a) — the capex is growth capital, owner earnings are understated, the floor is
closer than it looks: PARTIALLY SUPPORTED AND STILL LOSES.** It is supported by the
incremental-return arithmetic (47.8% pre-tax on FY2023-26 net new capital) and by the fact
that the fleet genuinely doubled rather than being replaced. But the most generous number the
hypothesis can produce — management's own lives, on the single best year in company history —
is **3.56%, still 1.68 points below a government bond.** The hypothesis cannot reach the
answer it needs even when granted everything.

**Hypothesis (b) — the life extensions have been flattering earnings and the honest number is
far below anything reported: SUPPORTED FOR REPORTED EARNINGS, IRRELEVANT TO OWNER EARNINGS.**
~13% of FY2026 operating income is life-extension (my scaling). But the capex end never
counted that income in the first place. **The evidence supports (b) about the income
statement and (a) about the cash — and both roads arrive at the same place**, which is the
strongest possible position for the verdict, because the two hypotheses were constructed to
point in opposite directions.
