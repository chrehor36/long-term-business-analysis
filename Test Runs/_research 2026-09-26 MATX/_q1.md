## Q1 — CAN I UNDERSTAND HOW THIS MAKES MONEY? **[E3-31]**
- **Unit economics in my own words, no management language.** Matson owns and charters container ships and barges and charges shippers per container (and per vehicle) to move freight on fixed weekly schedules, plus a fuel surcharge that tracks bunker prices. **Two different businesses share the ships.** (1) **The protected domestic trades**: the US West Coast to Hawaii (143,000 containers of forty-foot equivalent in 2025), Tacoma to Alaska (81,900), and Guam and the Micronesian islands (18,000 plus 17,200 "other"). The Hawaii and Alaska trades may be served only by ships *"built in the U.S., registered under the U.S. flag, be manned predominantly by U.S. crews, and owned and operated by U.S.-organized companies"* (Item 1); they were **51% of 2025 Ocean Transportation revenue** (50% 2024, 55% 2023). Freight is mostly westbound (food, retail goods, building materials, cars) and ships return part-empty. Rates in these trades are under the jurisdiction of the Surface Transportation Board (Item 1, quoted at Q2). (2) **The China service (CLX and MAX)**: ships that have unloaded in Honolulu and Guam continue to Ningbo and Shanghai and carry e-commerce goods, garments and electronics to a Matson-only terminal at Long Beach, sold as the fastest, fixed-day, fastest-availability service across the Pacific (130,400 containers in 2025, 64,000 in 2019, 60,000 in 2012). Price there is the transpacific market rate plus whatever premium speed commands. Costs are fuel, union crews (329 billets on the active US-flag fleet), stevedoring (partly through the 35%-owned SSAT terminal joint venture, whose income sits inside segment operating income), charters (six foreign-flag chartered ships for the China service), dry-docking and depreciation on an owned fleet the 10-K values at *"approximately $2.4 billion"*. A **Logistics** segment (freight brokerage, Alaska freight forwarding through Span Alaska, warehousing; $609.0M of 2025 revenue, $44.2M operating income, 7.3%) is asset-light and small. 2025: revenue $3,344.5M, operating income $499.8M (Ocean $455.6M including $32.5M from SSAT), operating cash $547.1M, capital spending $393.4M.
- **The scarce input this business controls.** A **Jones Act-qualified fleet and the terminal slots that go with it**: US-built ships cost what the 10-K shows (three 3,440-container Aloha Class ships for about $1.0 billion, about $335M each, from Philly Shipyard) and take years to deliver (ordered November 2022, delivery 2027-2028), so capacity in the Hawaii and Alaska trades cannot be added quickly by anyone; three dedicated West Coast terminals through SSAT; the Sand Island terminal in Honolulu; *"an industry-leading five weekly departures"* to Hawaii. **The statute, not Matson, supplies the scarcity**; whether an input someone else rations is the owner's franchise is Q2's question.
- **Will the fundamentals look broadly the same in ten years?** **The domestic trades, yes on the filings**: Hawaii and Alaska depend on ocean freight (*"The commerce of both Hawaii, as an island economy, and Alaska, due to its geographical location, are dependent on ocean transportation"*), the Jones Act dates from 1920, and Hawaii volume has sat between 137,200 and 160,200 containers in every year from 2012 to 2025 (10-Ks FY2012, FY2014, FY2015, FY2017, FY2020, FY2023, FY2025). **The China service, not predictably**: its volume doubled with MAX in 2020, its rates moved with the whole transpacific market (below, Q2), and its 2025-2026 filings are dominated by tariffs, port-entry fees and a trade deal of 2025-10-30. The statute's own continuance is a Q4 exposure (a February 2025 lawsuit challenges it; Item 1A).
- **Relatively simple and stable in character [E3-31]?** The way the money is made is simple and I can state it without the company's words: ships, containers, a per-box rate, a fuel pass-through, a protected trade and an open one. The instability sits in the China earnings, and that is a question of how reliable the earnings are (Q2, Q4), not of whether the business can be understood. **Not [E4-46]'s months-of-study case.**
- **VERDICT: [x] IN**: the money is made visibly, by carrying freight on scheduled ships in two trades, one closed to foreign ships by statute and rate-supervised, one open and priced by the market. **IN is not a finding on the franchise.**
