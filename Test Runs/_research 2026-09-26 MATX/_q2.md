## Q2 — IS IT A FRANCHISE? **[E3-03]**

*Every Matson figure below is read from the filed 10-Ks for FY2012, FY2014, FY2015, FY2017, FY2018, FY2019, FY2020, FY2021, FY2022, FY2023, FY2024 and FY2025 (selected-data tables, segment notes, balance sheets, MD&A; extractions `oi_reasons.txt`, `roc_out.txt`, `row_out.txt` in the research folder). Accessions: FY2012 `0001104659-13-016637`, FY2014 `0001104659-15-014988`, FY2015 `0001104659-16-100342`, FY2017 `0001558370-18-001004`, FY2018 `0001558370-19-001448`, FY2019 `0001558370-20-001757`, FY2020 `0001558370-21-001909`, FY2021 `0001558370-22-002040`, FY2022 `0001558370-23-001961`, FY2023 `0001558370-24-001570`, FY2024 `0001558370-25-001875`, FY2025 `0001104659-26-020944`. The FY2012 figures are continuing operations: the registrant was Alexander & Baldwin until the 2012 separation, and nothing before 2012 is used.*

**[E3-03], criterion by criterion, for each of the two businesses Q1 found.** *"An economic franchise arises from a product or service that: (1) is needed or desired; (2) is thought by its customers to have no close substitute and; (3) is not subject to price regulation. The existence of all three conditions will be demonstrated by a company's ability to regularly price its product or service aggressively and thereby to earn high rates of return on capital."* The same 1991 passage: *"In contrast, “a business” earns exceptional profits only if it is the low-cost operator or if supply of its product or service is tight. Tightness in supply usually does not last long"* **[E3-43]**.

- **(1) Needed or desired [x]: passes for both.** Island economies *"dependent on ocean transportation"*; e-commerce shippers pay for speed across the Pacific.
- **(3) Not subject to price regulation [ ]: FAILS for the protected half, in the registrant's own words.** 10-K FY2025, Item 1: *"Matson is subject to the jurisdiction of the Surface Transportation Board with respect to its domestic ocean rates. A rate in the non-contiguous domestic trade is presumed reasonable and will not be subject to investigation if the aggregate of increases and decreases is not more than 7.5 percent above, or more than 10 percent below, the rate in effect one year before the effective date of the proposed rate, subject to increase or decrease by the percentage change in the U.S. Producer Price Index."* The same paragraph stands in the FY2014 10-K, and the FY2012 10-K adds the contrast: *"Matson’s China Service is subject to the jurisdiction of the Federal Maritime Commission (“FMC”). No such zone of reasonableness applies under FMC regulation."* Item 1A adds that Matson *"is a common carrier, whose tariffs, rates, rules and practices in dealing with its customers are governed by extensive and complex foreign, federal, state and local regulations"*. The domestic price moves inside a federally supervised band, and the Hawaii and Alaska trades were 51% of 2025 Ocean revenue.
- **The protection that makes the domestic half look like a franchise is the statute, and the governing document has already ruled on that shape.** The Jones Act excludes foreign-built, foreign-flag ships from the Hawaii and Alaska trades (*"the carriage of cargo between the U.S. West Coast, Hawaii and Alaska on foreign-built or foreign-documented vessels is prohibited"*), which administers every carrier's costs and limits who may enter. **[E2-59]**: commodity businesses *"may be escaped, true, if prices or costs are administered in some manner and thereby insulated at least partially from normal market forces. This administration can be carried out (a) legally through government intervention (until recently, this category included pricing for truckers [...])"*. `THE FRAMEWORK v4.md` reads that passage as: *"the moat belongs to the **regime**"*; *"Regulation caps a franchise ([E3-03] criterion 3) and floors a commodity business; neither creates the class."* Here both halves of that sentence are in the filing at once: the statute floors the domestic trades' economics by rationing entry, and the STB band caps their price. The OTTR run of 2026-09-19 applied the same reading to a regulated utility and closed OUT at Q2; this file follows it. The regime's own continuance is on the page: *"in February 2025, a lawsuit was filed in the U.S. District Court for the District of Columbia challenging the Jones Act"* (Item 1A), and the industry's history under the same statute includes collusion, [E2-59]'s route (b): Horizon Lines, the Jones Act carrier Matson bought in 2015, reported in its FY2013 10-K (`0001193125-14-110319`) that *"In March 2011, we pled guilty to a charge of violating federal antitrust laws in our Puerto Rico tradelane"*.
- **(2) No close substitute [ ]: fails for the domestic half on customer behaviour, and for the China half in the registrant's own words.**
  - **Hawaii has a second carrier that customers use, and volume moves between the two when capacity moves.** Item 1: *"Matson’s Hawaii service has one primary U.S. flagged Jones Act competitor, Pasha"*, plus a Jones Act barge line, foreign-flag carriers for foreign-origin goods, and *"air freight carriers"*. Matson's own volume explanations: 2014, *"Hawaii automobile volume decreased 13.4 percent primarily due to certain customer losses"* (autos 81,500 in 2013, 39,400 in 2023, no longer reported after); 2017, Hawaii containers down 6.5% partly from *"the absence of competitive volume gains in the prior year"*; 2020, 2021 and 2025, volume gained from *"the dry-docking of a competitor’s vessel"*. When Horizon left Hawaii in 2015, **Pasha bought Horizon's Hawaii service** (FY2015 10-K: a *"competitor in the Hawaii market introduced a new vessel in 2015, and also acquired Horizon’s Hawaii service"*), and Pasha is moving to the new Kapalama Container Terminal in 2027 (Item 1). Matson's own outlook language for three years running is *"stable market share"*: share is a thing it defends.
  - **Alaska** has TOTE (roll-on/roll-off, Tacoma to Anchorage) and two Jones Act barge lines, plus air freight and trucking (Item 1).
  - **China**: *"Major competitors to Matson’s China service include international transpacific carriers such as CMA CGM, Zim, Hede and Cosco. Other competitors include air freight carriers."* Item 1A: *"The shipping industry is competitive with limited barriers to entry. Ocean carriers can shift vessels in and out of tradelanes or charter vessels to manage capacity"*; FY2021: *"in 2020 and 2021, in response to rising demand, several new carriers entered the China tradelane in competition with the Company’s China service"*; FY2020: *"the Company’s major competitor in the China service upsized its expedited service"*. *(Fairly stated: the FY2022 10-K qualified the barrier sentence, "limited barriers to entry, especially in international tradelanes"; the FY2023-FY2025 10-Ks drop the qualifier. Either wording puts the China half outside criterion (2).)*

- **Eight years before the boom (2012-2019): the Ocean segment earned 5.4% to 12.5% on revenue (8.6% over the eight years combined) and 3.7% to 11.7% pretax on its assets**, falling as the fleet was renewed (four new Jones Act ships entered 2018-2020): **company return on total capital went from 17-32% on a fleet carried at 1980s cost (property at cost $1,770.8M, accumulated depreciation $1,008.3M at 2012 year-end) to 7-10% in 2017-2019 once new ships were on the books.** From 2015 to 2019 capital rose by about $888M and operating income fell by $67M. That is ordinary money on the capital the trade requires at today's shipyard prices, earned while Matson was the largest carrier in a trade closed to foreign ships and while Horizon had left it.
- **The step up since 2020 came from China, in the registrant's own words, every year**, and moved with the market's supply: 2021-2022 *"higher freight rates in China"*, 2023 down on *"lower freight rates and volume in China"*, 2024 up on *"significantly higher freight rates in China"*, 2025 down on *"a lower contribution from China"*, and the Q2 2026 release: *"higher-than-expected freight rates and demand [...] against a backdrop of tighter supply conditions in the Transpacific tradelane"*. That is [E3-43]'s *"a business"* earning exceptional profits because *"supply of its product or service is tight"*, and [E2-58]'s *"ratio of supply-tight to supply-ample years"*.
- **The domestic price is recorded once in the filings read as a number** (FY2014: general rate increases of *"$ 175"* westbound and *"$ 85"* eastbound per Hawaii container in January 2013 and January 2014, no Guam increase in 2012-2013 while a competitor had just left), and otherwise only in words (*"higher freight rates in the domestic tradelanes"*, 2024). Hawaii volume stayed between 137,200 and 160,200 containers every year 2012-2025 (2011: 140,000), with no step up after 2016. [E2-44]'s first characteristic, price raised *"rather easily [...] without fear of significant loss of either market share or unit volume"*, is not shown; the second, dollar growth *"with only minor additional investment of capital"*, fails on a trade whose next ship costs about $335M.

**[E4-04] and [E4-23]:** the fleet is maintained and renewed, not rebuilt from zero by a technology race; no superstar dependence. Neither is relied on.

**Direction [E4-32], in units [E4-55]:** Hawaii containers flat for fourteen years; Hawaii automobiles 81,500 (2013) to 39,400 (2023); Alaska added by purchase (2015) and 67,400-84,900 since 2016 (up about a fifth, 2017 to 2025); Guam 25,500 (2012) to 18,000 (2025), after the sole-carrier years of 2011-2013 ended; China 60,000 to 130,400 by adding ships (CLX+ in 2020, since MAX). **The protected trades are flat; the growth is in the open trade.**

- **Peers named: 4 filers against the carriers Matson names in its own trades** (Pasha, TOTE, Alaska Marine Lines, Samson Tug & Barge, Aloha Marine Lines, APL, CMA CGM, Zim, Hede, Cosco, ONE, plus Horizon historically). **Not obtainable from SEC filings:** Pasha, TOTE (Saltchuk), Lynden's Alaska Marine Lines, Samson, Aloha Marine Lines (private, file nothing); APL and CMA CGM (French, private, file nothing with the SEC); Cosco and Hede (Chinese, no SEC filings). The two direct Jones Act liner rivals are private, so **the row cannot show Matson's relative position against Pasha or TOTE, and the verdict does not rest on it**: it rests on criterion (3) in the registrant's words, the statute read under [E2-59], and the registrant's own fourteen-year segment series.
- **What the row shows [E3-61]:** the one Jones Act liner carrier with a filed record in Matson's own trades, **Horizon, earned 0.4-3.0% in its better years and lost money in others, under the same statute, and left**: protection did not make the trade a franchise for the second carrier. **Matson's margin moved in the same direction as ZIM's in five of the six year-on-year moves 2019-2025** (both up in 2020 and 2021, both down in 2023, both up in 2024, both down in 2025; in 2022 Matson rose and ZIM fell), damped by the domestic half: the China profit is the transpacific market's. The Jones Act tanker and barge operators (Kirby, OSG) show the same ordinary, cyclical range under the same statute.
- **Untapped pricing power [E3-33], [E5-28]:** not claimed. A price that must stay inside a federally supervised band to avoid investigation is the opposite of the class, and no segment is a near-monopoly (Hawaii has two liner carriers; China many).
- **[E2-53], the dominance test:** fails in its own terms. *"Good or bad, it will prosper"* describes the dominant paper's economics; Matson's Ocean segment, the largest carrier in a protected trade and with its main rival gone from 2015, earned 3.7-6.5% pretax on its assets in 2017-2019.
- **[E2-45], the attacker's test:** entry by a stranger is barred by statute and by a $335M ship, but entry by the incumbent rival is not: Pasha bought Horizon's Hawaii business, added a ship in 2015 and moves to a new terminal in 2027. The barrier belongs to the regime.

**THE STRONGEST EVIDENCE AGAINST THIS VERDICT, hunted hardest [E4-26, E3-41, E4-51].**
1. **Dominance in Hawaii.** *"Matson is the largest carrier of ocean cargo between the U.S. West Coast and Hawaii"*; *"Matson provides over 150 westbound sailings per year, which is greater than its domestic liner ocean competitors’ combined sailings"* (FY2014); five weekly departures. The number-two carrier (Horizon) failed and Matson took the volume (2015 +8.1%, 2016 a record 160,200). That is the one-newspaper-town story [E2-53].
2. **The barrier is enormous and old.** US-built ships at about $335M each and years to deliver; the statute dates from 1920 and has *"broad support from both houses of Congress and the Executive Branch"*; Matson's next Jones Act build cycle is *"not anticipated until the mid-2030s"* (8-K 2025-07-24).
3. **The post-2020 returns are high even in the ample year.** In 2023, with transpacific rates collapsed (ZIM -8.2% adjusted), Matson still earned 11.9% on Ocean revenue and 16.3% pretax on capital without the CCF, more than any year 2016-2019; 2024-2025 earned 20-26% on that basis, and the MD&A credits *"higher freight rates in the domestic tradelanes"* in 2024.
4. **The CLX premium has lasted twenty years**: fixed-day, fastest-availability service to a Matson-only terminal, which the filings say rivals have copied (*"upsized its expedited service"*) without removing.
5. **Guam and the Pacific islands**: sole containership carrier from the US West Coast to Guam after *"the departure of its major competitor from the trade lane in mid-November of 2011"* (FY2014).

**Answered.** (1) Dominance did not produce dominant economics: the years after Horizon left (2016-2019) are the years of 3.7-8.4% on segment assets and 7-13% on total capital; Pasha took Horizon's Hawaii service and keeps investing; customers move when a vessel dry-docks, and the autos were lost to *"certain customer losses"*. The newspaper test is in the economics, and they are not there. (2) The barrier is real and belongs to the statute; [E2-59] and the framework's own ruling on it place a legally administered shelter outside the franchise class, and the same filing puts the price inside an STB band. A shelter the owner rents from Congress, whose challenge is pending in a federal court, is the regime's moat, not Matson's. (3) 2023 is the year that matters most against me, and it is answered by what the filer says moved it: China's volume had doubled with CLX+ and MAX, the CLX premium held, and 2023's 8.1% on segment assets is inside the 2012-2019 range; 2024-2025 are credited mainly to China rates, which [E4-41] instructs a reader to normalise down, not up. The domestic rate rises of 2024 followed three years of inflation in fuel, labour and ship costs, which is what a cost-recovery price inside a supervised band looks like. (4) The CLX premium is a genuine narrow advantage, and it is a spread over a price the transpacific market sets; the row shows it moving with ZIM's margin in five of six year-on-year moves. (5) Guam's sole-carrier years ended (APL runs a US-flag service, Item 1) and Guam volume fell from 25,500 to 18,000.

- **VERDICT: [x] OUT**: on the business. **[E3-03] fails on two criteria, one for each half of the company.** The protected domestic trades fail **criterion (3) in the registrant's own words** (domestic rates under Surface Transportation Board jurisdiction within a presumed-reasonable band), and the shelter that makes them look like a franchise is a statute: **[E2-59]'s legal administration of costs and entry, the regime's moat, which the framework rules creates neither class.** The China service fails **criterion (2) in the registrant's own words** (*"competitive with limited barriers to entry"*, named international carriers and air freight, new carriers entering in 2020-2021). **The demonstration clause fails on the filer's own fourteen-year series**: 5.4-12.5% on Ocean revenue and 3.7-11.7% on Ocean assets in 2012-2019 while Matson was the largest carrier in a closed trade, falling as the fleet was renewed at today's prices, and a step up since 2020 that the MD&A attributes every year to China freight rates set by transpacific supply [E3-43]. Matson is [E3-43]'s *"a business"*: a well-run carrier with a statutory shelter at home and a speed premium abroad. **The file closes here.** Q3 to Q6 are not opened as gates; the material gathered beneath the close follows without verdicts.
