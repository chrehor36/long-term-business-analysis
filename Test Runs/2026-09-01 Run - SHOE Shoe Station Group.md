## (d) PERIMETER — the Shoe Station and Rogan's acquisitions — **PASS, and this is NOT the HON artifact.**

| event | date | consideration | evidence of scale |

**Why this is not the HON perimeter mismatch:**
1. **Both are acquisitions INSIDE the window, consolidated from their dates and never disposed.**
   No business was sold, no segment restated out, no discontinued operation, **no proceeds of any
   disposal sit inside the owner-earnings line.** The cash paid appears in investing activities where
   it belongs. The income statement is continuous on one perimeter for all ten years shown.
2. **Both are small relative to the base.** Rogan's contributed **$80.3M (FY2024) and $75.6M (FY2025)**
   of net sales against $1.2bn and $1.1bn — 6.7% and 6.7%. Shoe Station's first full year (FY2022)

   assets** — the opposite of the PNR case, where the figure was 67.1%.
3. **The company reports the acquired revenue separately every year, which is a candour credit and is
   recorded as one before it is used.** FY2025 10-K, verbatim: *"Rogan's Net Sales were **$75.6 million
   in Fiscal 2025 and $80.3 million in Fiscal 2024**."*
4. **The one real distortion is comparability of "comparable stores Net Sales", and the company
   discloses it.** Rogan's entered the comp base only from the quarter ended 2025-08-02 and enters the

   rebannered"*). **So the −5.6% FY2025 comp is a clean like-for-like number excluding Rogan's, and

**Leg (d) verdict: the earnings history is a like-for-like perimeter. The screen's number is not a

## Q1 — CAN I UNDERSTAND HOW THIS MAKES MONEY? [E3-31]

- **Unit economics in my own words, no management language.** The company rents **426 boxes averaging
  11,600 square feet** in open-air strip centres across 35 states and Puerto Rico, fills them with
  shoes it buys wholesale from Nike, Skechers, Crocs, adidas, Puma, HEYDUDE, HOKA, Birkenstock,
  Converse and Brooks, and sells them to families at a **36.6 cent gross margin on the dollar** — a
  margin struck *after* the cost of buying, distributing and occupying the stores. **It keeps roughly
  6 cents of operating income on each revenue dollar.** Out of the 36.6 cents of gross profit,
  **30.7 cents goes to selling, general and administrative expense** — overwhelmingly store payroll and
  advertising. It runs **one distribution centre** in Evansville, Indiana, through which **97% of
  merchandise passes**, and it ships to each store **once per week**. E-commerce is **about 10% of
  merchandise sales** and is fulfilled mostly out of store stock and staff, so it consumes almost no
  incremental capital. It employs **about 5,000 people, 2,700 of them part-time**, and owns almost
  nothing: **every store is leased**, and the balance sheet's largest assets are **$439.6M of shoes**
  and **$185.6M of leasehold improvements and fixtures**. It has **no debt**, **$130.7M of cash and
  securities**, and it pays **$16.7M of dividends**. The whole business is: *buy shoes, put them in a
  rented room near a supermarket, and sell them for more than you paid, before the rent eats the
  difference.*
- **The scarce input this business controls.** **It does not control one.** The merchandise is other
  people's — **Nike 24%, Skechers 13%, Crocs 9%, together 46% of net sales**, on **no long-term
  contract**. The real estate is rented on a 6.9-year average remaining term. The customer is not
  contracted. **What it has is a 47-year-old habit in a set of secondary markets, a customer database,
  and one distribution centre.** That is a position, not a scarce input, and Q2 tests whether it is
  a moat.
- **Will the fundamentals look broadly the same in ten years?** **Yes as to the mechanism; the count
  is another matter.** People will still buy shoes and a meaningful share will still buy them by
  trying them on. Nothing about the physics is changing. What is uncertain is how many of these
  particular boxes are open — a question about the business, tested at Q2 and Q4, not about
  understandability.
- **[E4-46] check:** this decision does not need five months. One segment, one country, one currency,
  no debt, an eleven-note financial statement set, a product a child can identify. **It is inside the
  circle, and it is one of the simplest names this queue has run.**
