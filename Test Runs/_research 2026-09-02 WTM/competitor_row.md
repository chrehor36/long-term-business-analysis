Everest publishes an explicit table headed **"Outstanding Reserves and Ranges By Segment"**, with As Reported / Low Range / High Range columns:

| At 2025-12-31, gross ($M) | Low Range % | Low Range | As Reported | High Range % | High Range |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Reinsurance | (6.1)% | 21,338 | 22,730 | 6.1% | 24,123 |

| Other (excl. A&E) | (18.0)% | 959 | 1,169 | 18.0% | 1,380 |
| A&E | (21.6)% | 164 | 209 | 21.6% | 254 |
| **Total Gross Reserves** | **(7.5)%** | **31,743** | **34,312** | **7.5%** | **36,880** |

Position statement, verbatim:

> "The size of the range is dependent upon the level of confidence associated with the reserve estimates. **Within each range, management's best estimate of loss reserves is based upon the point estimate derived by our actuaries in detailed reserve studies.** Such ranges are necessarily subjective due to the lack of generally accepted actuarial standards with respect to their development. There can be no assurance that our claim obligations will not vary outside of these ranges."

And on how the range is constructed:

> "Our recorded reserves are an aggregation of our best point estimates for approximately 250 reserve groups and reflect our best point estimate of our liabilities. Our actuarial methodologies develop point estimates rather than ranges and the ranges are developed subsequently based upon historical and prospective variability measures."

Factual note on comparability with WTM: Everest's range is **symmetric** (±7.5% at the total level), constructed around the booked point estimate after the fact. WTM's disclosure runs the other way: an actuarial range computed first, with the booked reserve positioned inside it and stated to be "in the upper portion." Both are low/recorded/high triples; the construction is not the same.

Separately, the auditor's Critical Audit Matter states the audit team was "evaluating the Company's estimated reserve ... by comparing each one to an independently developed range of reasonable estimates" and "developing an overall range of reserve estimates to assess the position of the Company's recorded" reserve. That is the auditor's range, not the filer's disclosure, and it is not what is being counted here.

### (E) Range of reasonable loss reserve estimates: **YES**, and it is the closest match to WTM in this row

Markel publishes the low / recorded / high triple **and** a position statement:

> "Management also considers the range, or variability, of reasonably possible loss outcomes determined by our actuaries when establishing its best estimate for loss reserves. The actuarial ranges represent our actuaries' estimate of a likely lowest amount and likely highest amount that could ultimately be paid to settle the losses that have occurred at a particular point in time. ... Using statistical models, our actuaries establish a **range of reasonable reserve estimates**."

> "**The net loss reserves held for our underwriting operations at December 31, 2025 were $16.7 billion. The actuarially established low and high ends of a range of reasonable net reserve estimates at December 31, 2025 for our underwriting operations was $13.6 billion and $17.8 billion, respectively.**"

The position statement, which is a policy statement rather than a per-year location:

> "In developing its best estimate of loss reserves, **management's philosophy is to establish loss reserves that are more likely to be redundant rather than deficient**, and therefore, will ultimately prove to be adequate. **Management's approach to establishing loss reserves generally results in loss reserves that exceed the calculated actuarial point estimate.**"

with the caveat:

> "Undue reliance should not be placed on this range of estimates as they are only one of many points of reference used by management to determine its best estimate of ultimate losses."

So: low $13.6bn / recorded $16.7bn / high $17.8bn, net, at 2025-12-31, plus an explicit statement of reserving philosophy that places the booked number above the actuarial point estimate. Same species of disclosure as WTM's.
