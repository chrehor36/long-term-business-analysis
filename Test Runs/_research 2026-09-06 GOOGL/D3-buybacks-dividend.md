### 7b. **DID THE BUYBACKS SHRINK THE COUNT, OR ONLY OFFSET SBC? — THE ANSWER**

| Measure | Value |
|---|---|
| Shares outstanding FY2015 year-end | 13,747M |
| Shares outstanding FY2025 year-end | 12,088M |
| **Net change over 11 years** | **−1,659M, −12.07%** |
| Shares actually *repurchased and retired* 2015–2025 | **3,240M** |
| **Shares re-issued (net) into SBC and other over the same period** | **~1,581M** |
| **Share of buyback absorbed by re-issuance** | **~49%** |
| Cash spent 2015–2025 | **$347,944M** |
| Peak count (FY2018) → FY2025 | −13.11% |

**ANSWER: BOTH, roughly half and half.** Alphabet retired 3,240 million shares for $348
billion and the count fell by only 1,659 million. **Just under half of every buyback dollar
— roughly $170 billion over eleven years — went to standing still against stock
compensation, not to shrinking the owner's slice.** The other half was genuine
per-share accretion of about 12% over eleven years, or **roughly 1.16% a year compounded.**

That is the honest number to carry into Q5. It is not nothing, but a business marketed on
its buyback has retired about 1.2% of itself a year net. For scale, the dividend now yields
under 0.3%, so total net shareholder return from capital returns has run near 1.4%/yr —
before the 2026 reversal.

**The first four years are the sharpest evidence.** From 2015 to 2018 Alphabet spent
**$19.4 billion** on buybacks and the share count went **UP** every single year: 13,747 →
13,826 → 13,896 → 13,911. Nineteen billion dollars bought a 1.2% *increase* in shares
outstanding. Not until FY2019 did the buyback outrun dilution at all.

**The 2026 reversal.** The count has already risen 142 million shares (+1.17%) in the six
months to 2026-06-30. Add the mandatory-convertible dilution of **42.8–54.0 million shares
by May 2029** (D1 §5c) and the **$40 billion ATM authority** (D1 §5e), and the direction of
the share count is now up, not down, for the first sustained period since 2018.

### 7c. Class-mix drift — where the dilution lands

Between FY2015 and FY2025 (post-split basis): Class A fell 5,846 → 5,822 (−0.4%); Class B
fell 1,006 → 837 (**−16.8%**, one-way conversions to Class A, never reissued — see D1 §2h);
Class C fell 6,895 → 5,429 (−21.3%). **Class C absorbed nearly the whole reduction because
Class C was the only class being repurchased until July 2021 and is the class used to fund
employee equity.** Class A has been almost perfectly flat for a decade. This is the
control-preservation architecture working exactly as the risk factor describes it.

equity grants."* Alphabet is proposing to fund with new equity a cost it has funded with
operating cash for a decade. Net-settlement withholding *reduces* the share count; funding
it via an ATM *increases* it. **That is a genuine change in the economics of Alphabet's
compensation, and it is a Q3 rationality question.** Nothing has been drawn: *"As of June
30, 2026, we have not sold any shares under the ATM Program, and the full $ 40.0 billion

### 5d. **PAYOUT RATIOS**

| | FY2024 | FY2025 | H1-2026 |

| Dividends paid (cash-flow statement) | $7,363M | $10,049M | $5,231M |
| Net income | $100,118M | $132,170M | $174,771M |

| Operating cash flow | $125,299M | $164,713M | $84,859M |
| **Payout vs operating cash flow** | **5.9%** | **6.1%** | **6.2%** |

**CAVEAT ON THE H1-2026 NET-INCOME RATIO — DO NOT USE IT.** Reported H1-2026 net income of
$174,771M contains a **$135,803M non-operating "Loss (gain) on debt and equity securities,
net"** which the cash-flow statement backs out in full (H1-2025 comparative: $11,411M). Net
income excluding that gain is roughly **$38,968M**, against which the payout is **~13.4%**.
The 3.0% figure is an artifact of a mark-to-market windfall and is meaningless as a payout
ratio. *This gain is also the reason H1-2026 net income ($174.8bn) exceeds full-year 2025
net income ($132.2bn) — flagged here so no later stage of the run treats it as earnings
power.*

**The stable and honest number is the OCF payout: 5.9% → 6.1% → 6.2%.** The dividend is
**tiny and comfortably covered against operating cash flow.** It is not covered against
*free* cash flow in 2026: OCF less capex was $4,261M in H1-2026 against $5,317M of common
plus preferred dividends. **The dividend is currently being paid out of the balance sheet
and the capital markets, not out of free cash flow.**
