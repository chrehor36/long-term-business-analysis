**Summary verdict for Q3.** On the *disclosure* half of "are they honest," Alphabet scores

### 9c. **ARE THE INCENTIVE METRICS GAAP OR NON-GAAP? — NEITHER. THEY ARE MARKET-PRICE METRICS.**

**This is the important finding of section 9 and it deserves stating plainly. Alphabet's
executive incentive program contains no accounting metric of any kind.** Not GAAP revenue,
not GAAP EPS, not an adjusted or non-GAAP measure, not a margin, not a return on capital.
The sole financial performance measure is **relative total shareholder return.**

The proxy says so directly, verbatim:

> "As outlined in our CD&A, **the only financial performance measure we currently
> incorporate within our executive pay program is Alphabet's TSR relative to the companies
> comprising the S&P 100.** As such, and as outlined below, relative TSR is the sole and
> most important financial performance measure as it relates to CAP."

The PSU mechanics, verbatim:

> "The PSU payout depends on Alphabet's relative total shareholder return relative to S&P
> 100 companies (TSR). The PSU awards will vest, if at all, on December 31, 2027, based on
> Alphabet's TSR over a 2025-2027 performance period, subject to continued employment on
> the vesting date. … Depending upon performance, the number of PSUs that vest will range
> from **0%-200% of target**. Upon vesting, each PSU and GSU entitles the recipient to
> receive one share of Alphabet's Class C capital stock."

> "The number of PSUs vesting will be determined by linear interpolation for relative TSR
> ranks between the 25th and 50th percentiles and between the 50th and 75th percentiles."

**Read this two ways, and record both.**

*In Alphabet's favour:* there is **no adjusted-EBITDA game to play, no non-GAAP bridge to
manipulate, no target to sandbag, and no accounting metric management can engineer.** The
framework's usual complaint — that incentive metrics are management-defined non-GAAP
constructs — cannot be made here, because there are none. A 3-year relative TSR against the
S&P 100 is about as unmanipulable a metric as a comp committee can pick.

*Against:* TSR is not a measure of business performance, it is a measure of the share
price, and a share price is a vote of the same market the framework exists to disagree
with. Management is paid on what other people think Alphabet is worth. Nothing in the pay
design rewards return on incremental capital — the exact discipline most at issue while
capex doubles (D3 §6b). **A management paid solely on relative TSR has no compensation
reason to decline a bad project that the market currently applauds.** Under operator rule 9
and the incentive-power injunction **[E4-27]**, that is the incentive to name.

*Note also the settlement currency: PSUs and GSUs vest into **Class C** — the non-voting
class. Executive compensation, by design, cannot dilute founder control (D1 §4c).*

### 9d. CEO pay structure and the triennial cadence

> "The Compensation Committee currently follows a **triennial grant cadence** for CEO
> equity awards. Sundar's last equity award was granted in December 2022, and fully vested
> at the end of December 2025. In March 2026, the Compensation Committee granted a new
> equity award to Sundar to recognize his strong performance as CEO."

This explains the SCT sawtooth: $6.3M (2021) → **$226.0M (2022, grant year)** → $8.8M
(2023) → $10.8M (2024) → $10.9M (2025) → a new grant in March 2026 that will appear in the
2027 proxy. **The 2025 SCT total of $10.9M is not the CEO's economic pay; his CAP of
$213.9M is closer to it.** Any comparison of Alphabet CEO pay to peers on SCT alone is
meaningless without knowing which year of the three-year cycle you are in. The proxy is
candid about it, verbatim: *"Although no new awards were granted to our PEO in 2025, and
the number of unvested shares declined year-over-year, the company's increasing stock price
drove a higher value in the remaining unvested shares, reflecting a 2025 CAP that is only
slightly lower than 2024."*

**CAP reconciliation, 2025, verbatim from the proxy table:**

| 2025 | PEO ($) | Avg other NEOs ($) |

| SCT Total | 10,906,079 | 32,934,710 |
| Deduction for "Stock Awards" column in SCT | — | (31,748,922) |

*Note the $2,059,528 line: "Value of Dividends or Other Earnings Paid on Equity Awards Not
Otherwise Reflected in Total Compensation." This is the dividend-equivalent accrual on
unvested RSUs described in D3 §8b, showing up in CEO pay.*

### 9e. **CEO PAY RATIO**

Verbatim:

> "The 2025 annual total compensation of our median compensated employee was **$310,826**,
> and the 2025 annual total compensation of our CEO was **$10,906,079**. The resulting
> ratio of Chief Executive Officer to Median Employee total compensation is **35 to 1**."

Methodology, verbatim: *"To determine the median employee compensation, we analyzed all of
Alphabet's employees, excluding Alphabet's Chief Executive Officer, as of December 31,
2025. We annualized wages and salaries for employees that were not employed for the full
year. We used base salary and target bonus as the consistently applied compensation measure
to determine the median employee. If this resulted in more than one individual at the
median level, we assessed the grant date fair value of standard equity awards for these
individuals and selected the employee with the median award value."*

**35:1 is a strikingly low ratio for a company of this size — and it is an artifact of the
triennial cadence.** 2025 was a no-grant year. On CAP rather than SCT the ratio is
**$213,903,156 / $310,826 = 688:1**. Both numbers are true; only one is disclosed. **Do not
quote the 35:1 without the cycle caveat.** The median Alphabet employee earning $310,826 is
itself notable — this is a very highly paid workforce, which is the same fact that produces
$25 billion of annual SBC (D3 §8).

## 10. CLAWBACK, HEDGING, PLEDGING, SEVERANCE, REPRICING

### 10a. Clawback policy — verbatim

> "In **October 2023**, the Compensation Committee adopted the Alphabet Inc. Clawback Policy
> in order to comply with the final clawback rules adopted by the SEC under Section 10D and
> Rule 10D-1 of the Exchange Act, and the associated listing standards of Nasdaq."

Applied to equity awards, verbatim: *"Awards granted under the Plan are subject to the
terms of the Alphabet Inc. Clawback Policy, which provides that the company will recoup
compensation as required under the final clawback rules adopted by the SEC under Section
10D and Rule 10D-1 of the Exchange Act, and the associated listing standards of Nasdaq."*
The Plan text carries a "16. Recoupment/Clawback" article. Audit-committee charter duty,
verbatim: *"Implementing and administering any clawback policy allowing Alphabet to recoup
compensation paid to current and former named executive officers, members of senior
management and other employees consistent within applicable laws and the rules of Nasdaq."*

**Assessment: compliance-minimum, adopted on the SEC's deadline, not a voluntary
misconduct-based clawback that reaches beyond restatements.** Never invoked — there has
never been a restatement to invoke it on (§13).

### 10b. **HEDGING AND PLEDGING — PROHIBITED, AND THE PROHIBITION IS UNUSUALLY BROAD**

Verbatim:

> "Our policy against insider trading prohibits all employees and our non-employee directors
> from engaging in any speculative or hedging transactions in our securities. We prohibit
> hedging transactions and similar arrangements or instruments designed to hedge or offset
> decreases in the market value of Alphabet's securities. **No employee or non-employee
> director may engage in short sales of Alphabet securities, hold Alphabet securities in a
> margin account, or pledge Alphabet securities as collateral for a loan.** A copy of our
> policy against insider trading was filed as an exhibit to our Annual Report on Form 10-K
> for the fiscal year ended December 31, 2025."

Governance-highlights summary, verbatim: *"Prohibition of pledging and hedging ownership of
Alphabet shares by executive officers, directors, and employees"*, and: *"We prohibit all
speculative, short-sale, short-term, and hedging transactions involving our securities. **As
a result, our named executive officers cannot insulate themselves from the effects of poor
stock price performance.**"

**Assessment: IN, and better than the market standard.** The ban covers *all employees*,
not merely Section 16 officers; it explicitly bans margin accounts and pledging, which many
large-cap policies permit with pre-approval. Given the founders hold ~$250bn of stock
between them, a pledging ban is a real constraint, not boilerplate. **No pledging by any
insider is disclosed anywhere in the proxy.**

### 10c. **SEVERANCE, CHANGE-OF-CONTROL, GROSS-UPS — THERE ARE NONE**

**A full-text sweep of the 2026 DEF 14A returns no employment agreement, no severance
arrangement, no change-in-control payment, and no tax gross-up for any named executive
officer.** The only hits on "repricing" are the *prohibition* (§10d). The proxy's own
statement, verbatim:

> "Since we do not generally differentiate the benefits we offer our named executive
> officers from the benefits we offer other employees, **we do not maintain any benefit
> plans that cover only named executive officers. We also do not maintain any executive
> retirement programs such as executive pension plans or supplemental executive retirement
> plans.**"

**This is a genuinely strong result and should be recorded as such at Q3.** No golden
parachute, no SERP, no executive pension, no gross-up, no single-trigger acceleration
disclosed. NEO benefits are the ordinary employee benefits: the same 401(k) with *"a
maximum match of $11,750 per employee"*, medical/dental/vision, and so on.

**Perquisites, disclosed in full, verbatim:** *"In 2025, we paid for personal security for
Sundar and Philipp; personal use of a company car for Sundar, Ruth, and Philipp;
incremental costs related to the personal use of non-commercial aircraft for Sundar, Ruth,
Philipp, and Kent; and tax preparation services for Philipp. Pursuant to our Non-Commercial
Aircraft Policy, which sets forth the guidelines and procedures for the personal use of
non-commercial aircraft, named executive officers and their guests may use company aircraft
with appropriate approvals and pay tax on any associated imputed income."* Also: *"We also
pay life insurance premiums for all employees (**other than Larry and Sergey**)."*

The 2025 perquisite totals in the SCT are small — $12,004 to $44,007 for the non-PEO NEOs
in 2024 — against multi-million equity awards. This is not where the money is.

### 10d. Option repricing — prohibited without shareholder approval, verbatim

> "**Repricing Prohibited.** Alphabet may not reprice any stock option granted under the
> Plan without the approval of the shareholders of Alphabet. For this purpose, 'reprice'
> means (1) any of the following or any other action that has the same effect: (a) lowering
> the exercise price of a stock option…"

**No repricing has occurred.** Note the limit of the protection: shareholder approval means
approval by a body in which Page and Brin hold 52.7% of the vote (D1 §4a). The safeguard is
real against a rogue management and inert against the founders.

performance measure at Alphabet in the framework's sense — no metric whose numerator or
denominator management chooses.**

*The one caution: FCF is defined on TOTAL capex, so it is a liquidity measure, not owner
earnings. Alphabet does not disclose a maintenance-versus-growth capex split anywhere. With
capex at $91.4bn in 2025 and running at $80.6bn in the first half of 2026 alone (D3 §6b),
the (b) term of owner earnings is the largest single judgment in this file, and the filings
do not supply it. That is an UNRESEARCHED-or-UNKNOWABLE question for the run to resolve
explicitly, not a defect in Alphabet's disclosure.*

> "…or our competitors of acquisitions, divestitures, investments, new products,

**Alphabet lists its own refusal to guide as a risk factor for its shareholders.** That is

### 15b. **IS BERKSHIRE HATHAWAY AMONG THEM? — NO IN THE PROXY, BUT YES AS A HOLDER, AND THE 10-Q SAYS SO BY NAME**

**Berkshire Hathaway does NOT appear in the 2026 proxy's >5% holder table.** That is
expected and not evidence of absence: the proxy's record date is **2026-04-06**, and
Berkshire's purchase occurred **two months later**.

**Berkshire is named directly in the Q2-2026 Form 10-Q (accession 0001652044-26-000071),
Note 11, verbatim:**

> "Concurrently with the public offering, on June 4, 2026, the company completed a private
> placement of 14 million Class A and 14 million Class C shares to **an affiliate of
> Berkshire Hathaway Inc.** (the 'private placement'). The shares were issued in a private
> placement pursuant to an exemption from registration under section 4(a)(2) of the
> Securities Act of 1933, as amended."

> "The net proceeds received by the company were $ 20.5 billion from the public offering
> and **$ 10.0 billion from the private placement**…"

**ANSWER, stated precisely:**
- **In the 2026 proxy's 5% table: NO.** Only BlackRock is listed.
- **As a holder today: YES.** An affiliate of Berkshire Hathaway Inc. bought **28 million
  shares (14M Class A + 14M Class C) directly from Alphabet for $10.0 billion on
  2026-06-04**, in a primary private placement.
- **Would it ever reach 5%?** No. 28 million shares against 12,230 million outstanding is
  **~0.23%**. On the proxy's Class A-and-B voting base, 14 million Class A shares is
  **~0.21% of Class A and ~0.10% of total voting power.** Berkshire is far below any
  reporting threshold that would place it in the >5% table, and will not appear there.

**Two things follow, and the second matters more than the first.**

First, the mechanical point: **the next proxy (2027) will not list Berkshire**, so an
analyst looking for confirmation in the 5% table will not find it. The 10-Q is the
authoritative record.

Second, the discipline point. **Operator rule 9 says the analyst is a subject of the
psychology too.** The corpus's instruction is to *"hunt disconfirming evidence hardest for
your favourite hypothesis"* **[E4-26]** and that *"you must not fool yourself, and you're
the easiest person to fool"* **[E3-41]**. A $10 billion Berkshire purchase is the single
most seductive fact in this entire research file for a framework built on Buffett and
Munger — and it is precisely the fact most likely to short-circuit Q1 through Q4. It is
evidence about a security. **It is not a verdict, it is not a clearance, and it must not be
allowed to substitute for the gates.** Record it; do not lean on it.

### 16a. The announcement and the FY2023 charges — FY2023 10-K, accession 0001652044-24-000022, verbatim

> "**In January 2023, we announced a reduction of our workforce, and as a result we recorded
> employee severance and related charges of $2.1 billion for the year ended December 31,

### 16b. **AMOUNTS BY YEAR**

| | **FY2023** | **FY2024** | **FY2025** |
|---|---|---|---|
| Employee severance and related charges | **$2.1bn** | **$1.0bn** *(−$1.1bn YoY)* | **none disclosed** |
| Office space exit charges | **$1.8bn** | **$796M** *(incl. accelerated rent and depreciation; −$1.3bn YoY)* | **none disclosed** |
| Accelerated rent and accelerated depreciation | **$269M** *(in addition to exit charges)* | *(included in the $796M)* | **none disclosed** |

**FY2024, verbatim:** *"Employee severance and related charges for the year ended December
31, 2024 were $1.0 billion, a decrease of $1.1 billion as compared to the year ended
December 31, 2023. Office space charges, including accelerated rent and accelerated

were included in Alphabet-level activities."*

**FY2025: the program is over.** The word "severance" appears **exactly once** in the entire
FY2025 10-K, and it is a segment-accounting policy sentence carrying no charge, verbatim:
*"Charges associated with employee severance and office space reductions are also not
allocated to our segments. Additionally, hedging gains (losses) related to revenue are not
allocated to our segments."* Compare the FY2024 wording, which was tense-specific, verbatim:
*"Charges associated with employee severance and office space reductions **during 2023 and
2024** were also not allocated to our segments."*

### 16c. Where the FY2023 charges landed, by line — verbatim

- **Cost of revenues:** *"…was $133.3 billion, an increase of 6% year over year, primarily
  driven by increases in content acquisition costs, compensation expenses, and TAC. The
  increase in compensation expenses included charges related to employee severance
  associated with the reduction in our workforce."* Other cost of revenues carried **$479
  million** of severance.
- **Operating expenses:** *"…were $89.8 billion, an increase of 10% year over year,
  primarily driven by an increase in compensation expenses and charges related to our
  office space optimization efforts."*
- **R&D:** *"$870 million in charges related to our office space optimization efforts"*
- **Sales and marketing:** *"$497 million in employee severance charges"*
- **G&A:** *"$264 million in employee severance charges"*

### 16d. **HEADCOUNT**

From the Business section of each 10-K:

| At December 31 | Employees | Change |
|---|---|---|
| 2022 | **190,234** | — |
| 2023 | **182,502** | **−7,732 (−4.1%)** |
| 2024 | **183,323** | +821 (+0.4%) |
| 2025 | **190,820** | **+7,497 (+4.1%)** |

**The headcount round-trip is complete.** Alphabet ended 2025 with **190,820** employees
against **190,234** at the end of 2022 — **586 MORE people than before the layoffs**, three
years and ~$6 billion of restructuring charges later. The January 2023 announcement (widely
reported as ~12,000 roles, ~6% of the workforce) produced a net reduction of 7,732 in the
reported year-end figure, and every one of those was hired back by the end of 2025.

**Read this carefully at Q3.** It is not evidence of dishonesty — every number was
disclosed contemporaneously and accurately, and headcount is not a promise. But it is
evidence about capital-allocation forecasting: the 2023 reduction was framed as a permanent
resizing, and the resizing did not hold. **A management that cut 4% of its workforce and
rebuilt it within 24 months, while charging $4.2 billion for the exercise, has demonstrated
a forecast error about its own labour needs.** Note it beside the 2026 capex doubling and
the $48.5bn raise (D3 §6b): the pattern is a company reacting to the AI cycle at high
amplitude in both directions.

### 16e. The concurrent useful-life change — flagged, because it is the same January

> "In January 2023, we completed an assessment of the useful lives of our servers and
> network equipment, resulting in a change in the estimated useful life of our servers and
> certain network equipment to six years. **The effect of this change was a reduction in
> depreciation expense of $3.9 billion for the year ended December 31, 2023**, recognized
> primarily in cost of revenues and R&D expenses."

**In the same month Alphabet took $4.2 billion of restructuring charges, an accounting
estimate change reduced depreciation by $3.9 billion — very nearly offsetting it in
reported operating income.** Both were disclosed prominently and separately in the MD&A
highlights; there is no concealment here, and the framework's own D-file on useful lives
(`A1-useful-lives.md`) carries the fuller treatment. But the coincidence of magnitude and
timing is exactly the kind of thing the misjudgment catalogue tells the analyst to look at
twice, and it belongs in this file as a cross-reference.
