# HEADLINE — **FIVE** distinct useful-life movements found. The public narrative names TWO.

| # | Effective | Asset | From | To | Labelled a "Change in Accounting Estimate"? | $ effect disclosed? |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | FY2016 | Information technology assets | "two to five years" (blanket) | "up to 7 years" | **NO** | **NO** |
| 2 | FY2018 | IT assets — *shortened* | "up to 7 years" | "three to five years" | **NO** | **NO** |
| 3 | Jan 2021 | Servers 3→4 yr; certain network equipment 3→5 yr | 3 yr | 4 yr / 5 yr | **YES** | **YES** — $2.6bn |
| 4 | Jan 2023 | Servers 4→6 yr; certain network equipment 5→6 yr | 4 / 5 yr | 6 yr | **YES** | **YES** — $3.9bn |

Changes 1, 2 and 5 are visible **only by diffing Note 1 across vintages.** Change 5 — a
**60% extension of the building life ceiling, from 25 to 40 years** — is the material one, and
Alphabet filed no heading, no dollar effect and no rationale for it. It lands in the same fiscal
year that Alphabet's data-center construction spending steps up sharply (see A2).

# THE VERBATIM LIFE-RANGE LANGUAGE, YEAR BY YEAR

Every quote below is the depreciation-life sentence from **Note 1, "Property and Equipment"**
(in FY2015–FY2017 headed "Summary of Significant Accounting Policies"). Emphasis in **bold** is
mine and marks the changed words.

### FY2015 — 10-K filed 2016-02-11, acc. 0001652044-16-000012
> "Property and Equipment  We account for property and equipment at cost less accumulated
> depreciation and amortization. We compute depreciation using the straight-line method over the
> estimated useful lives of the assets, generally **two to five years**. We depreciate buildings
> over periods **up to 25 years**. We amortize leasehold improvements over the shorter of the
> remaining lease term or the estimated useful lives of the assets. Construction in progress is
> the construction or development of property and equipment that have not yet been placed in
> service for our intended use. Depreciation for equipment commences once it is placed in
> service and depreciation for buildings and leasehold improvements commences once they are
> ready for our intended use. Land is not depreciated."

*No servers/network-equipment breakout exists in this vintage. Life disclosure is a single
blanket range.*

### FY2016 — 10-K filed 2017-02-03, acc. 0001652044-17-000008
> "Property and Equipment  We account for property and equipment at cost less accumulated
> depreciation and amortization. We compute depreciation using the straight-line method over the
> estimated useful lives of the assets. We depreciate buildings over periods **up to 25 years**.
> **We generally depreciate information technology assets over periods up to 7 years.** We
> amortize leasehold improvements over the shorter of the remaining lease term or the estimated
> useful lives of the assets…"

**CHANGE 1 — UNLABELLED.** The blanket "generally two to five years" is deleted and replaced by
an "information technology assets … **up to 7 years**" category. This is a *lengthening* of the
stated ceiling for the IT asset class from five years to seven. No "Change in Accounting
Estimate" heading appears in the FY2016 10-K. No dollar effect is quantified. No rationale is
filed.

### FY2017 — 10-K filed 2018-02-06, acc. 0001652044-18-000007
> "Property and Equipment  We account for property and equipment at cost less accumulated
> depreciation. We compute depreciation using the straight-line method over the estimated useful
> lives of the assets. We depreciate buildings over periods **up to 25 years**. **We generally
> depreciate information technology assets over periods up to 7 years.** We depreciate leasehold
> improvements over the shorter of the remaining lease term or the estimated useful lives of the
> assets…"

*Unchanged from FY2016. (Note the word "amortization" is dropped from the first sentence and
"amortize leasehold improvements" becomes "depreciate leasehold improvements" — presentational.)*

### FY2018 — 10-K filed 2019-02-05, acc. 0001652044-19-000004
> "Property and Equipment  Property and equipment includes the following categories: land and
> buildings, information technology assets, construction in progress, leasehold improvements, and
> furniture and fixtures. Land and buildings include land, offices, data centers and related
> building improvements. **Information technology assets include servers and network equipment.**
> We account for property and equipment at cost less accumulated depreciation. We compute
> depreciation using the straight-line method over the estimated useful lives of the assets. We
> depreciate buildings over periods of **seven to 25 years**. **We generally depreciate
> information technology assets over periods of three to five years (specifically, three years
> for servers and three to five years for network equipment).** We depreciate leasehold
> improvements over the shorter of the remaining lease term or the estimated useful lives of the
> assets…"

**CHANGE 2 — UNLABELLED, and it is a *SHORTENING*.** The IT-asset ceiling goes from "up to 7
years" (FY2016–FY2017) to "three to five years" — a cut of the stated ceiling by two years, and
the first disclosure that servers are on **three** years. Also, the buildings floor is first
stated: "up to 25 years" becomes "**seven** to 25 years". Neither movement is headed as a change
in accounting estimate; neither is quantified; no rationale is filed.

*This is the counterpart of the Microsoft finding: a life change visible only by diffing. Note
that it runs the opposite way from every later change — Alphabet SHORTENED the disclosed IT life
in FY2018 and has lengthened it in every movement since.*

### FY2019 — 10-K filed 2020-02-04, acc. 0001652044-20-000008
> "…We depreciate buildings over periods of **seven to 25 years**. **We depreciate information
> technology assets generally over periods of three to five years (specifically, three years for
> servers and three to five years for network equipment).**"

*Substantively unchanged from FY2018 (word order only: "generally depreciate" → "depreciate …
generally").*

### FY2020 — 10-K filed 2021-02-03, acc. 0001652044-21-000010
> "…We compute depreciation using the straight-line method over the estimated useful lives of the
> assets, **which we regularly evaluate**. We depreciate buildings over periods of **seven to 25
> years**. **We depreciate information technology assets generally over periods of three to five
> years (specifically, three years for servers and three to five years for network equipment).**"

*Life ranges unchanged. But the phrase "**which we regularly evaluate**" is inserted into the
policy in this vintage — filed in the same document that announces the January 2021 change. The
hedge is added at the moment it becomes useful.*

### FY2021 — 10-K filed 2022-02-02, acc. 0001652044-22-000019
> "…We depreciate buildings over periods of **seven to 25 years**. **We depreciate information
> technology assets generally over periods of four to five years (generally, four years for
> servers and five years for network equipment).**"

**CHANGE 3 lands here.** Servers 3→4, certain network equipment 3→5.

### FY2022 — 10-K filed 2023-02-03, acc. 0001652044-23-000016
> "We account for property and equipment at cost less accumulated depreciation. We compute
> depreciation using the straight-line method over the estimated useful lives of the assets,
> which we regularly evaluate. Land is not depreciated. We depreciate buildings over periods of
> **seven to 25 years**. **We depreciate information technology assets generally over periods of
> four to five years (generally, four years for servers and five years for network equipment).**
> We depreciate leasehold improvements over the shorter of the remaining lease term or the
> estimated useful lives of the assets…"

*Unchanged from FY2021.*

### FY2023 — 10-K filed 2024-01-31, acc. 0001652044-24-000022
> "Property and equipment are stated at cost less accumulated depreciation. Depreciation is
> recorded using the straight-line method over the estimated useful lives of the assets, which we
> regularly evaluate. Land is not depreciated. We depreciate buildings over periods of **seven to
> 25 years**. **We depreciate information technology assets generally over a period of six years
> for servers and network equipment.** We depreciate leasehold improvements over the shorter of
> the remaining lease term or the estimated useful lives of the assets. Depreciation for
> buildings, information technology assets, leasehold improvements, and furniture and fixtures
> commences once they are ready for our intended use."

**CHANGE 4 lands here.** Servers 4→6, certain network equipment 5→6. Buildings **still 7–25**.

### FY2024 — 10-K filed 2025-02-05, acc. 0001652044-25-000014
> "Property and Equipment  Property and equipment is comprised of technical infrastructure,
> office space, corporate and other assets currently in service, and assets not yet in service.
> Technical infrastructure includes data center land, buildings and leasehold improvements, and
> servers and network equipment. Office space includes office land, buildings and leasehold
> improvements. Assets not yet in service are those that are not ready for our intended use,
> including data center buildings and servers in the process of construction or assembly.
>
> Property and equipment are stated at cost less accumulated depreciation. Depreciation commences
> once assets are ready for our intended use and is recorded using the straight-line method over
> the estimated useful lives of the assets, which we regularly evaluate **for factors such as
> technological obsolescence and our planned use and utilization**. **We depreciate data center
> and office buildings over periods of seven to 40 years.** We depreciate servers and network
> equipment generally over a period of **six years**. **We depreciate corporate and other assets
> over periods of two to 25 years.** We depreciate leasehold improvements over the shorter of the
> remaining lease term or the estimated useful lives of the assets. Land is not depreciated."

**CHANGE 5 — UNLABELLED, AND THE LARGEST OF THE FIVE.**
Buildings: **"seven to 25 years" → "seven to 40 years"**. A 60% extension of the ceiling.

Verification that it is undisclosed: a full-text scan of the FY2024 10-K (acc.
0001652044-25-000014) for "40 years" returns **exactly one hit** — the policy sentence above.
A scan for "Change in Accounting Estimate" returns **zero hits** in the FY2024 10-K. There is no
MD&A bullet, no Item 7 heading, no Note 1 sub-paragraph, and **no dollar effect on operating
income, net income or EPS** anywhere in the document. The same is true of the FY2025 10-K.

Also new in FY2024 and not previously disclosed: a **"corporate and other assets … two to 25
years"** class. The category architecture is rebuilt in this vintage (land/buildings/IT assets/
CIP/leasehold/furniture → technical infrastructure/office space/corporate and other/assets not
yet in service), which is what makes the buildings movement hard to see: the reader is comparing
across a relabelled taxonomy.

### FY2025 — 10-K filed 2026-02-05, acc. 0001652044-26-000018
> "Property and equipment are stated at cost less accumulated depreciation. Depreciation commences

*Identical to FY2024 on lives. Only "our intended use" → "their intended use" changes.*

### 2026 10-Qs — acc. 0001652044-26-000048 (Q1) and 0001652044-26-000071 (Q2)
Neither interim filing restates the depreciation-life ranges and neither discloses any further
change in estimate. Full-text scans for "six years", "40 years" and "change in accounting
estimate" return no policy hits in either 10-Q. **As at 2026-06-30 the lives in force are:
servers and network equipment 6 years; data center and office buildings 7–40 years; corporate
and other 2–25 years.**

# THE CHANGE-IN-ESTIMATE DISCLOSURES, VERBATIM, AND THEIR DECAY

## CHANGE 3 — servers 3→4 years, network equipment 3→5 years, effective FY2021

**First filed as a forward-looking estimate in the FY2020 10-K** (filed 2021-02-03, acc.
0001652044-21-000010), under an Item 7 MD&A heading "Change in Accounting Estimate":

> "**Change in Accounting Estimate**  In January 2021, we completed an assessment of the useful
> lives of our servers and network equipment and determined we should adjust the estimated useful
> life of our servers from three years to four years and the estimated useful life of certain
> network equipment from three years to five years. This change in accounting estimate will be
> effective beginning fiscal year 2021. **For assets that are in-service as of December 31, 2020,
> we expect operating results to be favorably impacted by approximately $2.1 billion for the full
> fiscal year 2021.** The effect of the change may be different due to our capital investments
> during the fiscal year 2021."

The same FY2020 10-K repeats the change in Note 1 without the dollar figure:
> "In January 2021, we completed an assessment of the useful lives of our servers and network
> equipment and determined we should adjust the estimated useful life of our servers from three
> years to four years and the estimated useful life of certain network equipment from three years
> to five years. This change in accounting estimate will be effective beginning fiscal year 2021."

**Actual effect, filed in the FY2021 10-K** (filed 2022-02-02, acc. 0001652044-22-000019), Note 1:
> "In January 2021, we completed an assessment of the useful lives of our servers and network
> equipment and adjusted the estimated useful life of our servers from three years to four years
> and the estimated useful life of certain network equipment from three years to five years. This
> change in accounting estimate was effective beginning in fiscal year 2021. Based on the carrying
> value of servers and certain network equipment as of December 31, 2020 and those acquired during
> the year ended December 31, 2021, **the effect of this change in estimate was a reduction in
> depreciation expense of $2.6 billion and an increase in net income of $2.0 billion, or $3.02 per
> basic share and $2.98 per diluted share, for the year ended December 31, 2021.**"

*(Per-share figures are PRE-stock-split: the 20-for-1 split had a 2022-07-15 effective date. Post-split
these are ~$0.151 basic / ~$0.149 diluted.)*

**Guided $2.1bn, delivered $2.6bn — 24% more than management's own forward estimate.**

The FY2021 10-K Item 7 keeps the "Change in Accounting Estimate" heading but **strips the dollar
figure out of MD&A**, pointing to the note instead:
> "**Change in Accounting Estimate**  In January 2021, we completed an assessment of the useful
> lives of our servers and certain network equipment. In doing so, we determined we should adjust
> the estimated useful life. This change in accounting estimate was effective beginning fiscal
> year 2021 and is detailed further in Note 1…"

**This is decay step one:** MD&A in the year of announcement carries "$2.1 billion"; MD&A in the
year of effect carries no number and does not even name the old and new lives.

## CHANGE 4 — servers 4→6 years, certain network equipment 5→6 years, effective FY2023

**Forward estimate, FY2022 10-K** (filed 2023-02-03, acc. 0001652044-23-000016), Item 7 heading
"Change in Accounting Estimate":
> "**Change in Accounting Estimate**  In January 2023, we completed an assessment of the useful
> lives of our servers and network equipment, resulting in a change in the estimated useful life
> of our servers and certain network equipment to six years, which we expect to result in **a
> reduction of depreciation of approximately $3.4 billion for the full fiscal year 2023** for
> assets in service as of December 31, 2022, recorded primarily in cost of revenues and R&D
> expenses."

FY2022 Note 1:
> "In January 2023, we completed an assessment of the useful lives of our servers and network
> equipment and adjusted the estimated useful life of our servers from four years to six years and
> the estimated useful life of certain network equipment from five years to six years. This change
> in accounting estimate is effective beginning in fiscal year 2023."

**Actual effect, FY2023 10-K** (filed 2024-01-31, acc. 0001652044-24-000022), Note 1:
> "In January 2023, we completed an assessment of the useful lives of our servers and network
> equipment and adjusted the estimated useful life of our servers from four years to six years and
> the estimated useful life of certain network equipment from five years to six years. This change
> in accounting estimate was effective beginning in fiscal year 2023. Based on the carrying value
> of servers and certain network equipment as of December 31, 2022, and those placed in service
> during the year ended December 31, 2023, **the effect of this change in estimate was a reduction
> in depreciation expense of $3.9 billion and an increase in net income of $3.0 billion, or $0.24
> per basic and $0.24 per diluted share, for the year ended December 31, 2023.**"

**Guided $3.4bn, delivered $3.9bn — 15% more than management's own forward estimate.**
*(Both times the realised benefit exceeded the guided benefit.)*

FY2023 Item 7 keeps the heading but again strips the dollar figure:
> "**Change in Accounting Estimate**  In January 2023, we completed an assessment of the useful
> lives of our servers and network equipment resulting in a change in the estimated useful life of
> our servers and certain network equipment to six years. This change in accounting estimate was
> effective beginning fiscal year 2023. For additional information, see Note 1…"

The FY2023 10-K does, however, put the $3.9bn in the MD&A "Executive Overview" bullets, and
attributes segment operating-income improvement to it in four separate places:
> "Cost of revenues was $133.3 billion… Additionally, cost of revenues benefited from a reduction
> in depreciation due to the change in estimated useful lives of our servers and network equipment."
> "Operating expenses were $89.8 billion… Operating expenses benefited from the change in the
> estimated useful lives of our servers and certain network equipment."
> "**Google Services** operating income increased $13.2 billion from 2022 to 2023… Additionally,
> operating income benefited from a reduction in costs driven by the change in the estimated useful
> lives of our servers and certain network equipment."
> "**Google Cloud** operating income of $1.7 billion for 2023 compared to an operating loss of $1.9
> billion for 2022 represents an increase of $3.6 billion… Additionally, operating income benefited
> from a reduction in costs driven by the change in the estimated useful lives of our servers and
> certain network equipment."

**Load-bearing observation for the run:** Google Cloud crossed from a $1.9bn operating LOSS to a
$1.7bn operating PROFIT — a $3.6bn swing — in the same year a $3.9bn depreciation benefit was
booked, and Alphabet itself names the life change as a driver of that segment's turn. Alphabet does
not disclose the segment-level split of the $3.9bn, so the share attributable to Cloud is
**UNRESEARCHED-not-obtainable from the filed document.** The question of whether Google Cloud's
maiden operating profit is an artefact of the life extension cannot be closed from the 10-K.

## IS ANY RATIONALE FILED?

**For changes 3 and 4: only a bare assertion of process, never a reason.** The filed rationale in
both cases is the identical formula — "we completed an assessment of the useful lives of our
servers and network equipment" — with no disclosed inputs, no evidence of longer service life, no
utilisation data, and no third-party corroboration. Alphabet never states *why* the assessment
concluded what it concluded.

**For changes 1, 2 and 5: no rationale at all**, because no change is acknowledged.

The nearest thing to a rationale is the Critical Accounting Estimates section, which only appears
in this form from FY2024. **FY2025 10-K (acc. 0001652044-26-000018), Item 7:**
> "**Property and Equipment**  We assess the reasonableness of the useful lives of our property and
> equipment periodically or when events indicate a change is necessary. To determine the useful
> lives of our technical infrastructure, we rely on multiple inputs, including **historical asset
> performance, expected technology advancements, and our future infrastructure deployment plans**.
> Any change in the estimated useful lives is recognized on a prospective basis."

FY2024 (acc. 0001652044-25-000014) carried the weaker version, without the named inputs:
> "**Property and Equipment**  We assess the reasonableness of the useful lives of our property and
> equipment periodically as well as when other changes occur, such as **changes to ongoing business
> operations, changes in the planned use and utilization of assets, or technological advancements**,
> that could indicate a change in the period over which we expect to benefit from the asset."

Alphabet elevated Property and Equipment useful lives to a **Critical Accounting Estimate** for the
first time in the FY2024 10-K. It was not a critical accounting estimate in FY2023 or earlier, in
the years when the two labelled changes were actually made.

## FORWARD SIGNAL — the risk factor language moved in the FY2025 10-K and the 2026 10-Qs

FY2025 10-K risk factor (acc. 0001652044-26-000018) and both 2026 10-Qs carry, verbatim:
> "We have invested and expect to significantly expand our investment in property and equipment,
> including our technical infrastructure, and we expect these assets to benefit our business over
> their estimated useful lives. Changes in facts and circumstances such as changes to **historical
> asset performance, expected technology advancements, and future network deployment plans** could
> change the period over which we expect to benefit from the asset and impact our financial
> condition and operating results."

FY2024 (acc. 0001652044-25-000014) had the older wording:
> "…Changes in facts and circumstances such as **changes to ongoing business operations, changes in
> the planned use and utilization of assets, and/or technological advancements**, could indicate a
> change in the period over which we expect to benefit from the asset and impact our financial
> condition and operating results."

The FY2025/2026 formulation newly names **"expected technology advancements"** as a trigger. Given
that servers are now on a six-year life inside an AI-accelerator refresh cycle, this is the
sentence to watch: it is drafted to support a life change in **either** direction, and it is the
only place Alphabet has pre-positioned for a *shortening*.

# WHAT THE FIVE CHANGES ARE WORTH, CUMULATIVELY

Filed, quantified benefits only (the two labelled changes):

| Change | Year of effect | Depreciation reduction | Net income increase |
|---|---|---|---|
| Servers 3→4 yr, network 3→5 yr | FY2021 | **$2.6bn** | **$2.0bn** |
| Servers 4→6 yr, network 5→6 yr | FY2023 | **$3.9bn** | **$3.0bn** |
| **Total filed and quantified** | | **$6.5bn** | **$5.0bn** |

These are the **first-year** effects only. Alphabet has never filed a cumulative or run-rate figure,
and never filed anything for the FY2016, FY2018 or FY2024 movements. **The benefit of the FY2024
buildings extension from 25 to 40 years is UNRESEARCHED-not-obtainable from the filed
documents** — it cannot be computed from disclosure because Alphabet does not break out gross
building cost separately from the "technical infrastructure" and "office space" aggregates from
FY2024 onward (see A3).

**Net effect on the asset base:** a server bought by Alphabet in 2020 was written off over three
years. The same server bought in 2023 is written off over six. **The disclosed life of Alphabet's
core revenue-producing asset has doubled in three years**, and the disclosed life ceiling of its
buildings has risen 60% in one, at exactly the moment capital expenditure is compounding fastest.
