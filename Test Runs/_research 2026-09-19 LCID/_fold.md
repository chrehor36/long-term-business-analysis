### THE PRIOR THE BRIEF ASKED TO BE ARGUED AGAINST, AND HOW IT FARED
The brief named its own likeliest error: that a pre-profit, capital-consuming carmaker is an easy close, and asked the run not to let that shortcut Q1 (the
non-vehicle revenue) or the choice between OUT and UNKNOWABLE. **Half right.** The close did come, but at Q2 on Lucid's own pricing record, not at Q4 on the losses;
and Q1's look at the non-vehicle revenue found the thing the prior did not anticipate: **the one technology licensee (Aston Martin) and the largest identifiable
customer (the Saudi government) are both in the controlling holder's circle**, and no licensing revenue from an unrelated party appears in any filing read.
UNKNOWABLE was argued at Q1 ([E3-31]'s *"subject to constant change"*) and refused, because the business the filings show can be judged on the record; the
future programmes were put outside the circle so that they could not be used later to rescue it.

### WHY Q2 CLOSED
- **Every car cost more to build than it sold for, in every year**: cost of revenue per car delivered $376.8k, $322.6k, $169.0k, $164.8k (FY2022-25), then
  **$202.4k in H1 2026**, against $75-98k of revenue per car; gross margin -92.8% (FY2025), -107.4% (H1 2026), negative even before the write-downs.
- **Prices were cut to sell more** [E3-03] criterion 2, [E2-44], [E4-37]: FY2023 deliveries +37%, revenue -2%, *"pricing and incentives offered"*; FY2024 *"a lower
  average selling price"*; vehicle revenue per delivery $96.9k to $73.5k.
- **The row** (eleven names, eight with figures): Lucid last in every year, -258.7% operating margin in FY2025 against Rivian -66.5% and Ford Model e -67.1%; about
  $221k of operating loss per car against Rivian's $85k. **My recomputation of Lucid reproduced the RIVN run's row to the decimal**, which checks both runs.
- **[E4-04] and [E4-23]**: each programme buys a replacement; the engineer-CEO who personified the efficiency claim resigned in February 2025.

### THE SKIP REASON, FROM THE FILED RECORD
- **The label was wrong in both words.** The sanity guard fired on its **lower yield bound**, from the correct post-split count: -176.3%, -161.1% and -192.4% on the
  `a8bc84f` owner earnings over a $1,911.2M cap at the 2026-08-31 close. Nothing was broken; the guard caught a real finding.
- **The HBB layers do not exist here** (one class, one undimensioned dei fact). **The reverse split (1:10, 2025-08-29) is real** and leaves unadjusted pre-split
  counts in companyfacts' dei series, which make the `a8bc84f` `share_count_shift()` read 0.1308.
- **The general lesson for the tooling**: a range guard on the quotient cannot tell a broken denominator from a business that consumes more than its market value.
  **Had the stale pre-split count been used, the yield would have been about -22.6%, inside the guard, and the broken input would have passed.** The guard's
  label should say "outside range: read", not "broken input".
