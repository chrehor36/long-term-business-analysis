### The (c) judgment — a DISCLOSED JUDGMENT, and the filing decides it

**Which case is this under [E5-20]?** The corpus default is D&A **[E3-44, E2-41]**; for
capital-intensive businesses — *"in the case of all railroads, merely spending their
depreciation expense will not keep them in the same place"* — the D&A end is **INVALID**.
**Otis is the opposite of that class on the plant side and identical to it on the intangible
side, and the second half is the finding:**

- **Plant.** Fixed assets, net are **$743M on $14,431M of sales**. Capital expenditures ran
  $115–156M against depreciation of $113–126M. This is one of the least capital-intensive
  businesses this project has run. **[E5-20]'s railroad exception plainly does not apply to the
  plant.**
- **The intangible, and this is where the flattering construction dies.** Note 7 discloses the
  gross composition of intangible assets: **"Purchased service portfolios | $2,133 | $(1,818)"**
  — Otis has bought **$2,133 million** of service portfolios, of which **$1,818 million, or
  85%, has already amortised away.** Patents and trademarks are $19M and customer relationships
  $61M; **96% of the gross intangible is purchased maintenance contracts.**
  **A purchased service portfolio is not an acquired brand that never wears out. It is exactly
  the asset that churns, and Otis's own Item 1 says the portfolio is grown "through conversion
  of newly installed units into maintenance contracts, through prospecting and winning units
  already in service from customers using another service provider and **through
  acquisitions**."** The **$62M of annual amortisation is therefore a genuine renewal charge**,
  not the acquired-intangible padding that made the D&A end invalid at HON (61% of D&A) and
  UNH. **The generous construction that excludes amortisation is refused, and refusing it costs
  $70M a year of owner earnings.**
- **And the cash spend on portfolio renewal now runs above the amortisation charge:**
  $36M (2023) → $87M (2024) → **$109M (2025)** → **$193M in the six months to 2026-06-30
  alone**, *"primarily in our Service segment"* (Note 7), against a $62M amortisation charge and
  a five-year-mean $72M. **[E2-23]'s (c) covers what the business "requires to fully maintain
  its long-term competitive position and its unit volume", and for Otis the unit volume IS the
  maintenance portfolio.** How much of that spend is maintenance and how much is growth is **not
  disclosed** — Otis does not publish the split of portfolio additions between conversion,
  recapture and acquisition. **That gap is recorded as a work order, not filled with an
  assumption, and it is carried as the bottom of the band.**

### **Maintenance capex — a DISCLOSED JUDGMENT, and at Otis the DIRECTION of the default is

**[E5-20]** makes the D&A end **INVALID** for a capital-intensive business — *"in the case of all
