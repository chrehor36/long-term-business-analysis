## Q1 — CAN I UNDERSTAND HOW THIS MAKES MONEY? [E3-31]

- **Unit economics in my own words, no management language.** Home Depot owns 2,359 big-box
  stores averaging about 104,000 square feet of enclosed space plus 24,000 square feet of
  outdoor garden area, **90% of them owned outright** (including stores on ground leases),
  sited over forty-five years in the parking-lot positions that serve American suburbs. It
  buys 30,000 to 40,000 items — lumber, appliances, paint, plumbing, tools — from vendors
  worldwide, marks them up **33.3%**, and sells them to **1.6 billion** customer visits a
  year at an average ticket of **$90.56**. Out of that 33.3% gross margin it spends 18.6
  points running the stores and 2.0 points depreciating them, leaving **12.7 points of
  consolidated operating margin** — and **13.5 points in the retail segment**, because the
  distribution business bolted on in 2024–2026 earns 2.5. Suppliers finance part of the
  inventory ($11,491M of payables against $25,817M of stock). Interest takes 1.4 points and
  tax 2.7, so **8.6 cents of every dollar of sales becomes net income**. The business
  converts that into roughly **$16–21bn of operating cash a year**, spends **$3.7bn** keeping
  the stores, the supply chain and the technology current, pays **$9.2bn** of dividends, and
  since 2024 has spent **$24.2bn** buying trade distributors instead of its own stock. There
  is no annuity, no subscription, no float, no deferred-fee pool. **Every dollar has to be
  sold again next year.**
- **The scarce input this business controls: real estate and the drive time attached to it.**
  Not the merchandise — HD's own competition section lists the substitutes at length. Not the
  brands, though HD owns more proprietary ones than Lowe's does. What is scarce is 2,359
  large-format sites with parking, loading, garden centre and highway access, in
  neighbourhoods where the land is now zoned and priced to make a new one impossible; and
  the ten-minute drive that sends a $40 emergency plumbing trip to Home Depot rather than
  anywhere else. That asset is genuinely scarce and genuinely owned. It is also, precisely,
  a **local** asset — which is why the moat is measured store by store at Q2 and not as a
  national aggregate. **Note what the $24.2bn bought: 1,340+ leased distribution branches
  where that scarcity does not apply.** Scored at Q2.
- **Will the fundamentals look broadly the same in ten years?** Yes. Americans will still
  own 145 million housing units with a median age near forty; those units will still need
  roofs, water heaters, paint and lumber; the heavy, bulky, urgent half of that demand will
  still be bought within driving distance. The format has been essentially unchanged since
  the 1990s and survived Amazon's first twenty-five years intact. The residual uncertainty —
  whether specialty trade distribution earns a decent return on the capital HD has now put
  into it — is named at Q2 and carried as a quantified death at Q4.
- **[E4-46] check:** this decision does not need five months. It is one of the most
  intelligible businesses on the exchange.

## Q2 — IS IT A FRANCHISE? [E3-03]

**The question as posed: is the two-player big-box duopoly a franchise?** The answer this
run reaches is that **the format is a franchise, Home Depot holds the stronger half of it,
and Home Depot has just spent $24.2 billion buying its way into an adjacent business where
the franchise does not apply.** Class **NARROW**, direction **NARROWING absolutely, WIDENING
relative to Lowe's, and DILUTING by acquisition**. Worked below.

**[E3-03], the three criteria:**
- **Needed or desired [x]** — 1.6 billion transactions a year against an aged housing stock;
  a material share of the spend (a failed water heater, a leaking roof) is not discretionary.
- **No close substitute — [ ] FAILED on the issuer's own words, and recorded as such.**
  HD's Item 1: "Our industry is **highly competitive, highly fragmented, and evolving**… we
  face competition… from a variety of retailers (including those operating reseller
  marketplaces), suppliers, service providers, distributors and manufacturers… a number of
  other home improvement retailers; local, regional and national hardware stores; electrical,
  plumbing and building materials supply houses; and lumber yards… specialty design stores,
  showrooms, discount stores, paint stores, specialty and mass digital retailers, warehouse
  clubs, MRO distributors, national and local wholesale supply distributors, home décor
  retailers… **Online and other digital capabilities, as well as AI tools, facilitate
  competitive entry, price transparency, and comparison shopping, increasing the level of
  competition we face.**" A 2×4 has close substitutes everywhere. **What has no close
  substitute is the site, not the product** — a local monopoly of convenience, not a product
  franchise. Same finding as Lowe's; Home Depot holds the better half of it.
- **Not price-regulated [x].**

**Must the moat be continuously rebuilt? [E4-04]** No — it must be continuously *defended*,
which [E5-23] prescribes for every moat. The basis (owned sites, local density, supplier
scale) is not a depleting asset being periodically replaced. **But note what the capital
account actually did across FY2024–FY2026: $10.4bn of capex defending the same advantage,
and $24.2bn buying a different one.** The [E4-04] test — "does the spending defend the same
advantage, or buy its replacement?" — returns *both*, and the acquisition is **2.3 times the
larger number**. At Lowe's the same test returned both with the acquisition 4.6x the capex
in a single year. Recorded at both companies; scored at Q3.

**Does success depend on a great manager? [E4-23], the Mayo test.** No single surgeon. The
format has survived four CEOs since 2000, including a well-documented mediocre stretch, and
**is being run at this moment by an interim Office of the CEO** after the chief executive
took medical leave on 2026-08-12 — with the fiscal 2026 guidance reaffirmed six days later.
That is close to a live demonstration of the Mayo property. The dependence is on *ordinary
daily competence at 2,359 sites*, which is the [E3-38] class and is recorded as the Q3
weight case, where it belongs — not here as a compliment to anyone.
