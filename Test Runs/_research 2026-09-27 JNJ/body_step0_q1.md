## Q1 — CAN I UNDERSTAND HOW THIS MAKES MONEY? **[E3-31]**
- **Unit economics in my own words, no management language.** Johnson & Johnson is two businesses under one balance sheet. **(i) Prescription medicines, 64% of sales and 84% of segment profit.** It discovers or buys a molecule, spends a decade and billions proving it safe and effective, and then sells it under patent to wholesalers, hospitals and pharmacies, paid for by Medicare, Medicaid, insurers and national health systems after rebates (*"Accrued rebates, returns and promotions | 19,990"* at 2026-06-28). The price is highest while the patent and the regulatory exclusivity hold, and falls hard when they end (*"loss of patent exclusivity for a product often is followed by a substantial reduction in sales"*, 10-K 2025, Item 1A); STELARA, its largest product in 2023 ($10,858M), sold $6,078M in 2025. The segment keeps about 37 cents of each sales dollar before tax after spending 20 cents on research. **(ii) Medical devices, 36% of sales and 16% of segment profit.** Staplers, sutures, energy devices and surgical instruments; hip, knee, trauma and spine implants; heart-rhythm catheters and mapping systems, heart pumps (Abiomed) and calcium-cracking catheters (Shockwave); contact lenses and cataract lenses. Sold to hospitals, surgeons and eye-care practices per procedure, paid under the payers' fixed procedure rates and national tenders; the segment keeps about 12 cents of each sales dollar before tax. Three wholesalers took *"approximately 21.8%, 15.5% and 11.1% of the total gross revenues"* in 2025.
- **The scarce input this business controls.** In medicines, **the patent and regulatory exclusivity on each product**, for its term, and the clinical data behind it; in devices, **regulatory approvals, the surgeon training and installed platforms (mapping systems, robots, pumps) that tie procedures to its disposables**, and distribution to hospitals in most countries. The filer: *"The development of new and innovative products, as well as protecting the underlying intellectual property of the Company’s product portfolio, is important to the Company's success in all areas of its business."* Whether any of these is a franchise in [E3-03]'s sense is Q2's question.
- **Will the fundamentals look broadly the same in ten years?** **The mechanism, yes**: in 2036 JNJ will still sell patented medicines and procedure devices to payer-funded buyers. **The products, not on the filer's own words**: *"New products introduced within the past five years accounted for approximately 25% of 2025 sales"*; *"Development of successful products and technologies is also necessary to offset revenue losses when the Company’s existing products lose market share due to various factors such as competition and loss of patent exclusivity"* (10-K 2025, Item 1A). The two largest products of 2025 (DARZALEX, STELARA) will both be past their exclusivity within the decade on the filer's own disclosures of biosimilar filings; that is [E4-04]'s question, carried to Q2 under the 2026-09-20 ruling that applies it there as a competence limit.
- **Relatively simple and stable in character [E3-31]? Understood, with the technology limit named.** The economic mechanism in each segment (a protected product sold at a payer-administered price for a term; a procedure consumable sold into hospitals under fixed procedure rates) can be stated in five minutes **[E4-46]**, and the filer reports each segment's sales by product and franchise, its income before tax, and the company's price and volume separately. What is not simple is judging which molecule or device generation will win; the framework places that at Q2, not Q1 (*"So [E4-04] is applied as a competence limit, never as a fourth franchise criterion"*), and the unit of the test is the company **[E4-08]**. The same reading gave Q1 IN at Merck and AbbVie (medicines) and at Medtronic and Abbott (devices). **IN at Q1 is not a finding that the durability of either segment can be judged.**
- **VERDICT: [x] IN**: the money is made visibly, as patented medicines and procedure devices sold to wholesalers, hospitals and pharmacies and paid largely by third parties, measured by segment, franchise, product, price and volume in the 10-K. **IN is not a finding on the franchise; the technology-durability question is Q2's.**
