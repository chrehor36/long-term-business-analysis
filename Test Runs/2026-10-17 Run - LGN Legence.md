- **What the business is, from the filing.** A mechanical, electrical and plumbing (MEP) engineer and contractor:
  Engineering & Consulting (design, energy consulting, program management; $726.3M of 2025 revenue) and Installation &
  Maintenance (fabrication and installation of HVAC and process piping, and service; $1,824.2M). "Historically, the vast
  majority of our revenues have been generated by customer contracts that are fixed-price"; "Our largest expense is the
  wages and salaries of our employees"; "we typically complete most of our jobs within nine months"; 44 collective
  bargaining agreements cover about 4,500 of 7,000 employees (10-K Item 1 and Item 7, `0002052568-26-000008`).
- **The key variables and whether they are foreseeable** **[M1998-044]**: (a) the margin a fixed-price bid contractor
  earns on labor it must estimate; (b) the volume of nonresidential construction and retrofit work, and inside it the
  share from data centers and technology (42.7% in 2025, up from 32.0% in 2023); (c) what the company pays for the
  businesses it buys. The first is old and slow to change: mechanical contracting has run on fixed-price labor bids for
  generations, and a rival's filings (Comfort Systems USA, 2009 to 2025, Q2) show how its margins behave through a full
  construction cycle. The second is a demand cycle, read through the field's returns over the cycle at Q2 and the cycle
  base at Q7; it is not a change in how the business makes money. The third is a use of the money (Q6).
- **Test 5, would the insiders write it down** **[M2000-105]**: the company issues one-year guidance only; no insider
  forecast of data-center construction ten years out exists in any filing read. **Test 6, can I name the winner, not just
  the industry** **[M2012-067]**: "Just because Charlie and I can clearly see dramatic growth ahead for an industry does
  not mean we can judge what its profit margins and returns on capital will be as a host of competitors battle for

  not make the contractor's economics unknowable, because the margins and returns of the field can be read from the

  earns its money, so it is carried forward, not used to close the file. This is a narrow IN, written as such.

**The castle tests, applied to the deciding part, each with its filing fact.**
1. **Key factors and their permanence** **[M1995-038]**. The filer names one: "our strategy of providing a comprehensive

   larger project"; and under plan-and-specification work "our client is responsible for designing the project and we

2. **The money test** **[M2011-015]**: could a well-funded attacker build this? The filing answers it. The credit
   agreement that funds the company is dated 2020-12-16 (8-K 2026-01-02), and "We have made approximately 24 acquisitions
   since December 2020" (10-K Item 1), to stand today as "a leading provider" with $2.55bn of 2025 revenue. A sponsor with

   is not the same as beating them, and I set that down against my own reading; but the test asks whether money can put a
   rival into the field at the same scale, and here it did. The row's verdict on a yes: "If the answer had been yes, we
   wouldn’t have done it." **[M2011-015]**. And what is gained quickly can be lost quickly: "usually if something can gain
   competitive advantage very quickly, you have to worry about them losing it quickly, too" **[M2002-050]**.
3. **Pricing power and the agony before a rise** **[M2005-020]**. The filer, in its own words: "If our costs are subject

   customer contracts are typically fixed-price" (10-K Item 7). A business that prices each job by bid and absorbs the

## WHAT IN THE FRAMEWORK WAS WRONG OR UNCLEAR
(1) Q2 carries two closes that overlap here: the standing castle that protects only ordinary returns over a full cycle is
OUT, and a change for the better is credited only on a full cycle, TOO HARD (NATURE) until then. A field with an ordinary
full-cycle record and a recent, untested improvement fits both; the text does not say which governs. I rested the box on
the money test, the pricing test and the low-cost comparison, which do not wait for a downturn, and said so. (2) The
business-of-parts paragraph decides on five-year operating profit; this filer reports three years of segment gross profit
only, and the run had to confess a substitute. (3) Q4 asks for eight to ten years of balance sheets and Q7 for a five-year
window; a company formed in 2020 and public since 2025 has four and a half years on the record, and the text gives no rule
for a short history (shorter window, or TOO HARD (WORK) for want of a cycle). (4) The Up-C and its TRA: the text says nothing
on whether a TRA liability is net debt, how "the company's own tax" applies when 85% of the tax savings go to former owners,
or whose stock pay counts when the sponsor's vehicles settle it; each was confessed as a CONVENTION of the run. (5) The
serial-acquirer convention takes "organic growth as the filer reports it"; this filer reports none for the window years,
and the run derived it from the MD&A's attribution percentages.
