## GATE 2 — MOAT
Franchise test [E3-03], from Best Buy's own 10-K: **"Our competitors are
primarily multi-channel retailers, e-commerce businesses, technology
service providers, traditional store-based retailers, vendors and mobile
network carriers who offer their products and services directly to
customers. We believe our ability to help customers online, in our stores
and in their homes, and to connect technology product and solutions with
customer needs, provide us key competitive advantages. Some of our
competitors have lower cost operating structures and seek to compete for
sales primarily on price."** — a direct, disclosed concession that named
competitors (plainly including Amazon, though not named explicitly in this
excerpt) operate lower-cost structures and compete on price.
- **No close substitute**: **weak** — the company's own words concede a
  structural cost disadvantage against e-commerce competitors; its stated
  differentiation (services, omnichannel fulfillment, in-home
  installation) is real but modest, not a pricing-power moat.
- Net income has been volatile and net-declining: $1,419M (FY2023) →
  $1,241M (FY2024) → $927M (FY2025) → $1,069M (FY2026), a partial recovery
  in the most recent year after a deeper trough, consistent with ongoing
  e-commerce pressure on consumer electronics retail generally.
**Class: NARROW-to-NONE** (real but modest services/fulfillment
differentiation against a self-conceded structural cost disadvantage),
**Direction: NARROWING**. This run resolves the classification to the
weak end (treated as NARROW for Gate 6 sizing, but flagged as close to
NONE) given the company's own concession.
**VERDICT: PASS with a weak NARROW classification**, explicitly flagged as
close to the NONE boundary rather than comfortably NARROW.
