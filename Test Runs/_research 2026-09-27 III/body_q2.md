## Q2 — IS IT A FRANCHISE? **[E3-03]**

*Every ISG figure below is read from its filed 10-Ks for fiscal 2007 to 2025 (accessions in `submissions.json`; texts in `filings/`, flattened in `flat/` by `flat.py`), the 2026 10-Qs and the 8-K releases, or from SEC companyfacts checked against those statements (Step 0). Peer figures are each peer's SEC companyfacts, built by the construction the JKHY and WFCF runs used, unchanged (`peers/row.py`; outputs `peers/<TICKER>_row.txt`, summary `peers/summary.py`, `peers/summary_out.txt`). The pricing and competition sweep of every downloaded 10-K, 10-Q and release is `sweep.py`, output `pricing_hits.txt`.*

**The hypotheses I formed while reading, both stated so they can be attacked [E4-26].** *(a) The firm large buyers call when they sign a big technology-services contract: the successor to TPI, the brand in sourcing advice for over thirty years, holding what it calls nearly ten million benchmark data points and a watch on 180,000 service contracts that no newcomer has, serving 75 of the world's 100 largest enterprises, with 46% of revenue now recurring (research subscriptions, provider ratings that the vendors themselves pay for, a supplier-governance platform, multi-year public contracts), independent of the vendors, and riding the AI wave (30% of revenue).* *(b) A consulting firm that sells its advisers' hours in a market its own 10-Ks call "highly competitive, fragmented" with "limited barriers to entry", where "a significant number of independent sourcing and advisory firms offer similar services", whose clients can end an engagement on 30 days' notice, whose best operating margin in fourteen years is 10.3%, whose revenue has grown 1.6% a year for a decade with acquisitions in it, and whose purchase of its own founding business was written down by $195 million in four years.* The file does not get to pick. Each is tested on the filings below.

**Where the money is (10-K 2025 and releases).** One reportable segment; revenue by region only (Americas $160.9M, Europe $65.5M, Asia Pacific $18.3M). No split between adviser fees and recurring revenue is given on the face of the statements; the releases give the recurring share as a percentage and a dollar figure (*"Recurring revenues, highly valued for their predictability, represented 46 percent of our firmwide total in 2025"*, 10-K 2025; *"recurring revenues were $118 million dollars, or 48 percent of firm revenues"* in an earlier release; *"recurring revenue streams, which are targeted to reach 35 percent of firm revenue over the next few years"* in an older one). The *"recurring"* class is the company's definition and includes *"multi-year Public Sector contracts"*, which are adviser time under a longer contract, not subscriptions.

**[E3-03], criterion by criterion.** *"An economic franchise arises from a product or service that: (1) is needed or desired; (2) is thought by its customers to have no close substitute and; (3) is not subject to price regulation."*
- **(1) Needed or desired [x]: passes.** Large buyers of outsourcing, cloud and software pay for an adviser at the table; the business has sold that service since TPI's founding and through every 10-K from fiscal 2007 to 2025.
- **(3) Not subject to price regulation [x]: passes.** No regulator sets an advisory fee. Public-sector work is won by tender, which is price competition rather than price regulation, and is read under (2).
- **(2) Thought by its customers to have no close substitute: FAILS in the filer's own words, in every annual report it has filed as an operating company.**
  - *"The business information services and advisory sector is highly competitive, fragmented and subject to rapid change. We face competition from many other providers ranging from large organizations to small firms and independent contractors that provide specialized services. Our competitors include any firm that provides sourcing or benchmarking advisory services, IT strategy or business process consulting, which may include a variety of consulting firms, service providers, niche advisors and, potentially, advisors currently or formerly employed by us."* (10-K 2025, Item 1A)
  - *"In addition, limited barriers to entry exist in the markets in which we do business."* The phrase *"limited barriers to entry"* is in **all nineteen 10-Ks the operating company has filed, fiscal 2007 to fiscal 2025** (`pricing_hits.txt`; the twentieth 10-K, fiscal 2006, is the blank-check shell's).
  - *"A significant number of independent sourcing and advisory firms offer similar services to us."* (10-K 2025, Item 1, Competition.) The earlier form of the same paragraph claimed more: *"these firms generally lack the benchmarking data, scale and diversity of expertise that we possess"* (10-K for fiscal 2016) and, in 2008, *"that TPI possess as a result of our nineteen years of experience"*. By 2025 the claim reads *"We believe we set ourselves apart with our data repository"*. **The filer names price among the grounds of competition in every year**: *"primarily driven by independence and objectivity, expertise, possession of relevant benchmarking data, breadth of service capabilities, reputation and price."*
  - **The contract form**: *"We principally derive revenues from fees for services generated on a project-by-project basis"*; *"Advisory engagements typically are project-based"*; agreements *"typically allow our clients to terminate early due to breach or for convenience with 30 days’ notice"*. **Every engagement is a fresh purchase**, and the buyer can stop mid-course.
  - **Concentration and the buyer's power**: *"Our 25 largest clients accounted for approximately 30% of revenue in both 2025 and 2024."* The clients are *"primarily large businesses ranked in the Forbes Global 2000"*: the most sophisticated buyers of professional services there are, running competitive procurement for a living, which is the service ISG itself sells them.

- **Returns, read against [E3-46]** (*"the best businesses, by definition, are going to be businesses that earn very high returns on capital employed over time"*): **10-72% pre-tax on tangible operating capital in 2012-2025 outside 2016, and 4-20% with the acquired goodwill and intangibles counted.** The tangible base is $17-65M because the assets are advisers, who are not on the balance sheet. **The owners' capital went into goodwill, and most of it was written off**: $195.2M of impairments in 2008-2011 took shareholders' equity from $190.8M (2007) to $35.9M (2011), on the purchase of TPI itself and the acquisitions after it. A franchise bought at a full price does not usually need writing down by that much within four years of the purchase.
- **The operating margin, where price would show, has not exceeded 10.3% in any year since the business was bought** (TPI's own 2005 margin was 10.5%), and averaged **4.9% over 2016-2025** and **6.8% over 2021-2025**. The two best years, 2021 and 2022, were the post-pandemic advisory boom; 2024 fell to 2.3%.
- **Pricing, read against [E2-44]'s first characteristic** (*"an ability to increase prices rather easily (even when product demand is flat and capacity is not fully utilized) without fear of significant loss of either market share or unit volume"*). **The one sentence in the filings that points toward pricing power is gone**: eleven 10-Ks (fiscal 2007 to fiscal 2017) said earnings *"are also impacted by the timing of the adoption of annual price increases"*; the sentence stops with the 10-K for fiscal 2017 (filed 2018-03-16), and no amount, rate or realised price was ever filed beside it. From the 10-K for fiscal 2022 onward the sentence in its place reads the other way: *"If our costs, in particular personnel-related costs, were to become subject to significant inflationary pressures, we may not be able to fully offset such higher costs through price increases in future periods."* The 10-K for fiscal 2012 recorded the direction on a specific line of business: the contingent consideration on the STA Consulting purchase was cut by $1.9M *"due to increased competition in the public sector and increased pricing pressures"*. **No bill rate, realised rate or utilisation figure is filed in any 10-K** (`pricing_hits.txt`), so the price series cannot be built from the filings; that absence is itself the finding [E4-37]'s inverse metric would read: a firm with pricing power has no reason to hide it.
- **Units [E4-55]: people are this business's physical series.** Revenue per year-end employee fell from about $378K (2008) to $237K (2012), $206K (2019), $179K (2022) and $190K (2025), in nominal dollars. **Not read as a price series**: the company moved delivery offshore on purpose (*"ISG iFlex™ global delivery model"*), and a cheaper adviser in a lower-cost country lowers revenue per head by design. It is recorded because it is the only unit series filed, and because it does not rise.
- **Growth, read against [E2-44]'s second characteristic** (*"an ability to accommodate large dollar volume increases in business ... with only minor additional investment of capital"*): the capital test is met on the balance sheet (little plant) and failed in the economics, because volume is added by hiring or by buying firms. **Revenue grew 1.6% a year from fiscal 2015 ($209.2M) to fiscal 2025 ($244.7M)** with the 2016 Alsbridge purchase in and its 2024 sale out; 2025 revenue sits below 2017's.
- **The low-cost door and tight supply, the two routes [E3-43] says "a business" uses:** the low-cost door is not claimed; the row below shows ISG's margins at the bottom of its peers. Supply of advisers was tight in 2021-2022 (the two best margins) and passed.

**[E4-04], the subject matter is rebuilt each cycle (supporting, not the ground).** The advice sold follows what the buyer is buying: outsourcing in the TPI years, automation (Alsbridge, bought 2016 for $55.2M net and sold 2024 for $27M), and now *"Enterprise AI consulting and research ... now represents about 30 percent of our firmwide revenue, up from 10 percent last year"*. The filer's own risk factor: *"We may not have the ability to develop and offer the new services and products that we need to remain competitive."* The one durable piece is the benchmark data, and its value depends on being current (*"With each engagement we conduct, we enhance both the quantity and quality of the intellectual property"*), which is continuous defence rather than replacement [E5-23, E3-49]. This does not take the perimeter form (the advisory business's durability is judgeable from the filings, and they judge it), so it is supporting evidence only.

**The key-person defect, recorded at Q2 as [E4-23] requires.** Not a founder, as at smaller firms: the filer names four executives (*"The success of our business is dependent upon the continued service of a relatively small group of key executives"*), and, more to the point for the moat, says of its advisers that departing ones become competitors (*"potentially, advisors currently or formerly employed by us"*; *"face competition from these former managers if the restrictive covenants are unenforceable"*), with *"voluntary advisor turnover"* of 11-13% a year. **The relationships and the judgment that clients buy walk out of the door each evening**; *"if a business requires a superstar to produce great results, the business itself cannot be deemed great"* **[E4-23]**. Recorded as a moat defect.

**The attacker's test [E2-45]** (*"how I would like, assuming I had ample capital and skilled personnel, to compete with it"*). The inputs are advisers and data. ISG itself *"recruit[s] advisors from service providers and consulting firms"*; it built its breadth by buying competitors (TPI in 2007; Compass, *"a premier independent global provider of business and information technology benchmarking"*, and STA Consulting in 2011; CTP in 2014; Alsbridge in 2016 (*"network carrier services, robotic process automation and incremental sourcing advisory capabilities"*, 8-K EX-99.1 2016-12-02); Change4Growth, Ventana Research and Martino & Partners since); and it says entry barriers are limited. **With capital and skilled people, I would compete with it without fear.**

- **Peers named: 5 SEC filers measured, of a field the filer itself calls "fragmented" and unbounded** (*"any firm that provides sourcing or benchmarking advisory services, IT strategy or business process consulting"*). Unmeasured and named: Everest Group (private, the closest sourcing-advisory rival by trade reputation, not by any filing read here), the advisory arms of the large accounting firms (Deloitte, KPMG, EY, PwC: private partnerships, no segment filing on any rung), Accenture and the large IT-services firms that the filer lists as *"service providers"* (segment figures do not isolate sourcing advice). **The row is partial and says so**; the verdict rests on the filer's own words and figures, which are complete, and the measured part of the row points the same way.
- **What the row shows.** ISG's margins are the lowest of the six outside Forrester (a research house in decline), about half of Hackett's, which sells the nearest service at nearly the same revenue with the same 1.6% growth. **Gartner is what a franchise in this field looks like in the numbers**: clients pay ahead for a subscription (negative net tangible operating assets every year), margins near 19-21%, revenue compounding. ISG's research arm is the part of its business shaped like that, and it is a minority of revenue inside a consulting firm.
- **The row's limit [E3-61]:** it shows position, not conduct; it cannot show how rivals would respond to an ISG price rise. The filer's own sentences answer that question for it.
- **Untapped pricing power [E3-33]:** none claimed and none visible. Claiming the class is claiming *"a monopoly or a near monopoly"* **[E5-28]**; the filer says entry barriers are limited.
- **Direction [E4-32]: flat, not widening.** Margins 3-6% in 2012-2020, 9-10% in the 2021-2022 boom, 2.3% in 2024, 7.3% in 2025 and 8.6% in H1 2026; revenue in 2025 below 2017's; the competitive-advantage paragraph softened from *"these firms generally lack the benchmarking data, scale and diversity of expertise that we possess"* (fiscal 2016) to *"We believe we set ourselves apart"* (2025).

**THE STRONGEST EVIDENCE AGAINST THIS VERDICT, hunted hardest [E4-26, E3-41, E4-51].**
1. **Recurring revenue is 46% of the total and rising** (research, provider ratings, GovernX, events). Answer: the company's *"recurring"* class includes *"multi-year Public Sector contracts"*, which are adviser time; no filed split shows how much is true subscription, and the part that is (research) competes with Gartner and Forrester, the second of which is shrinking. The consolidated margin, which includes the recurring half, has never passed 10.3%. If the recurring half carried Gartner's margin, the consolidated figure would show it.
2. **The benchmark data is real and not easily copied** (*"nearly 10 million real-world data points"*, *"180,000 unique technology service contracts"*). Answer: the filer itself ranks it as one ground of competition among six, beside price, and its competitors are said to offer *"similar services"*; data that must be refreshed engagement by engagement is only as good as the flow of engagements, which the row shows ISG winning at low margins. The filings show no premium the data commands.
3. **Returns on tangible capital are high (up to 71%).** Answer: [E3-03] ties the return to pricing; these returns come from a tiny tangible base in a people business (Hackett's are higher still and CRA's are 52-66%); with the acquired goodwill counted, 4-20%; and the $195M of impairments say the capital actually spent to own the business earned little.
4. **The AI wave**: AI work 30% of 2025 revenue, H1 2026 revenue up 5% and margin 8.6%. Answer: this is a surfing run in the framework's reading of [E3-51] (*"A surfing run is not a moat"*, THE FRAMEWORK v4, Q2); every adviser in the field is selling AI advice, and a gain in revenue from demand, not price, is [E3-43]'s *"supply ... is tight"* route, which *"usually does not last long"*.
5. **The brand and the client list** (*"75 of the world’s top 100 enterprises"*, *"more than 900 clients"*). Answer: the same buyers use several advisers, the 25 largest are 30% of revenue, and a 30-day termination right means the relationship is re-won continuously; a brand that shows no price premium in fourteen years of margins is a reputation, not a franchise [E2-44].

None of the five turns the pricing evidence. Reading (b) survives its own hardest test.

- **Must the moat be continuously rebuilt? Does success depend on a great manager? [E4-04]** The subject matter is rebuilt each technology cycle and the advisers carry the relationships (above); the data is defended continuously. Recorded as supporting, not as the ground.
- **Class: [ ] WIDE [ ] NARROW [x] NONE [ ] PROVISIONAL · Direction: flat.** The row is partial (five SEC filers of an unbounded field) and would ordinarily hold a class PROVISIONAL; it does not here, because the verdict rests on the filer's own evidence, which is complete across nineteen annual reports, and the measured part of the row agrees with it.
- **VERDICT: [ ] IN  [x] OUT  [ ] UNRESEARCHED  [ ] UNKNOWABLE**: on the business. **[E3-03] criterion (2) fails in the registrant's own filings**: *"highly competitive, fragmented"*, *"limited barriers to entry"* in all nineteen of its operating 10-Ks, *"A significant number of independent sourcing and advisory firms offer similar services to us"*, competitors that include its own former advisers, price named among the grounds of competition, project-based engagements the client can end on 30 days' notice. **[E3-43]'s demonstration is absent in its pricing half**: an operating margin never above 10.3% since the business was bought, averaging 4.9% over ten years and lowest of five SEC-filing peers outside a declining research house; the only filed reference to annual price increases withdrawn after fiscal 2017 and replaced by a warning that costs may not be passed on; no bill rate or realised rate ever filed; revenue up 1.6% a year for a decade. **The high return on tangible capital is the arithmetic of a people business, and the capital the owners actually supplied was mostly written off ($195.2M, 2008-2011).** Key-person defect recorded [E4-23]. **The file closes here.** Q3 to Q6 are not opened as gates; the material gathered beneath the close follows without verdicts.

**THE REVERSAL CONDITION, in words (a Q2 OUT arms no price band; the QLYS ruling of 2026-09-07).** Q2 would be re-opened only on filed evidence of price-setting, not on a lower price: (1) the subscription part of the business (research, GovernX, Inform) filed as its own line with its revenue and margin, and shown to be the majority of revenue with a Gartner-like shape (clients paying ahead, margins well above the consulting line); **and** (2) a consolidated operating margin held above the 2021-2022 peak (about 10%) through a year of flat or falling demand, with a filed rate increase or realised-rate series; **and** (3) the *"limited barriers to entry"* and *"similar services"* sentences no longer true on the filer's own reading. Any one alone would not re-open the file.
