### THE FINDINGS
- **Q2 IN, class NARROW, and the three findings that keep it out of WIDE are recorded rather than waived.** Colgate reports its
  own primary moat metric in the same MD&A sentence every year, and read across twelve 10-Ks it says something the company's
  commentary does not: **global toothpaste share 44.4% (2014), 44.7% (2015), then down every year to 39.4% (2021), then back to
  41.3% (2025)** — still **3.4 points below its 2015 level** — while **advertising went from 9.4% of sales (2016) to 13.3%
  (2025)**. **[E4-32]'s direction test fails across the decade** and is satisfied only across 2022-2025, and the re-widening was
  bought with 3.9 more points of every sales dollar. It is also a **dollar** share by the company's own definition, not a unit
  share, so the pricing below flatters it.
- **The physical series [E4-55] is the sharpest single table in the run.** Filed components summed 2016-2025: **worldwide volume
  +7.7 points, price +38.0, FX -20.4**; the Oral, Personal and Home Care segment, 77% of sales, **+2.9 points of volume in ten
  years**. Dollar sales rose 34%; units did not move. **And the price was mostly devaluation recovery, not [E2-44] pricing
  power**: Latin America priced **+76.8 against FX of -56.2** and Africa/Eurasia **+76.2 against -62.6**, recovering roughly
  80-85% of the currency loss and no more, while the three hard-currency regions priced at about **1% a year** (North America
  +12.9, Europe +11.0, Asia Pacific +17.2 against FX -16.8, so **+0.4 points of dollar price in ten years**).
- **[E4-37]'s inverse metric is firing, in the home market, on the filed record.** North American net selling price was
  **-1.9% in 2024 and -0.2% in 2025** — two consecutive years of price given back — with volume **-9.3 points over 2021-2025**
  and advertising at 13.5% and 13.3% of sales. *"it's not a great business when you have to have a prayer session before you
  raise your prices a penny."*
- **The counter-evidence is in the file at full strength and it is strong:** in 2022 and 2023 Colgate put through **+9.5% and
  +10.0%** of worldwide price and its **global toothpaste share ROSE, 39.4% to 41.1%**. That is [E2-44] criterion (1) passing in
  the hardest conditions the decade offered, and it is why the verdict is IN rather than OUT.
- **The competitor row, ten peers, is the reason the verdict holds.** PG, Unilever, Haleon, Kenvue, Church & Dwight,
  Kimberly-Clark, Clorox, General Mills, J.M. Smucker and Freshpet, on the same metrics and the identical net-tangible-operating-

  tonnage negative in four of six — with Unilever the only peer positive in each filed year. **Every branded staple in this row

### NEW FOR THE OPERATOR
- **The first name in the register whose Q2 verdict is IN with a segment held PROVISIONAL for want of a filing that will never
  exist.** Purina sits inside Nestlé's consolidated accounts and Mars is private. The run held Pet Nutrition PROVISIONAL rather
  than marking the verdict UNRESEARCHED, on the ground that **UNRESEARCHED requires a namable document** and there is none. If
  the operator wants that class researched, the only routes are Nestlé's own annual report (a primary filing, but not an SEC
  one, and outside the current source rules) or nothing.
- **A new survival shape is proposed, pending the operator: #19 THE SHELF** — *the brand is owned but the route to the buyer is
  rented from a counterparty who stocks a cheaper copy beside it, holds the buyer's data, and reprices the terms every year.*
  It is not **#11 THE PASS-THROUGH** (the gains are taken by **one concentrated counterparty at the point of sale**, not
  competed away) and it is the inverse of **#13 THE TENANT** (here the **product** is owned and only the **distribution** is
  rented). Quantified from North America, where the mechanism has already run: company-wide for a decade it **halves owner
  earnings to about $1,450M**. Likelihood **a real possibility for the developed-market third, a low-level possibility for the
  whole**, because two thirds of profit is earned where no customer has 11% of anything.
- **The [E5-11] strength-2 failure is now a pattern in this queue, not an outlier.** LOW, ITW, SHW and now CL: large, highly
  profitable American businesses run on days of cash with a committed revolver behind commercial paper. Four instances is
  enough that the operator may want a standing note on whether the corpus's *"massive liquid assets"* is a realistic test for
  investment-grade industrials, or whether the framework should record it as a known systematic fail. **This run did not decide
  that; it scored the fail as written.**
- **Colgate's pay plan and the framework measure different things, and the proxy says so plainly.** Bonus is 40% Base Business
  EPS, 40% organic sales growth, 20% strategic initiatives; long-term awards run on relative growth and free-cash-flow
  productivity with a TSR modifier. **Nothing vests on return on capital**, which is the number **[E2-01]** and **[E3-46]**
  make the test. CEO 2025 total $16,478,196 against a median employee's $59,655, **276x**.
- **The buyback is the live [E5-08] question and it was closed at Q5, not at Q3.** Purchases ran at **$78.54** (Q4 2025),
  **$90.28** (Q1 2026, February at **$95.90**) and **$87.40** (Q2 2026) against a value range of roughly **$50-$75**. Condition
  (1) passes; condition (2) does not, on this run's own range, with **[E4-13]**'s humility clause attached.
