*Every Caterpillar figure below is read from its filed annual reports 2000-2025 (the Exhibit 13 appendix for 2001-2014, the 10-K itself from 2015: the MD&A sales and operating-profit bridges, the segment discussions, the Supplemental Consolidating Data, Items 1 and 1A), from the 10-Q to 2026-06-30 and the release of 2026-08-04. **The question this file has to answer: is Caterpillar the best-placed maker in a price-competitive, cyclical capital-goods trade, whose customers have close substitutes and whose margin collapses when the cycle turns, or does it hold something its customers cannot buy elsewhere, visible in how it prices through the cycle?** **The two hypotheses, stated so they can be attacked [E4-26].** *(a) A narrow franchise of position: the largest installed base and dealer system in heavy equipment, serviced with its own parts (37% of MP&E sales), which has taken price in nineteen of twenty-three filed years including the 2009 collapse and has out-earned every rival whose segment is filed, in every year the row reaches.* *(b) A cyclical manufacturer in a trade the filer itself describes as competing on price with discounting, twelve named global construction rivals including state-backed Chinese makers, excess capacity in the downturns, price given back in 2001, 2016, 2020 and 2025, a manufacturing margin that fell to 1.5% (2009) and 2.1% (2016), and pricing that over twenty-two years compounded at about the rate of general inflation.* Each is tested on the filings below.

**[E3-03], criterion by criterion, the unit being the company [E4-08].** *"An economic franchise arises from a product or service that: (1) is needed or desired; (2) is thought by its customers to have no close substitute and; (3) is not subject to price regulation."*
- **(1) Needed or desired [x]: passes.**
- **(3) Not subject to price regulation [x]: passes.** Prices are set with dealers; emissions and trade rules bear on cost, not on price.
- **(2) Thought by its customers to have no close substitute: NOT MET AS A MONOPOLY, MET AS A PREFERENCE THAT CARRIES A PRICE.** The filer names twelve global construction rivals and says its customers buy on *"product performance, customer service, quality and price"*: a contractor can buy a Komatsu, a Deere, a Volvo or a Sany excavator, and the filer discounts through merchandising programs and subsidised finance to keep the sale. What the filings show the customer paying for is not the absence of a substitute but a preference: price taken in nineteen of twenty-three years, including 2009, and a margin above every filed rival's in every year the row below reaches, earned while the rivals were offering the substitute. **This is [E2-58]'s exception door, not criterion (2)'s front door.**

**[E3-43]'s demonstration, *"a company’s ability to regularly price its product or service aggressively and thereby to earn high rates of return on capital"*, and its other doors.**
- **The pricing half: REGULAR, NOT AGGRESSIVE.** Nineteen positive years of twenty-three, compounding at about the rate of general inflation; given back in 2016 on *"excess industry capacity"*, in 2020 in China, and in 2025 in construction and mining while volume rose. That is a level held in real terms through most of the cycle, the NDSN and ATR form (*"pricing by level, not by increase"*), with a record of giving ground when the industry is over-supplied.
- **The return half: MET AT MID-CYCLE AND ABOVE, NOT AT THE BOTTOMS.** Pre-tax on manufacturing assets 11-22% in most years and 28% in 2023-2024; 1.5% in 2009, 2.0% in 2016, 6.1-7.9% in 2014-2015.
- **The low-cost door [E2-58]: the door this file goes through.** *"persistent over-capacity without administered prices (or costs) equals poor profitability"*, long-term profitability set by *"the ratio of supply-tight to supply-ample years"*, and the one exception *"a cost advantage that is both **wide and sustainable** … By definition such exceptions are few."* The filer's own word for 2016 is the equation's own word (*"excess industry capacity"*), and in that year Caterpillar's construction segment still earned 10.6% against Deere's 3.7%. **Wide:** 0.9 to 11.0 points above Deere's construction and forestry margin in each of fifteen years (row below), 5.0 points above Komatsu's whole-company margin on average over FY2015-FY2025, 14 to 19 points above CNH's construction segment (2021-2025). **Sustainable:** held in every year from 2011 to 2025, in both troughs the row reaches, and in the 2025 price war (Caterpillar's construction margin 18.7% after giving 4.5% of price; Deere's 9.0% after giving 2%). The advantage's filed sources are the installed base serviced through the dealer system (*"MP&E Services Revenues ... totaled $24 billion in 2025"*) and the integration of its own components (*"We believe our ability to control the integration and design of key machine components and innovative technologies represents a competitive advantage"*). **What the filings do not split:** whether the margin gap is lower cost at a common price or a higher price for a preferred machine; no filer publishes its transaction prices. [E2-58] names a *cost* advantage; read strictly, this file shows a margin advantage of unfiled composition, and either half of it is an advantage over the rivals (the price-premium half would be evidence toward criterion (2), not against it). Stated as the limit of the door, not resolved by preference.
- **The tight-supply door: not the source.** The 2022-2023 price came in a supply-tight inflation and lifted every maker; the margin gap over Deere existed before it (2016-2020: 6.5-11.0 points) and after it.

**[E2-44], the two characteristics.**
- **(1) *"an ability to increase prices rather easily (even when product demand is flat and capacity is not fully utilized) without fear of significant loss of either market share or unit volume"*: SHOWN IN SOME DOWNTURNS, FAILED IN OTHERS.** Shown in 2009 (+$910M, 1.9%, with sales down 38.5%), 2019 (+$710M with volume −$1,017M) and 2024 (+$1,238M with volume −$3,543M); failed in 2016 (−$760M on excess capacity), 2020 (−$435M) and 2025 (−$817M, with volume up). Not *"rather easily"*: the 2015 10-K speaks of *"pricing pressure from the competitive nature of the business"*, which is [E4-37]'s agony end.
- **(2) *"an ability to accommodate large dollar volume increases in business ... with only minor additional investment of capital"*: PARTLY.** Manufacturing capital expenditures ran at 0.54 to 0.90 times depreciation in 2014-2021 while sales rose from $35.8bn (2016) to $48.2bn (2021); they have risen to 2.1 times in 2025 ($2,794M against $1,328M of depreciation excluding acquired amortization) for the Power & Energy capacity build (Q4). Carried to Q4's great, good or gruesome.

### THE STRONGEST EVIDENCE AGAINST THIS VERDICT, stated as its holders would state it [E4-51, E4-26, E3-47]
The verdict below is IN, NARROW, so the case for OUT is set out first, at full strength:
1. **The filer says its customers buy on price and that it discounts.** *"In all markets, we compete on the basis of product performance, customer service, quality and price. From time to time, the intensity of competition results in price discounting in a particular industry or region."* It names twelve global construction rivals, keeps a $2.5bn reserve for post-sale discounts to dealers, and funds below-market financing to move machines. [E3-03]'s second criterion is about what the customer thinks, and the filer has answered it. The AGCO file (2026-09-21) and the OSK file (2026-09-26) closed OUT on sentences of this kind.
2. **In its own words, 2016 was [E2-58]'s equation at work**: *"competitive market conditions resulting from excess industry capacity"*, and the manufacturing margin went to 2.1% and the return on manufacturing assets to 2.0%. In 2009 the margin was 1.5%. A franchise can tolerate a bad year; this business has two near-zero years and two more at 5-8% in seventeen.
3. **The price record is inflation, not pricing power.** About 2.5% a year compounded over twenty-two years, with the two big years (2022-2023) the general inflation passed through; and in 2025 Caterpillar gave back 4.5% of construction price in a year its construction volume rose, which is [E2-44]'s first characteristic failed in the segment that carries the dealer-network claim.
4. **The mining third is a commodity business and the company paid for more of it at the top** (Bucyrus, 2011), which turned to losses within four years.

**Answered.** (1) is accepted as the filer's sentence and weighed against the filer's numbers, which is the ATR ruling's method: the sentence describes the new-machine sale in over-supplied years, and the filings show when (2015-2016, 2020, 2025); against it, price was taken in nineteen of twenty-three years including the worst collapse in the record, and the margin exceeded every filed rival's in every year of the row. **AGCO is the contrast, not the precedent**: AGCO was third of three at the peak and at the trough of one cycle and posted an operating loss in 2024 while its rivals earned 10-19%; Caterpillar is first in every year and every trough the row reaches. OSK failed the demonstration on the whole company; Caterpillar's demonstration is [E2-58]'s wide-and-sustainable exception, measured. (2) is accepted and is why the class is NARROW: the advantage is relative, and in the industry's worst years it has meant the best of poor margins, not a good one; it is carried to Q4 as the named way this business dies. (3) is accepted as to the level: the pricing is the level held in real terms, not the increase taken, the NDSN form; and the 2025 give-back was partly reversed in Q2 2026 (+$309M in construction). (4) is accepted and carried to Q3 ([E2-56], [E3-40]) and Q4.

**For the operator (not decided here; PRIME RULE 5):** the ABT and ATR files asked whether [E3-03] is applied at the company level or segment by segment. Here the three segments differ in degree, not in kind (construction and engines first in their rows; mining the weakest and cyclical), so the company-level reading and a segment reading give the same NARROW class for two-thirds of the profit; a strict segment reading would mark Resource Industries OUT on its 2015-2016 losses. And **this file passes Q2 through [E2-58]'s exception on a measured relative margin while criterion (2) is not met as the customer's belief**; the AGCO file read [E2-58] the same way and found the subject outside it. If the operator reads [E3-03] criterion (2) as a gate that [E2-58] cannot open for a non-commodity product, the nearest alternative is **OUT on the filer's price sentence**, as for AGCO and OSK. Recorded so the ruling, when made, can re-read this verdict without a re-run.

**The analyst's own incentive, stated [operator rule 9, E4-27, E3-41].** Caterpillar is a famous name and the pull toward IN by reputation is real; against that, the last two machinery files (AGCO, OSK) closed OUT on sentences like this filer's, a pull toward OUT by analogy. The verdict rests on the filer's price bridges for twenty-three years, the manufacturing margin and asset return for twenty-one, the segment margins for fifteen, and a row of seven filers including the two largest rivals; the case for OUT was written first, and the class is NARROW.
