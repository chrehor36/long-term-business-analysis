### THE SKIP REASON, TESTED
The triage called SONY a *"foreign 20-F filer ... [with] too few annual periods because their XBRL history is short."* **Half right.**
Companyfacts carries IFRS annual facts back to FY3/21, so the history is not short; **what it lacked on 2026-09-13 was the FY3/26 20-F
itself** (latest 20-F accession in the facts: `0001193125-25-143137`), and **what it could never supply is the perimeter**: the
consolidated operating cash flow before the spin-off runs the insurer's and bank's policyholder and deposit flows through operations
(FY3/23 consolidated OCF ¥314.7bn against ¥415.5bn for the industrial business alone; FY3/25 ¥2,321.7bn). **A mechanical owner-earnings
line on the XBRL would have been an insurer-contaminated number.** The skip reason was a prompt to read the filing, which is what it
turned out to need.

### THE PERIMETER - HOW A DISCONTINUED OPERATION WAS HANDLED (the CNR rule, a new form)
- **What the filing says:** partial spin-off of Sony Financial Group decided 2025-05-14, executed **2025-10-01** by dividend in kind
  (¥955,700M), **16.40% retained** and equity-accounted; discontinued operations, **comparatives re-presented three years deep**.
- **Where a longer window came from:** Sony published an unaudited *"Condensed Statements of Cash Flows"* separating *"Sony without
  Financial Services"* in Item 5 of every 20-F. **The two bases differ by one intra-group flow** - the FS dividend to the parent, counted
  in the separated schedule and eliminated in continuing operations - so FY3/21-FY3/23 were rebuilt by removing it, and **the splice was
  measured in both overlap years before use** (residual ¥24.1bn, 2.2%, in FY3/24; ¥1.1bn in FY3/25). **Method note for the next
  spin-off filer:** look for the company's own separated schedule before refusing a window; measure the splice; state the residual.

### THE CONTENT TRAP - a (c) defect the D&A default would have made silently
Sony classifies film, TV, catalog and game-content additions **inside operating cash flow** (Note 27(1)), **and** its cash-flow
*"Depreciation and amortization"* **includes content amortization** (Pictures D&A ¥517.8bn in FY3/26). **The [E3-44] default applied to
reported D&A would have charged content twice** - once as cash inside OCF, again as (c) - understating owner earnings by ~¥400-600bn a
year. The run used D&A less content amortization at the D&A end. **This is the RESUME STATE's ELF finding ("the D&A end of (c)
triple-counts on filers that capitalise displays or cloud costs") in a new and much larger class: every studio, label and publisher
that capitalises content into operating assets.** A tooling screen cannot see it; the reader must check where content cash sits.

### HOW A CONGLOMERATE'S Q2 WAS DECIDED - the third precedent, and where it sits
GHC (no leg passes -> OUT), HAS (profit overwhelmingly in the franchise leg and the rest eating no capital -> IN), BRK (a group-level
structural moat carries substitutable legs -> IN). **SONY is the case between them:** two NARROW legs (Music; G&NS, generation-contingent)
at **54.6% of five-year segment profit**, three legs that fail [E3-03] or [E4-04] at ~45% **and the largest capital programme** (I&SS
¥265.3bn of ¥804.9bn FY3/26 capex; Pictures content additions ¥100bn a year above amortization), no filed group-level moat, and the
whole's ROE down five years running. **Decided OUT on the conjunction, with no numeric mix threshold invented** - none exists in the
corpus, and the verdict names the two filed facts it rests on.
