
# HESM (Hess Midstream LP) - Q2 OUT on the business. Wave 7, name 6, register entry 138. 2026-09-20

Run file: `Test Runs/2026-09-20 Run - HESM Hess Midstream.md`. Research:
`Test Runs/_research 2026-09-20 HESM/`. Second midstream name of the day, run immediately after
PAGP and deliberately briefed against it, because HESM sat as a row inside PAGP's own competitor
table and scored first of eleven there.

## The verdict in one paragraph

**Q1 IN, Q2 OUT, on the business, permanently.** [E3-03]'s three criteria are all met on their
face - and clause (3), the one PAGP failed on, passes here, because HESM's gathering assets are
NGA-exempt and its crude lines sit outside the ICA. But each of the three is held up by **one
contract with the counterparty that owns the general partner**. That counterparty supplies 97% of
revenue, owes the Class A holders no fiduciary duty, and is free to compete. The fee under the
contract was written *"in order to target a return on capital deployed"*, and since 2023 it is
frozen at a 2021-23 average escalated by a CPI index capped at 3% and floored at last year's
number. That is administered pricing, and **[E2-59]** is the ruling row: it can floor a commodity
business's profits, but **the moat belongs to the regime**, and *"That day is gone"* is how it
ends. Here the regime is Chevron.

## The finding that matters most: a table nobody had turned into a series

Every HESM 10-K prints a three-year table of Chevron's minimum volume commitments, which are set
at **80% of Chevron's own nominations** in each development plan, three years forward. One table
is a fact. **Four vintages side by side is a series, and the series is the finding.**

| 10-K vintage | year 1 | year 2 | year 3 | gas gathering, third year |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| FY2021, nominated by Hess | 2022: 363 | 2023: 317 | 2024: **351** | **up 11%** |
| FY2023, nominated by Hess | 2024: 365 | 2025: 380 | 2026: **412** | **up 8%** |
| FY2024, nominated by Hess | 2025: 382 | 2026: 418 | 2027: **418** | **flat** |
| **FY2025, nominated by CHEVRON** | 2026: **419** | 2027: **422** | 2028: **346** | **DOWN 18%** |

Crude gathering 2028 is down 21% against 2027, terminaling down 20%, processing down 17%, water
down 6%. **No footnote explains the step down**, and the filer is capable of footnoting one: the
FY2024 10-K attached a note to its own soft year for planned Tioga turnaround maintenance. Grossed
back up by the 80%, Chevron's 2028 development plan nominates about **433 MMcf/d against about 528
for 2027**.

**And the actuals have already turned, in the same direction, in the furnished release.** The Q2
2026 EX-99.1: *"Throughput volumes decreased 15% for oil terminaling and 12% for water gathering
compared with the second quarter of 2025, primarily due to lower production as a result of **lower
new-well activity**"*, with revenue down $15.2M *"primarily due to lower throughput volumes,
partially offset by higher tariff rates."* **[E4-55]** in its textbook shape: dollar revenue held
up by pricing while the physical series turns down, and *"the physical series is the honest one."*

This is worth carrying forward as a method note, not only as a HESM fact. **Any filer that
publishes a rolling N-year forward commitment table publishes a panel, and the panel is invisible
in any single filing.** The screen reads the newest filing. The vintage comparison costs three
extra fetches and it produced the single most decisive number in this run. Names to try it on:
every gathering-and-processing partnership with sponsor MVCs, and any take-or-pay or
capacity-reservation disclosure.

## The prior the brief asked to be tested hardest, and what happened to it

PAGP's competitor table scored **HESM 25.5%, first of eleven**, on operating income over net PP&E
plus equity-method investments plus net intangibles, FY2021-25 means. The brief was explicit that
this number was computed by another run for another subject and was UNVERIFIED as an input here,
and that **[E4-26]** applies with force to a number that flatters the subject.

**The number reproduces.** Rebuilt independently from HESM's own filings: **25.56%** (operating
income mean $852.5M, capital base mean $3,335.3M). Ten of PAGP's other ten cells reproduced within
1.2 points. **The arithmetic is sound and PAGP's row stands.**

**The inference does not survive.** Two things kill it:

1. **The return is the contract's target, not a market outcome.** Item 1 says the fee mechanism
   existed *"in order to target a return on capital deployed over the Initial Term."* A number a
   related-party contract was written to produce is not evidence of relative position won against
   rivals. It is evidence of what the affiliate agreed to pay.
2. **The one peer with the same structure earns half.** This run added **Antero Midstream (AM)** to
   PAGP's list, because it is the closest structural analogue anywhere in listed US midstream: a
   single-sponsor Appalachian gathering-and-water business with sponsor-dedicated acreage and an
   Up-C-adjacent history. On the same metric over the same window AM earns **12.4%** against HESM's
   25.6%, and the peer median of twelve is 12.3%. **The pipes are not twice as good. The contract
   is.**

That is the whole Q2 case in miniature: **a high return on deployed capital is a fact about
arithmetic, and a moat is a relative claim [E3-28] that has to survive the question of WHY the
number is high.**

## [E4-04], and why this is an OUT rather than the perimeter close

The 2026-09-20 amendment reserves **UNKNOWABLE at Q2** for a name that passes [E3-03] and whose
durability *"cannot be judged from filings"*, and reserves **OUT** for a name whose advantage the
filings show must be rebuilt. **HESM's durability can be judged from its filings, because the
registrant judges it in Item 1A and writes the answer down:**

> *"Because of the natural decline in production from existing wells in our areas of operation,
> our success depends, in part, on Chevron and other producers replacing declining production …
> volumes … **will naturally decline over time.** As a result, our cash flows associated with these
> wells will also decline over time … **We have no control over** the level of drilling activity in
> our areas of operation, the amount of reserves associated with wells connected to our systems or
> the rate at which production from a well declines."*

The framework's [E4-04] scope paragraph names **depleting assets** in the excluded class, and
**[E3-51]** supplies the exact form: a surfing run is not a moat, and *"the advantage lives in the
wave, not the surfer."* HESM has surfed a very good wave for nine filed years. It does not own the
wave, and the wave's owner has just nominated a smaller one.

**[E4-36]** closes it: of the four causes of extreme success, HESM's record comes from wave-riding
and an administered price, and *"only some of them are ownable."*

## Tooling and screen defects found

**1. `deal_note` misdirected for the second consecutive midstream name, and in a new way.** It read
*"1 8-K Item 1.01 filing(s) since 2026-02-25, none carrying a merger agreement (EX-2.1) - most
likely a credit facility or offering; open them only if something else is odd."* The whole 8-K
index since `newest_periodic` was pulled from `submissions.json` and read by hand, as the brief
required. **Since 2026-06-30 there is exactly one 8-K, the Q2 earnings release, and nothing
material sits after the newest periodic filing** - so on the narrow question the note was harmless.
But its **guess** was wrong, and wrong about the most structurally revealing filing of the year:
the Item 1.01 of 2026-03-04 is a **Unit Repurchase Agreement with Hess Investments North Dakota
LLC, a wholly owned Chevron subsidiary**, buying 455,811 Class B units for ~$18M at $39.49 (the
Class A close of 2026-03-02), alongside a **$42M accelerated share repurchase with JPMorgan**, both
approved by the Conflicts Committee, both funded *"with borrowings under its existing revolving
credit facility."* **The pattern across PAGP and HESM is the same: the note's confident guess about
what an unread Item 1.01 probably is points the reader away from the filing.** PAGP's version
missed a $3.9bn divestiture filed as EX-2.2 to EX-2.4 and $1.5bn of junior subordinated notes;
HESM's version labelled a related-party buyback from the controlling owner "most likely a credit
facility." **Recommendation, stated and not acted on:** the note should print the 8-K's item codes
and date and stop, with no guess at all. A flag is a prompt to read, never a score.

**2. The consolidated-over-Class-A yield error recurred, at 1.61x.** `growth_required -0.0281` and
`yield_bottom 0.1281` are both artefacts of dividing a **whole-group** owner-earnings band by a
**Class-A-only** market capitalisation. HESM's Class A owns **62.25%** of Hess Midstream Operations
LP, and three filed statements agree on it: Item 1's *"62.3% controlling interest"*, the share
counts (128,350,881 / 206,178,366), and the pre-tax income split (127.5 of 204.8 in Q2 2026).
Corrected to one tier the yield is **7.85% to 8.76%**, not 12.81%. **PAGP's version of this error
was ~4x; HESM's is 1.61x, because HESM's public float owns much more of its own opco.** The general
rule now has two worked instances and should be written as one: **in any Up-C, the screen's
owner-earnings band is consolidated and the screen's cap is the public class. Never divide one by
the other.** Both ends of the HESM band were reproduced to the dollar from the consolidated
statements, which is how the tier was proved rather than assumed: `oe_bottom 650` is the 5-year
operating-cash mean less SBC less 5-year total capex ($650.48M), `oe_top 725` is the 3-year mean
less SBC less 3-year depreciation ($725.24M).

**3. The `PropertyPlantAndEquipmentNet` tag trap is NOT industry-wide.** PAGP's fold found PAA
abandoning the tag after FY2020 for the finance-lease-inclusive element, silently dropping the
filer from any screen keyed to the first tag. **HESM tags it continuously FY2018 to FY2025.** The
defect is filer-specific. The competitor row built here carried a dual-tag fallback, which is why
the PAA cell resolved at all, and that fallback is the right general fix.

**4. The two level flags disagreed and both were describing the same thing.** `level_note` read
"no step" at 1.56 on operating cash while `level_note_oe` read "STEP UP - normalize down [E4-41]"
at 1.84 on owner earnings; they differ only because owner earnings subtracts a lumpy capex line
from a smooth cash line. The underlying nine-year rise from $336.5M to $983.8M of operating cash is
a **contracted build-out ramp** plus fee redeterminations that ran to 2023, and **[E4-41]**
normalises down for *favourable exogenous breaks*, which a completed capital programme is not.
**So the flag fires for the wrong reason - and points the right way for a reason neither flag could
see.** Capex is guided at **$105M for 2026** against $247.5M actually spent in 2025 and $288.5M in
2024, the compression expansion is finished (Q2 2026 capex $30.6M, *"a 56% decrease"*), and the
finished capacity is nominated 18% emptier in 2028. **The recent level is a peak, not a base, and
the reason is forward, not historical.**

**5. `working_capital_flag()` had nothing to net here**, and the hand check confirms it: FY2025
detail lines are receivables -3.1 and -8.0, other assets -0.4, payables -31.0 and -9.4, accrued
+6.4, other liabilities -4.3, a net drag of $49.8M against $983.8M of operating cash. No PAGP-style
matched counter-line exists. Recorded so the next midstream run does not re-derive it.

**6. A claim in this run's own brief was refuted.** The brief stated that *"the filer segregates
expansion capital expenditures from maintenance in its own capital program disclosure."* **It does
not.** Neither the FY2025 10-K nor either furnished release splits them; the 10-K gives one line,
*"Total capital expenditures $247.5"*, reconciled to the cash figure, and the words *"maintenance
capital"* and *"expansion capital"* appear nowhere in the Q2 2026 release. Had Q4 opened, (c) would
have had to be a **disclosed guess** under [E2-23] and [E3-44], with no filer split to lean on.
Worth recording because briefs carry priors and this one was checkable in thirty seconds.

## What was seen beneath the close and not scored

The standing [E4-29] instruction from the CGNX run was performed **before** the gate closed, and
the findings are recorded unscored, as PAGP did. **Adjusted EBITDA appears 21 times and Adjusted
Free Cash Flow 16 times in the Q2 2026 EX-99.1, and Adjusted EBITDA 27 times in the FY2025 one**,
both leading the highlights bullets. Adjusted Free Cash Flow is defined to deduct capital
expenditure but **not** depreciation, which is **[E5-41]**'s reverse float in its purest form: the
money is already spent and the charge is the one deleted. Nine lines of numeric full-year guidance
were issued 2025-12-09 and reaffirmed twice, with a multi-year *"approximately $1 billion of
Adjusted Free Cash Flow after Distributions through 2028"* target. **$1,015.7M was returned to
holders in 2025** against $983.8M of operating cash and $255.6M of capex, with long-term debt up
$290.1M over the same year - **[E2-60]**'s restricted-earnings shape. And cash at 2025-12-31 was
**$1.9 million** against $3,800.5M of debt, with liquidity consisting of the revolver and nothing
else - the **[E5-39]** *"kindness of strangers"* test would have been the first thing scored at Q4.

**None of that contributed to the verdict**, and the run says so explicitly, because Q3 and Q4 do
not open behind a Q2 OUT.

## The uncomfortable part, written down rather than hedged

**This is a good business and the run should not be read as saying otherwise.** A 25.6% five-year
return on deployed capital, first of twelve; nine straight years of rising operating cash; volumes
and rates both up 2023 to 2025, which is the inverse of PAGP and a genuine pass on the historical
[E4-55] test; fee-based contracts with MVCs running to 2033; essentially no commodity exposure; and
a completed capital programme whose maintenance level looks to be under half of depreciation. Had
Q4 been reached, **[E4-20]** would probably have called it *good* and possibly better.

**The file closed at Q2 for one structural reason.** Everything good in the paragraph above is a
term of a contract with a counterparty that owns the general partner, applied to volumes from a
depleting resource the registrant says it cannot control, and the counterparty has just filed a
development plan that nominates a fifth less gas for 2028. **Whose moat is it [E2-59]?** The
filings answer: Chevron's.

**The honest counterweight, because [E4-26] runs both ways.** Third-party revenue is real and
growing - $8.0M of $1,348.6M in 2023, $33.5M of $789.1M in H1 2026. If that crossed over into a
majority under contracts negotiated at arm's length with parties that do not own the general
partner, HESM would be a different company and would need a new run rather than a revision of this
one. The reversal condition is recorded in words in the run file and in the register entry: a
majority of revenue from arm's-length third parties, **or** a general partner that owes the Class A
holders fiduciary duties, **and** a development-plan vintage in which the third year stops falling.
**No price band is armed and no `PORTFOLIO.md` row is added**, because the name failed on the
business and a band would be the QLYS category error.
