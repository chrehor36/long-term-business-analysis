### WHAT THE ROW SHOWS, AND IT IS NOT WHAT THE BANKED RUN ASSUMED
**The moat is real and wide on returns.** TJX earns **53.9% ROE against 34.7% and 33.8%**
— a 19-point gap over both competitors, sustained, and the strongest single piece of
evidence for the franchise in this file. Net margin 9.1% and *rising* every year, versus
ROST *falling* (9.9% → 9.4%) and BURL still only 5.3%.

**But the direction claim does not survive.** The banked run classed TJX **WIDENING**. On
the row, **TJX grew +7.1% in FY2026 while ROST grew +7.7% and BURL +8.8%.** TJX is
growing **slower than both competitors**, and its largest division, Marmaxx at 60% of
sales, comped **+1%** in Q2 FY27.

**This answers the question the last run could not.** Marmaxx's +1% is **not** an
off-price sector problem — the sector is growing 8-9%. It is a TJX-specific issue.

Mitigating and recorded: ROST and BURL grow off far smaller bases ($22.8B and $11.6B vs
$60.4B), so slower percentage growth at TJX is partly arithmetic. And TJX's returns are
in a different league. **The class survives; the direction does not.**

## WHY THE OTHER SEVEN ARE NOT DONE, STATED HONESTLY
The competitor row is expensive in exactly the way the corpus implies it should be. TJX's
row alone required pulling and reconciling three companies' filed figures, and the XBRL
shortcut **failed** on the first attempt: tag drift meant TJX's revenue series appeared to
stop in 2018 and Burlington returned nothing at all. Each competitor row needs the same
per-filer verification that Ruling 11 demands of the subject company.

**That is the cost of the rule, and it was foreseeable.** The alternative — asserting moat
class without a comparison — is what the framework did for 121 runs, and it is why nobody
could say whether Marmaxx's +1% was a TJX problem or a sector problem until today.
