## THE FOUNDATIONS (not a gate)

## Q1: CAN I UNDERSTAND IT? Where its economics will be in ten years, inside my perimeter. STOP.
- **What the business is, on its own filing.** "A group of specialist businesses" distributing "typically low-cost components funded from operating expenditure, where the value-add far exceeds the cost of the product" (AR2025, business model, p. 15), seventeen businesses run in a decentralised model, in three Sectors: Controls (FY2025 revenue £836.4m: Windy City Wire, US low-voltage wire and cable, 36% of Sector revenue; Peerless, US aerospace fasteners, 23%; IS Group, interconnect, 17%; Clarendon, specialty fasteners, 13%; Shoal, T.I.E., Techsil), Seals (£456.0m: hydraulic and industrial seals and fluid-power parts, mostly aftermarket) and Life Sciences (£232.1m: in-vitro diagnostics and surgical consumables and service). It manufactures little; it holds inventory, kits, certifies and delivers fast.
- **The test as the framework states it, applied.** Understanding means "a reasonable fix on about what the earning power and competitive position will look like in five or 10 years" **[M2012-065]**; the chemistry of the product need not be understood if "the economic dynamics of the industry" are **[M2011-014]**. The key variables **[M1998-044]**: (a) the gross margin a value-add distributor keeps on a low-cost critical part; (b) organic volume in its end markets (aerospace build rates, US non-residential and datacentre wiring, industrial aftermarket, hospital diagnostics); (c) what each pound spent on acquisitions earns. (a) is readable: gross margin was 35.6% to 37.0% on the old cost-of-sales basis from FY2016 to FY2022 and 44.6% to 46.7% on the cost-of-inventories basis from FY2022 to FY2025 (AR2016 to AR2025 income statements), through the 2021 to 2023 inflation. (b) is a set of end markets an outsider can follow; none is a fast-changing technology; the risk register names electrification, digitalisation and AI as "monitored but have not yet become material" (AR2025 p. 44). (c) is knowable from the filings: the price of each deal, the ROATCE the filer reports, and the cash that comes out after the deals.
- **Is it important and knowable** **[M2006-076]**, and would insiders write the forecast down **[M2000-105]**? A distributor of fasteners, cable and seals would write down its ten-year margin and volume range; this is not the tech field. Can I name the winner, not only the industry **[M2012-067]**? Within each niche, yes: the filer's units are named leaders in their niches, and Q2 tests whether that lasts.
- **The holding company by its parts (Q1's paragraph, with section B's threshold).** Controls carries more than half of five-year Sector profit (Q2 parts table: 55.6% adjusted, 55.1% statutory), so it is the part that "matters". Its businesses (cable distribution, aerospace and specialty fasteners, interconnect) are each understood by the same three variables. The stated policy to keep buying businesses is weighed at Q6, not here.
- **The doubt rule** **[M2002-092]**: the doubt I hold is about the price of the bought growth and about how much of Controls' margin is a passing aerospace shortage. Both are doubts about value and durability, which Q2 and Q7 own; they are not doubts about whether the economics can be understood.
- **VERDICT: IN.** A value-add distributor of low-cost, critical parts into end markets that change slowly; the key variables are named and readable in ten years of filed statements **[M1995-051]**, **[M2012-065]**, **[M1998-044]**.
