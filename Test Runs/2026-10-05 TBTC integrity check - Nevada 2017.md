# TBTC integrity check: the Nevada licensing matter of 2016 to 2017
## Table Trac, Inc. (OTC: TBTC, SEC CIK 0001090396). One question, for Q5 of `Framework/THE FRAMEWORK v5.md`

Written 2026-10-05. Primary records only for findings; press is quoted as a pointer and marked **[PRESS]**. Every text used is saved in `Test Runs/_research 2026-10-05 TBTC/integrity/` (file names given in brackets). No holding file and no portfolio file was opened.

**Conclusion: NOT ESTABLISHED.** The regulators' published records show a Board recommendation of denial and a Commission rejection of one individual, Brian Hinchley, with no written grounds, no finding against the company, and no later action against the company. The claim that the company gave the regulators altered emails exists only in press reports of the hearing; the primary record that would confirm or refute it (the hearing transcript) is not online and was not obtained. The STOP is therefore neither engaged nor cleared by the record; see section 6 for what that means under Q5's rule that doubt, not proof, is enough.

## 3. The five questions answered

**(1) What the Board and Commission found, and on what grounds.** Found: the Board recommended denial of Hinchley's individual application (November 2017, after two referrals back to staff, August 2016 and April 2017); the Commission rejected it 3-1 on 2017-11-16, Moran dissenting in favour of denial **[PRESS for the reason of the dissent]**. Grounds: **not in any published primary record.** Per press only: Hinchley's contact with Nevada prospects and his handling of emails and documents after an oral instruction of August 2016 not to be involved in Nevada gaming.

**(2) Whether the company itself was found to have given altered or misleading documents.** **No such finding exists in the published primary record.** The concern is press-reported, attributed to commissioners, and answered at the hearing by the company's attorney (edited for file size). Whether the edits removed substance, and who made them, would be in the transcript and in the Board's investigative file (the latter confidential under Nevada law; not sought).

**(3) Later regulatory action against the company.** None found. The company's Nevada licence of 2016-08-25 continued; the NGC complaints register (2009 to 2026) carries no Table Trac matter; the Board recommended Gilbert's licensure in October 2018 and the company reports the Commission approved it unanimously on 2018-10-19 (FY2018 10-K). The company's later 10-Ks report Legal Proceedings "None." Other jurisdictions: no action found in the company's filings; regulators outside Nevada were not searched directly.

Because the regulators' published records also carry no grounds, no contradiction between the two accounts can be shown; the company's account is incomplete, not shown to be false. Two small reporting slips were noticed and are recorded as such, not as integrity findings: the 2018 proxy dates the resignation to December 2017; and the FY2018 10-K's compensation table does not foot for 2017 (Hinchley 285,500 salary against a 177,000 total, which the FY2017 10-K gives as 177,000; Hoehne 327,298 against 337,298).

## 4. The framework's test, Q5

Q5 is "STOP on integrity, WEIGHING on ability." The rows make integrity absolute: "we wouldn’t hire anybody, no matter how able, if we didn’t trust them." **[M2015-047]** The STOP is applied on doubt, not on proof: "If you’ve got doubts, forget it." **[M2013-088]**, and the cost of wrongly excluding is accepted: "we rule out 90 percent of the times. And we may be wrong about a fair number that we’re ruling out. The important thing is whether the ones we’re ruling in we’re right about." **[M2007-092]** No safeguard repairs a bad counterparty: "you can’t make a good deal with a bad person. And you can — that means we just forget about it." **[M1995-084]**; "Never deal with a rascal under the expectation that you can prevent him from cheating you." **[L2023-003]** Past conduct is the evidence: "people leave track records in life." **[M1998-168]**; "we have to have some evidence of behavior in front of us." **[M1998-166]** And what a management does when it learns of a problem is itself read: "the main problem was they didn’t act when they learned about it." **[M2017-005]**

Applied to this record:

- **The individual.** The Board recommended denial and the Commission rejected him; that is a regulator's judgment against Hinchley, and he left all offices the next day. He is not in management now. Q5 judges the people who will run the business; his conduct, whatever it was, does not by itself engage the STOP for today's management.
- **The company and Hoehne.** The record shows: no finding against the company; no complaint, fine or condition; the licence kept; a new officer licensed within a year; a compliance committee with a former Board member added. It does not show who altered or trimmed the emails, whether the trimming removed anything the Board needed, or what Hoehne, as controlling owner and director, did about the 2016 instruction. Those are exactly the facts that would decide whether the remaining person in authority was party to giving regulators an incomplete record, which would be a lapse of integrity the rows treat as final ("trustworthiness is more important than the brains" **[M2015-047]**).
- **The company's own account.** Accurate in what it says, silent on the grounds and on the Board's denial recommendation. Silence on a matter the regulators' own published records also leave without grounds is not, on this record, the dishonest report Q5's tells describe; it is incomplete, and the run should weigh it at Q5's reading test 3 ("whether the management is telling us about the things that we would want to know about if we owned a hundred percent of the company" **[M1998-036]**).

**Answer: NOT ESTABLISHED.** The primary record does not show a lapse of integrity by the company or by its current management, and it does not clear them either: the one allegation that touches the company (altered emails given to the Board) is unconfirmed press, with a recorded company explanation, and the document that would settle it was not obtained.

## 6. For the run that reads this

Q5's rule is that doubt, not proof, closes the file **[M2013-088]**, **[M2007-092]**. Whether an unconfirmed press report of commissioners' concern, with the company's recorded explanation, is a doubt about **today's** management is a judgment for the analyst of the run, not a fact this check can supply; this file states only that the primary record neither establishes nor rules out a lapse. The cheapest way to turn NOT ESTABLISHED into YES or NO is the NGC transcript of 2017-11-16, which names who edited the emails, what the commissioners concluded about it, and whether Hoehne spoke to it.
