## THE FOUNDATIONS (not a gate)
A share is a business: the question is whether I would be happy owning this stock "if the market closed for five years" **[M1997-109]**. The market was loud in the year read: the chairman's letter reports that the share price "increased by around 240%" in fiscal 2024/25 (AR p.6). That is the market serving a price: "it doesn’t tell us anything. It just tells us prices." **[M2006-077]**, and "the fact that a given asset has appreciated in the recent past is never a reason to buy it" **[L2013-007]**. No macro view enters; the Iran conflict, tariffs and the EU steel measures appear in the filings and are read only as facts about the business **[M2000-094]**. The analyst's habits govern the reading: look for "what’s wrong in things" and "what you’re missing" **[M2025-013]**, and the worst anchor "is always your previous conclusion" **[M2016-054]** (see the contamination declaration in the self-audit: I saw another steel company's box in a commit subject before starting). **Contrary evidence, written down as found** **[M1997-127]**: (1) owner cash was negative over the five-year window, and the company's own free cash flow before M&A was negative in six of the nine years FY2017 to FY2025; (2) the group survived FY2019 only by selling its best business; (3) the company will not write a margin target for Steel Europe ("Target for adjusted EBIT margin subject to the business plan being prepared", AR p.26); (4) the target it does write for Materials Services is an adjusted EBIT margin of 2% to 3%, below the decade margin of every rival read; (5) the group is being split into stand-alone companies, so the past statements describe a group that will not exist in ten years; (6) for the steel route change, "Prices for hydrogen supplies may be higher than assumed because there is no functioning hydrogen market" (AR p.202).

## THE STANDING RULE
Nothing in owning a part-interest need put the buyer at risk of ruin if it is bought without borrowed money and sized so that a large fall can be borne; the rule is the buyer's conduct: "borrowed money has no place in the investor's tool kit" **[L2014-005]**, and "Never risk permanent loss of capital." **[L2023-005]**. Noted for the buyer: the target's own value is exposed to the loss of a whole segment (Steel Europe) and to pension obligations larger than its net cash, so any position would be sized on the view that it could fall to a fraction; "it’s our job to figure out what can really go wrong" **[M2012-081]**.

**The routing for a company of parts.** thyssenkrupp reports five segments with different economics and is turning itself into "a financial holding company that serves as the umbrella for majority investments in strong and independent companies" (AR p.26). Q1's holding-company paragraph reads it by its parts: a part that cannot be understood, and that matters, keeps the whole outside the circle, since "if you have doubts about something being into your circle of competence, it isn’t" **[M2002-092]**; a part "matters" when it decides under Q2's business-of-parts paragraph (more than half of five-year operating profit, or else the largest), and the paragraph reaches operating segments. **[M2023-031]** stays OPEN against it.

**CONVENTION of this run: the "operating profit" of the parts test is the filer's segment EBIT after special items, with adjusted EBIT shown beside.** Rationale: the parts paragraph names no measure and the filer reports two; Q4's rows count restructuring and recurring special charges as real costs, "a large chunk of costs that should properly be attributed to a number of years is dumped into a single quarter" **[L1998-031]** and "we do not ask you to forget about those costs" **[M1999-023]**, and this filer's special items recur in every year of the window, while a management "highlighting "adjusted per-share earnings" makes us nervous" **[L2016-006]**. Steel Europe's EUR 4.1 billion of impairments in FY2022 to FY2025 are past spending found unrecoverable, the cost its adjusted figure leaves out. **Stated plainly, the box depends on this choice:** on adjusted EBIT, Steel Europe (EUR 2,233 million, 47% of the group's five-year adjusted EBIT) would be the largest part and would decide; on EBIT, Materials Services decides. The run proceeds on the convention; what Steel Europe's reading would show is recorded below and in "What in the framework was wrong or unclear".

**Q1 on the deciding part, Materials Services.**
- *Where it will be in ten years; the key variables.* A distributor and processor of materials, "one of the world’s leading materials distributors and service providers", core markets North America and Europe (AR p.23); 7.6 million tons sold in FY2025 (p.51); sales EUR 11.4 billion. The key variables are the metal prices it buys and sells at, which no one forecasts ("We don’t think we can predict commodity prices." **[M2016-025]**), its volumes, and the spread it earns for processing and logistics over its cost to serve. Dependence on a commodity price is never by itself a STOP at Q1 or Q2 (Q2, A commodity business); Q1 asks whether the business can be read off its figures: "if you can’t make a decision on something like PetroChina off the figures, forget about going further" **[M2008-069]**.
- *Do the past statements tell me the future ones?* **[M2008-033]** Yes, in the plain sense: ten years of the segment (FY2016 to FY2025) show a thin-spread business with an EBIT margin of 1.5% of sales over the decade, one price spike (FY2021 and FY2022, 4.8% and 5.9%) and a loss in FY2020. Its economics are a spread over metal, not a technology.
- *Would the insiders write it down?* **[M2000-105]** They do: the medium-term target is "Adjusted EBIT margin of 2% to 3%" (AR p.26), and the FY2025/26 forecast for the segment is written as a range, EUR 200 to 250 million (9M report p.23).
- *Is it important and knowable?* **[M2006-076]** The deciding question for this part, whether it holds a cost or service position that protects its returns, is knowable from its own and its rivals' filings, and it is Q2's.
- *Ownership:* the extraordinary general meeting of August 7, 2026 approved the spin-off of the segment (tk accelis) with thyssenkrupp keeping 51% and full consolidation (9M Note 17); the part's economics do not change by it.

**Contrary evidence for Q1, written down as found** **[M1997-127]**: the part that does not decide on EBIT, Steel Europe, cannot be foreseen ten years out on the company's own words. It gives no margin target (AR p.26); its route is being replaced by a hydrogen-capable direct reduction plant whose fuel has "no functioning hydrogen market" (p.202); the free allocation of CO2 certificates "is to be phased out gradually" (p.198); the EU Steel Safeguard was to expire in mid-2026 and "it is currently hard to gauge whether the EU will replace it" (p.198); the Jindal talks were paused on May 2, 2026 (H1 report p.7). Were Steel Europe the deciding part (the adjusted-EBIT reading), this is test 5's case, a forecast the insiders "would not want to put down on paper" **[M2000-105]**. It is recorded and not used for the box, by the convention above.

**VERDICT on Q1: IN** for the deciding part, Materials Services: a visible spread business that can be read off ten years of figures and whose management writes its own target. "the first question is, can I understand it?" **[M1995-051]**; understanding is "a reasonable fix on about what the earning power and competitive position will look like in five or 10 years" **[M2012-065]**, which for a distributor is the economics, not the chemistry, "What is important is that I understand the economic dynamics of the industry." **[M2011-014]**. Proceed to Q2 on the same part.
