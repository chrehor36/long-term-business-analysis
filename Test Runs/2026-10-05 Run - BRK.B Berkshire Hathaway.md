## Q1 — CAN I UNDERSTAND IT? Where its economics will be in ten years, inside my perimeter. STOP.
The test: "a reasonable fix on about what the earning power and competitive position will look like in five or 10 years" **[M2012-065]**;
"a reasonable probability of being able to asses where the business will be in 10 years" **[M2000-037]**. A holding company is
understood by its parts; "a part that cannot be, and that matters, keeps the whole outside the circle", since "if you have doubts
about something being into your circle of competence, it isn't." **[M2002-092]** (the by-parts CONVENTION; **[M2023-031]** OPEN
against it).

- **GEICO (2025 pre-tax underwriting $6.8B):** private passenger auto, written direct; key variables are loss cost per policy,
  price against Progressive and State Farm, and expense ratio. The industry's ten-year shape is foreseeable; GEICO's place in it is
  a castle question (Q2), not an understanding question.
- **BH Primary and BHRG (2025: $0.8B and $1.9B):** the insurer's door (C8): both sides of the balance sheet readable. The reserve is
  "the biggest single element that is very difficult to evaluate, even if you own the company" **[M2005-067]**, and long-tail
  writers "can report almost any earnings that the CEO desires" **[L2003-015]**; "the claims pop up 10 or 20 or 30 years later"
  **[M1999-098]**. The filer publishes ten accident years of development for medical professional liability, workers'
  compensation and other casualty, property reinsurance and casualty reinsurance, and five for GEICO's auto liability (10-K FY2025
  Note 16; $110.6B of $120.7B of net unpaid losses). Retroactive contracts "generally stipulate aggregate policy limits" (10-Q
  Note on retroactive reinsurance). Readable: the history shows the direction of each line's errors (Q4).
- **BNSF (2025 net $5.5B):** a western freight railroad; volumes, price and operating ratio are the variables; foreseeable.
- **BHE (2025 net $4.0B):** regulated utilities and pipelines. The return is set by regulators; the variables are rate base, allowed
  return, and liabilities the rate base does not cover. **The doubt:** PacifiCorp "is currently unable to reasonably estimate a
  specific range of possible additional losses" from the 2020 wildfires (10-Q Note 22), and a rule from the rows names the
  condition: "it is difficult to project both earnings and asset values in what was once regarded as among the most stable
  industries" **[L2023-011]** (**HAZARD**: this is the seller writing of his own subsidiary; it is used here as a statement of what
  makes a utility hard to forecast, and the filing fact is Note 22). Judgment: the tail is unknowable but bounded by BHE's own
  equity, since "Berkshire does not guarantee the repayment of debt issued by BNSF, BHE or any of their subsidiaries" (10-Q MD&A);
  BHE's owner cash after capex is already negative (Q7), so the part's contribution to value is small either way. It is weighed
  at Q9; it does not by itself put the whole outside the circle.
- **Manufacturing, service and retailing (2025 net $13.6B):** many industrial, building, consumer, retail and distribution
  businesses; aggregate earning power has moved within $16.8B to $17.6B pre-tax over 2023 to 2025 (10-K FY2025 MD&A). Foreseeable
  as an aggregate.
- **The investments:** valued at the filed fair value (component 1); the five largest holdings (Alphabet, American Express, Apple,
  Bank of America, Coca-Cola) are 66% of the $323.8B (10-Q Note 4). Two are technology businesses. The method takes them at
  market; the framework does not say whether marketable holdings are "parts" for Q1 (last section).

The question that will decide most of the value ten years out is what is done with $359.2B of cash and the earnings to come: "the
biggest judgment you have to make is how well capital will be deployed in the future" **[M2001-111]**. That is Q6's subject, and
it is knowable only as a weighing.

**VERDICT: IN**, by parts **[M2012-065]**, **[M2000-037]**, with the BHE wildfire tail and the long-tail casualty book carried as
doubts to Q4 and Q9. Not TOO HARD: the insurers' development history is published and read (C8), and the one unknowable item
(PacifiCorp's tail) is capped by non-recourse structure.
