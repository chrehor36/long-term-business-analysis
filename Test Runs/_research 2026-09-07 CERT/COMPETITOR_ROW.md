## 0. THE HEADLINE: WHAT THE FILINGS SAY

Three things dominate this row, and all three are R1:

1. **Certara names its competitors, by name, in its own 10-K, and has done so in every year from FY2021 to FY2025.** This is unusual and useful: the moat question does not have to be constructed from outside. It also means the *change* in that list over five years is itself evidence, and the change runs against the moat (see §1).
2. **The closest listed pure-play, Simulations Plus, wrote off $77.2 million in a single quarter (39% of its total assets at the prior year end, including 53.7% of its goodwill), cut its own revenue guidance by ~15% inside that same quarter, and has since agreed to be taken private at $18.50/share.** The comparable is being removed from the tape at a ~$375 million implied equity value.
3. **Certara has itself impaired one reporting unit and then sold it at a loss.** The Regulatory Writing unit took a $46,984k goodwill charge in 2023 and was sold to Veristat in May 2026 at a **$65,481k pre-tax loss on disposal**. Combined value destruction on that one unit: roughly $112 million.

A fourth point emerges only once all four companies are laid side by side: **every disclosed customer-retention or growth metric in this row is decelerating at once.** Certara net retention 108.4% → 105.3% → 101.5% (Q2 2026). Schrodinger net dollar retention 113% → 100%. Dassault Life Sciences +6% → -1% → -2%, with Q4 2025 at -4% and management citing *"lower study starts volume of -7% YOY in 2025"*. Simulations Plus guiding to 0-4% growth. **The deceleration is end-market, not Certara-specific, which cuts both ways: it is not evidence of Certara losing share, and it is not evidence of a moat either.**

## 1. CERTARA'S OWN COMPETITION SECTION: VERBATIM

**Source: CERT 10-K FY2025, accession `0001827090-26-000011`, filed 2026-02-26, period 2025-12-31, Item 1, "Competition" (p. 13-14).**

**Answer to the question asked: YES. Certara names competitors explicitly, and names them twice (software and services separately).**

> "The market for our biosimulation products and related services for the biopharmaceutical industry is competitive and highly fragmented. The solutions offered by our competitors vary in size, capabilities and breadth. In addition to competition from other solution providers, another challenge is that some biopharmaceutical industry participants may still rely on or over-index the status quo method of conducting research without fully leveraging the benefits of modeling, simulation, AI, dataflow and analytics platforms to inform and de-risk decisions across each phase of research and development."

> "In our view, the principal competitive factors in our market are the functionality and quality of models, the breadth of molecular types, therapeutic areas, and modalities supported, **regulator acceptance of our solutions**, ease of use and functionality of applications, depth of experience in drug development, brand awareness and reputation, total cost, and the ability to securely integrate with other enterprise applications and the overall drug research and development process in the customer. We believe that we compete favorably based on these factors and that **the time, effort, and investment necessary to develop validated models, modeling solutions, enterprise software and extensive MIDD experience presents a significant barrier to new entrants.**"

> "**In the biosimulation software market, we compete with other technology companies including Mathworks, Dassault Systemes, Ansys, Simulations Plus, and NONMEM, a division of ICON.** Other competitors include open-sourced solutions such as R and PK-Sim and internally developed software from biopharmaceutical companies. There are also many clinical research organizations that provide various data, decision tools, and advice on drug candidates and drug trial decisions. Our technology-enabled services generally compete with in-house teams at biopharmaceutical companies. **Additionally, we compete with other specialized service providers including Metrum Research, qPharmetra, and Pharmetheus.**"

**Three things changed in the FY2023 10-K and none of them has been reversed:**
1. The named software competitor set **grew from three to four, then five**, adding Mathworks, Ansys and Dassault Systemes, all of them very large companies.
2. **Certara stopped describing its competitors as "smaller than ourselves."** That phrase appears in FY2021 and FY2022 and in no filing since.
3. It **substituted a barrier-to-entry assertion** for the size comparison. A company that stops saying its rivals are small and starts saying entry is hard has, on the face of its own filings, revised its own view of the moat.

Schrodinger was dropped from Certara's list in FY2023, but **Schrodinger still names Certara** in its FY2025 10-K. The de-listing is one-directional.

### The naming is reciprocal (a useful triangulation)

- **SLP names Certara.** SLP 10-K FY2025 (`0001023459-25-000060`), Item 1, "COMPETITION": *"Our competitors include, but are not limited to, Optibrium, **Certara**, ICON, Metrum Research Group, Veeva, and WCG."*

- **Note the collision:** Schrodinger names **Chemaxon** as a competitor. Certara **acquired** Chemaxon and lists it as a Certara registered trademark in its FY2025 10-K trademark paragraph. Certara bought one of Schrodinger's named competitors.

**All three companies name each other. Nobody in this set claims an unnamed field.**

### CAVEAT ON GROSS MARGIN COMPARABILITY (material: do not skip)

**These three gross margins are NOT like-for-like, and the difference flatters Certara.**

- **CERT** tags its cost line `CostOfGoodsAndServiceExcludingDepreciationDepletionAndAmortization`. Its income statement carries a **separate** "Depreciation and amortization expense" line of **$56,556k** (FY2025) below cost of revenues. Certara's 61.5% therefore excludes all amortization.
- **SLP** puts amortization **inside** cost of revenue. Its FY2025 segment note footnote states: *"Cost of revenue includes $6.7 million of amortization within our Software reportable segment."* So SLP's 58.4% is **after** amortization.
- If Certara's full D&A were charged to cost of revenues, its gross margin would fall to **48.0%**. The true comparable is somewhere between 48.0% and 61.5% and the filings do not let you locate it precisely, because Certara does not disclose how much of the $56,556k D&A is cost-of-revenue amortization.
- **This is an UNRESEARCHED-class gap, not UNKNOWABLE.** The document that would resolve it is a Certara cost-of-revenue disaggregation. It is not in the 10-K.

## 5. THE FDA / EMA MODEL-INFORMED-DRUG-DEVELOPMENT CLAIM: THE CRUX

**Question: do the peers' filings state that FDA/EMA accept MIDD submissions built on their tools?**

**Finding: Certara makes the strongest and most specific claim, by a wide margin. SLP makes a weaker version and simultaneously disclaims regulatory approval of its own products. Schrodinger makes no such claim at all about its software.**

### CERTARA: the strongest claims (10-K FY2025, `0001827090-26-000011`)

Item 1, Business, p. 7:
> "**According to our internal data, Certara's customers have received 90% or more of all novel drug approvals by the U.S. Food and Drug Administration (the "FDA") from 2014 through 2025.** We have worked with more than 2,600 life sciences companies and academic institutions and have collaborated on more than 10,000 customer projects in the last decade ... **Our software products are licensed by more than 160,000 users and are also used by 20 global drug regulatory agencies, including the FDA, Japan's Pharmaceuticals and Medical Devices Agency (the "PMDA"), and China's Center for Drug Evaluation (the "CDE") within the National Medical Product Administration (the "NMPA").**"

Item 1, Customers:
> "We also derive limited revenue from contracts with U.S. government agencies, including the FDA and the U.S. Department of Health and Human Services (DHHS), as well as some foreign governments. For example, **our software products are licensed by 20 global drug regulatory agencies, including the FDA, EU's EMA, Japan's PMDA, China's CDE within the NMPA and the UK's Medicines and Healthcare Products Regulatory Agency ("MHRA").**"

MD&A, "Our Operating Environment":
> "**The acceptance of model-informed biopharmaceutical discovery and development by regulatory authorities affects the demand for our products and services. Support for the use of biosimulation in discovery and development from regulatory bodies, such as the FDA and EMA, has been critical to its rapid adoption by the biopharmaceutical industry.** There has been a steady increase in the recognition by regulatory and academic institutions of the role that modeling and simulation can play in the biopharmaceutical development and approval process, as demonstrated by new regulations and guidance documents describing and encouraging the use of modeling and simulation ..."

Item 1A, Risk Factors, and this is the load-bearing sentence for the moat AND its main qualifier:
> "**Our software products are licensed by the FDA and other regulatory authorities, who may use them in assessing new drug applications. These licenses are typically renewed on an annual basis, and there is no obligation for these regulatory authorities to renew these licenses at the same or any level. While these licenses account for a small amount of our annual revenue,** a reduction or the elimination of the use of any of our software products that are currently licensed by regulatory authorities could diminish our reputation and negatively impact our ability to effectively market and sell our software products, particularly if such action were part of a wider reversal of government or regulatory acceptance of in silico data."

Item 1, Intellectual Property, on the datasets:
> "Several of our biosimulation tools, **including the Simcyp Simulator, incorporate proprietary datasets compiled over decades from both public and private sources. These datasets, combined with our proprietary source code and algorithms, form advanced modeling capabilities that cannot be readily replicated.**"

**Two audit notes on the Certara claims:**
1. The **90% of novel drug approvals** figure is explicitly *"According to our internal data"*. It is a management assertion, unaudited, with no stated methodology and no third-party attestation. ## 10. WHAT THE ROW SAYS ABOUT THE MOAT

### FOR Certara having a moat (all R1, filing-sourced)

1. **Regulators are licensees, and only Certara can say so.** *"Our software products are licensed by the FDA and other regulatory authorities, who may use them in assessing new drug applications"* and *"used by 20 global drug regulatory agencies, including the FDA, EU's EMA, Japan's PMDA, China's CDE ... and the UK's MHRA."* Neither peer makes this statement. SLP goes the other way and disclaims that its products are approvable at all. If a regulator runs your simulator when it reviews your customer's dossier, your customer's switching cost is not a licence fee, it is a review risk.
2. **Scale is roughly 5x the nearest pure-play.** $418.8M vs $79.2M. And Certara reaches *"38 of the top 40 biopharmaceutical companies by R&D spend in 2025"*.
3. **Certara is the only one of the three generating GAAP operating profit and substantial cash.** FY2025 operating income $21.0M and **operating cash flow $96.3M**, versus SLP's $18.1M and SDGR's $13.9M (SDGR's first positive OCF year after burning $157.4M in 2024). OCF of 23.0% of revenue is the best in the row by a distance.
4. **Certara quantifies retention and bookings; SLP does not disclose retention at all.** A company that publishes a 12-quarter net-retention table is either confident or disciplined; either way the data exists to be checked.
5. **Lowest customer concentration in the row.** No customer over 10%, top ten at 24%. SDGR has a single 17% customer and had one customer at 68% of receivables at end-2024.
6. **The proprietary-dataset claim is specific, not generic.** *"the Simcyp Simulator, incorporate proprietary datasets compiled over decades from both public and private sources ... cannot be readily replicated."*
7. **The competitive set is fragmenting toward Certara, not away.** SLP is exiting the public market via Altaris. Certara acquired Chemaxon, which Schrodinger names as a competitor.
8. **The largest named competitor is shrinking in the relevant line while Certara grows.** Dassault's Life Sciences product line fell 2% cc in FY2025 to €1,081.1m and 4% in Q4; Certara's software revenue grew 18% and total revenue 9% in the same calendar year. **Certara is taking share from the biggest name on its own competitor list**, at least on the reported numbers.
9. **Nobody else claims what Certara claims about regulators.** Dassault's regulator language is 21 CFR Part 11 records compliance. SLP's is FDA grants and disclaims approvability. Schrodinger makes no software-regulator claim at all. **Certara alone says regulators license and run its software when assessing new drug applications.**

### AGAINST Certara having a moat (all R1, filing-sourced)

1. **Certara will not show the software gross margin.** Both peers disclose the software/services split; Certara discloses only a revenue split and a single blended cost line. **56.2% of Certara's revenue is Services.** SLP's disclosed services gross margin is 30-43%. The one number that would prove or destroy the "high-margin software franchise" thesis is the one Certara does not file.
2. **The 61.5% headline gross margin is not comparable and flatters.** Certara excludes all D&A from cost of revenues while SLP includes $6.7M of amortization in it. On a full-D&A basis Certara's gross margin is 48.0%.
3. **Three consecutive years of GAAP net losses:** -$55,357k (2023), -$12,051k (2024), -$1,595k (2025). The franchise has not yet earned a GAAP dollar in three years.
4. **Certara has impaired and then dumped an acquired unit for a combined ~$112M loss.** A moat argument that rests on "we bought the ecosystem" has to reckon with the ecosystem being sold to Veristat at a $65.5M pre-tax loss 20 months after a $47.0M write-down.
5. **78.4% of total assets is goodwill and intangibles**, against 55.3% at SLP and **0.66%** at Schrodinger. Certara's balance sheet is a record of prices paid, not of assets built. It is the only one of the three with debt ($293.1M, net debt $103.7M).
6. **Net retention is deteriorating: 108.4% -> 108.8% -> 105.3%, and 101.5% in Q2 2026, the lowest disclosed figure.** Bookings are flat year over year in H1 2026. **A moat with 101.5% net retention is a moat with no water in it.** Note also that Schrodinger's fell to exactly 100% and SLP does not disclose the metric, so the whole category is decelerating together, which argues the deceleration is end-market, not Certara-specific.
7. **Continuing-operations growth has fallen to +1% (Q2 2026) with Services down 3%.** FY revenue growth of 8.7% was partly bought (Chemaxon).
8. **The 90%-of-novel-approvals statistic is unverifiable.** *"According to our internal data"*, unaudited, no methodology, and it counts customers rather than software use in the approval.
9. **The regulator relationship is thin where it matters.** Certara's own risk factor: *"These licenses are typically renewed on an annual basis, and there is no obligation for these regulatory authorities to renew ... **these licenses account for a small amount of our annual revenue**."* Annual, revocable, immaterial in revenue.
10. **Capitalized software is large and growing fast: $24.8M in FY2025, up from $13.5M in FY2023, +84% in two years** while R&D expense grew only 20%. Total software+PP&E capex is 6.3% of revenue against SLP's 4.2% and Schrodinger's 0.6% (Schrodinger capitalizes nothing: *"Historically, the Company has not capitalized any software development costs"*). **Rising capitalization suppresses reported operating expense and inflates reported operating margin.** For owner earnings this is a maintenance-capex question that must be answered, not assumed.
11. **The market is unmistakably fragmented and Certara says so:** *"competitive and highly fragmented"*, with 8 competitors named plus open-source R and PK-Sim plus in-house teams. Fragmentation is the opposite of the franchise pattern.
12. **The comparable's clearing price is ~4.3x EV/revenue for a business with an 79% software gross margin, no debt, and net cash, sold to a financial sponsor after a 54% goodwill write-off.** That is the market's revealed price for a biosimulation pure-play in 2026.
13. **Certara's own competition disclosure has moved against the moat over five years.** It named three software competitors in FY2021-22 and called them *"companies smaller than ourselves"*; by FY2025 it names five, including Mathworks, Ansys and Dassault Systemes, and the "smaller than ourselves" phrase is gone. **Management's own description of its competitive position has been quietly downgraded in successive filings.** Per the framework's own instruction to hunt disconfirming evidence hardest for the favoured hypothesis [E4-26], this is the company itself supplying the disconfirmation.
14. **The end market is contracting, on the competitors' own numbers.** Dassault: *"lower study starts volume of -7% YOY in 2025"* and Life Sciences down 4% in Q4 2025. Every disclosed retention metric in the row is falling simultaneously. **A franchise is supposed to be visible precisely when the tide goes out; Certara's net retention fell to 101.5% in Q2 2026, its lowest disclosed reading, on flat bookings.**
15. **Certara's largest named competitor is roughly three times its size in the relevant line.** Dassault's Life Sciences product line is €1,081.1m against Certara's $418.8m of total revenue, and Dassault carries €2.19bn of MEDIDATA goodwill it has never impaired. That is a competitor with the balance sheet to price aggressively for a long time. The FY2021-22 characterisation of competitors as *"companies smaller than ourselves"* was already untrue by the time it was dropped.

### The one question this row raises that the run must answer

**Certara's Q1 (how it makes money) and Q2 (is it a franchise) both collapse into a single unanswered number: the gross margin of the software business.** Both peers file it. Certara does not. Until that number is obtained, "Certara is a high-margin software franchise with regulatory lock-in attached to a lower-margin services arm" is a hypothesis with 56.2% of revenue behind an undisclosed cost line.

Per the framework test: **can I name the document that would resolve this?** A Certara cost-of-revenue disaggregation by segment. It does not exist in any 10-K, 10-Q, or proxy filed 2021-2026. **Verdict on that sub-question: UNRESEARCHED becoming UNKNOWABLE if the company has never filed it and is not required to.** Certara reports as a single operating segment, so ASC 280 does not compel the split. **That closes the question at UNKNOWABLE, and per the protocol, UNKNOWABLE closes the file at Q2.**
