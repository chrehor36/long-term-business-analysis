## Q3 — HOW MUCH CAPITAL MUST GO IN to get the earnings out, and what does the added capital earn? WEIGHING.
- **The capital it needs.** Net property, plant and equipment rose from $49,964M at 2016-12-31 to $78,967M at 2025-12-31 (FY2017 and FY2025 10-Ks), while the company sold its gas transmission (2020-21), Cove Point (2019, 2023) and three gas utilities (2024) along the way. Capital spending in 2025 was $12,653M (segment table, Note 26) against depreciation and amortization of $2,387M (the same table) and operating cash of $5,361M: about five dollars spent for each dollar of depreciation, and more than twice the operating cash. The plan for 2026-2030 is $65.7 billion (Item 7). "To earn more, their owners must invest more" is the speakers' own description of their utilities **[L2011-019]**.
- **What it earns on the capital, over time.** Return on average shareholders' equity, consolidated, as first filed: 15.6%, 18.9%, 13.1%, 5.2%, -1.4%, 12.3%, 3.6%, 7.2%, 7.8%, 10.6% for 2016 to 2025, a ten-year mean of 9.3% and a 2021-2025 mean of 8.3% (competitor row, Q2). The deciding part, Virginia Power, on its own statements (net income / average shareholder's equity, FY2021, FY2023 and FY2025 10-Ks): 2021 11.2% ($1,712M), 2022 6.8% ($1,112M), 2023 7.5% ($1,452M), 2024 8.7% ($1,897M), 2025 8.5% ($2,101M); its shareholder's equity went from $15,980M (2021) to $27,205M (2025), helped by $3,250M (2023) and $3,500M (2025) of equity put in by the parent (Virginia Power's cash-flow statements).
- **What the added capital earns** **[M2001-019]**. On Virginia Power: average net income of 2024-25 ($1,999M) less that of 2021-22 ($1,412M) is $587M; average year-end equity of 2024-25 ($24,681M) less that of 2021-22 ($16,465M) is $8,216M; **about 7.1% after tax on the added equity**, below the 9.70% the legislature set for 2023-24 and the 9.80% authorized from 2025. Part of the gap is construction not yet in service (CVOW's turbines are due "by the end of 2026 with the remainder in early 2027", Item 1), and part is the earned shortfall the commission measured: "a combined ROE of 7.77% on its generation and distribution services for the test period, compared to the ROE of 9.70% authorized" (Note 13). The speakers' measure is the one used: "It’s measured by the operating earnings after depreciation" **[M2014-086]**.
- **Reinvestment to stand still, and to grow** **[L1999-024]**, **[M2000-144]**. The filing gives no maintenance figure. The guess used at Q7 is depreciation, the speakers' usual proxy, "not inappropriate in most companies to use as a proxy for required capital expenditures" **[M1998-127]**; and the speakers warn that in "the utility business [...] your depreciation charges are inadequate and you’re kidding yourself as to your real economic profits" **[M2015-049]**, so a maintenance case built on depreciation is the generous side, said so at Q7.
- **The growth arithmetic.** Growth here is bought: earnings rise because "We just put way more capital into the business" **[M2023-081]**, at a return the commission sets near 9.8% on the equity part and that the company has earned below that in the span. That is the second-best business the rows accept, "still a good choice" but "not as good as the best choice" **[M2018-055]**, on the condition "that these businesses have reasonable expectations of earning decent returns on the incremental sums they invest" **[L2009-012]**.
- **WEIGHS AGAINST.** The rows accept the regulated capital-heavy business as a second best only on decent returns on the added sums **[L2009-012]**, **[M2012-057]**; here the added equity at the deciding part earned about 7% after tax over 2021-2025, below the allowed return, while the owners were asked for fresh equity in most years (Q6). Not a stop: Q3 grades, and of the regulated return the speakers say "there’s no way we get rich on our utility investments. But there’s no way we get poor, either" **[M2009-104]**. (The "little or no debt" criterion is applied at Q9.)
