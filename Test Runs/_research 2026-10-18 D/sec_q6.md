## Q6 — WHAT WILL THEY DO WITH THE MONEY AND THE OWNERS? WEIGHING.
### Part A: the money
- **The retention test** **[R1995-009]**, **[M1998-110]**. Dominion has not retained its earnings; it has paid out more than it earned and raised fresh equity to build. Retained earnings fell from $6,854M (2016) to $2,318M (2025) (Q4). Common dividends paid were $2,036M, $2,209M, $2,233M, $2,239M and $2,278M in 2021 to 2025, against net income attributable of $3,288M, $994M, $1,994M, $2,124M and $2,998M as first filed (Q2's row) and against operating cash of $4,037M to $6,572M that capital spending exceeded every year. Common stock issued: $1,302M (2017), $2,461M (2018), $2,515M (2019), $159M (2020), $192M (2021), $1,866M (2022), $94M (2023), $732M (2024), $1,488M (2025), plus the all-stock SCANA combination of 2019, against one buyback of $3,080M in 2020 (cash-flow statements). The market's verdict on the money put in: the price was $72.83 on 2016-10-17 and $63.56 on 2026-10-16 (aggregator), while basic shares rose 44% (Q2); a market value up roughly $11 billion (about $45 billion at 2016's 616 million average basic shares, to $55.9 billion today) against $10.8 billion of common stock sold for cash in 2017 to 2025 (gross), less the $3.1 billion buyback, plus the shares issued for SCANA (not priced here); with retained earnings falling, that is less than a dollar of market value for each dollar the owners put in over the decade. The forward test, "Can you keep using all of the capital you generate, effectively, for a very long time?" **[M2010-097]**, is answered by the plan ($65.7 billion) for the using, and by Q3 (about 7% on the added equity) for the "effectively".
- **Buybacks** **[L1999-023]**, **[L2016-002]**. One programme in the span: in 2020 "Dominion Energy repurchased 38.9 million shares of Dominion Energy common stock for $3.1 billion" (FY2021 10-K), an average of about $79.18 a share, after the BHE sale; the authorization (November 2020, $1.0 billion) "does not include a specific timetable or price or volume targets" (Item 7). The company then sold shares in 2022 to 2025 at forward prices of $55.26 to $61.11 (Item 7): it bought in at about $79 and sold out at $55 to $61, the order the rows describe as selling "low and then they buy high" **[M1998-027]** run backwards. *Buyback line, written back from Q7:* see the end of Part A.
- **Issuance and deals.** "The intrinsic value of the shares you give in an acquisition must not be greater than the intrinsic value of the business you receive" **[L2014-012]**. Three deals bear: (1) **SCANA (2019)**, paid in Dominion shares, an all-stock merger; the framework's one STOP in Part A falls on an all-stock deal made in undervalued stock **[L2009-019]**; whether Dominion's stock was below its value in 2019 is tested against Q7's range in the written-back line below. (2) **The asset sales of 2020 to 2024** (gas transmission to BHE, Cove Point, the three gas utilities to Enbridge): cash received, the Questar sale at a goodwill loss (Note 3); value given against value got cannot be scored from the filings beyond that. (3) **The NextEra merger (signed 2026-05-15, approved by Dominion's owners 2026-09-03, not closed)**: here Dominion is the business sold, paid in NextEra shares; for Dominion's owners the test is the same one turned round, "Only if we receive less in value than we give" **[M1995-001]**, and it is read at Q7, beside the central figure, where the value given (Dominion) and the value got (0.8138 NextEra shares and about $0.41 of cash a share) are set side by side. Q6's STOP is the acquirer's, NextEra's, and is not Dominion's to fail.
- **Serial issuance.** Equity was issued in every year of 2017 to 2025, with $1.6 to $1.8 billion more planned through at-the-market programs in 2026 and forward sales outstanding (Step 0). "we have never invested in companies that are hell-bent on issuing shares" **[L2014-015]**; the framework carries this as a candidate second STOP, unresolved (section VI), so it is weighed here, heavily. In fairness to the business: a regulated utility funds its rate base at the commission's debt-to-equity split, so growth of this size needs equity from somewhere, and here, with dividends above retained earnings, it comes from new shares.
- **Dividends** **[L2012-015]**. The dividend was cut in 2020 (payments $2,983M in 2019, $2,873M in 2020, $2,036M in 2021) and has been held at $2.67 a share for 2023 to 2026 (Item 7). A dividend paid out of earnings while shares are sold to fund the plant is a round trip for the owner, less the issuing costs; the policy is consistent since 2021, but it is not the rows' rule, which keeps the dollar where it earns more than a dollar **[M2004-089]** and pays it out otherwise.
- **Buyback line, written back from Q7 (committed with Q7).** The 2020 purchases averaged about $79.18 a share, more than twice the bottom of Q7's equity-only range ($26.14) and above its top ($38.39): by the framework's reading of a programme with no stated price, the prices paid did not sit at or below the bottom of the range, so the missing price weighs against **[L2016-002]**, and buying above value harms the continuing owners "just as if you issue shares beneath that figure" **[M1996-013]**. The shares sold in 2022 to 2025 at $55 to $61 were sold above the range's top: on this range, issuing then did not harm the owners. *The SCANA STOP:* the framework's one STOP in Part A needs the acquirer's stock to have been below its value when it paid in stock **[L2009-019]**; Dominion's stock closed between $67.97 and $83.46 in 2019 (aggregator) against today's range of $26.14 to $38.39, so on this evidence the stock was not undervalued and the STOP's condition is not shown (today's range is not 2019's, and that is the limit of the test).
- **WEIGHS AGAINST.** Less than a dollar of market value for each dollar the owners put in over the span **[R1995-009]**; serial issuance **[L2014-015]**; a buyback at about $79 followed by sales at $55 to $61 **[M1998-027]**; a programme that names no price **[L2016-002]**.

### Part B: the pay, the board and the owners
- **Pay tied to what the person controls** **[M2003-019]**, **[L1996-018]**. The 2025 annual incentive "was tied solely to achievement of predetermined consolidated operating EPS goals"; the long-term awards are 50% relative TSR, 40% cumulative operating EPS and 10% "Non-Carbon Emitting Generation Capacity" for the PSUs, with the CEO's performance shares on relative TSR (DEF 14A 2026). Both measures are per-share earnings on the adjusted figure and the stock against peers; neither charges the managers for the capital they use, which in this business is the whole question **[L1994-019]**: a growing rate base raises operating EPS whatever the return on the added equity, provided enough of it is debt.
- **The ratchet** **[L2005-015]**. "In 2025, the CTD Committee increased the target annual award opportunity for Mr. Blue from 130% to 150% to better align his total compensation with companies in the Compensation Peer Group", and raised his long-term target by $1,200,000 (DEF 14A 2026). The rows: "comp committees have become slaves to comparative data" **[L2005-015]**.
- **Pay that followed results down.** The CEO's 2023 performance-based stock award paid 0% on missed relative TSR (DEF 14A 2026): a partner in both directions on that part **[L1994-020]**.
- **The merger pay.** Potential payments to Blue of $72.8 million on a change in control with termination, single-trigger vesting for some officers' restricted stock, and 42% of the votes cast against the advisory pay vote (Q5).
- **The board.** The CEO is also Chair, with an independent lead director: "I've seen how hard it is to replace a mediocre CEO if that person is also Chairman" **[L2014-026]**. Directors' ownership guideline: "Lesser of 12,000 shares or 5x retainers" (DEF 14A 2026); whether the shares were bought "with their savings, rather than simply having been the recipients of grants" **[L2019-008]** was not established from the proxy read.
- **Owners as partners.** Earnings guidance every year and a long-term EPS growth promise, which the rows by inversion rule against: "forecasting earnings, I can’t imagine anything more destructive" **[M2022-054]**. "$40+ million contributed to social betterment through our charitable foundation, energy assistance, and other programs in 2025" (DEF 14A 2026, highlights), the owners' money given to causes the managers chose **[L1993-030]**; in a regulated utility part of such giving may be expected by the commissions, and the filing does not split it.
- **WEIGHS AGAINST.** Pay on adjusted EPS and relative TSR with no charge for capital **[L1994-019]**, a peer-group ratchet **[L2005-015]**, a combined Chair and CEO **[L2014-026]**, guidance **[M2022-054]**, and a merger pay package 42% of the votes opposed; for it, the CEO's TSR award paid nothing when TSR lagged.
