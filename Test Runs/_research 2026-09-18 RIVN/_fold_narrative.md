### WHY Q2 CLOSED, ON RIVIAN'S OWN RECORD
- **The company names its substitutes and says they are stronger.** FY2025 10-K Item 1A: *"we are competing for sales with both EV manufacturers and traditional
  automotive companies ... Many of our current and potential competitors have significantly greater financial, technical, manufacturing, marketing, or other resources
  than we do."*
- **The customer behaved as if they were substitutes.** Deliveries 50,122, 51,579, 42,247 (FY2023-25); the FY2025 fall of 18% was *"due in part to the expiration of
  45W tax credits after September 30, 2025"*. A 2022 price rise is filed as a publicity and customer-loss risk [E4-37]; the FY2025 rise in average price came with the
  18% unit fall [E2-44].
- **Every vehicle has been sold below its cost.** Automotive gross profit per vehicle delivered about -$40.3k, -$23.4k, -$10.2k (FY2023-25) and -$4.3k (H1 2026). The
  plant ran at 38%, 33% and 28% of its stated 150,000 units (FY2023-25).
- **No return on capital in any year** [E3-46, E3-43]: pre-tax ROCE net of cash -117%, -86%, -70% (FY2023-25); equity $13.8bn (2022) to $4.6bn (2025), refilled by
  issuance. **The row** (Rivian, Lucid, Ford's Model e segment, Tesla, GM and Ford recomputed from their faces; Toyota, Stellantis and Honda from the TM run's row):
  FY2025 operating margin Rivian -66.5%, Ford Model e -67.1%, Lucid -258.7%; every profitable maker is a volume maker. Rivian is the best of the loss-makers, which is
  not a position.
- **Government sets part of the price and part of the cost** [E2-59]: regulatory credits ($197M FY2025, being phased out), manufacturing tax credits netted in cost of
  revenues, and the 45W credit whose expiry cut volume.
- **The platform is replaced every two to three years** [E4-04, E5-23]: the R1 line retooled in Q2 2024 with accelerated depreciation; R2 on a new midsize platform in
  2026; a second plant under construction.
- **Key-person dependence, as a moat defect [E4-23]**: *"We are highly dependent on the services and reputation of Robert J. Scaringe, our Founder and CEO."*
- **The full-strength case, recorded first**: Volkswagen paid $1,295M for a licence to the electrical architecture and about $2.75bn for 15.9% of the equity; software
  and services gross margin 42% in Q2 2026; average price up while discounting fell; Amazon runs more than 40,000 of the vans. But the architecture now sits in a 50/50
  joint venture with that rival, and the JV revenue ($836M of FY2025's $1,557M software and services revenue) is one prepaid related-party contract expected to fall
  *"during 2028"*. At most a narrow, provisional position, and the automotive segment (71% of revenue) has never earned a gross profit.

### ONE THING TO KEEP
**A first positive gross profit can be a partner's prepayment.** Rivian's FY2025 consolidated gross profit ($144M) and about half of its FY2024 operating-cash
improvement were Volkswagen paying in advance for engineering that ends in 2028; the vehicles still lost money. Read the segment note and the deferred-revenue line
before a "turned gross-profit positive" headline argues for a business.
