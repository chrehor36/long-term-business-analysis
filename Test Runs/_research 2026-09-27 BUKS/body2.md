### The casino (Professional Services)

**[E3-03], criterion by criterion.**
- **(1) Needed or desired [x]: passes.** People gamble; the casino has operated since 2009-12-15.
- **(3) Not subject to price regulation: FAILS in the form that matters to this owner.** The state owns the games and fixes the split. *"As required by Kansas law, all games, gaming equipment and gaming operations, including sports wagering, at Boot Hill are owned and operated by the Kansas Lottery."* The company's revenue is what the state leaves: *"Our revenue is determined after the revenue share is distributed to the state and mandated regulatory expenses are paid."* The split is written by the state and moved by it: the 10-Ks for fiscal 2013 to 2024 record *"total taxes between 27% and 31% of gross gaming revenue, based on achievement of the following revenue levels"*; the 2026 10-K, *"a total revenue share of 29% of gross legacy gaming revenue (sports wagering revenue share is 10% to the State)"* and *"On December 15, 2024, the tax rate to the state increased by 2% and we commenced our second 15-year management contract"*. **At the one renewal on the record, the state took two more points.** Sports wagering runs on the same model: the company *"will be paid 90% of all of the sports wagering revenue generated by it"* under a renewal the state granted for three years from 2027-09-01, and the 10-K lists among the factors beyond its control *"the ultimate revenue share rates and license fees charged by the state of Kansas"*. On top, the manager pays *"its proportionate share of certain expenses incurred by the Kansas Lottery Commission and the Kansas Racing and Gaming Commission"* ($2.6M in fiscal 2026). This is **[E2-59]**'s case: prices *"administered in some manner"*, a position whose protection belongs to the regime. The framework's text governs how that is read (Section III, Q2): regulation *"caps a franchise"* and *"floors a commodity business; neither creates the class."*
- **(2) No close substitute: FAILS on the filer's words.** *"The casino entertainment business is highly competitive."* *"We compete with other casino facilities in Kansas and Oklahoma. We also compete with other non-gaming resorts and vacation destinations, various other entertainment businesses, and other forms of gaming, such as state lotteries, on-track and off-track wagering, third party online sports betting platforms and other mobile-based gaming platforms, prediction markets and other event contracts"*; *"the Kansas governor recently entered into a compact with a Native American tribe to allow Native American sports wagering throughout the state and to increase the permissible types of games in certain casino locations"*; and *"Smoking is permitted in Native American casinos in the State of Kansas and in casinos in neighboring states. Such a ban, if enacted, would put us at a competitive disadvantage."* The zone exclusivity keeps out a second **state** casino in Dodge City; it does not keep out the substitutes the filer itself names.

**[E3-43]'s demonstration** (*"a company’s ability to regularly price its product or service aggressively and thereby to earn high rates of return on capital"*), on the record. **Revenue in fiscal 2026, $37.4M, is the same nominal figure as fiscal 2012's $37.2M, and it now includes $6.5M of sports wagering that did not exist before fiscal 2023** (*"Sports wagering through the DraftKings sports wagering platform brought in $6.5 million of revenue during fiscal 2026"*): the legacy casino and its restaurant brought in about **$30.9M against $37.2M fourteen years earlier**, a nominal decline. Fiscal 2026: *"traditional casino gaming revenue decreased $1.3 million due to a decrease in patron visits"*, and the outlook expects the local downturn *"will likely continue through fiscal year 2027 and put downward pressure on Professional Services revenue"*. **The margin step from about 5% (fiscal 2014-2020) to 22-26% (fiscal 2022-2026) is not a price**: it coincides with the purchase of the casino building in December 2020 for $41.25M of bank debt and the October 2021 purchase of the manager's remaining units for about $16.4M; the capital that bought the margin sits beneath it, and its interest ($1.5M in fiscal 2026) is below the segment line. The segment's assets are not disclosed (*"does not evaluate operating segments using asset or liability information"*); against the building's cost alone, fiscal 2026 segment operating income of $8.3M is **at most 20%** pre-tax, and less once the gaming equipment the manager must buy for the state ($8.5M gross) and the $5.5M privilege fee are counted. **Units [E4-55]:** no visit or admission count is published in any 10-K read; the filer's words are the only physical series (*"decrease in patron visits"*, 2026; *"lower customer headcount, which has been off-set by a larger net revenue per customer"*, 2021-2022).

### The aerospace shops (Aerospace Products)

**[E3-03], criterion by criterion.**
- **(1) Needed or desired [x]: passes.** Special-mission aircraft, avionics and gun-control units are bought by governments, contractors and operators.
- **(2) No close substitute: FAILS on the filer's words.** *"In the manufacture of aircraft structures, systems components, subassemblies and parts in addition to services related to aircraft modifications, we compete globally against subsidiaries of much larger companies, original equipment manufacturers and smaller independent integrators or operators."* *"Participants in the aerospace industry compete primarily based on size of business, technical staffing, quality, turnaround time, capacity and price."* *"Some of our competitors have substantially greater financial and other resources than we have, and others may price their products and services below our selling prices."* And a substitute for the modification itself: *"There are several entities that have purchased Avcon-modified planes and leased them as an alternative to potential customers purchasing a modification for their airplane."* The filer's own counter-claim is recorded in full: *"While we believe that we have unique products and proprietary designs that provide a competitive advantage to other modification businesses, the risk exists that other businesses could expand into the marketplace."*
- **(3) Not subject to price regulation: partly fails.** Government work is priced under the FAR: *"Our contracts with the U.S. government and contracts in which we serve as a subcontractor to a prime government contractor are subject to the Federal Acquisition Regulation (“FAR”) and related agency supplements, which govern pricing, cost allowability, audit rights"*, and *"Our government contract costs are subject to audit by government agencies, and such audits may result in downward adjustments to our contract costs or pricing."* The share of revenue under the FAR is not disclosed.

**[E2-44]'s first characteristic (*"an ability to increase prices rather easily ... without fear of significant loss of either market share or unit volume"*), in the filer's words, fiscal 2026 MD&A:** *"With costs increasing, we may have to increase our prices to maintain the same level of profitability"*, and Item 1A: *"If we are unable to increase our prices sufficiently to offset increasing expenses, then inflation may have a material adverse effect."* *"Our Aerospace Products business generated approximately 53% of its 2026 revenue from fixed-price contracts"*, with *"cost overruns and losses on such contracts"* named as a risk. That is the language of a price-taker. **What the filer says produced the fiscal 2026 margin is mix and efficiency, not price**: *"reflecting sales of previously FAA-approved modifications, both from an installation perspective as well as a kit sale perspective and increased efficiencies of our fabrication labor"*, and for special mission electronics, *"efficiencies in production, including pre-building components for shipment upon receipt of orders."*

**[E3-43] on the record:** a segment margin of −10.9% to +18.7% for thirteen years, then 16.8% and 33.3%; a mean of 5.6% over fiscal 2012-2024. The company-level pre-tax return on average net tangible operating assets (`peers/BUKS_row.txt`, both segments together) ran **2.2% to 21.6% from fiscal 2013 to 2025** and **40.7% in fiscal 2026**. One year does not demonstrate *"regularly"*. **Customer concentration:** *"we derived 39.1% of our revenue from five customers, and we had two “major customers” (10 percent or more of consolidated revenue) that provided 29.9% of total revenue"*; special mission electronics in Tempe *"sell almost entirely to one customer"*: a 52% margin earned from a single buyer is that buyer's decision to renew, not the seller's power to price.

**THE STRONGEST EVIDENCE AGAINST THE VERDICT BELOW, hunted hardest [E4-26, E3-41, E4-51].** The verdict below is OUT, so the case for IN is set out first, as its holders would state it:
1. **The numbers have turned.** Company operating margin 29% and pre-tax return on tangible operating capital 40.7% in fiscal 2026, aerospace segment 33%, special mission electronics 52%, first-quarter fiscal 2027 operating income up 59% with a $51.1M backlog, and the share price up about fivefold from the fiscal 2025 bids ($0.81-$1.98) to $4.04. [E3-43] says the franchise *"will be demonstrated by"* high returns; here they are.
2. **The certificates are a real barrier.** An STC is FAA-granted, costly and slow to obtain (*"The FAA licensing process may be costly and time-consuming"*), has *"indefinite"* legal life, and each new one widens the catalogue that kit sales monetise at high margin; the filer calls its designs *"unique products and proprietary designs"*.
3. **The casino is a legal monopoly for thirteen more years.** One licence per zone, renewed to 2039; nobody else may open a state casino in the Southwest zone.
4. **Special mission electronics has held one customer for years** at 40-52% margins (fiscal 2025-2026), which looks like a sole-source position.

**Answered.** (1) is the strongest fact in the file, and it is two years long against thirteen years of a contractor's margins (mean 5.6%, three loss years) in the same segment; the filer attributes the rise to *"sales of previously FAA-approved modifications"*, kit sales and *"efficiencies"*, not to price, and its own MD&A says it *"may have to increase our prices to maintain the same level of profitability"*. A franchise under [E3-43] is demonstrated by pricing *"regularly ... aggressively"* **and** the returns it earns; the record shows the returns arrive when the mix is good and leave when it is not (fiscal 2021 2.7%, fiscal 2023 0.2%, between 17.9% and 11.4%). The price move is the market's, and is not evidence for Q2. (2) is accepted as a barrier to entry at the level of a single certificate; it does not make the modification a thing customers think has *"no close substitute"*, which the filer's competition paragraph and the leased-aircraft risk deny, and the certificates must be bought afresh for each new platform, which is [E4-04]'s replacement class. (3) is the reading [E2-59] exists to refuse: the monopoly is the state's, the state sets and has just raised its share, owns the games, and licenses the substitutes (tribal compacts, iLottery, sports-wagering terms), and the casino's legacy revenue is lower in nominal dollars than fourteen years ago. (4) is a single buyer: a sole-supplier position to one customer is that customer's renewal decision, and the 10-K names the dependence as a risk (*"A loss of business from ... one or more of any of these major customers may have a material adverse effect"*); the line's revenue swung from $14.1M (fiscal 2020) to $6.9M (fiscal 2021) and back to $19.2M (fiscal 2026). **[E3-47]** (a wrongly closed file inside the circle of competence is the costliest error) was weighed against OUT in writing; it is why the reversal conditions below reopen the file on filed documents.

**The alternative readings, recorded for the operator [E4-51].** A reader could split the verdict: OUT on the casino (criteria 2 and 3 on the filer's words) and **UNKNOWABLE** on aerospace, on the ground that two years of 17-33% margins cannot yet be told apart from a moat that has *"not yet shown up in the numbers"* [E3-33].
