## Q1 — CAN I UNDERSTAND HOW THIS MAKES MONEY? **[E3-31]**
- **Unit economics in my own words, no management language.** Two unrelated businesses under one small holding company.
  - **The casino.** Kansas law makes the state the owner and operator of every game (*"all games, gaming equipment and gaming operations, including sports wagering, at Boot Hill are owned and operated by the Kansas Lottery"*); the company is the state's contracted manager for the one casino licensed in the Southwest Gaming Zone (named *"developer and manager of the Southwest Gaming Zone located in Dodge City, Kansas"* in 2008, opened 2009-12-15). Gamblers lose money at about 500 slots and 15 tables; the state takes its share off the top (*"a total revenue share of 29% of gross legacy gaming revenue"*, raised by two points when the second 15-year contract began on 2024-12-15), plus a share of the regulators' costs ($2.6M in fiscal 2026); the company keeps the rest as revenue (*"Our revenue is determined after the revenue share is distributed to the state and mandated regulatory expenses are paid"*), pays the staff, and since December 2020 owns the building (bought for $41.25M with bank debt). Sports betting runs through DraftKings on the state's licence; the company is paid *"90% of all of the sports wagering revenue generated by it"*. Fiscal 2026: segment revenue **$37.4M** (of which sports wagering $6.5M, non-gaming $4.2M), segment operating income **$8.3M**, before $1.5M of interest on the building debt.
  - **The aerospace shops.** Avcon holds FAA supplemental type certificates (STCs) for modifying specific business aircraft (Learjet, King Air, Cessna turboprops, lately Challenger and CASA CN-235) into special-mission aircraft: pods, radomes, sensor mounts, bigger doors, longer range. It is paid per modification contract, mostly recognised as the work progresses, and increasingly sells kits for others to install. The Tempe plant makes gun control units and cabling for Apache and Blackhawk helicopters and military vehicles, *"almost entirely to one customer"*. Avionics installs radios and displays. Fiscal 2026 operating income by line (10-K Note 15): **aircraft modification $9.7M** on $37.2M, **special mission electronics $10.0M** on $19.2M, avionics $1.5M on $4.2M, unallocated corporate −$1.1M: **segment $20.2M on $60.6M**, 53% of it under fixed-price contracts.
