Case declared, and why: **on Union Pacific alone, none is ticked and Q3 would be an overlay**: the network carried the company through its worst managed year on the record without a loss (2004, operating ratio 89.4%, net income $604M, FY2004 10-K) and the leverage is not a bank's (debt $31.8bn against $69.7bn of assets and $9.8bn of operating income). **On the combined perimeter, which Union Pacific has contracted to reach, Daily execution is ticked, from the company's own filed record of its last merger**: the ten-year table of the FY2002 10-K (footnote: *"the acquisitions of ... Southern Pacific Rail Corporation as of October 1, 1996"*) reads rail operating ratio **79.1% (1996), 87.4% (1997), 95.4% (1998)** and return on equity **12.4%, 5.3%, (8.1)%**. Integrating two networks is a have-to-be-smart-every-day task for years, and the filer's own history shows what a lapse costs. **So Q3 is judged as a BINARY GATE** (the stricter case covers both perimeters), and no price compensates for a disqualifying finding.

**STEP 2 — THE FOUR FLAGS [E4-22, E5-15].** *Accounting and disclosure, not litigation. Each is a prompt to READ, never a verdict.*

With an annual bonus paid 35% on operating ratio, capitalising a borderline cost improves the paid metric; the capex-to-depreciation ratio has not risen to suggest it (1.54 in 2025 against 1.72 over 23 years). Recorded as an incentive to watch **[E4-27]**, not a finding.
- [ ] unintelligible footnotes — **not found.** The acquisition note (Note 20 / 10-Q Note 18) gives the terms, the STB timetable including the rejection, the costs by line and the termination fee.
- [x] trumpeted earnings projections / growth targets — **fires.** The Q2 2026 release: *"Reported earnings per share growth increased to high-single digit; consistent with attaining 3-year CAGR target of high-single to low-double digit through 2027"* and *"Pricing dollars in excess of inflation dollars"*; the merger release: *"more than $30 billion of potential value creation through the expected achievement of approximately $2.75 billion in annualized synergy opportunity"* and *"expected to be accretive to Union Pacific's adjusted EPS per share in the second full year after closing and rising to high single digit accretion thereafter."* What the filing says: the 10-K carries none of these targets and lists, in its risk factors, that the combined company may not realise *"synergies, cost savings, innovation, and operational efficiencies"*. - [x] EBITDA / adjusted-earnings promotion **[E4-29]** — **fires in the releases, not in the 10-K.** The merger release puts *"EBITDA of approximately $18 billion"* in its financial-impact paragraph and sells the deal on *"adjusted EPS"* accretion; the quarterly releases lead with *"adjusted diluted EPS"* and *"adjusted OR"* beside the GAAP figures. A prompt to read, and read: the adjusted items are quantified line by line (merger costs, a crew-staffing ratification charge, a deferred-tax adjustment).
- [x] filed-figure tells **[E4-30]** — **cash tax as a share of pretax income is falling**: cash income taxes paid (federal, state, foreign, net of refunds) **$1,486M (2023), $1,340M (2024), $877M (2025)** against pretax income $8,233M, $8,794M, $9,166M: **18.0%, 15.2%, 9.6%**. **STEP 3 — THE PRIMARY TEST [E2-01].** Earnings rate on equity capital employed, *without undue leverage or accounting gimmickry* — **not** EPS growth. Balance sheet first **[E5-27]**:

Repurchases 2016-2025 **$43.3bn** (filed cash-flow statements); dividends 2016-2025 $27.0bn. About $17bn of the repurchases was carried by new debt.
- **Return on average equity, the filer's own (selected data and MD&A):** 7.8% (2005), 11.1 (2006), 12.0 (2007), 15.1 (2008), 11.7 (2009), 16.1 (2010), 18.1 (2011), 20.5 (2012), 21.4 (2013), 24.4 (2014), 22.8 (2015), 20.8 (2016), 47.8 (2017, tax-reform credit), 26.4 (2018), 30.7 (2019), 30.5 (2020), 41.9 (2021), 53.2 (2022), 47.3 (2023), 42.6 (2024), 40.4% (2025). **The last seven years are leverage-made** (equity held near $12-18bn while debt doubled), which is the *"undue leverage"* [E2-01] excludes, so the primary series is the unleveraged one.

**The half-owner test [E2-26]:** does this reporting tell me what I would want to know if the positions were reversed? **The 10-K passes; the merger release does not fully.** The 10-K reports the STB's rejection of the first application in plain words (*"the STB rejected the application without prejudice"*), the $2.5bn fee, the leverage covenant the combined company will face (*"the combined company will likely be required to comply with a leverage covenant"*), and every adjusted figure with a reconciliation. The merger release sold the deal to owners on EBITDA, adjusted-EPS accretion and a *"$30 billion of potential value creation"* figure whose arithmetic it does not show, and **did not state what an owner of a Union Pacific share would own per share, in cash earnings, before synergies** (about 18% less on the capex-end construction: $8.86 standalone against $7.26 combined, Q5 below). That is the number a half-owner would want first.

**Capital allocation — the two buyback conditions [E5-08]:**
- (1) ample funds for operations and liquidity? **Yes, with borrowed money.** Capital spending was fully funded in every year, and about $17bn of the $43.3bn repurchased in 2016-2025 was financed by debt, with the ratings kept.
- (2) repurchases at a **material discount** to conservatively calculated IV? **The 2018-2025 prices ($143.70, $165.77, $167.92, $218.36, $231.51, $202.67, $240.51, $234.15 a share, filed averages) bought owner earnings (capex end) of 4.8%, 4.3%, 4.9%, 4.2%, 3.9%, 3.8%, 3.9% and 3.8% of the price** (per-share owner earnings of the same year over the average price; this run's arithmetic). **Every year was far below the ~10% floor this framework quits on [E4-28]** (the long-bond yields of those years are not restated here from memory; they are not needed for the flag). At the conservative value range built at Q5 (about $128 to $218 a share today), the 2021-2025 purchases at $202-241 were not at a material discount.
- **CAPITAL ALLOCATION FLAG — on two counts, stated with the humility clause [E4-13]**: this rests on our own IV range, and management knows the business better than we do. (a) The 2021-2025 repurchases, above the conservative value, partly debt-funded. (b) **The merger**: at today's price Union Pacific pays about **$81.6bn** (225M shares at $273.79 plus $20bn) for owner earnings of **$1,512M (three-year capex end), $2,094M (2025 alone) or $2,468M (three-year depreciation end)**, **1.85% to 3.02% of the price**; with every dollar of the claimed $2.75bn pre-tax synergies realised after tax, **5.2% to 5.7%**, about the bond. **Binds position size, never the discount rate.**
