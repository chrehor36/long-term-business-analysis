## Q2 — IS IT A FRANCHISE? **[E3-03]**

**The rule, quoted from the ledger row itself** (`principle_ledger.csv`, E3-03, 1991 letter): *"An economic franchise
arises from a product or service that: (1) is needed or desired; (2) is thought by its customers to have no close
substitute and; (3) is not subject to price regulation. The existence of all three conditions will be demonstrated
by a company's ability to regularly price its product or service aggressively and thereby to earn high rates of
return on capital."* Its companion, same letter **[E3-43]**: *"In contrast, "a business" earns exceptional profits
only if it is the low-cost operator or if supply of its product or service is tight. Tightness in supply usually
does not last long."* **The commodity end has its own row, [E2-58] (1982 letter)**: *"For the great majority of
companies selling "commodity"products, a depressing equation of business economics prevails: persistent
over-capacity without administered prices (or costs) equals poor profitability."* Its one exception is *"a cost
advantage that is both wide and sustainable"*, and *"What finally determines levels of long-term profitability in
such industries is the ratio of supply-tight to supply-ample years."*

- Needed or desired **[x]** (fruit, vegetables and canned food) · **no close substitute [ ] — refuted, below** · not
  price-regulated **[x]**, with a caveat: *"In Ecuador and Costa Rica, there are minimum export prices for the sale
  of bananas, which are established and reviewed on a periodic basis by the respective governments"* (10-K FY2025).
  That administers the grower's floor, a cost to this company, not its selling price **[E2-59]**.

### THE REGISTRANT'S OWN WORDS ABOUT SUBSTITUTES, ENTRY AND PRICE
All from the **10-K FY2025, `0001047340-26-000015`**, unless dated otherwise.
- **The whole industry**: *"The global fresh produce industry is a highly competitive business, and the effect of
  competition is intensified because of the perishable nature of the products. We compete based on a variety of
  factors, including price, overall product quality, brand recognition and customer loyalty, reliability and
  consistency ... Our sales are also affected by the availability of seasonal and alternative produce."* The last
  clause is criterion (2) failed in the filer's own words: the substitute for a banana in a glut month is another
  fruit.
- **Bananas (34% of 2025 sales)**: *"Unlike the pineapple market, there are few barriers to entry into the banana
  market. Supplies of bananas can be increased relatively quickly due to bananas having a short growing cycle and
  the limited capital investment required for banana growing. As a result of changes in supply and demand, plus
  seasonal factors, banana prices fluctuate significantly."* And: *"At times, particularly when demand is greater
  than supply, we also face competition from a large number of relatively small banana producers."* This is
  [E2-58]'s equation stated by the producer: supply that expands on price, and profitability set by the supply-tight
  years [E3-43].
- **Pineapples (16% of 2025 sales), the category the filer defends**: *"the high degree of capital investment and
  cultivation expertise required, as well as the longer length of the growing cycle, makes it relatively difficult
  to enter the market. Our primary competitors in this category are large multinational producers, as well as
  smaller exporters and importers. Our profitability has depended significantly on the sale of our Del Monte Gold®
  Extra Sweet pineapples."* And, in the same 10-K: *"The production and sale of premium pineapples in the market
  has also led to increased competition."* The FY2016 10-K (`0001047340-17-000020`) said it plainer: *"Increased
  competition in the production and sale of Del Monte Gold ® Extra Sweet pineapples could adversely affect our
  results. We expect these competitive pressures to continue in 2017."* A variety the company launched in 1996
  (*"From 1996 to 2016, our volume of the Del Monte Gold® Extra Sweet pineapple increased from 2.5 million boxes to
  27.1 million boxes"*, FY2016 10-K) is now grown by rivals; the company answers with new proprietary varieties
  (Pinkglow 2020, Honeyglow relaunched 2021, Rubyglow 2024).
- **Fresh-cut**: *"The fresh-cut produce market is highly fragmented, and we compete with a wide variety of local and
  regional distributors of branded and unbranded fresh-cut produce"*.
- **Prepared foods, now including the Del Monte Foods purchase**: *"The prepared food products market is mature and
  characterized by high levels of competition and consumer awareness. Consumer choices are driven by price and/or
  quality. Large retailers with their "buyers own label" ("BOL") products appeal to price-conscious consumers"*, and
  *"the strength and sophistication of the large retailers there, account in part for the increasing presence of
  BOL products in many food and beverage categories"*. This is **[E4-65]**'s battle between brands and retailers,
  *"because the retailer would like his name to be the brand"*, and **[E5-57]**'s Kirkland. **The strongest single
  fact about the brand's power in US canned food is that its owner of four decades sold that business out of
  Chapter 11**: a *"competitive bankruptcy auction process under Section 363"* (8-K 2026-02-12), and this
  management's own description of what it bought: *"we stabilized a business facing significant financial and
  operational challenges"* (Q2 2026 release).
- **Pricing, in the filer's words [E4-37]**: *"We may not be able to pass along any resulting price increases to our
  consumers to help offset elevated costs"*; *"Even if we are able to pass along costs, increased product prices may
  result in reductions in sales volume if consumers are less willing to pay a price differential for our branded
  products and instead elect to purchase lower-priced offerings or forgo some purchases altogether"* (Item 1A); and
  *"we have not established further inflation-justified price increases and surcharges during 2024 and 2025"*
  (MD&A). The 2025 tariff was passed on (*"which we were able to mostly mitigate through increased selling prices"*),
  and banana gross margin still fell from 5.9% to 4.8%.

### THE OTHER Q2 TESTS
- **[E2-44], the two characteristics**: (1) raising prices *"even when product demand is flat and capacity is not
  fully utilized"* fails on the filer's words above; (2) dollar volume growth *"with only minor additional investment
  of capital"* fails: capital expenditures ran **$1,884M across 2008-2025** (companyfacts, sum of the eighteen annual
  lines in `series_DMC.txt`) against a sales line that rose $791M nominal, part of it bought.
- **[E2-45], the attacker's test**: for bananas the registrant answers it: *"few barriers to entry"*, *"the limited
  capital investment required"*. For pineapples the barrier is time and capital, and the filer reports competitors
  crossing it.
- **[E2-53], the dominance class**: no position here sets its own economics. Walmart is about 7% of sales and the top
  ten customers 29%; the product sits in supermarkets beside Dole, Chiquita, private-label and local fruit.
- **[E3-46], the second question is a number**: 4.6% on equity over sixteen years.
- **[E4-04] is not the ground and no perimeter close is taken.** This business does not change quickly; it changes
  with the weather and the supply cycle. Nothing about its durability is beyond judgment from the filings; what they
  show is a durable commodity business, a finding on the business under [E3-03] and [E2-58], not a competence limit.
- **Key-person dependence [E4-23]**: not needed for the verdict; recorded beneath the close (*"Since 1996, Mr.
  Abu-Ghazaleh has served as our Chairman and Chief Executive Officer"*, DEF 14A 2026, age 84; his family owns about
  30%).

### THE STRONGEST EVIDENCE AGAINST THIS VERDICT, stated as its holders would state it [E4-51, E3-47, E4-26]
1. **"The pineapple business is a real moat, and it is the larger half."** Fresh and value-added gross margin rose
   from 6.8% to 11.4% in two years on *"higher per unit selling prices of pineapple due to a favorable sales mix of
   our premium pineapple varieties"*; 81% of pineapples come from the company's own farms; the filer calls entry
   *"relatively difficult"*; it holds a patented variety (Rubyglow, *"patented as Vintage Ruby™"*). A holder would
   say the company is the largest pineapple marketer in the United States and is re-running the 1996 Del Monte Gold
   play with Pinkglow and Rubyglow.
2. **"The brand is whole again."** For the first time in about four decades one company owns the Del Monte® name
   across fresh and packaged food, bought out of bankruptcy for $285M against net assets fair-valued at $341.9M.
3. **"The cold chain is a scale advantage."** Owned ships, ports and ripening centers lower the per-box logistics
   cost and let the company *"manage the timing of our sales to optimize our margins"*.
4. **"2024-2025 is the new level."** Operating income $196.3M and $137.4M after impairments; operating cash $182.5M
   and $245.1M.

**Answered in the run, each against the filing:**
1. The pineapple advantage is a lead that must be **re-won with each variety**: the filer's own record is that the
   1996 variety drew competitors into *"the production and sale of Del Monte Gold ® Extra Sweet pineapples"*, and it
   says the same of premium pineapples today. That is a maintained lead, not a moat that changes the class, and **it
   is earned inside a segment making 11% gross before SG&A**. The two-year rise sits against an eighteen-year record
   whose best consolidated operating margin is 6.1%.
2. A brand whose largest US packaged category went through Chapter 11 is not evidence of pricing power there; the
   filer's own description of prepared foods is price and private label [E4-65, E5-57].
3. The cold chain is real and is shared: Dole runs the same structure and earns within about a point of DMC. A scale
   advantage that both scale players hold is not a relative advantage [E3-28].
4. 2016 was better than either year (6.1% operating margin, 12.9% on equity) and 2018-2021 followed at 0.9-2.6%. The
   corpus's instruction for a favourable break is to normalise it down **[E4-41]**, and [E2-58]'s *"nothing fails like
   success"* names what follows it in this class.
