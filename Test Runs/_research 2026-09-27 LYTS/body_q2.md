## Q2 — IS IT A FRANCHISE? **[E3-03]**

*Every LSI figure below is read from the filed 10-Ks for FY2001-FY2026 (accessions in Step 0) or from SEC companyfacts checked against those statements. The thirty-year sales and operating-income record is `record.py` / `record_out.txt` (FY1997-FY2009 from the selected-data tables of the FY2001, FY2004, FY2008 and FY2013 10-Ks; FY2010-FY2026 from companyfacts, FY2024-FY2026 equal to the filed segment tables). Peer figures are from each peer's own SEC companyfacts, fetched fresh for this run, through `peers/row.py`; outputs `peers/<TICKER>_row.txt`, in `Test Runs/_research 2026-09-27 LYTS/`. The AYI run of 2026-09-26 used LSI as a peer; that file was read to know what it said, and nothing below is copied from it.*

**The hypotheses I formed while reading, both stated so they can be attacked [E4-26].** *(a) LSI is a business that has found its footing: since FY2022 it has earned 4.7-7.6% operating margins and 18.7-31.5% pre-tax on net tangible operating assets, its Lighting segment now earns 11.6-12.7% (close to the industry leader), and in Display Solutions it has become the chain owner's single supplier for multi-site re-imaging, a relationship with switching costs that the competitor row cannot see.* *(b) LSI is a quoted-price manufacturer in two competitive trades: the filer says in every recent 10-K that it competes on price, its thirty-year record shows a decade of 7-12% margins followed by fifteen years of 0-6% with four loss years, its sales did not grow for twenty-one years except by purchase, and the recent improvement coincides with purchases bought on debt and new shares.* Each is tested on the filings below.

**[E3-03], criterion by criterion.** *"An economic franchise arises from a product or service that: (1) is needed or desired; (2) is thought by its customers to have no close substitute and; (3) is not subject to price regulation."*
- **(1) Needed or desired [x]: passes.** Chains relight and re-image their sites; FY2026 net sales $689.4M, Display Solutions organic growth *"of 17% driven by increased sales across all product categories and vertical markets supported mostly by the grocery and refueling/ C-Store verticals"* (10-K FY2026, MD&A).
- **(3) Not subject to price regulation [x]: passes.** No product is priced by a government or regulator in any filing read; the regulation named is environmental and trade (tariffs raise LSI's costs, they do not set its prices).
- **(2) Thought by its customers to have no close substitute: FAILS, in the filer's own words, across the whole record read.**
  - FY2026 10-K, Item 1A: *"Our businesses operate in markets that are highly competitive, and we compete on the basis of price, quality, service and/or brand name across the industries and markets served. Some of our competitors for certain products, primarily in the Lighting Segment, have greater sales, assets, and financial resources."* and *"Aggressive pricing actions of our competitors could affect prices we charge our customers or demand for our products, which could adversely affect our operating results. Additionally, customers for our products may attempt to reduce the number of vendors from which they purchase"*; Item 1: *"We experience competition in both segments and in all markets we serve based on numerous factors, including price, brand name recognition, product quality, product design, prompt delivery"*.
  - FY2012 10-K, MD&A: *"In response to rising material prices, the Company's Lighting Segment announced price increases ranging from 5% to 7%, depending on the product, effective with September 2011 orders. While competitors of the Company's lighting business have announced similar price increases, the lighting market remains very price competitive."*
  - FY2019 10-K, MD&A: *"In fiscal 2019, the Company announced price increases for all lighting and pole products impacted by tariffs along with the raw material price inflation of certain commodities. The price increases with customers did not fully offset the increases in cost in certain products."*
  - The pass-through is stated as a hope, not a power, in every 10-K from FY2022 to FY2026: *"Although the Company attempts to pass along increased costs in the form of price increases to its customers, the Company may be unsuccessful in doing so for competitive reasons."* And the cost is shared with the rivals: *"the price risk for materials the Company purchases is related to price increases in commodity items that affect all users of the materials, including the Company's competitors."*
  - **Display Solutions is priced per programme, at its start, from cost**: *"The Company's Display Solutions Segment generally establishes new sales prices, reflective of the then current raw material prices, for each program as it begins with further price increases throughout the life of the program when warranted."* The customer's substitute is the next supplier at the next programme; the filer names the customer's leverage (*"customers ... may attempt to reduce the number of vendors"*) and the risk that it walks mid-programme (*"to canceling the program before its completion and before the inventory is sold to the customer"*).
  - **No patent or input is scarce**: *"no single patent or patent application is individually material to us"*; *"alternative sources of supply exist and could be readily arranged"*; research and development $3.1M (0.45% of FY2026 sales). Lighting reaches the customer through independent agents that rivals compete for: *"these sales representative changes can be widespread as a result of the competitive nature of the lighting industry as LSI and its competition vie for the strongest sales agency in a particular region."*

**The remaining Q2 tests.**
- **[E3-43]'s classification.** *"In contrast, “a business” earns exceptional profits only if it is the low-cost operator or if supply of its product or service is tight. Tightness in supply usually does not last long."* LSI is not the low-cost operator on the filed evidence (the leader's margin is two to three times its own), and its best years (FY1997-FY2001, 10-12%; FY2023-FY2024, 7.5-7.6%) are the years of tight or rising demand. *"And a business, unlike a franchise, can be killed by poor management."*
- **[E2-58], the commodity equation, read against LSI's own words.** *"In many industries, differentiation simply can’t be made meaningful. A few producers in such industries may consistently do well if they have a cost advantage that is both wide and sustainable."* LSI's products are differentiated in design and service (custom programmes, one supplier for lighting and display), and the filer claims that differentiation; the record answers whether customers pay for it: fifteen years of 0-6% margins and pass-through described as *"may be unsuccessful ... for competitive reasons"*. Not classed as a pure commodity; classed on [E3-43]'s side of the line.
- **[E4-04], the durability question.** Not the limb this verdict rests on. The filer does ask for continuous rebuilding (*"To remain competitive, we will need to invest continuously in research and development, manufacturing, marketing, and customer service and support. We may not have sufficient resources to continue to make such investments, and we may be unable to maintain our competitive position."*), and the company has twice changed what it is by purchase (Atlas in lighting, then JSI, EMI, CBH and Royston in display), but the perimeter close applies only to *"A name that passes [E3-03] and whose durability cannot be judged from filings"*, which this name does not.
- **[E4-23], key-person dependence.** No dependence on one named manager was found in the filings read; recorded as no finding. What the record does show is that results track management's purchase decisions: the FY2018-FY2019 losses are the write-off of Atlas within two years of buying it, and the FY2022-FY2026 growth is four purchases. [E3-43]: *"a business, unlike a franchise, can be killed by poor management."*
- **Untapped pricing power [E3-33] / [E5-28]: no.** *"If you name some business that has incredible pricing power, you’re talking about a business that’s a monopoly or a near monopoly"*; the row places LSI below the leader in every year and the filer describes a price-competitive trade.
- **[E4-37], the agony metric: present.** Price increases are announced to offset costs, matched by competitors, and described as lagging (*"Even when price increases are successful, the timing of such price increases may lag behind the incurrence of higher costs."*).
- **[E2-53], dominance.** Not claimed; no customer or programme is 10% of sales, and the filer claims a leading position only in words (*"a leading producer of non-residential lighting and retail display solutions"*).
- **[E2-45], the attacker's test.** An attacker with capital and people could buy what LSI bought: LSI itself entered display fixtures, food-display cases and Canadian store fixtures in five years by purchasing four private companies. The attack is shown to be available by the defender's own record.
- **[E3-46], the second question is a number.** Pre-tax return on capital including what was paid: a sixteen-year mean of 5.7%, 12.6-16.2% in FY2023-FY2025, below Acuity in every year.
- **[E4-32], direction: improving on margin since FY2020, and changing on composition.** Operating margin from 0.1% (FY2019 before impairment) to 7.5-7.6% (FY2023-FY2024) and 5.6% (FY2026); the business being measured is not the same business (Display from 32% to 62% of sales by purchase).
- **[E4-36], which cause of success?** None of the four is shown on the record: no extreme on one variable (the leader is lower-cost), no lollapalooza, no extreme over many factors, and no wave ridden for long (the LED transition was a wave the whole industry rode, and LSI's margins in it, FY2010-FY2019, were 0-7%).

**THE STRONGEST EVIDENCE AGAINST THE VERDICT BELOW, hunted hardest [E4-26, E3-41].** The verdict below is OUT, so the case for IN is set out as its holders would state it:
1. **The last five years are a different company**: operating margins of 4.7-7.6% and pre-tax returns on tangible capital of 19.3-31.5% (FY2022-FY2025), with the Lighting segment at 11.6-12.7% in FY2023-FY2026, near the industry leader's consolidated margin.
2. **Display Solutions is a relationship business with switching costs**: the filer's customers *"are usually established and have a long-term relationship with LSI"*, the programmes run *"over a period of time"* across *"hundreds to thousands of individual customer sites"*, prices rise *"throughout the life of the program when warranted"*, and *"Increasingly, we have become the primary supplier of exterior and interior visual image and display elements for our customers."*. A chain mid-roll-out does not change supplier easily; the competitor row cannot see this.
3. **Organic growth is real now**: Display Solutions organic growth of 17% in FY2026 and 18% in its fourth quarter, the filer's own non-GAAP figure.
4. **The thirty-year record includes an industry-wide technology change** (LED) that punished every fixture maker, so the FY2008-FY2021 margins may be the transition, not the steady state.

**Answered.** (1) is accepted as fact and is the strongest point; it is five years against twenty-five, the five include the post-2021 inflation in which the filer itself says pass-through was partial (*"selling price increase realization offset the majority of rapid cost increases"*, FY2022), and on capital including what was paid the returns were 9.4-16.2%, below Acuity in every year. [E3-43] classes exactly this: a business whose profits are exceptional *"only if it is the low-cost operator or if supply of its product or service is tight"*. (2) is weighed, not waved away: the switching cost exists within a programme, and the filer prices each programme at its start from cost and names the customer's power to cancel and to cut vendors; a relationship that must be re-won at each programme on price is not a product *"thought by its customers to have no close substitute"*. The row cannot see conduct, which [E3-61] states, and that limit is recorded; the verdict rests on the filer's words, which can. (3) is volume, not price, and it is a non-GAAP figure; [E4-55]'s units are not filed. (4) is partly right: the LED transition hurt the whole industry, but Acuity earned 9.3-15.0% operating margins through the same years while LSI earned 0-7%, so the transition explains the industry's pressure and not LSI's position within it. **[E3-47]** (a wrongly closed file inside the circle of competence is the costliest error) was weighed against OUT in writing; the reversal conditions below name the filed evidence that would reopen the file.
