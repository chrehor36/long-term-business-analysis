## Q1 — CAN I UNDERSTAND HOW THIS MAKES MONEY? **[E3-31]**
- **Unit economics in my own words, no management language.** LSI makes two kinds of physical goods in its own plants (*"twenty three manufacturing facilities located within North America"*) and sells them mostly to the owners of chains of small commercial sites. **Lighting (38% of FY2026 sales)**: LED fixtures, poles and controls for petrol-station canopies, car parks, restaurants, shops, car dealers, sports fields and warehouses, sold on quoted projects through commissioned sales agents and electrical distributors, or as standard stock products through distributors. **Display Solutions (62%)**: the custom physical fittings of a store's brand and layout, printed and structural signage, canopy and pump graphics, digital menu boards, store fixtures, checkout counters, millwork and (since EMI, CBH and Royston) refrigerated and heated food display cases, delivered as multi-site re-imaging programmes for petrol-station, convenience-store, grocery and restaurant chains, with the installation and project management. The money is the gap between a price quoted per project or per programme and the cost of steel, aluminium, LEDs, drivers, substrates, cases and labour: gross margin **21.9% (FY2010) to 28.4% (FY2024), 25.2% in FY2026** (companyfacts `GrossProfit` over sales), and operating margin **−6.3% to 7.6% over FY2008-FY2026** after selling and administrative costs of about a fifth of sales. The Display prices are set at the start of each programme: *"The Company's Display Solutions Segment generally establishes new sales prices, reflective of the then current raw material prices, for each program as it begins with further price increases throughout the life of the program when warranted."* Demand follows the customers' building and re-imaging budgets: *"Sales to the refueling and convenience store market are dependent upon the general conditions prevailing in and the profitability of the Petroleum industry"*, and *"The construction market is cyclical in nature"*.
- **The scarce input this business controls.** Little that is scarce in the legal or physical sense. The filer: *"While patents and patent applications in the aggregate are important to our competitive position, no single patent or patent application is individually material to us"*; *"we believe generally that alternative sources of supply exist and could be readily arranged"*; research and development of **$3.1M in FY2026** (0.45% of sales). What LSI controls is **the relationship and the execution record with chain owners** (*"These customers are usually established and have a long-term relationship with LSI"*; *"Our professional services group manages and executes the implementation of large rollout programs, which can include hundreds to thousands of individual customer sites"*) and **a combined offer across lighting and display** (*"providing value for the customer by working with one solutions partner to manage their regional and national location programs, versus multiple suppliers"*). Whether either is a franchise in [E3-03]'s sense is Q2's question.
- **Will the fundamentals look broadly the same in ten years?** **Yes, in mechanism**: chains will still re-image, relight and refit their sites, and LSI will still quote and build for them. The product within Lighting changed once already, completely, in the record read here (from metal-halide and fluorescent fixtures to LED, visible in the FY2016 10-K's *"a 6% to 8% price increase for all non-LED lighting and pole products"* and the FY2013 10-K's technology language), and the mix of the company has changed by purchase (Display from 32% to 62% of sales in six years). The customers' verticals are named by the filer as a risk: the petrol-station business depends on the oil industry, and grocery is *"characterized by the fragmentation of local, regional, and national retailers, including both retail and digital formats, market consolidation, intense competition"*.
- **Relatively simple and stable in character [E3-31]?** The business is simple: quote, build to order, ship, install. I can state it in five minutes **[E4-46]**, and there is no technology, regulatory or scientific judgment in it beyond the LED transition already past. It is not stable in its results (operating losses in four of the last nineteen years, FY2008, FY2009, FY2018, FY2019), but [E3-31] asks whether I understand the character, and the instability is an economic finding that belongs at Q2 and Q4, not a gap in understanding.
