## Q1 — CAN I UNDERSTAND HOW THIS MAKES MONEY? **[E3-31]**
- Unit economics in my own words, no management language: Applied buys bearings, belts, motors, gearboxes, hydraulic and pneumatic parts, pumps, valves, hose and automation components from the companies that make them, keeps them on shelves in about 580 buildings near factories, and resells them, mostly one repair at a time, to the maintenance departments of those factories; a growing share (36% of FY2026 sales, the Engineered Solutions segment) is assembled into hydraulic, flow-control or automation systems before sale. On each $100 of sales in FY2026 it paid **$69.70** for the goods, spent **$19.30** on branches, people, trucks and systems, and kept **$11.10** before interest and tax (FY2026: sales $4,966.7M, gross profit $1,506.8M, operating income $549.5M). The capital is working capital, not plant: receivables $831.2M and inventory $509.0M less payables $341.1M, about **$1.0bn, a fifth of a year's sales**, against net property of only **$131.7M**; capital expenditures run $16-27M a year. The growth capital has been **purchase of other distributors**: $2,150.9M of acquisition cash in FY1998-FY2026 against $482.3M of capital expenditures, leaving $704.7M of goodwill and $312.8M of intangibles on a $3,010.0M balance sheet. **Profit is (a markup on other companies' products x the volume of repair demand in industrial North America) minus a branch-and-people cost that is largely fixed in the short run**, so earnings swing with industrial production: operating margin 7.3% (FY2008) to 5.7% in FY2009 before that year's $36.6M goodwill impairment (3.8% after it).
- The scarce input this business controls: **local stock, technically trained counter and field staff, and the manufacturers' authorizations to sell their lines in a territory**. The filer says the authorizations are not exclusive and not permanent: *"Products we distribute are generally supplied to us by manufacturers whom we serve as a non-exclusive distributor"* and *"Many of our relationships with key product suppliers are longstanding, but are terminable by either party."* Whether any of these inputs is scarce against the other distributors is Q2's question, not Q1's.
- Will the fundamentals look broadly the same in ten years? **Yes.** Factories will still need replacement bearings and hydraulic repairs delivered the same day; the business has done this since 1923 and the 10-Ks of FY2000 and FY2026 describe the same trade. The mix will keep moving toward engineered fluid power and automation (the Fluid Power Businesses segment 18% of sales in FY2017; Engineered Solutions, a wider segment defined later, 36% in FY2026), and the filer says growth will keep coming by purchase: *"Many industries we serve are mature. As a result, acquisitions have been, and will continue to be, important to our growth."*
- **Simple and stable in character [E3-31]; a decision that can be made on the filings [E4-46].** Nothing in the business requires a forecast of technology to understand.

### THE STRONGEST EVIDENCE AGAINST THIS VERDICT, stated as its holders would state it [E4-51, E4-26, E3-47]
