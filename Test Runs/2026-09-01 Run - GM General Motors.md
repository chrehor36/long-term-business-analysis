## (d) LEASED-VEHICLE DEPRECIATION — THE TREATMENT, DISCLOSED

The operator named this as the specific trap. It is, and here is exactly how it operates and
exactly what was done about it.

**The mechanism, from the filed statements.** The consolidated cash flow statement adds back
**"Depreciation and impairment of Equipment on operating leases, net $4,942"** inside
operating activities. The cash that bought those vehicles is **"Purchases of leased vehicles
$(15,793)"** and the cash returned is **"Proceeds from termination of leased vehicles
$10,095"**, both in **investing**. The screen's (c) is *expenditures for property*, which is
**$9,303M and contains none of it.** So the screen adds back $4,942M of depreciation and
charges nothing whatever against it.

**Net leased-vehicle capital consumption (purchases less termination proceeds), filed:**

| FY | 2020 | 2021 | 2022 | 2023 | 2024 | 2025 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| net leased-vehicle capital, $M | 1,834 | 209 | **−2,285** | 607 | 4,387 | **5,698** |
| lease depreciation added back, $M | — | — | — | 4,904 | 4,844 | 4,942 |

**Note that the sign flips.** When the lease book shrinks (2022) it releases cash and the
depreciation add-back overstates earnings by the full charge; when it grows (2024-25) the
cash drain exceeds the depreciation. **This is why a single year is worthless here and why
[E2-23] constraint 1 requires "the average annual amount."**

**THE TREATMENT ADOPTED, AND IT IS DISCLOSED AS A JUDGMENT:** **the primary construction
removes both the lease depreciation and the lease capital by excluding GM Financial
entirely**, using GM's own filed automotive cash flow. Leased-vehicle depreciation is a real
economic consumption of assets GM owns, and it is properly matched against leasing revenue,
which is **46% of GM Financial's revenue**; both belong to the finance company or neither
does, and taking one without the other is the error. **The secondary construction keeps the
consolidated numerator and charges net leased-vehicle capital into (c)**, giving $8,175M.
The two agree. **Neither construction uses the screen's version, in which the depreciation
is added back and nothing is charged.**

**One residual disclosure, found and reported because it cuts against tidiness.** GM
Financial's own statement shows **$5,314M** of D&A; GM's segment note shows **$4,976M**; the
consolidated cash flow shows **$4,942M** of lease depreciation. The consolidated figure is
**lower** because GM's manufacturing profit on vehicles sold into GM Financial's lease book
is eliminated on consolidation, reducing the depreciable basis. **The consolidated statement
therefore charges roughly $372M a year LESS lease depreciation than the finance company's
own audited statement does.** It is not material to the verdict and it is stated rather than
smoothed, per PRIME RULE 1.

## (e) THE TEST THE CASH-FLOW TEST MISSED — AND HERE THE HYPOTHESIS IS VINDICATED

file.*

The operator's hypothesis was that GM's headline yield is "financing cash flow wearing an
industrial's clothes." **On the cash-flow statement that is not what the evidence shows, as
section (c) reports.** But the hypothesis is not wrong; **it was aimed one statement too

| | 2023 | 2024 | **2025** |

**In 2025 the bank earned $2,802M of pre-tax income and everything else GM owns, the
trucks, the SUVs, Cadillac, GMI, China and Corporate combined, earned $315M.** GM
Financial's pre-tax income has been remarkably flat across all three years ($2,985M,
$2,645M, $2,802M) while the automotive side collapsed from $7,418M to $315M. **The
consolidated result did not hold up in 2025 because the automaker held up. It held up
because the lending book did.**

### THE COMMODITY DOCTRINE APPLIES, AND IT APPLIES ON THE FILING'S OWN TERMS **[E2-58]**

The framework requires the commodity doctrine be applied or the exemption explained. **It
applies, and GM's own risk factor states all three of its elements.** [E2-58]:

> "For the great majority of companies selling 'commodity' products, a depressing equation
> of business economics prevails: **persistent over-capacity without administered prices (or
> costs) equals poor profitability.** ... What finally determines levels of long-term
> profitability in such industries is **the ratio of supply-tight to supply-ample years.**"

- **Persistent over-capacity:** GM's own words, "an industry that has historically had
  excess manufacturing capacity."
- **Without administered prices:** no regulation floors vehicle prices. **[E2-59]'s rescue
  is absent, and worse, it runs backwards here.** [E2-59] permits escape where "prices or

  wrong side.
- **The ratio of supply-tight to supply-ample years:** **this is the entire owner-earnings

  sustainable** ... By definition such exceptions are few." **GM does not claim one and the
