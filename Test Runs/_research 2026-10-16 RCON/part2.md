## Q2 — WHY IS THE CASTLE STILL STANDING, and what keeps it standing ten to twenty years? STOP.
The question: "why is that castle still standing? And what’s going to keep it standing or cause it not to be standing five, 10, 20 years from now." **[M1995-038]**. A moat is what "protects excellent returns on invested capital" **[L2007-004]**.

**The castle tests, each with its filing fact.**
- **The castle questions** **[M1995-038]**. Why does CNPC buy from RCON? The filer's own answer is nationality and geography: Chinese state companies "would prefer to hire a Chinese company to assist in their business operations if a Chinese company exists with the ability to fulfill their needs on a timely and cost-efficient basis" (Item 4, strengths), and "we have a geographical advantage" in some oilfields (Item 5, growth strategy). Neither is a reason the customer must come to this company rather than to the five domestic rivals, who are Chinese as well.
- **The attacker with money** **[M2011-015]**, **[M1997-103]**. The attackers are already in the field and larger: "several international companies [...] have significantly greater financial and marketing resources and name recognition than we have. In addition, at least five domestic private competitors also compete with us, and more competitors may enter the market" (Item 3.D); "our competitors hold a substantial market share" (Item 4, Competition); "Our company is much smaller than our main foreign competitors, including Schneider-electric, Siemens, Honeywell International, Emerson Process Management and Rockwell Automation" (Item 3.D). The answer to the See's question is yes: a funded rival can take this business, and rivals hold most of it already.
- **Pricing power and the agony before a rise** **[M2005-020]**. No price rise is recorded in any filing read; the filer lists "competition and related pricing pressure from other oilfield service solution providers" among the conditions that drive its results (Item 5). Gross margin has ranged from 4.9% to 33.2% in twelve years, and the automation segment's gross margin was 6.6%, 11.3%, 11.1% and 16.1% in FY2022 to FY2025 (segment notes) before the overseas projects of FY2026; "Excluding the overseas projects, our domestic automation gross margin remained relatively stable" (Item 5).
- **The low-cost position** **[M1997-010]**. Nothing in the filings shows RCON as the low-cost producer, and the cost structure shows the opposite: operating expenses exceeded gross profit in every one of the twelve years; general and administrative expense alone was 50.5% of revenue in FY2026 and 74.9% in FY2025 (Item 5). "commodity businesses have risk unless you’re the low-cost producer, because the low-cost producer can put you out of business." **[M1997-010]**
- **Would the customer still choose it over the low bid?** **[M2017-009]**. The work is tendered: "As a standard practice in the petroleum extraction industry, the Company generally must submit a bid in order to secure the sales contract" (Item 5), and CNPC's agreements are "each of which is terminable without notice" (Item 3.D). A buyer that tenders each contract and can leave without notice is the customer who buys "for the low bid" **[M2017-009]**; "most insureds don't care from whom they buy." **[L2004-003]** is the same position in another trade.
- **Unit volume and share of mind.** Revenue was ¥51.5M in FY2015 and ¥66.3M in FY2025 before the overseas year; Sinopec's share fell below 10% from FY2017 (Item 3.D); the gas-station platform segment's revenue fell from ¥9.3M in FY2022 to nil in FY2026, with "higher severance compensation for FGS employees" (Item 5).
- **Ask the competitors.** Not on the public record for the domestic rivals, who are unnamed. The named foreign rivals' own filings are the competitor row below.
- **Widening or narrowing** **[M1999-108]**. The one improvement is FY2026: automation revenue doubled to ¥69.8M, with "higher-margin overseas oilfield projects, which contributed ¥44.2 million of revenue" (Item 5). One year of a new market is not a full cycle; a change for the better "is credited only on the evidence of a full cycle" (Q2, Change, the convention of 2026-10-06 as amended 2026-10-10), and the overseas work is itself won from the same oil companies' projects. Concentration widened instead: CNPC rose from 44% to 65% of revenue.
- **What could destroy it** **[M2000-014]**. "any termination of our business relationships with CNPC would materially harm our operations" (Item 3.D), on agreements terminable without notice; and "one competitor is frequently enough to ruin a business." **[M2012-108]**

No part carries more than half of five-year gross profit, so the largest decides. The company's operating loss over the five years was ¥321.8M against ¥110.9M of gross profit for all parts together, so no part's castle protected enough to cover the costs of running the company.

**The field's returns over the last full cycle.** The twelve years span the oil-price fall of 2015 and 2016, the collapse of 2020 and the high prices of 2022 and 2023, by the filer's own descriptions of its customers' spending; in none of them did RCON earn an operating profit, and the three named rivals that file earned net margins between 8% and 24% in at least ten of their eleven years each. The returns are not merely ordinary; for this company they are losses through the whole cycle, while the business's own rivals hold the market share and the resources. "you can have only two competitors and they’re still terrible businesses" **[M2013-052]**; here there are many.

- **VERDICT: OUT.** A castle shown open on the evidence: a sub-scale, bid-won supplier whose principal customer can end its agreements without notice, whose larger rivals hold "a substantial market share", and whose deciding part, with the rest of the company, has not earned an operating profit in any of twelve years spanning a full cycle **[M2011-015]**, **[L2007-004]**, **[M1997-010]**, **[M2017-009]**, **[L2004-003]**. This is not the tenuous moat whose value cannot be known **[M2000-019]**; the returns are known, and they are losses. The file closes here; Q3 to Q12 are NOT REACHED as clearances.
