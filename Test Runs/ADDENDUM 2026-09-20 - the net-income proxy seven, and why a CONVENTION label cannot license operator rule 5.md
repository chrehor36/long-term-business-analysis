## THE CORPUS RULES ON THIS DIRECTLY. IT IS NOT A FRAMEWORK PREFERENCE.

The operator's instruction of 2026-09-20: *"The corpus is the law. All answers are in the
corpus."* So the general rule below is not argued from the framework's convenience. It is
quoted.

**First, reported earnings is (a). It is the input to owner earnings, not the output.**

> "we can gain some insights about what may be called "owner earnings." These represent (a)
> reported earnings plus (b) depreciation, depletion, amortization, and certain other non-cash
> charges such as Company N's items (1) and (4) less (c) the average annual amount of
> capitalized expenditures for plant and equipment, etc. that the business requires to fully
> maintain its long-term competitive position and its unit volume. (If the business requires
> additional working capital to maintain its competitive position and unit volume, the
> increment also should be included in (c). However, businesses following the LIFO inventory
> method usually do not require additional working capital if unit volume does not change.)"
> **[E2-08]**, 1986 Letter (appendix)

*(Citation note: this 1986 appendix passage is carried in the ledger under **two** ids,
**[E2-08]** and **[E2-23]**, and `THE FRAMEWORK v4.md` quotes it as **[E2-23]**. Both are cited
here so this addendum survives the duplicate-id consolidation in progress on 2026-09-20. The
text is identical; **[E2-23]** carries the fuller TEXT NOTE, including that the source .txt
splits the literal "(c)" across four lines, an extraction artifact reassembled and not
smoothed.)*

**Second, the corpus names the GAAP figure and rejects it for this exact purpose.**

> "Our owner-earnings equation does not yield the deceptively precise figures provided by GAAP,
> since (c) must be a guess - and one sometimes very difficult to make. Despite this problem,
> we consider the owner earnings figure, not the GAAP figure, to be the relevant item for
> valuation purposes [...] We agree with Keynes's observation: "I would rather be vaguely
> right than precisely wrong.""
> **[E2-09]**, 1986 Letter (appendix)

Net income is the GAAP figure. **[E2-09]** is the sentence that decides it: the two are named
in one breath and one of them is chosen against.

**Third, reported earnings can be ersatz, and the corpus says so in its own word.**

> "The first point to understand is that all earnings are not created equal. In many businesses
> particularly those that have high asset/profit ratios - inflation causes some or all of the
> reported earnings to become ersatz. The ersatz portion - let�s call these earnings
> �restricted� - cannot, if the business is to retain its economic position, be distributed as
> dividends."
> **[E2-60]**, 1984 Letter

*(The three `�` are in the ledger row as stored: the 1984 letter's curly apostrophe and curly
quotation marks did not survive extraction. Carried as found, flagged, not smoothed, per PRIME
RULE 1.)*

**Fourth, and this is the part the seven files got exactly backwards: the corpus anticipated
the missing figure and answered it.** The stated reason in all seven is that a clean
maintenance-capex disclosure was not obtainable. **[E2-09]** says (c) *"must be a guess."* The
instruction where (c) cannot be looked up is therefore to **guess it and disclose the guess**,
which is what v4 means by calling (c) a disclosed judgment. **Setting (c) to zero is not a
guess. It is an omission wearing the word convention.** And the corpus supplies the default
guess, so the figure was never actually unavailable:

> "But by and large, the depreciation charge is not inappropriate in most companies to use as a
> proxy for required capital expenditures. Which is why we think that reported earnings plus
> amortization of intangibles usually gives a pretty good indication of earning power, [...]"
> **[E3-44]**, 1998 Annual Meeting

*(Elision marked: the same answer continues into the exception class, "an airline business is a
good case", which is why this default is a default and not a licence.)*

> "At 95% of American businesses, capital expenditures that over time roughly approximate
> depreciation are a necessity and are every bit as real an expense as labor or utility costs."
> **[E2-41]**, 1989 Letter

**Fifth, the two reasons MTSUY gave for abandoning the formula are the two items the corpus
requires you to put INTO it.**

- MTSUY: *"trading-related working capital."* **[E2-08]**: *"If the business requires
  additional working capital to maintain its competitive position and unit volume, the
  increment also should be included in (c)."* The confession names the very term it drops.
- MTSUY: *"equity-method income in associated companies."* The corpus has a construction for
  exactly that, and computes it in eleven consecutive letters:

> "look-through earnings, which consist of: (1) the operating earnings reported in the previous
> section, plus; (2) the retained operating earnings of major investees that, under GAAP
> accounting, are not reflected in our profits, less; (3) an allowance for the tax that would
> be paid by Berkshire if these retained earnings of investees had instead been distributed to
> us."
> **[E3-04]**, 1991 Letter

**A sogo shosha is the case [E2-08] and [E3-04] were written for, not the case that escapes
them.**

**Sixth, and this is the governing text for the general rule: the corpus says what an input
you cannot pin down licenses, and it is not a substitute number.**

> "Alas, though Aesop's proposition and the third variable  that is, interest rates  are
> simple, plugging in numbers for the other two variables is a difficult task. Using precise
> numbers is, in fact, foolish; working with a range of possibilities is the better approach.
> Usually, the range must be so wide that no useful conclusion can be reached. Occasionally,
> though, even very conservative estimates about the future emergence of birds reveal that the
> price quoted is startlingly low in relation to value."
> **[E4-25]**, 2000 Letter

*(OCR, not smoothed: the 2000 letter renders em dashes as double spaces around "that is,
interest rates". Flagged per PRIME RULE 1, carried as found.)*

**"Usually, the range must be so wide that no useful conclusion can be reached."** That is the
corpus's own ruling on a missing input. It licenses **abstention**, and it says abstention is a
real outcome rather than a failure. It does not license a stand-in figure, and it does not
license a stand-in figure any more merely because the stand-in is announced out loud.

## THE GENERAL RULE THIS ESTABLISHES

**A CONVENTION label may confess an invention; it may never license conduct a rule forbids.**

Stated in the corpus's own terms, so it is checkable against a source rather than against this
document: where an owner-earnings input cannot be established, **[E2-09]** requires a disclosed
guess and **[E3-44]** and **[E2-41]** supply its default; where even the guess will not carry,
**[E4-25]** requires the range to be carried and *"no useful conclusion"* to be accepted as the
answer. **Nowhere does the corpus permit the missing term to be deleted and (a) alone reported
as owner earnings.** A confession changes who knows about a defect. It does not change whether
it is one.

The reader's lesson, recorded at 07:32 and repeated here because it is the durable half:
**a rule can be obeyed and broken by the same sentence.** No check that reads one rule at a
time can see it.

## V4 CLOSED THE HOLE FROM THE OTHER SIDE, AND HERE IS HOW

None of what follows was written to answer these seven files. It closed the gap structurally,
which is why the hole has stayed shut in all runs since.

1. **The OE construction no longer starts from net income.** `THE FRAMEWORK v4.md` section VI
   confesses the opposite construction: *"Owner earnings via operating cash flow less SBC, less
   the (c) guess | OCF nets working capital from one audited line, with [E2-23]'s LIFO carve-out
   applied; (c) now carries the corpus default [E3-44, E2-41] and exception class [E5-20]."*
   A net-income proxy is not a weaker version of that build. It is not reachable from it.
2. **The reason given by all seven is no longer available.** Q4 states: *"So: D&A is the default
   guess for (c)."* Where a maintenance-capex disclosure cannot be found, the default applies.
   *"A clean, disclosed maintenance-capex figure was not obtainable"* stopped being a licence
   the moment the default was written down.
3. **The equity-method case is handled by name.** Q4's convention paragraph: *"For companies
   with material equity-method or unconsolidated minority stakes, add the look-through share of
   undistributed investee earnings **[E3-04]** ... or the yield is understated."* That is the
   sogo shosha case, addressed directly.
4. **A missing filing is now a verdict, not a workaround.** The four verdicts split the old
   "too hard" into UNRESEARCHED and UNKNOWABLE, and *"UNRESEARCHED and UNKNOWABLE both close the
   file; neither is a pass."* The annual securities report that was never pulled makes these
   names UNRESEARCHED. UNRESEARCHED closes the file, and a closed file cannot carry a proxy
   because it does not reach the arithmetic.
5. **The label these files wore would now fail on its own.** *"A question marked IN that carries
   "unverified", "general knowledge" or "provisional" is a protocol violation."* Every one of
   the seven carries that wording at one gate or more, in its own text.
6. **And the absence claim itself is now regulated.** v4's ABSENCE-CLAIM RULE, added 2026-08-28:
   *"this document may assert that the corpus lacks something only if a recorded sweep looked
   for it."* MTSUY's *"not attributable to Buffett/Munger"* is an unswept absence claim, and it
   was false: **[E2-08]**, **[E2-09]**, **[E3-44]**, **[E2-41]** and **[E3-04]** were all in the
   ledger and all bear on the question.

**The repository had already ruled on the narrow point too, and before v4.** `Framework/ARCHIVE
- RULINGS 1-14 (resolved into v4, 2026-08-26).md`, **RULING 10**, ratified 2026-08-26, quotes
**[E2-23]** in full and settles that the working-capital increment is required and that (c) is
maintenance capex, closing with *"The prior omission is recorded, not edited out of history"*.
That archive is history and is not applied here per `CLAUDE.md`. It is cited only to date the
knowledge: **by 2026-08-26 the project knew.**
