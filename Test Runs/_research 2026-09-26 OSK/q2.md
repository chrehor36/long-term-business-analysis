## Q2 — IS IT A FRANCHISE? **[E3-03]**

*Every Oshkosh figure below is read from the filed 10-Ks for FY2007 to 2025 (Items 1 and 1A, the MD&A segment discussions, the statements and the segment notes), the 10-QT and the 10-Q to 2026-06-30, or from SEC XBRL companyfacts checked against the filed statements (Step 0). Extractions in `Test Runs/_research 2026-09-26 OSK/`: `cfgrep.py` and `cfparse.py` (`cf_text.txt`), `bs.py` (`bs.json`), `roc.py` (`roc_out.txt`), and the peer filings and scripts in `peers/`.*

**The hypothesis I formed while reading, stated so it can be attacked [E4-26]:** *Oshkosh is three businesses with three different weaknesses against [E3-03]. Access is an oligopoly of global equipment makers selling to consolidating rental companies, where price follows cost and the cycle, and JLG earns what Genie earns. Transport sells to one buyer that re-competes its programmes, buys the designs to make them competable, and audits the price; that is criterion (2) and in part criterion (3). Vocational holds the best position (Pierce), but its recent pricing looks like the whole fire-apparatus industry repricing in a period of tight supply, not one firm's power, and its record before 2017 is thin. The company as a whole should show [E3-43]'s "business": margins of mid to high single digits, returns that move with the cycle, and prices that recover cost rather than lead it.* The evidence against, above all Pierce and JLG's premium positions and the tangible returns, is hunted and answered before the verdict.

**[E3-03], criterion by criterion.** *"An economic franchise arises from a product or service that: (1) is needed or desired; (2) is thought by its customers to have no close substitute and; (3) is not subject to price regulation. The existence of all three conditions will be demonstrated by a company's ability to regularly price its product or service aggressively and thereby to earn high rates of return on capital."*

- **(1) Needed or desired [x]: passes.** Fire departments, airports, waste haulers, rental fleets and the Army need these machines, and much of the demand is required by rule (airport rescue vehicles *"are required to be in service at most airports worldwide"*).
- **(3) Not subject to price regulation [ ]: passes for Access and Vocational; FAILS IN PART for Transport (20% of 2025 sales).** Access and Vocational prices are set by the company against competitors. Transport's are not: *"Certain of the Company's sales in the defense segment are made pursuant to contracts with the U.S. government with pricing based on the costs as determined by the Company to produce products or perform services under the contracts. Cost-based pricing is determined under the Federal Acquisition Regulations ("FAR"). The FAR provide guidance on the types of costs that are allowable in establishing prices for goods and services under U.S. government contracts"* (FY2013 10-K note); and in 2025: *"Like most large government contractors, the Company is audited and reviewed by the government on a continual basis. Costs and prices under such contracts may be subject to adjustment based upon the results of such audits and reviews."* The project's precedents read the same language as criterion (3) failing on the government half (HII, 2026-09-26; ICFI, 2026-09-25). Most Oshkosh defense contracts are firm fixed-price rather than cost-type, so the regulation bears less than at HII; criterion (2) is the sharper failure there (next).
- **(2) Thought by its customers to have no close substitute [ ]: FAILS, in the registrant's own words, in Access and Transport, which together were 63% of 2025 sales and have carried most of the company's operating income in most years.**
  - *Access, the market in the company's words.* 2025 Item 1: *"competition for sales of aerial work platform equipment includes Genie Industries, Inc. (a subsidiary of Terex Corporation), Skyjack Inc. (a subsidiary of Linamar Corporation), Haulotte Group, Xuzhou Construction Machinery Group Co., Ltd. (XCMG), Zhejiang Dingli Machinery Co., Ltd. and numerous other manufacturers. Global competition for sales of telehandler equipment includes J C Bamford Excavators Ltd., the Manitou Group, Merlo SpA, Genie Industries, Inc., Skyjack Inc. and numerous other manufacturers"*; *"Principal methods of competition include brand awareness, product innovation and performance, price, quality, service and support"*. Item 1A: *"We also face pricing pressure from international competitors that attempt to gain domestic market share through importing and selling products at below market prices, particularly in the Access segment. If competition in our industries intensifies or if competitors lower their prices for competing products, we may lose sales or be required to lower the prices we charge for our products"*; *"price increases may not be accepted by our customers and may result in them choosing to order from our competitors"*; and on the buyer: *"the Access segment's largest customers are rental companies ... Should access equipment customers consolidate through mergers and acquisitions, or should larger access equipment customers continue to grow through the acquisition of smaller rental companies, the buying influence of access equipment customers may grow"*. In FY2009: *"we are facing competitive pricing from new entrants in certain markets"*.
  - *Access, the price in the filed record.* The MD&As state the price line and its offset most years, and it moves with cost and cycle, not ahead of them:

  - *Transport, in the company's words.* *"The principal method of competition involves a competitive bid process that considers factors as determined by the customer, such as price"*; *"The Competition in Contracting Act requires competition for U.S. defense programs in most circumstances. Competition for DoD programs currently supplied by the Company could result in the U.S. government awarding future contracts to another manufacturer or the U.S. government awarding the contracts to the Company at lower prices and operating margins"*; *"Certain of the Company's contracts with the DoD, including the FMTV A2 contract, require that the Company effectively transfer the “technical know-how” necessary to produce and support the vehicles"*; *"Conditions in the defense marketplace are also currently highly favorable for the success of non-traditional competitors"*. **The substitution happened on the record**: *"The DoD conducted a full and open competition for follow-on JLTV production in which Oshkosh Defense participated"*, the Army having *"purchased Government Purpose Rights to the Oshkosh JLTV design"*, and *"in February 2023, the DoD awarded the JLTV Family of Vehicles follow on contract to another company based on, at least in part, a lower price"*. The customer bought the design, re-competed its production and chose a cheaper builder: a customer who thinks it has a close substitute, and acted on it.
  - *Vocational, in the company's words.* Fire apparatus: *"Competitors for Pierce and Maxi-Metal firefighting vehicles include Rosenbauer International AG, REV Group, Inc. (a subsidiary of Terex Corporation) and numerous smaller, regional manufacturers. Principal methods of competition include brand awareness, ability to meet or exceed customer specifications, price, lead times"*; the channel: *"the municipal fire apparatus market distribution channel is comprised of a relatively small number of dealers that, if they were to consolidate, may create additional pricing pressure"*. Refuse: *"Environmental Solutions Group (a subsidiary of Terex Corporation), New Way Trucks (a subsidiary of Federal Signal), Labrie Enviroquip Group ... The principal methods of competition are product innovation, quality and performance, service and price"*. Airport ground equipment: *"TLD Group and ITW GSE Inc. as well as other regional competitors"*. Criterion (2) is closest to passing here, for Pierce; it is taken up under the strongest evidence against.

**THE STRONGEST EVIDENCE AGAINST THIS VERDICT, hunted hardest [E4-26, E3-41, E4-51].**
1. **Pierce's pricing, 2023-2025.** *"The increase in operating income in the Vocational segment was largely a result of improved pricing ($157 million)"* (2025), *"improved pricing ($160 million)"* (2024), *"improved pricing ($161 million)"* (2023); Vocational gross margin *"improved pricing (300 basis points), offset in part by higher material costs (90 basis points)"* (2025), which is price ahead of cost; Vocational margin 7.2% to 14.7% in two years, municipal fire apparatus sales $1,102M to $1,516M. And Pierce earned 13-14% before this, in FY2019-2021, when REV's fire segment earned 3.5-5.1% on EBITDA. That is a customer paying more each year to a supplier it apparently prefers.
2. **JLG's premium.** *"The Company frequently achieves premium pricing due to the quality, durability and low total cost of ownership of its products"*; *"product residual values that are generally higher than competitors' units"*; a leading share in a market where JLG and Genie are the two large North American makers.
3. **The returns on tangible capital**, 21-57% in every year but one, which [E3-03] says demonstrate the conditions.
4. **Sole source.** *"the sole-source supplier for several key vehicle platforms to the DoD"*: the heavy tactical trucks (HEMTT, HET, PLS, LVSR) have been Oshkosh's for decades.
5. **[E3-47]**: a wrongly closed file on an understood business is the costliest error.

**Answered.** (1) Pierce is real and it is the best business inside Oshkosh, but it is about 15% of sales ($1,516M of $10,422M), its segment earned −1.0% to 3.5% in FY2011-FY2014 (*"The fire & emergency segment reported an operating loss of $8.2 million, or 1.0% of sales, in fiscal 2011"*, on *"weak municipal spending"*), and its 2023-2025 pricing ran alongside REV's: REV's fire-and-ambulance segment went from 5.5% to 12.5% on *"price realization"* over the same three years. An industry that reprices together out of multi-year backlogs after an inflation is [E3-43]'s *"supply of its product or service is tight. Tightness in supply usually does not last long"*, not yet a franchise; the corpus test is whether the price holds when the backlog is gone, and the filings cannot show that yet. It is recorded for Q6 and for a separate test if Pierce were ever owned alone. (2) JLG's premium is stated and not visible in the row: its margin is level with Genie's through the cycle, and its own MD&As record a *"more competitive pricing environment"* (FY2016), a price increase that *"only covers anticipated cost increases and does not include any profit"* (FY2018 outlook), and pricing *"completely offset by higher material costs"* (FY2019). A premium that the competitor also earns is the industry's margin. (3) The tangible returns are high because customers pay in advance (contract liabilities $2,179.5M at 2025-12-31 against net tangible operating assets of $3,377M) and the plants are cheap relative to throughput; [E3-03]'s demonstration is a return earned **by** pricing aggressively, and on the capital the owners provided, including the goodwill of the JLG purchase, the return has run 11-23% outside FY2010, on GAAP margins of 4.5-9.5%. (4) Sole-source positions are held by the grace of a buyer bound by statute to compete them, who buys the designs, and who took the JLTV away on price; the heavy trucks' own pricing is audited and adjustable. (5) I weighed [E3-47]. The file closes on the registrant's own account of how its prices are made in its two largest-profit segments of most years, and on sixteen years of ordinary margins, not on the manager or the share price; **[E5-42] keeps the judgments apart**, and nothing here says the operation is badly run.

**The analyst's own incentive, stated [operator rule 9, E4-27, E3-41].** This would be the third Q2 OUT today in the same general shape (AYI, ST); the pull toward a pattern is itself a bias, and so is the opposite pull toward finding a franchise after a run of failures. Both were tested by putting Pierce's pricing and the tangible returns at the head of the case against, by reading the Access price lines year by year rather than taking the risk factors alone, and by building the verdict from this registrant's filings and not from the previous files' reasoning.

Oshkosh does not pass [E3-03]: criterion (2) fails in its own Items 1 and 1A for Access and Transport, and the demonstration is absent on the whole company.
