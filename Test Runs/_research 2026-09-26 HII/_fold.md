### The finding
- **[E3-03] criterion (3) fails in the registrant's own words**, while (2) passes:
  *"costs billed to contracts with the U.S. Government are treated as allowable and allocable costs under the FAR and CAS
  regulations"*; *"The U.S. Government also regulates the methods by which allowable costs, including overhead, are
  allocated to government contracts"*; unallowable costs *"must be refunded if already reimbursed"*; *"CAS prescribes the
  determination, allocation, and recovery of retirement related benefit plan costs"*; 96% of 2025 revenue under
  fixed-price incentive (46%) or cost-type (50%) contracts, which reimburse allowable cost plus a negotiated fee.
- **Criterion (2) passes and is recorded as passing**: *"the only company currently capable of building, refueling, and
  inactivating the U.S. Navy's nuclear-powered aircraft carriers"*, *"the sole builder of amphibious assault ships"*, one
  of two submarine yards (the two share the work by a teaming agreement).
- **[E3-43]'s demonstration runs the wrong way.** Pre-tax segment income on net tangible operating assets was 14-25% in
  every year 2012-2025, high because the customer finances the capital (progress payments, capital grants, a state-leased
  yard, the pension through CAS); it fell from 25.4% (2016) to 14.2% (2024) while the segment margin halved from 10.1% to
  5.0%, with inflation, labour and supplier costs unrecovered in the filer's own risk factor.
- **The competitor row is one company and it moves in step**: GD Marine Systems 9.3% (2012-19) to 7.5% (2020-25), HII
  8.4% to 6.3%. Two holders of non-substitutable yards converge on one regulated band. BWXT's naval-reactor segment, the
  sole supplier inside HII's ships, earns 17-22% under the same rules; recorded as the strongest counter in the row and
  answered: the regime permits a different fee for a different product, and nobody in it sets price.
