## Q1 — CAN I UNDERSTAND HOW THIS MAKES MONEY? **[E3-31]**
- **Unit economics in my own words, no management language.** A bank or credit union has to keep its ledger of deposits, loans and the general ledger on some system every day; almost none write their own. Jack Henry sells that system (the "core"): about 900 banks and 700 credit unions run one of its four core programs. Most now rent it rather than install it: Jack Henry runs the computers and processes the ledger in its own data centres for a monthly fee per account, under contracts that *"typically sign contracts for six years that include "per account" fees and minimum guaranteed payments during the contract period"*; the rest license the program, pay about 20% of the licence each year for support, and buy hardware through Jack Henry. On top of the core it sells add-ons to the same institutions and to institutions on rival cores: card processing, bill pay and transfers, online and mobile banking, fraud tools, item imaging, priced per transaction or per active user. Fiscal 2026 revenue **$2,544.3M**: private and public cloud $816.1M, processing $1,096.3M, on-premise support $336.8M, licences, implementation, hardware and deconversion fees $295.1M. When a client is bought by a bank that uses another system and leaves early, it pays a **deconversion fee** (*"amounts due from the early termination"*; $42.8M in fiscal 2026). Costs are people and data centres; the capital is software development, capitalized at $184.2M in fiscal 2026 against $67.1M of equipment.
- **The scarce input this business controls.** The installed base: the ledgers of about 1,600 core clients sitting on its systems, under multi-year contracts, with the cost and risk of moving a bank's ledger borne by the client (*"Broad experience converting diverse banks and credit unions to our core platforms from competitive platforms"* is listed as a selling point; the conversion is the switching event). Behind it, the four core programs and the regulator's acceptance of Jack Henry as a supervised service provider (*"Our private cloud services are subject to examination by FBA regulators under the Bank Service Company Act"*).
- **Will the fundamentals look broadly the same in ten years?** The need is permanent while banks exist; the number of clients shrinks every year (*"The FDIC reports the number of commercial banks and savings institutions declined 13% from the end of calendar year 2020 to the end of calendar year 2025"*; credit unions down 16%), while their assets grow. The product is being rebuilt for the public cloud (*"The Jack Henry Platform TM ... which we are developing into a fully functional modern alternative for existing core functions"*). Whether the position survives that rebuild is Q2's question ([E4-04], carried there as the BR, MRK and MDT runs carried theirs); the mechanism itself is simple and the same in every 10-K from fiscal 2000 to 2026.
- **Relatively simple and stable in character [E3-31]?** A recurring fee per account or per transaction for keeping a bank's books and moving its money, under multi-year contracts, with a small licence-and-hardware tail. I can state how it makes money in under five minutes **[E4-46]**; the technology is the product, but the economics are a subscription to a regulated institution's record-keeping.
