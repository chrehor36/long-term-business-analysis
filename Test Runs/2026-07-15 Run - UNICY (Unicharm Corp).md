A consumer hygiene-products company — disposable diapers (both baby and,
notably, a growing adult-incontinence product line relevant to Japan's
aging population), feminine hygiene products, and pet care. A classic,
easily understandable consumer-staples business model (branded, repeat-
purchase, non-discretionary hygiene products), similar in kind to how
Buffett/Munger discuss Procter & Gamble-style categories generally, though
not a corpus-sourced comparison for this specific company. **VERDICT:
PASS** — one of the more straightforwardly understandable business models
in this batch.

## GATE 2 — MOAT
Franchise test: needed/desired — yes, genuinely non-discretionary; no close
substitute — **real brand loyalty in hygiene categories is a well-known
consumer-staples characteristic** (parents/caregivers are often reluctant
to switch diaper brands once satisfied — a general industry observation,
not independently verified with Unicharm's own repeat-purchase data);
unregulated — largely yes. **Class: NARROW-to-WIDE** — the adult-
incontinence product line specifically benefits from Japan's (and,
increasingly, the region's) aging demographics as a **secular tailwind**,
a genuinely distinctive positive among this batch's otherwise-headwind-
facing demographic exposures (contrast with Sekisui House/Daito Trust/
Nitori, all facing the SAME demographic trend as a headwind for housing
demand — Unicharm turns it into a tailwind for adult-care products).
**VERDICT: PASS with NARROW-to-WIDE classification** — one of the more
qualitatively attractive cases in the batch given the demographic tailwind.
