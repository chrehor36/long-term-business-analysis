the fund's investments financed by the fund's own borrowings and outside capital (financing lines

carry and preferred dividends leave through financing; they are not the Class A owner's, so they come
out — this **lowers** owner earnings. (3) Look-through [E3-04] enters the generous end only.

### Name the specific way THIS business dies **[E2-27, E3-24, E4-40, E4-51]**
**The bear case its holders would accept as fairly stated:** *BAM is a fee manager on locked capital that
has been turning itself into a lender to its own deal flow while paying out more than it earns.*
- **The mechanism — THE WAREHOUSE, carrying the fifth shape's distribution in a debt-funded form.** In a
  real-asset and credit downturn of the kind already visible in its own filings (FY2025: −$734M of accrued
  carry on mature real-estate flagships; −$180M on BSREP III), four things arrive together: (i) the
  **$8.0bn of bridge and equity commitments** stop syndicating and land on BAM's balance sheet; (ii) the
  commercial paper and revolver that would fund them are the markets that close first; (iii) fees on
  **$374bn of permanent, perpetual and liquid FBC** (billed on market capitalisation or NAV)
  fall with prices, and new vintages slip; (iv) the dividend, set at *"at least approximately 90%"* of an
  accrual measure that already runs ahead of cash, must be cut or borrowed for. **The exposure, not the
  experience [E4-40]:** the commitment total grew 2.4x in eighteen months, in a benign market.
- **Quantified from filed figures:** if **half** of the $8.0bn failed to syndicate and were held at a 20%
  mark-down, BAM would need ~$4.0bn of funding against ~$2.9bn of cash and undrawn revolver and would
  book ~$0.8bn of losses — **about 40-50% of a year's conservative owner earnings, and a funding gap of
  ~$1.1bn** before any dividend. **Fees on the $298bn of closed-end FBC would keep coming** (committed
  capital, 10-12 year terms), which is why this kills the payout path and the growth plan, not the
  business. **A 25% fall in BEP/BIP/BBU prices** would take a share of the $561M of incentive distributions
  and the market-cap base fees with it (magnitude not separable from the 10-K's text tables; named, not
  computed).
- **The second mechanism, slower: the controlling holder.** BN is the largest client (BWS $108bn FBC; the
  listed affiliates' capital, *"exclusive"*), the carry partner, the name's licensor (terminable if BN falls
  below 25%), the depository of BAM's cash and a lender; BWS has pledged 65M BAM shares for a $1.0bn margin
  loan. **A stress at BN transmits to BAM through every one of those channels at once.**
- **Likelihood:** insolvency of BAM — **a low-level possibility** (no maturity before 2030, 9-24x coverage,
  locked fees). **The dividend path breaking — a real possibility** (it has exceeded owner earnings in
  every filed period).
- **Survival shape: a NINTH, THE WAREHOUSE** — the asset-light manager that lends its own balance sheet to
  its funds' deals, so that its commitments grow faster than its liquidity and the syndication risk lands
  in the same downturn that shuts its short-term funding — **carrying the FIFTH shape's feature (the
  distribution above owner earnings, [E2-60]) in a debt-funded rather than divestiture-funded form.** Not
  ORCL's (the commitments are bridges, not a multi-year build it contracted not to stop), not ARM's (SBC is
  8.2% of operating cash), not BE's (three restated years, and the carve-outs exist), not ACVA's (the
  deposits are related-party, not customers').

- **VERDICT (__Q4VERDICTLABEL__): [x] IN — the business survives its named death; the payout does not.**
  Owner earnings $1,670M-$2,024M on every window and both (c) ends, 21% wide; SBC resolves and is complete;
  coverage ~9-24x; no maturity before 2030. **Staying power scores 1 of 3**, the restricted-earnings flag
  [E2-60] fires, and the ninth survival shape is named — **none of which is a finding that BAM dies, and
  all of which are findings about the dividend and the growth plan.** *(If this Q4 were governing, strength
  (3) would need the commitment maturity schedule — not in the 10-Q — before an IN could be written without
  a caveat; see the register.)*
