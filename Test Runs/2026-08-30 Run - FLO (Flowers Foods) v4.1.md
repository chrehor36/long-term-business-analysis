## Q1 — CAN I UNDERSTAND HOW THIS MAKES MONEY? **[E3-31]**

- **Unit economics in my own words:** FLO bakes fresh packaged bread, buns, and snacks in
  44 bakeries and sells ~$5.3B/yr of it, two-thirds branded (Nature's Own — the top loaf
  brand, Dave's Killer Bread — organic, Canyon Bakehouse — gluten-free, Wonder,
  Tastykake, and now Simple Mills crackers/cookies), one-third store-brand and
  non-retail. Fresh bread's economics are freshness logistics: product must reach the
  shelf in days and stales fast, so distribution runs through independent distributor
  partners (IDPs) who own geographic route rights FLO sold them — largely on FLO's own
  financing ($122M gross notes receivable from incorporated IDPs, $108M noncurrent on
  the balance sheet, earning ~$17M/yr of interest income — a small lending book inside
  the P&L, which also books ~$1.1B/yr of shipping costs paid to those IDPs). Margins are
  thin (adj EBITDA ~10%, operating ~3-7%) and the profit engine is brand price premium
  over a retailer store brand that sells for roughly half the price at 35% unit share.
- **The scarce input the business controls:** the #1 and #2 brand positions (Nature's
  Own, DKB) and the national DSD route network — shelf-stocking reach a regional baker
  cannot match. Only Bimbo (2x FLO's US scale) owns both at national scale.
- **Ten years:** bread will be baked, branded and delivered the same way; the open
  questions are the category's unit decline, private-label share, and the debt — Q2/Q4
  matters, not intelligibility failures.
- **VERDICT: [x] IN** — a simple, stable-character business [E3-31]; what is in doubt is
  its franchise and trajectory, not how it makes money.
