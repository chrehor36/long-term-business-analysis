## Q1 — CAN I UNDERSTAND HOW THIS MAKES MONEY? **[E3-31]**
- **Unit economics in my own words, no management language.** Medtronic designs, makes and sells medical devices to hospitals and surgeons in about 150 countries: pacemakers, defibrillators and a leadless pacemaker implanted in the heart; catheters that ablate heart tissue to stop irregular rhythms; replacement heart valves delivered by catheter; stents, balloons and grafts for arteries and the aorta; spinal implants, the navigation and robotic systems used to place them, and bone-graft products; implanted nerve stimulators and drug pumps for pain and movement disorders; devices for strokes and brain aneurysms; surgical staplers, vessel-sealing instruments, sutures, hernia mesh and a surgical robot; patient monitors, airway and pulse-oximetry products; and (until the split-off) insulin pumps and glucose sensors. The customer is a hospital or clinic; the payer is an insurer or a government; the chooser is a physician. Most products are used once per patient (an implant, a stapler load, a catheter), so revenue repeats with procedures, and capital systems (robots, navigation, generators) pull the disposables. Gross margin (cost of products sold excluding amortization, segment note, fiscal 2026): the three segments earned **$8,862M of segment operating profit on $33,079M of sales, 26.8%**, after research and development of $2,438M (7.4%) and selling and administration of $10,451M (31.6%); consolidated cost of products sold excluding amortization was **$12,721M, 35.0% of sales** (a gross margin before amortization of 65.0%), and operating profit as filed **$6,467M, 17.8%**. The money is the gap between what a hospital is paid for a procedure and the device's manufacturing cost, defended by clinical evidence, regulatory approval and a sales force that stands in the operating room.
- **The scarce input this business controls.** **Regulatory approvals backed by clinical trials** (the 10-K: *"our initiation and participation in hundreds of clinical trials each fiscal year"*), **the installed base and the physicians trained on it** (implanted devices followed by remote monitoring and replacements; surgeons trained on a stapler or a navigation system), and **the breadth of the portfolio and sales force across 70 conditions**. The components and materials are not scarce. Whether these inputs are scarce in [E3-03]'s sense is Q2's question.
- **Will the fundamentals look broadly the same in ten years?** **The mechanism, yes**: people will have heart rhythm disorders, failing valves, spinal disease and surgery in ten years, and hospitals will buy devices to treat them from a concentrated set of suppliers under a regulatory approval system. **The particular products will not**: the filings describe a product cycle (pulsed field ablation replacing thermal ablation in Cardiac Ablation Solutions, leadless pacemakers, renal denervation, a surgical robot entering a market an incumbent created), which is [E4-04]'s question, carried to Q2 under the 2026-09-20 ruling that applies it as a competence limit there.
- **Relatively simple and stable in character [E3-31]? Understood, with the technology limit named.** The economic mechanism (procedure-driven disposables and implants, reimbursed by payers, chosen by physicians, gated by regulators) is simple and has been stable for decades. The technology inside each product is not simple, and the corpus names the limit: *"simple businesses (if there's lots of technology, we won't understand it)"* **[E2-75]**, and *"we have no insights into which participants in the tech field possess a truly durable competitive advantage"* **[E4-57]**. The framework places that limit at Q2, not Q1 (*"So [E4-04] is applied as a competence limit, never as a fourth franchise criterion"*, the 2026-09-20 ruling), and the unit of the test is the company **[E4-08]**. The question at Q1 is whether I can say how the money is made in five minutes **[E4-46]**, and I can: the filings say it, the segment note measures it, and nothing in it depends on understanding the engineering. **IN at Q1 is not a finding that the durability of the products can be judged.**
