## THE FOUNDATIONS (not a gate)
Three foundations bear here. The first is the market as servant **[M2006-077]**: the 10-K's five-year graph shows the
stock compounding from $100 to $234 to September 2025 and the price has since risen to $332.89; a rise "is never a reason
to buy it" **[L2013-007]**, and nothing in this run reads the quotation as evidence about the business. The second is
no macro **[M2000-094]**: the filings are full of tariffs, the Supreme Court's tariff ruling and refunds, a memory and
storage shortage, and an industry-wide race for AI compute; these enter only as properties of the business (its cost of
components, its pricing), never as forecasts. The third is the analyst's own habits: the anchor of a famous holding of
the speakers themselves is "always your previous conclusion" **[M2016-054]**, and the look is for "what’s wrong in
things" **[M2025-013]**. **Contrary evidence, written down as found** **[M1997-127]**: (a) the 10-K's own risk factors
call its markets "highly competitive and subject to rapid technological change" with "short product life cycles", and
say the company's success "depends heavily on ensuring the continuing and timely introduction of innovative new
products"; (b) a federal court found the company in violation of an injunction and referred it for a criminal-contempt
determination (10-K Item 3); (c) part of Services revenue, the Google search payments, depends on an antitrust remedy
still on appeal (10-K Item 1A); (d) the 10-Q reports a component shortage the company "expects [...] to intensify";
(e) owner cash was flat from FY2021 to FY2025 (0.25% a year) while the price more than doubled; (f) the chief executive
changed on 2026-09-01 (8-K of 2026-04-20).

## Q1 — CAN I UNDERSTAND IT? Where its economics will be in ten years, inside my perimeter. STOP.
**The test.** Understanding is "a reasonable fix on about what the earning power and competitive position will look like
in five or 10 years" **[M2012-065]**, "where the business will be in 10 years" **[M2000-037]**; the product may stay
opaque if "the economic dynamics of the industry" are understood **[M2011-014]**; and the precision asked is low, "a
feel for it" **[M2015-086]**.

**What the business is, from the filings.** Apple designs, has others manufacture, and sells phones, computers, tablets
and wearables, and sells services to the people who own them (10-K FY2025, Item 1). FY2025 net sales $416,161M: iPhone
$209,586M (50%), Services $109,158M (26%), Mac $33,708M, iPad $28,023M, Wearables, Home and Accessories $35,686M. Gross
margin $195,201M, of which Products $112,887M (36.8% of product sales) and Services $82,314M (75.4% of services sales):
Services is 26% of sales and 42% of gross margin (10-K FY2025, MD&A). Services comprise advertising (including "licensing
arrangements with Google LLC [...] to offer their search services"), AppleCare, cloud, the App Store and subscriptions,
and payments (10-K Item 1 and Item 1A). Sales are 40% direct, 60% through carriers and resellers.

**Key variables** **[M1998-044]**: (1) whether the owners of the installed base keep buying the next device from Apple
rather than from an Android maker; (2) what Apple can charge on that base, in the device price and in the Services toll
(App Store commission, search payments). The first is consumer behaviour; the second is consumer behaviour plus the
courts and regulators. **The record of variable (1)** (10-K FY2015 `0001193125-15-356351`, FY2019 `0000320193-19-000119`,
FY2022 `0000320193-22-000108`, FY2025 `0000320193-25-000079`; 10-Q Q3 FY2026): iPhone net sales $91,279M (FY2013),
$155,041M (FY2015), $139,337M (FY2017), $164,888M (FY2018), $142,381M (FY2019), $137,781M (FY2020), $191,973M (FY2021),
$205,489M (FY2022), $200,583M (FY2023), $201,183M (FY2024), $209,586M (FY2025), and $196,515M in the first nine months of
FY2026, up 22%. Unit sales were last disclosed in FY2018; from FY2019 the company reports only that its "installed base of
active devices also reached a new all-time high across all major product categories and geographic segments" (CFO,
EX-99.1 of 2026-07-30). Services: $16,051M (FY2013), $32,700M (FY2017), $53,768M (FY2020), $109,158M (FY2025).

**Is the forecast about customers or about technology** (test 7, **[M2017-019]**, **[M2023-030]**)? The framework itself
names this company as the case: "a technology label on a consumer business" is what Q1 "does not rule out", with
**[M2017-019]** as the row. I do not take the speakers' word for it (contamination note); I take the record above, which
is a record of what customers did through three falls in iPhone sales (FY2015 to FY2017, FY2018 to FY2020, and FY2023),
two years of falling Greater China sales (FY2024 and FY2025, down 8% and 4%), and the recoveries after each. The product's technology changes every year; the economic structure of the
industry, two platforms with Apple the premium one and the other side many makers who "seek to compete primarily through
aggressive pricing" (10-K Item 1), has not changed over the thirteen years shown. "Do I understand enough about this
business so that the financial statements will tell me [...] what the future financial statements are going to look
like" **[M2008-033]**: for the device franchise, yes, in the low-precision sense of **[M2015-086]**.

**Doubts written down** **[M1997-127]**, and what is done with them. (a) The company's own words: its markets are
"subject to rapid technological change", with "short product life cycles", and success "depends heavily on ensuring the
continuing and timely introduction of innovative new products" (10-K Item 1A); a business that lives on continued
invention is listed among what Q1 rules out **[M1999-075]**. (b) A technology transition is visible in the latest 10-Q:
R&D up 33% for nine months "including investments in artificial intelligence"; gross intangible assets up from $24,950M
to $38,220M in nine months with other purchase obligations ("licensed intellectual property and content") up from
$14.8B to $29.3B, the filing not saying for what; and a new risk factor that the business "depends on access to
sufficient computing resources" from "third-party cloud service providers". (c) The Services toll is being re-set by
courts and regulators (10-K Item 3). The rule is "if you have doubts about something being into your circle of
competence, it isn’t" **[M2002-092]**. My reading: doubts (a) and (b) are about whether a new technology could take the
customer away from the device, which is the castle's test 11, "what can happen [...] that will destroy, or modify, or
reduce the economic strengths that we perceive currently exist" **[M2000-014]**; and doubt (c) is a question about the
size of a toll, not about how the business makes money. I can say how the business earns and what would have to change
for it to stop; I cannot say the change will not come, and that is Q2's question. This reading of where Q1 ends and Q2
begins is mine, and it is the most contestable step of the run; it is confessed in the last section.

**VERDICT: IN**, narrowly. The economics are those of a consumer franchise whose key variable has a thirteen-year filed
record **[M2012-065]**, **[M2017-019]**; the threats from technology and regulation are carried to Q2 as the castle
questions they are **[M2000-014]**, with the routing to TOO HARD for fast change **[M1998-008]** held open there if the
evidence shows the change reaching the customer.

## Q2 — WHY IS THE CASTLE STILL STANDING, and what keeps it standing ten to twenty years? STOP.
The question: "why is that castle still standing? And what’s going to keep it standing" **[M1995-038]**; the capitalist
world "will repeatedly assault any business "castle" that is earning high returns" **[L2007-004]**.

**The record** (net sales, operating margin and gross margin from the filed 10-Ks cited at Q1 and the XBRL facts in
`facts_aapl.txt`): operating margin 31.2% (FY2011), 27.8% (FY2016), 24.6% (FY2019), 29.8% (FY2021), 32.0% (FY2025), 33.6%
(nine months FY2026). Gross margin 39.1% (FY2016), 37.8% (FY2019), 41.8% (FY2021), 46.9% (FY2025), 49.1% (nine months
FY2026, of which "approximately 2 percentage points" in the June quarter came from tariff refunds, EX-99.1). Products
gross margin 35.7% (FY2017), 32.2% (FY2019), 31.5% (FY2020), 36.3% (FY2022), 36.8% (FY2025), 39.9% (nine months FY2026);
Services gross margin 55.0% (FY2017), 63.7% (FY2019), 71.7% (FY2022), 75.4% (FY2025).

**The castle tests, each with its filing fact.**
1. *What keeps it standing, and how permanent* **[M1995-038]**. The reason the customer comes back is the device and
   what is built around it: the operating system, the third-party apps ("decisions by customers to purchase its hardware
   products depend in part on the availability of third-party software applications", 10-K Item 1A), and the services
   that live on the owner's account. The permanence is shown by thirteen years of iPhone sales (Q1) and an installed base
   at an all-time high in every major category (EX-99.1). The same risk factor states the weakness: developer support
   depends on "the perceived benefits of creating software [...] compared to competitors’ platforms".
2. *Would it stand without the lord* **[L2007-006]**, **[M1996-037]**. It has stood through one change: the chief
   executive changed in 2011 (proxy: "Since Mr. Cook was promoted to CEO in 2011"), when net sales were $108,249M at a
   31.2% operating margin; FY2025 was $416,161M at 32.0%. It is being tested again: a new chief executive from 2026-09-01
   (8-K of 2026-04-20). No dependence on a single superstar is visible in the record.
3. *The money test* **[M2011-015]**, **[M1997-103]**. The filing says the attackers exist and are rich: competitors with
   "the resources, experience or cost structures to provide products and services at little or no profit or even at a
   loss", competition "particularly intense as competitors have aggressively cut prices", and Apple "has a minority market
   share in the global smartphone [...] markets" (10-K Item 1). Against that, iPhone sales rose from $91,279M to
   $209,586M over FY2013 to FY2025 at a premium price. The attackers with money have not taken the premium customer.
4. *Pricing power and the agony* **[M2005-020]**. The 10-Q (Item 2 and Item 1A) reports "supply constraints and
   increasing costs for components [...] memory (DRAM)" and says "Actions, such as price increases, that have been and may
   in the future be taken by the Company may not effectively mitigate these negative impacts, and may also reduce demand".
   In the same nine months iPhone sales rose 22% and products gross margin rose from 36.9% to 39.9%. Price rises taken in
   a cost squeeze with sales up is the evidence the row describes, "charge more for a product and maintain or increase
   market share" **[M2000-031]**; strong positions "pass through increases in raw material costs" **[M2005-017]**. The
   company still states the agony in writing ("may also reduce demand").
5. *Share of mind* **[M1997-099]**, **[M2000-030]**: units are not disclosed; the installed base at a new high (EX-99.1)
   and Greater China sales up 30% in nine months after two falling years (10-Q) are the nearest filed evidence.
6. *The low-cost position*: not claimed and not shown; Apple sells at the top of the price range.
7. *The brand asked for by name* **[M2023-073]**, and does the brand hold against the retailer **[M2001-090]**: 60% of
   sales go through carriers and resellers "many of which distribute products and services from competitors" (10-K Item
   1A), and the company pays to staff their stores; the retailer has not taken the margin (gross margin above).
8. *Would the customer still choose it over the low bid* **[M2017-009]**: the competitors are described by the company
   as low-priced and loss-tolerant (test 3), and the premium has held. The speakers' own answer for this company
   **[M2023-034]** is not used as evidence (contamination note).
9. *Ask the competitors* **[M1999-130]**. The main smartphone rivals (Samsung, Xiaomi) do not file with the SEC; no
   instance of their figures was read this session, and their absence from the row is recorded. The SEC-filing makers of
   the products Apple sells against (PCs, wearables, accessories) are in the competitor row below.
10. *Widening or narrowing* **[M1999-108]**, **[L2005-010]**. Margins widened from FY2019 to FY2026 in both products and
   services. Against it, a notch is being cut by law in the most profitable part, the Services toll **[L1995-023]**: in
   the US the company is "subject to a court order preventing it from imposing any commission or fee on certain purchases"
   made outside an app, and the Ninth Circuit has allowed "some commission" on remand, now before the Supreme Court; in
   the EU the DMA has forced "alternative fee structures", "alternative methods of distribution" and free
   "interoperability obligations", and preliminary findings could bring fines "up to 10% of the Company’s annual worldwide
   net sales"; and the search payments depend on an antitrust remedy against Google that is on appeal and could
   prohibit "Google from offering the Company commercial terms for search distribution" (10-K Items 1A and 3; 10-Q Part
   II Items 1 and 1A). The filings do not give the size of the search payments or of App Store commissions, so the notch
   cannot be measured. Services sales rose 14% in FY2025 and 14% in nine months of FY2026, after the April 2025 injunction.
11. *What could destroy, modify or reduce it, five to fifteen years out* **[M2000-014]**: (a) a shift in how people
   compute that takes the customer away from the device, the newspaper's fate in **[L2006-008]** and **[M2006-065]**; the
   10-Q shows the company buying its way into AI (R&D up 33%, licensed intellectual property, dependence on third-party
   compute), not leading from strength; (b) the toll cut further by law (test 10); (c) manufacturing "primarily in China
   mainland, India, Japan, South Korea, Taiwan and Vietnam" and final assembly of "substantially all" hardware in Asia
   (10-K Item 1A), against tariffs and conflict; (d) one-sixth of sales in Greater China ($64,377M of $416,161M in FY2025).

**The competitor row** (same metric from each company's own 10-K, XBRL first-filed values, `peers_facts.txt`; operating
income over revenue, gross profit over revenue):

**Verdict.** The castle is shown standing on the evidence: a premium position held for thirteen years against richer,
cheaper and loss-tolerant rivals, through one change of chief executive, with margins widening and a price rise taken in
a cost squeeze with volume up. It is not shown filling in, which is what would close it OUT **[M2011-015]**: the notch in
the Services toll is real and unmeasured, but the filed Services figures have not yet shown it. Whether its future can be
judged **[M2000-019]**: the threats in test 11 are named in the company's own filings and I cannot rule them out for

which the record shows. **VERDICT: IN**, with the toll's legal notch and the AI transition carried forward as the

## Q3 — HOW MUCH CAPITAL MUST GO IN to get the earnings out, and what does the added capital earn? WEIGHING.
- **Return on the capital actually needed** **[M2010-090]**, **[M2011-060]**. Apple carries no goodwill line and,
  until FY2026, no separate intangibles line; its operating capital is small. At FY2025 year-end (10-K balance sheet):
  property, plant and equipment net $49,834M; trade receivables $39,777M, vendor non-trade receivables $33,180M and
  inventory $5,718M ($78,675M together) against accounts payable $69,860M and deferred revenue $9,055M ($78,915M
  together), so the suppliers and customers fund the working capital. Operating income of $133,050M on $49,834M of net
  fixed assets is about 2.7 times the fixed capital each year, pre-tax. Return on equity is not used: equity has been
  shrunk by buybacks to the point where it can be "whatever you want" **[M1998-017]** (it was $73,733M at FY2025 with an
  accumulated deficit of $14,264M).
- **Reinvestment to stand still and to grow** **[L1999-024]**, **[M2000-144]**. Capital spending over FY2021 to FY2025
  totalled $54,914M against depreciation and amortization of $57,050M (`owner_cash.py`); capex ran 2.4% to 3.1% of sales.
  My maintenance guess, stated as a guess per **[M2000-144]**: about the depreciation charge, $11B to $12B a year, with
  the growth outlays small; the filings do not split the two. Two outlays sit outside capex and must not be missed: the
  licensed intellectual property and content now carried as intangible assets (gross $38,220M at 2026-06-27 against
  $24,950M nine months earlier, 10-Q Note 5), paid through operating cash and so already inside owner cash; and compute
  bought from "third-party cloud service providers" (10-Q Item 1A), an operating cost, also inside it. These amortize as
  "very real expenses" **[L2012-003]**.
- **What the added capital earned** **[M2001-019]**. From FY2016 to FY2025 operating income rose from $60,024M to
  $133,050M (+$73,026M) while net fixed assets rose from $27,010M to $49,834M (+$22,824M): about three dollars of
  pre-tax operating income a year for each added dollar of fixed capital. The business has needed none of its earnings
  back: over FY2021 to FY2025 owner cash totalled $459,226M while buybacks ($438,583M) and dividends ($74,988M) totalled
  $513,571M (XBRL cash-flow facts), the excess drawn from the cash pile (cash and marketable securities $237.1B at FY2018,
  $132.4B at FY2025). Read with the warning about "a cyclical peak in earnings, a monopolistic position, or leverage"
  **[L1994-009]**: none of the three explains the return; the business is the first kind in **[M1998-081]**, "more and
  more money every year without putting up anything to get it, or very little".
- **The cap on growth** (for Q7): the growth arithmetic cannot run past its base **[M2003-120]**; aggregate owner cash grew
  0.25% a year over FY2021 to FY2025 and 5.24% over FY2020 to FY2025 (STEP 0).
- **WEIGHS FOR**: the best of the three kinds in **[M1998-081]** on the capital it needs; the one caution is that the
  AI-era outlays (licensed intangibles and rented compute) are new, run through operating cash, and are not yet long
  enough on the record to show what they earn.

## Q5 — WHO RUNS IT: able, honest, in love with the business, the same after being paid. STOP on integrity.
For a marketable stock the speakers "read a lot" rather than meet **[M2007-081]**; the yardsticks and the reading tests
carry the weight. Read for this question, beyond the filings in STEP 0: the order of the US District Court for the
Northern District of California of 2025-04-30 in *Epic Games, Inc. v. Apple Inc.*, No. 4:20-cv-05640-YGR, Dkt. 1508
(80 pages, public record, fetched from CourtListener's RECAP archive), and the opinion of the US Court of Appeals for
the Ninth Circuit of 2025-12-11, No. 25-2935 (fetched from the court's site). Both are primary documents; the 10-K and
10-Q describe them in summary (Item 3; Part II Item 1).

**The first yardstick, how well they run the business** **[M1994-008]**. The record against the hand dealt is strong: net
sales from $108,249M (FY2011, the year the chief executive took office) to $416,161M (FY2025) at a steady or rising
operating margin, against rivals whose margins are a fraction of it (Q2); capital needs held near depreciation while
sales nearly quadrupled (Q3); plain accounts (Q4). **Ability WEIGHS FOR.**

**The second yardstick and the integrity STOP.** The rows make integrity absolute and apply it "on doubt, not on proof":
"If you’ve got doubts, forget it." **[M2013-088]**; "you can’t make a good deal with a bad person" **[M1995-084]**;
"we wouldn’t hire anybody, no matter how able, if we didn’t trust them" **[M2015-047]**. The law is the floor, not the
test: "not only do they behave in a way that conforms with the laws, but [...] if a story were written by an unfriendly
but intelligent reporter" **[M2008-011]**. What the record shows, contrary evidence written as found **[M1997-127]**:
1. **A court order defied in bad faith, found twice.** The district court: "the Court FINDS Apple in willful violation of
   this Court’s Injunction"; "Apple, despite knowing its obligations thereunder, thwarted the Injunction’s goals, and
   continued its anticompetitive conduct solely to maintain its revenue stream"; and it referred the matter to the
   United States Attorney for criminal contempt (Dkt. 1508, pages 1 to 3). The Ninth Circuit affirmed the contempt
   findings and held that the district court did not "clearly err in finding that Apple acted in bad faith", noting the
   finding that Apple "attempted to mislead" in its Notice of Compliance with "pretextual" justifications (No. 25-2935,
   pages 21 to 23); it reversed in part only on the remedy (allowing some commission and some design rules on remand). The
   Supreme Court has granted review of "the applicable legal standard for civil contempt" (10-Q, Part II Item 1); the
   10-Q describes the grant as limited to that standard, and I read the facts found as standing until a court says
   otherwise.
2. **Testimony found false, and the decision traced to the top.** The district court: "To hide the truth, Vice-President
   of Finance, Alex Roman, outright lied under oath. Internally, Phillip Schiller had advocated that Apple comply with
   the Injunction, but Tim Cook ignored Schiller and instead allowed Chief Financial Officer Luca Maestri and his finance
   team to convince him otherwise. Cook chose poorly." (Dkt. 1508, page 2.) The Ninth Circuit records that the district
   court "referred Apple and one of its officers for criminal investigation" (opinion, page 16). The chief executive so
   named became Executive Chair on 2026-09-01 (8-K of 2026-04-20); the former CFO "continues to report to Mr. Cook and
   lead Apple’s Corporate Services teams" (proxy, CD&A).
3. **What was done when the finding came** **[M2017-005]**: "the CEO has to act [...] the main problem was they didn’t
   act when they learned about it." The 10-K, filed six months after the finding, says "The Company will continue to
   vigorously defend its actions and employees". The proxy, filed 2026-01-08, after both the finding and its affirmance,
   does not mention it (a search of the proxy text for "Epic", "injunction" and "contempt" found no instance); the
   compensation committee paid the maximum cash incentive to every named executive officer and cited the chief
   executive's "continued exceptional performance". No instance was found in the documents read of any personal
   consequence for the conduct the courts found.
4. **How they treat themselves against the owners** **[M1994-009]** (proxy, CD&A and summary compensation table): chief
   executive total pay $74,294,811 for FY2025, target $59M, three-quarters in performance RSUs that vest on total return
   relative to the S&P 500; the FY2025 cash-bonus target for operating income ($118.5B) was set below the prior year's
   actual ($123,216M), and the threshold at $113B, so a flat year paid above target; the maximum was paid. Weighed, not
   decisive: the rows put "any compensation sins" below "having somebody that’s mediocre running a huge company", which
   is not the case here **[M2007-006]**.
5. **An allegation, not weighed.** A securities-fraud class action alleges that Apple and its officers made false
   statements about "Apple’s purported compliance with an injunction issued in Epic Games" and about the Siri AI rollout;
   the court stayed it on 2026-07-17 without deciding it (N.D. Cal. No. 5:25-cv-06252, Dkt. 114). It is recorded so a
   reader knows it exists; it is not evidence.

**The other side's case, stated as well as I can** **[M2016-055]**. Apple's argument to the Ninth Circuit was that it was
penalized "for choosing what [it] understood to be the most advantageous option for its business and shareholders" (the
opinion, page 23). A company that, when someone must suffer, chooses that it not be the shareholders is "a different kind
of a company to invest in" **[M1998-121]**; the dispute is a commercial one about a commission, not about the owners'
money; the business is run with plain accounts and no tells of the kind Q4 lists; and the new chief executive is not
named in the findings read. The answer the courts gave is the answer here: the district court "did not find Apple in
contempt because it picked the most advantageous of several purportedly compliant options. Instead, it found that 'at
every step Apple considered whether its actions would comply, and at every step Apple chose to maintain its
anticompetitive revenue stream over compliance'" (opinion, page 23). My reading, not a speaker's: the rows do not let a
management's honesty toward its owners be inferred from conduct the courts call bad faith toward a court, since the
habit travels ("if you start fooling your shareholders, you will soon believe your own baloney" **[L2024-003]**), and the
culture rows are explicit that people "adopt the norms
of what they think is happening up above them" **[M2000-154]** and that "if you have a culture of lying, the processes
really don’t — they just disappear" **[M2022-055]**. Professed integrity is not evidence: "everybody mouths the integrity,
even when it’s lacking" **[M2010-069]**. The Executive Chair who made the decision still chairs the board that judges the
new chief executive, so the change of chief executive does not remove the doubt.

**VERDICT on integrity: OUT.** Two courts found, by clear and convincing evidence, that the company defied a court order
in bad faith to keep a revenue stream, that its compliance filing was pretextual, and (the district court) that an
officer lied under oath and the chief executive chose the course; the company defended the conduct and its people, and
the board paid maximum bonuses without a word. That is a doubt about integrity, and "If you’ve got doubts, forget it"
**[M2013-088]**; "Never deal with a rascal under the expectation that you can prevent him from cheating you"
**[L2023-003]**. The STOP closes the run. **Ability WEIGHS FOR** **[M1994-008]**, and it does not reopen the file
**[M2005-003]**.
