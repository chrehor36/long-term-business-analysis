## Q1 — CAN I UNDERSTAND IT? Where its economics will be in ten years, inside my perimeter. STOP.
- **The test.** Understanding is "a reasonable fix on about what the earning power and competitive position will look like in five or 10 years" **[M2012-065]**, "where the business will be in 10 years" **[M2000-037]**; the product may stay opaque if "I understand the economic dynamics of the industry" **[M2011-014]**.
- **What the business is (10-K FY2025, Item 1 and Note 18).** Three reporting segments: Pharma (46% of 2025 net sales, $1,737M; "69% of our Adjusted EBITDA excluding non-allocated corporate costs"): nasal spray pumps and metered-dose inhaler valves, of which Aptar says it is "a leading supplier", elastomer stoppers and plungers for injectables, of which it is "an important supplier", active-material (desiccant polymer) parts, digital health. Beauty (35%, $1,309M; 18% of segment Adjusted EBITDA): pumps, airless systems and valves for fragrance, skin care and personal care. Closures (19%, $730M; 13%): dispensing closures for food, beverage and personal care. About 5,000 customers, "no single customer or group of affiliated customers accounting for greater than 4% of our 2025 Net Sales". Europe is 49% of sales by destination. The work is injection molding, elastomer formulation, clean-room assembly.
- **The key variables and whether they are foreseeable** **[M1998-044]**: (1) unit demand for drug-delivery devices already designed into approved drugs (nasal allergy, asthma/COPD, central nervous system, emergency medicines, GLP-1 and biologic injectables); (2) what Aptar can charge for them, given that switching a device inside an approved drug means re-registration ("Combination Products specific guidelines [...] have increased the complexity of the registration process"; "We typically work for years modifying our dispensing device to work in conjunction with a customer’s drug formulation", 10-K FY2025 Items 1 and 1A); (3) resin, rubber and metal costs and the speed of passing them through; (4) the consumer parts' volumes against low-cost Asian supply. None of these is a technology race: the device platforms (spray pumps, valves, elastomer stoppers) are decades old and the 10-K says the business "was started in the late 1940’s, manufacturing and selling aerosol valves". Do the past statements tell the future ones **[M2008-033]**? For Pharma, broadly yes: twelve years of segment figures (FY2014-2025, read below at Q2) show a steady margin band. The one forecast the insiders would not write down is which customer drug wins (emergency medicines falling in 2026 is the instance), but with 5,000 customers and none over 4% that is a mix question, not the economics of the whole.
- **Routing.** Not a fast-changing industry: the digital-health line is the only technology component and it is a small part of Pharma's prescription division (no separate figure filed). Not a financial institution. Three segments with different economics: Q2 reads the parts (section B), and Pharma decides there.
- **Contrary.** The rows warn that "if they had never invented any more pharmaceuticals, it would be a terrible business" **[M1999-075]**: Aptar does not invent drugs, but its Pharma volumes ride on its customers' launches ("If the customer’s pharmaceutical product is not approved by regulatory bodies or it is not successful on the market, we may not achieve a future return", 10-K FY2025 Item 1A). I read that as a dependence spread over many customers' drugs rather than a business that lives on its own invention.
- **VERDICT: IN.** The economics of a regulated component maker with five thousand customers can be pictured ten years out, with the customers' drug mix as the main unknown **[M2012-065]**, **[M2011-014]**; the important variables are knowable from the filings **[M2006-076]**.
