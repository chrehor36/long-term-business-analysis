## Q1 — CAN I UNDERSTAND HOW THIS MAKES MONEY? **[E3-31]**
- Unit economics in my own words, no management language: Cisco designs the boxes that move data inside and between buildings (switches, routers, wireless access points, firewalls, optics), writes the software that runs them, has contract manufacturers build them (*"We rely on contract manufacturers for our manufacturing needs"*), sells them mostly through resellers, distributors and integrators to companies, governments and carriers, and then charges every year to support what it sold. FY2026: revenue **$63,325M**, of which product $48,295M (76%: Networking $34,668M, Security $8,232M, Collaboration $4,300M, Observability $1,095M) and services $15,030M (24%). On each $100 of revenue it paid **$35.50** to make and support what it sold (product gross margin 63.2%, services 68.8%), spent **$15.10** on engineering, **$18.30** on selling, **$4.40** on administration and **$2.50** on acquired-intangible amortization and restructuring, and kept **$24.30** of operating income. **The customer pays ahead**: deferred revenue of $29,781M (current $16,988M plus long-term $12,793M) exceeds receivables, inventory and customer-financing receivables together ($7,470M + $5,694M + $8,332M). Plant is small: net property $2,760M on a $129,637M balance sheet, capital expenditures $1,410M. **The owners' capital is mostly purchase price**: goodwill $59,477M and purchased intangibles $7,557M, 52% of total assets, the largest piece Splunk ($25,994M of acquisition cash in FY2024). Profit is (price per box and per support contract, set against rivals' products) x (the volume of network spending) minus an engineering and sales cost that is largely fixed in the short run.
- The scarce input this business controls: **the installed base of Cisco equipment running Cisco software under Cisco support contracts, and the channel that sells and services it.** Services, the renewal stream on that base, earned $15,030M at a 68.8% gross margin; remaining performance obligations are $46,734M. Whether that base is scarce against the other vendors is Q2's question, not Q1's.
- Will the fundamentals look broadly the same in ten years? **The way the money is made, yes; which products make it, not on the record.** The FY1995 10-K describes the same trade (*"The principal competitive factors in the market for internetworking products are performance, price, value-added features, reliability, conformance to industry standards, service, and market presence"*) and the FY2026 10-K sells the same kind of boxes, software and support through the same kind of channel. But the products and the rivals turn over: the FY1998 10-K's competitors were *"Lucent, Nortel, Ericsson, 3Com, Ascend, Cabletron, Fore and IBM"*; the FY2001 10-K's *"Alcatel, Ciena, Ericsson, Extreme Networks, Foundry Networks, Juniper, Lucent, Nortel Networks, Redback Networks, Siemens AG, and Sycamore Networks"*; the FY2026 10-K names eighteen, of which only Ciena appears in 2001's list: *"Amazon Web Services LLC; Arista Networks, Inc.; Broadcom Inc.; Ciena Corporation; CrowdStrike Holdings, Inc.; Datadog Inc.; Dell Technologies Inc.; Dynatrace Inc.; Fortinet, Inc.; Hewlett-Packard Enterprise Company; Huawei Technologies Co., Ltd.; Microsoft Corporation; Nokia Corporation; Nvidia Corporation; Palo Alto Networks, Inc.; RingCentral, Inc.; Zoom Video Communications, Inc.; and Zscaler, Inc."* The filer: *"The industry in which we compete is subject to rapid technological developments, evolving standards, changes in customer requirements, and new product introductions and enhancements."*
- **Simple and stable in character [E3-31]; a decision on the filings [E4-46].** The money-making is simple enough to state from the filings in a paragraph, and nothing in Q1 needs a forecast of technology. **The stability is the open question, and it is a durability question**: the ruling of 2026-09-20 places it at Q2, where [E4-04] is applied as a competence limit (*"Predicting the long-term economics of companies that operate in fast-changing industries is simply far beyond our perimeter"* [E4-58]) and a Q1 OUT is reserved for a business that *"cannot be understood"*.
