## GATE 2 — MOAT
Franchise test [E3-03], from PulteGroup's own 10-K: **"The housing industry
in the U.S. is fragmented and highly competitive. While we are one of the
largest homebuilders in the U.S., our national market share represented
only approximately 4% of U.S. new home sales in 2025. In each of our local
markets, there are numerous national, regional, and local homebuilders with
whom we compete... we also compete with sales of existing house inventory
and any provider of for-sale or rental housing units... We compete primarily
on the basis of location, price, quality, reputation..."**
- **No close substitute**: **FAILS** — the company's own filing states a
  4% national share in a fragmented market and names three substitute
  categories (other builders, existing-home resales, rentals).
- **Unregulated**: no — land-use/zoning is material.
- **Proven by pricing power + returns on capital**: **FAILS** — no
  economies-of-scale or structural cost-advantage claim appears anywhere in
  this section, unlike D.R. Horton's filing (run separately, PASSED at this
  gate on that basis) or NVR's disclosed lot-option capital-efficiency
  model (also run separately, PASSED). PulteGroup's own words describe
  competing on the same generic factors (location, price, quality,
  reputation) every builder competes on, with an explicit concession of
  fragmentation (4% share) rather than a scale claim.

**Class: NONE.** **VERDICT: FAIL — STOP.**
