## GATE 1 — CIRCLE OF COMPETENCE
Analog and embedded-processing semiconductors — chips sold into industrial,
automotive, personal electronics, communications, and enterprise systems markets.
Two reportable product lines (Analog, Embedded Processing) plus Other; both
evaluable — same manufacturing/design economics, no exotic segment (no captive
finance arm, no JV structure). Unit economics: design-in sales model — a TI part
gets specified into a customer's system design, then earns recurring revenue over
that system's multi-year (often 10-15yr, industrial/auto) production life; TI
manufactures ~80% of its own wafers (unusual among fabless-heavy peers), giving
direct control of cost and supply. **PASS.**

## GATE 2 — MOAT
Franchise test [E3-03]:
- Needed/desired: yes — analog/embedded chips are non-substitutable components in
  virtually every electronic system.
- No close substitute: partial — competitors exist (ADI, ON Semi, Infineon,
  STMicro), but design-in switching costs are real (customer requalification of a
  new part in a qualified industrial/auto system is costly and slow).
- Unregulated: yes, though subject to export-control and tariff risk (filed: 4
  "geopolitical" references, 8 "China" references in risk factors).
- Proven by pricing power + returns on capital: **filed 20-year record — TI has
  not printed a single operating-loss year since at least 2007**, spanning the
  2008-09 GFC (op margin still 14.3% GFC trough), the 2015-16 correction, 2020
  COVID, and the current 2022-24 post-COVID inventory downcycle (op margin fell
  from 50.6% in FY2022 to 34.9% in FY2024 — real compression, but still solidly
  profitable at the cycle trough). This is the opposite fact pattern from this
  week's memory-sector runs (MU: a $5.8B loss year in the same window).
- Company's own words [10-K FY2025, Item 7]: **"The four sustainable competitive
  advantages are a strong foundation of manufacturing and technology, a broad
  portfolio of analog and embedded processing products, the reach of our market
  channels, and diversity and longevity of our products, markets and customer
  positions. In combination, these four competitive advantages provide tangible
  benefits, are difficult to replicate and ultimately separate us from our best
  peers."**
- Counter-evidence, recorded honestly: filed risk factor names a live threat —
  **"we may face increased competition as a result of China actively promoting
  and reshaping its domestic semiconductor industry through policy changes and
  investment, which could prevent us from competing effectively."** China is a
  meaningful revenue geography; margin compression since 2022 is consistent with
  both the disclosed capex/D&A ramp AND this competitive pressure — the filed
  record doesn't let me cleanly separate the two, so both are carried forward as
  live risk, not resolved as pure cyclicality.
**Class: NARROW** (real, filed, multi-cycle evidence of durability — but live
erosion risk (China) and post-2022 margin compression are large enough that WIDE
is not supportable on the current record; this is a downgrade from what the
"never posted a loss" fact alone would suggest, mirroring the same discipline
applied against Home Depot/Sherwin-Williams-style ordinary-debt names this month).
**VERDICT: PASS.**
