## Q1 — CAN I UNDERSTAND HOW THIS MAKES MONEY? **[E3-31]**
- **Unit economics in my own words, no management language.** J & J makes cheap, frozen, ready-to-heat snacks in its own plants and sells them to the places that heat and sell them one at a time (stadiums, cinemas, convenience stores, warehouse-club snack bars, school cafeterias, fast-food chains) and, to a lesser extent, in boxes to supermarkets. The products: soft pretzels (18% of FY2025 sales), frozen novelties including Dippin' Dots, Luigi's and Whole Fruit (16%), churros (6%), handheld sandwiches, mostly private label (7%), bakery goods, mostly private label and contract packing (*"fig and fruit bars, cookies, breads, rolls, crumb, muffins, and donuts"*, 27%), and frozen beverages under ICEE and Slush Puppie (14%), plus the sale and repair of the machines (9%). Two things are distinctive in how the money is made. First, **J & J owns and places the equipment the customer sells from** (*"The Company retains ownership of the equipment placed in customer locations, and as a result, customers are not required to make an investment in equipment"*; marketing equipment at cost $319.6M at 2026-06-27), so a placement ties the counter to its product. Second, **in ICEE it runs a service route**: it installs dispensers, *"services the machines, arranges to supply customers with ingredients"* and repairs customer-owned machines, from *"172 Company managed warehouse and distribution facilities located in 45 states, Mexico, and Canada"*, across *"approximately 132,000 Company-owned and customer-owned dispensers"*. FY2025: net sales **$1,583.2M**, gross margin **29.7%**, operating income **$84.3M (5.3%)** after $24.1M of plant closures and a $10.6M insurance gain, net earnings $65.6M. By segment: Food Service $1,001.4M of sales and $64.8M of segment operating income, Retail Supermarkets $213.8M and $13.3M, Frozen Beverages $368.1M and $49.5M, less $29.9M of corporate cost (10-K FY2025, segment table). The money is the gap between a gross margin of about 30% on flour, sugar, shortening, cocoa, cheese, packaging and plant labour, and about 23-24% of sales spent on selling, freight and overhead. About 4,600 employees and 600 agency workers.
- **The scarce input this business controls.** The candidates, stated before Q2 tests them: (a) **placements**, the ovens, warmers, display cases and 24,000 company-owned frozen-beverage dispensers standing in customers' outlets; (b) **the ICEE service route**, 172 depots servicing 132,000 dispensers in three countries; (c) **brands** it owns (SUPERPRETZEL, ICEE in the United States, Mexico and Canada, Dippin' Dots, Luigi's, Whole Fruit, ¡Hola!; *"The trademarks, when renewed and continuously used, have an indefinite term"*), and (d) **scale in soft pretzels** (*"We believe we are the largest manufacturer of soft pretzels in the United States"*; *"one of the only national distributors of soft pretzels"*). Whether any of these lets J & J set its price, rather than accept it, is Q2's question. The inputs it does not control are named by the filer: *"raw materials which are readily available from numerous sources"* bought *"mainly in the open market"*, and syrup bought *"primarily from The Coca-Cola Company, Keurig Dr. Pepper, Inc., the Pepsi Cola Company, and Jogue, Inc."*
