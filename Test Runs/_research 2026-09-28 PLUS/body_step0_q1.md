## Q1 — CAN I UNDERSTAND HOW THIS MAKES MONEY? **[E3-31]**
- **Unit economics in my own words, no management language.** ePlus buys other companies' networking, security, storage, server and software products (Cisco was 29% of FY2026 net sales, 32% in FY2025 and 44% in FY2024; NetApp, HPE, Juniper, Dell and Arista another 23-28%), mostly after a customer's purchase order and often drop-shipped from the maker or from a distributor (*"our three largest distributors, Ingram Micro, Arrow Electronics, and TD SYNNEX, collectively accounted for over 30% of our purchases"*), and resells them to about 4,200 mid-sized and large organizations, with Verizon alone 24% of FY2026 net sales and 36% of trade receivables at 2026-03-31. It keeps the difference between the maker's price to it (less the maker's rebates, *"volume incentive programs, shared support programs, and shared marketing expense programs"*) and what the customer pays: in FY2026 **gross profit of $616.1M on gross billings of $3,838.5M, 16.1 cents a dollar billed** (25.2% of net sales, because $214.5M of third-party maintenance and subscriptions is booked net, as agent). Around the resale it sells labour: design, installation, staff augmentation and project work (professional services, 38.7% gross margin) and monitoring and support contracts (managed services, 29.8%). Its costs are mainly sales commissions and salaried engineers (SG&A $423.4M, 2,148 employees, 705 of them in sales); it owns almost no plant (capital purchases $4.4M in FY2026). **Operating income $166.1M, 6.8% of net sales and 4.3% of gross billings.** It is paid for goods it has often not yet paid for (trade payables and a floor plan facility that pays the vendor, repaid *"generally 45 to 60 days from the invoice date"*), and it waits on customers with *"payment terms greater than or equal to net 60 days"*, so the cash moves with the timing of a few large orders.
- **The scarce input this business controls.** The candidates, stated before Q2 tests them: (a) **the vendor authorisations and certifications** (*"We hold various technical and IT product sales-related certifications from leading vendors and software publishers, which authorizes us to market their products"*; the claim to be *"the only partner in North America with both NVIDIA® DGX SuperPOD™ Specialization and DGX-Ready Managed Services Provider status"*); (b) **about a thousand engineers and the customer relationships they carry**; (c) **the balance sheet** to carry customers' receivables (*"Our scale and financial resources enable us to continue investing in engineering and technology resources"*). The filer grants the authorisations, not ePlus, and says so: *"The loss of a key vendor or changes in its policies could adversely impact our financial results."* Whether any of them lets ePlus set its price is Q2's question.
- **Will the fundamentals look broadly the same in ten years?** The trade, yes; the mix, no, and the filer says so. The FY2005 10-K describes the same activity (*"IT Sales: We are an authorized reseller of leading IT hardware and software products and have technical support personnel to support sales and implementations"*), the FY2014 10-K lists *"direct marketing of information technology equipment, third-party software and third-party maintenance and services"*, and the technology segment's sales and income are filed every year since FY2009; the reseller spread and the services attached to it are the same unit economics in FY2009 and FY2026. What changes is what is resold and how it is booked: *"the legacy resale model has continued to shift from an upfront sale to a recurring revenue model"*, *"increasing revenues recognized on a net basis"*, vendors *"building and launching their own adoption and managed services"*. None of that requires predicting technology; it is a question of whether the middleman keeps its cut, which is Q2's.
- **Relatively simple and stable in character [E3-31]? A decision in five minutes [E4-46]?** Yes: a reseller with a services arm. Revenue is billings less what is booked net; margin is the vendor discount and rebates plus labour. The financing arm, the part that was harder to read (lease residuals, non-recourse notes), is sold, and the $0.4M of retained international leases is immaterial. Nothing requires months of study; whether the spread is protected is Q2's question, and what the business earns in cash after its working capital is Q4's.
