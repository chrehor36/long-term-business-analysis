## Q2 — IS IT A FRANCHISE? **[E3-03]**

*Every ePlus figure below is read from its filed annual reports FY2009 to FY2026 (the segment note of each 10-K, the MD&A tables of the FY2026 10-K and of the 8-K recast of 2026-01-26; accessions in Step 0 and `k_list.txt`) or from SEC companyfacts checked against the FY2026 statements. Scripts and outputs, all in `Test Runs/_research 2026-09-28 PLUS/`: `segx.py` (`segx_out.txt`, the technology-segment rows as filed), `q2_series.py` (`q2_series.txt`, the assembled series), `pricegrep.py` (`price_sentences.txt`, every pricing and competition sentence in every 10-K FY2005-FY2026), `sgrep.py` (sentence search), `peers.py` (`peers_out.txt`).*

**The question this file has to answer: is ePlus's spread its own, or is it the price the makers and the customers allow a middleman?** A reseller of Cisco, NetApp, HPE, Dell, Arista and NVIDIA equipment that has earned money every year since FY2009, whose operating margin has roughly doubled over that span, and which earns about a quarter of its operating capital pre-tax, is the kind of record that can hide a moat or a borrowed one. **The two hypotheses, stated so they can be attacked [E4-26].** *(a) A service franchise: top vendor certifications, the only North American partner holding NVIDIA's two named designations, a thousand engineers embedded in 4,200 customers, services growing faster than product, margins that rose from 2.9% (FY2010) to 6.8% (FY2026) and returns on capital that would be high in any industry.* *(b) A middleman in a trade the filer calls price-competitive, selling other companies' products that any authorised reseller or the maker itself can sell, to customers on 30-day non-exclusive contracts, one of whom takes a quarter of the sales, whose margin moves with product mix, accounting presentation and shortages, and whose returns on capital are the reseller class's, not its own.* Each is tested on the filings below.

**How the filer describes competition and price, in its own words.**
- **Competition (10-K FY2026, Item 1):** *"The market for IT solutions is highly competitive, subject to macro-economic cycles, and the entry of new competitors."* *"Some of our competitors are direct marketers with little value-add and sell products as commodities, which can place downward pressure on product pricing. In addition, many vendors may sell directly to our customers, and our continued ability to compete effectively may be affected by the strategies and business practices of such vendors. Also, some distributors have or are creating entities which can resell directly to customers and hold SLED contracts. Some vendors are building and launching their own adoption and managed services to better ensure customer satisfaction and retainment."*
- **The risk factor, the same sentence for twenty years:** FY2006, *"Many competitors compete principally on the basis of price and may have lower costs than us and, therefore, current gross margins may not be maintainable."*; FY2009 and FY2016, *"... may have lower costs or accept lower selling prices than us and, therefore, current gross margins may not be maintainable"*; FY2018, FY2024 and FY2026, *"Many competitors compete principally based on price and may have lower costs or accept lower selling prices than we do and, therefore, our gross margins may not be maintainable."* (`price_sentences.txt`).
- **The customers' terms:** *"The contracts for the provision of products and services from us to our customers are generally non-exclusive agreements without volume purchase commitments and are terminable by either party upon 30 days’ notice"* (every 10-K since FY2009 in substance). *"Sales to Verizon Communications Inc. represented 24%, 17%, and 19% of our net sales"* (FY2026, FY2025, FY2024); Verizon was 36% of trade receivables at 2026-03-31.
- **Price given away, by year:** FY2012 and FY2013, *"Our gross margin was affected by the mix between product and services, vendor incentives earned, and competitive pricing pressures."*; FY2015 and FY2019, *"we face pressure from our customers for competitive pricing and contract terms"*; FY2009 to FY2013, a downturn *"could cause our current and potential customers to ... delay or reduce technology purchases and result in ... increased price competition"*; **FY2025, *"We are experiencing pricing pressure and project delays within our enterprise accounts impacting our gross profit"***, and the product-margin sentence of that year, *"partially offset by lower margins to certain enterprise customers."*
- **Who sets the cost of the goods:** *"We rely on a small number of key vendors and third parties, and do not have long-term supply or guaranteed price agreements"*; Cisco *"approximately 29%, 32%, and 44% of net sales"*; the margin is partly the vendors' rebates, *"consideration pursuant to volume incentive programs, shared support programs, and shared marketing expense programs. These programs are usually of finite terms and may not be renewed or may be discontinued or changed in ways that adversely affect us"*; *"Vendor consideration earned as a percentage of sales for the year ended March 31, 2026 increased by 30 basis points."* And the dependency stated plainly: *"The loss of a key vendor or changes in its policies could adversely impact our financial results."*

Third, **the dollars depend on a few counterparties the filer does not control**: one maker at 29-44% of sales, one customer at 17-24%, three distributors at over 30% of purchases.

**[E3-03], criterion by criterion.** *"An economic franchise arises from a product or service that: (1) is needed or desired; (2) is thought by its customers to have no close substitute and; (3) is not subject to price regulation."*
- **(1) Needed or desired [x]: passes.** Organisations must buy and run network, security and storage equipment.
- **(3) Not subject to price regulation [x]: passes.** Prices are negotiated; SLED sales run through public contracts, which are procurement rules, not price regulation of ePlus.
- **(2) Thought by its customers to have no close substitute: FAILS, on the filer's own words and on its customers' terms.** The product is another company's (a Cisco switch is the same switch from any authorised reseller, from a distributor's direct entity, or from Cisco); the filer says competitors *"sell products as commodities"*, *"compete principally based on price"* and *"accept lower selling prices than we do"*, that *"many vendors may sell directly to our customers"*, and that its customer contracts are *"non-exclusive ... without volume purchase commitments and are terminable by either party upon 30 days’ notice."* A customer who can leave on thirty days' notice and buy the identical product elsewhere has a close substitute by construction; the largest customer, at a quarter of sales, holds the price.

**[E3-43]'s demonstration, *"a company’s ability to regularly price its product or service aggressively and thereby to earn high rates of return on capital"*, and its other door: *"In contrast, “a business” earns exceptional profits only if it is the low-cost operator or if supply of its product or service is tight. Tightness in supply usually does not last long."***
- **The pricing half: NOT MET.** ePlus does not set the price of what it sells; the maker sets the list and the discount, the rebate programmes are the maker's *"of finite terms"*, and the filer reports price pressure (FY2012, FY2013, FY2015, FY2019, FY2025), never price leadership. Gross profit on billings of 15-16 cents a dollar, with the vendor's rebate inside it, is a commission, not an aggressive price.
- **The return half: MET in the number, NOT MET as the passage means it.** Operating income on operating capital (equity less cash, no debt) was **24.7% in FY2025 and 25.2% in FY2026** on the continuing business, and 42% on tangible operating capital in FY2026. That is a high return, and it is recorded below as the strongest evidence against. But [E3-43] reads the return as the *consequence* of pricing (*"and thereby"*); here the return is the consequence of a balance sheet that the makers and customers finance (trade payables of $264.6M, a floor plan of $119.7M that pays the vendor and is repaid *"45 to 60 days from the invoice date"*, $251.1M of deferred revenue), and it is the class's return, not ePlus's: in the row below PC Connection earns 13-15% and Insight 13-17% on the same construction with no pricing claim at all, and CDW, the largest, 21-24%.
- **The low-cost door: NOT ePlus's.** ePlus's own risk factor says the price competitors *"may have lower costs"*. At the scale end of the trade CDW (net sales $22.4bn against ePlus's $2.4bn) earned 7.4-7.9% on revenue in 2023-2025 against ePlus's 5.0-6.8% in FY2024-FY2026; nothing in the filings claims a cost advantage over it.
- **The tight-supply door: OPEN, and the recent peaks came through it.** FY2022-FY2023 (the semiconductor shortage and backlog) and FY2026 (the memory shortage and *"rapid price increases"*) are the three years at 6.3% or more; FY2025, between the shortages, fell to 5.0% under *"pricing pressure"*. *"Tightness in supply usually does not last long."* The filer itself expects the present one to last *"at least the next few quarters"*.

**[E2-58], the commodity doctrine, read carefully.** A reseller's "product" is the service of supplying another maker's goods; the filer calls its rivals' version of it a commodity (*"sell products as commodities"*) and prices it as a commission. *"In many industries, differentiation simply can’t be made meaningful. A few producers in such industries may consistently do well if they have a cost advantage that is both wide and sustainable."* ePlus does well consistently in the sense of never losing money since FY2009; the filings show no wide cost advantage to explain it, and the explanation the filings do support is the asset-light structure the whole class shares (row below).

**[E2-44], the two characteristics.**
- **(1) *"an ability to increase prices rather easily (even when product demand is flat and capacity is not fully utilized) without fear of significant loss of either market share or unit volume"*: FAILS.** FY2025 is the test the passage describes: demand soft (net sales −8.2%, gross billings −1.5%), and the filer reports *"pricing pressure"* and *"lower margins to certain enterprise customers"*; operating income fell 25.5%. Prices went down to hold customers, not up.
- **(2) *"an ability to accommodate large dollar volume increases in business ... with only minor additional investment of capital"*: FAILS in the working capital, passes in the plant.** Plant is negligible (capital purchases $4.4-7.7M a year). But growth consumes receivables and inventory: in FY2026, gross billings +17.0%, and operating working capital (receivables, inventories, deferred costs and other current assets, less payables, the floor plan, salaries, deferred revenue and other liabilities) rose from about $32M to about $296M, so operating cash of the continuing business was **−$117.4M** in a year of record operating income. *(Constraint 3 of [E2-23], carried to Q4.)*

**The remaining Q2 tests.**
- **[E3-46], the return question asked of the business: yes in the number** (above); answered by the row and by [E3-43]'s *"thereby"*.
- **[E4-32], direction: level.** Operating margin in a 4-7% band for fifteen years with its recent highs on shortages; Cisco's share falling from 44% to 29% in two years is diversification of the maker it depends on, not widening of anything ePlus owns. **[E4-55], units:** a reseller has no physical unit series; gross billings, the filer's volume measure, were $3,329.8M, $3,280.4M and $3,838.5M in FY2024-FY2026.
- **[E3-33] untapped pricing power / [E5-28]: not claimed.** A near-monopoly is the opposite of what the filer describes.
- **[E4-37], the agony metric:** the filer does not raise prices; it reports *"pricing pressure"* and rebates *"of finite terms"*. The question is asked of the vendors, not of ePlus.
- **[E2-53], dominance: FAILS.** No customer, product line or region is claimed as dominated; the largest customer is 24% of ePlus's sales and ePlus is not claimed to be a comparable share of Verizon's purchases.
- **[E2-45], the attacker's test.** An attacker with capital and engineers needs the makers' authorisations, which the makers grant to many (the filer's rivals are *"local, regional, national, and international firms, including vendors, consulting firms ... resellers, and service providers"*), and the makers themselves are the most dangerous attackers: *"many vendors may sell directly to our customers"*, and *"Some vendors are building and launching their own adoption and managed services."*
- **[E4-23], key-person dependence:** not found as a moat defect; the filer names succession *"particularly for the Chief Executive Officer"* as a risk, and nothing in the record ties the margin to one person.
- **[E4-36], which cause of success.** The steady part is an extreme of good performance over many small factors in a price-competitive trade (a clean balance sheet, a services mix, customer service); the peaks are wave-riding on shortages. **[E3-51]**: the waves (cloud, security, AI infrastructure) belong to the makers; the reseller rides whichever the makers ship.
- **[E3-62], who keeps the gains.** The makers' product improvements flow to the customer and to the maker; the reseller's cut is a percentage set by the maker's programme, which is why the filer lists *"the reduction of vendor incentive programs"* among the causes of its quarterly fluctuations.
- **[E4-04]:** not applied. ePlus resells technology but its unit economics do not depend on predicting which technology wins; the rule is a competence limit for durability that cannot be judged, and here the filings answer the franchise question directly.

### THE STRONGEST EVIDENCE AGAINST THIS VERDICT, stated as its holders would state it [E4-51, E4-26, E3-47]
The verdict below is OUT, so the case for IN is set out first, at full strength:
1. **The returns are high by any standard.** 25% pre-tax on operating capital in FY2025 and FY2026 with no debt, 42% on tangible capital in FY2026; [E3-46] says *"the best businesses, by definition, are going to be businesses that earn very high returns on capital employed over time"*, and this one has.
2. **It has never lost money in eighteen years**, including the 2009 recession and the 2020 pandemic, and its operating margin has more than doubled (2.9% in FY2010, 6.8% in FY2026) while net sales rose almost fivefold; its margin sits above four of the five SEC peers.
3. **It is more than a box-shifter.** Professional and managed services were $462.9M of FY2026 net sales at 29.8-38.7% gross margins, up from $292.1M two years earlier; it claims to be *"the only partner in North America with both NVIDIA® DGX SuperPOD™ Specialization and DGX-Ready Managed Services Provider status"*; twenty percent of its employees have ten or more years of tenure; customers stay (Verizon has been 15-24% of net sales in every year FY2020-FY2026).
4. **The makers need resellers.** The filer's trade exists because makers such as Cisco sell mostly through partners, and a partner with top certifications and engineers is not easily replaced by a price-cutter.

**Answered.** (1) is accepted as a fact and weighed with [E3-43]'s *"and thereby"*: the return comes from other people's money (payables, floor plan and deferred revenue fund most of the working assets), not from price, and the row shows the whole class earning 9-45% on the same construction at margins of 3-8%, including a distributor whose margin has been negative. (2) is accepted and weighed with the filer's explanation of the recent margins (mix, net-basis presentation, vendor consideration) and with the shortages it names in FY2022, FY2023 and FY2026; between them, FY2025, the filer reported *"pricing pressure"* and operating income fell a quarter. Consistency is a Q4 fact about survival, not a Q2 fact about price. (3) is accepted and is the best of the case; but the services are labour sold at cost-plus in a *"competitive labor market"*, and the NVIDIA designations are the maker's to grant and to multiply. (4) is accepted about the channel and weighed with the unit of the test [E4-08]: the makers need resellers, not this reseller; the filer's own sentence is that *"many vendors may sell directly to our customers"*. **[E3-47]** (a wrongly closed file inside the circle is the costliest error) was weighed: the file is inside the circle, and it closes on the business's own twenty-year sentence that its competitors *"compete principally based on price"* and that its *"gross margins may not be maintainable"*, which is the evidence [E3-03]'s second criterion asks for.

**The analyst's own incentive, stated [operator rule 9, E4-27, E3-41].** The previous names in the order file (ARCB, JNJ, HWKN, KWR) closed at Q2, a pull toward OUT by analogy, and a reseller "obviously" looks like a middleman, a pull toward OUT by stereotype; against both, the record is unusually clean (no losses, no debt, high returns), a pull toward IN by admiration. The verdict rests on the filer's own sentences across twenty 10-Ks, on its segment series as filed, and on a row of five SEC filers; the case for IN was written first and at full strength.
