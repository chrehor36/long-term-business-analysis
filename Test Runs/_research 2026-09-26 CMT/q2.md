**The hypothesis I formed while reading, stated so it can be attacked [E4-26]:** *Core Molding moulds parts to its customers' designs, for a handful of truck and recreational-vehicle makers who award each programme by competitive bid, demand and receive price reductions, and move the next programme to another moulder when it suits them. Its own 10-K has said so in the same words for twenty years. That is a supplier, not a franchise, and twenty-two years of 5% operating margins say the same.* The evidence against it is hunted first and answered before the verdict.

**[E3-03], criterion by criterion.** *"An economic franchise arises from a product or service that: (1) is needed or desired; (2) is thought by its customers to have no close substitute and; (3) is not subject to price regulation."* The same 1991 passage: *"In contrast, "a business" earns exceptional profits only if it is the low-cost operator or if supply of its product or service is tight."* **[E3-43]**

- **(1) Needed or desired [x]: passes.** Truck hoods, roofs and fenders, snowmobile and watercraft bodies, utility enclosures: $273.8M of sales in FY2025.
- **(3) Not subject to price regulation [x]: passes.** No filing read places Core Molding's prices under a regulator. Prices are set in contracts with OEMs (below).
- **(2) No close substitute [ ]: FAILS, in the registrant's own words every year for twenty years, and in what its customers did.**
  - *The customer runs a competition and takes the price down.* Item 1A, in every 10-K from FY2006 to FY2025 (checked with whitespace normalised): *"We are primarily a components supplier to large original equipment manufacturers ("OEMs") that are able to exert considerable pressure on components suppliers to reduce costs, improve quality, and provide additional design and engineering capabilities. OEMs continue to demand and receive price reductions and measurable increases in quality through their use of competitive selection processes, rating programs, and various other arrangements."* And (FY2025): *"OEMs may also seek to save costs by purchasing components from suppliers that are geographically closer to their production facilities or relocating production to locations with lower cost structures and purchasing components from suppliers with lower production costs."* And: *"OEMs may choose to purchase from these alternative suppliers, and as a result the Company may lose existing business or not qualify for new business with OEMs."*
  - *The product is not the company's.* Item 1, every 10-K from FY2006 to FY2025: *"Our manufacturing facilities utilize various production processes; however, end products are similar and are not unique to a facility or customer base."* The design is the OEM's; the mould is built for the OEM's programme and billed to it as tooling. *"the Company does not believe that any single patent, trade name, or trademark or related group of such rights is materially important to its business or its ability to compete."*
  - *The competition is on cost, with named rivals.* Item 1A, every 10-K FY2006-FY2025: *"We compete with a number of other manufacturers that produce and sell similar products. Our products primarily compete on the basis of capability, product quality, cost, and delivery. Some of our competitors have greater financial resources, research and development facilities, design engineering, manufacturing, and marketing capabilities."* Item 1 (FY2025): *"The Company faces competition from a number of other molders including, most significantly, Molded Fiber Glass Companies, CSP, Ashley Industrial Molding, René Matériaux Composite Ltée ("RMC"), STS Group, and 20/20 Custom Molded Plastics."*
  - *Even the founding customer's supply was conditional on staying competitive on cost.* The Navistar/International supply agreement, in every 10-K from FY2006 to FY2019: the company is *"the primary supplier of International's original equipment and service requirements for fiberglass reinforced parts using the SMC process, as long as the Company remains competitive in cost, quality, and delivery"* (FY2006 wording). The clause is the customer telling the supplier it has close substitutes.
  - *What the customers did.* **Freightliner**, a major customer in FY2006, fell below 10% of sales in 2007 (*"in 2007 Freightliner's individual sales represented less than ten percent"*, FY2007 10-K) and is not named as a major customer again; the filings read do not say why, and 2007 was a truck downturn, so it is recorded as turnover, not as re-sourcing. **Volvo**, a major customer from FY2015 (16% of 2023 sales), *"transitioned from production programs that the Company supplied to new programs that the Company does not support"* (FY2025 10-K), falling to 4% of 2025 sales; its sales were $51.6M (2023), $58.2M (2024) and $41.0M (2025), and the registrant says it *"continues to actively bid for new Volvo business"*. **International's** product sales fell from $71.4M (2023) to $43.9M (2025), and **PACCAR's** from $38.5M (2024) to $29.6M (2025), in a truck downturn. Universal Forest Products was a major customer in FY2018 only. **Volvo is the one case where the filing says in terms that the business moved to programmes the company does not supply.** Item 1A: *"Sales to five customers constituted approximately 65% of our 2025 total sales"*; four or five customers were 62-87% of sales in every year FY2013-FY2025.

**The demonstration clause, both halves, on twenty-two years of the filer's figures.** [E3-43]: the conditions *"will be demonstrated by a company's ability to regularly price its product or service aggressively and thereby to earn high rates of return on capital."*

*Half one: pricing.* **[E2-44]'s first characteristic fails in the registrant's own words.** Item 1A (FY2025): *"In order to obtain new business in a competitive environment, the Company enters into long-term contracts that fix the customer product price and requires the Company to accept all product orders pursuant to such contracts. These fixed price customer contracts allow for certain price increases but may not provide for recovery of all of the Company's cost increases."* The MD&A: *"The Company has certain contractual commitments that restrict its ability to pass through changes in input costs to certain customers."* The gross-margin bridges in the MD&A show the lag: in 2021 (FY2021 10-K) *"net changes in selling price and raw material co st of 4.8%"* [sic: a split word in the extracted text, not smoothed] **cut** the gross margin as resin and glass rose; it took 2022 (+2.5 points), 2023 (+5.3), 2024 (+2.3) and 2025 (+0.8) of *"net changes in selling price and raw material cost"* to recover it, while large-compression-press utilisation fell from 89% (2022) to 50% (2025). The company's own investor deck (June 2026, 8-K `0001026655-26-000041`) names the 2019-2020 pricing action as *"Pricing • Fix or get out, contract adjustments"*: repricing under losses, which is re-negotiation, not [E2-44]'s *"ability to increase prices rather easily ... without fear of significant loss of either market share or unit volume"*. [E2-44]'s second characteristic (growth with *"only minor additional investment of capital"*) is not met either: capital spending ran 1.26 times depreciation over FY2006-FY2025 (2.1 times in FY2006-FY2010), and the 2026 plan is *"approximately $25,000,000 to $30,000,000"* of capex (10-Q) against about $12M of depreciation and amortisation, for the Mexico expansion that carries the new Volvo roof programme.

**What the series says.** Across FY2004-FY2025 the operating margin averaged 5.8% (median 6.3%), ranged from −4.1% to 11.8%, and summed to 5.3% of sales; gross margin never exceeded 20.8%. Pre-tax operating income on average equity averaged 14.1% over FY2010-FY2025 (median 14.3%), with two loss years. [E3-46] asks whether the business earns *"very high returns on capital employed over time"*: it earns middling ones, with a cycle through zero. **These are the returns of a competent supplier in good years, given back in bad ones, not those of a business that prices aggressively.**

**The remaining Q2 tests.**
- **[E3-33] / [E5-28], untapped pricing power: no.** Claiming it would be claiming *"a monopoly or a near monopoly"*; six named moulders, OEM competitive selection, fixed-price contracts.
- **[E4-37], the agony metric.** The agony is written into the contracts: they *"may not provide for recovery of all of the Company's cost increases"*; the 2021 lag took four years to recover.
- **[E2-45], the attacker's test.** An attacker does not need to build a franchise: it needs presses, a plant near the OEM, and a lower bid at the next programme award, which is how the registrant says it wins business itself (*"resourcing of existing structural products from another supplier by an original equipment manufacturer"*).
- **[E2-53], the dominance class.** *"one of the largest compounders and molders of thermoset and thermoplastic structural products in North America"* (Item 1) is size among moulders, not a position the customer cannot route around.
- **[E3-62], the second step.** Where the savings go: the registrant says OEMs *"demand and receive price reductions"* and *"may also seek to save costs by purchasing components from suppliers ... with lower production costs"*. The savings of the Mexico expansion (*"creating transportation savings"*, June 2026 deck) meet a customer that bids the next programme; *"All of the advantages from great improvements are going to flow through to the customers."*
- **[E4-32], direction.** Gross margin from 7.6% (2019) to 17-18% (2023-2025): up since the 2019 loss. **[E4-55]** weights the physical series: large-compression-press utilisation 55% (2020), 85%, 89%, 83%, 73%, **50% (2025)**; large-injection-press utilisation 89% (2020) to **46% (2025)** (Item 1, FY2021-FY2025 10-Ks). Product sales fell from $347.4M (2023) to $232.2M (2025).
- **[E4-36] / [E3-51], which cause of success.** The good years (2006, 2011, 2015, 2022-2023) are truck-cycle years; the record is a cycle, not a maximised variable the company owns.
- **[E4-04] and [E4-23].** Not the ground. The business is maintained, not rebuilt from zero each generation, though each programme is re-won; no key-person dependence is recorded beyond the generic *"Our success largely depends on the efforts and abilities of our key personnel"* (Item 1A).
- **The unit of the test [E4-08].** One reporting segment and one reporting unit (*"one operating segment as a molder of thermoplastic and thermoset structural products"*). Truck, powersports, building products and industrial markets are sold the same way, by programme to OEMs; the non-truck awards of 2026 (*"approximately 65% originated from markets outside of our traditional truck and powersports sectors"*, release of 2026-08-04) are the same kind of award.

**THE STRONGEST EVIDENCE AGAINST THIS VERDICT, hunted hardest [E4-26, E3-41, E4-51].**
1. **Management says the opposite.** The investor deck furnished 2026-06-08 (and in 2025-11-17 and 2026-03-20) lists, under *"Why Invest in Core"*: *"Competitive Moat, Sole Sourced, High Barriers to Entry"*, *"Long-term relationships with blue-chip companies providing single-source manufacturing arrangements with key customers"*, and *"Specialized Ultra Large Molding Capability"*.
2. **Thirty years with the founding customer.** International (Navistar) has been the largest or second-largest customer since 1996; Yamaha and PACCAR for most of the span.
3. **Volvo came back.** The same deck: *"Won $150M contract for Volvo roofs to be launched in our Matamoros facility, with production starting in Q1 of 2027"*, *"Contractual agreement aligned to return on capital employed"*, with two new 4,500-ton presses to make Core *"the #1 sleeper roof provided in North America"*.
4. **The margin recovery 2019-2025.** Gross margin from 7.6% to 17-18%, with price and material adding 0.8-5.3 points a year FY2022-FY2025, while utilisation fell.
5. **[E3-47]**: a wrongly closed file on an understood business is the costliest error, and this business is understood.

**Answered.** (1) A sole-source arrangement lasts for a programme and is re-bid at the next one; the registrant's own risk factors, not a deck, say how the award is made (*"competitive selection processes"*) and how the price moves after it (*"demand and receive price reductions"*). Management's words in a furnished deck are a claim; the filed Item 1A under the same management is the evidence. (2) Thirty years with International came under a supply agreement that held *"as long as the Company remains competitive in cost, quality, and delivery"*: a long relationship on the customer's terms, and International's product purchases fell by 39% in two years. (3) Volvo returning through a competitive award, with Core committing $25M of capital before the first roof ships, is the programme cycle itself: the supplier invests, the OEM's contract sets the return, and the previous Volvo programmes went elsewhere. The award is not yet in production and not in any figure here. (4) The recovery took the gross margin back to the 15-21% it earned in FY2004-FY2015, not above it; the operating margin in FY2023-FY2025 (5.2-7.4%) sits inside its twenty-two-year range and below FY2006, FY2011 and FY2015. That is a repair after a loss (the deck's own words: *"Fix or get out, contract adjustments"*), not [E3-43]'s *"regularly price ... aggressively"*. (5) I weighed [E3-47]. The file closes on the product and the customer, not on the price or the manager: **[E5-42] keeps the judgments apart**, and nothing here says Core Molding is badly run; it says the customer holds the price.

**Why OUT and not the perimeter close (UNKNOWABLE at Q2), and why the incomplete row does not make it UNRESEARCHED.** The 2026-09-20 ruling reserves UNKNOWABLE for *"A name that passes [E3-03] and whose durability cannot be judged from filings"*. Core Molding does not pass [E3-03]: criterion (2) fails in its own Item 1 and Item 1A, in the same words every year from FY2006 to FY2025, in its founding customer's supply terms, and in its customers' re-sourcing. The missing peer margins could only matter if a rival showed that moulders in this trade earn franchise returns; no rival's filing could make the product one that its customers think has no close substitute, when the customers' own purchasing conduct is on the registrant's record. The row's gap is recorded, not used to hold the file open.
