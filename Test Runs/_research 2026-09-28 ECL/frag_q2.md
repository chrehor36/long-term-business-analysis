### THE STRONGEST EVIDENCE AGAINST THIS VERDICT, stated as its holders would state it [E4-51, E4-26, E3-47]
The verdict below is IN, so the case for OUT is set out first, at full strength.
1. *"The price is cost pass-through, with a lag, which is the commodity signature. When raw materials rose in 2021-2022 Ecolab's gross margin fell six points (43.9% in 2019 to 37.8% in 2022) and took until 2024 to recover. The filer says so itself: 2021, 'the 1.8 percentage point positive impact from accelerating pricing was more than offset by the 3.4 percentage point negative impact of significantly higher delivered product costs'; 2022, 'the 9.0 percentage point positive impacts of accelerating pricing was more than offset by the 12.7 percentage point negative impacts of higher delivered product costs'. Nalco, the water half of today's Ecolab, told its own shareholders in its last 10-K that its growth was measured 'excluding price increases designed to only offset higher raw material costs'. A franchise sets the price; this business chases its costs."* **Answer:** accepted as the fact of 2021-2022, and it is the best fact against the verdict. The question [E2-44] asks is the other half: what happens to price when costs and demand fall. In 2009 volume fell 3% and price rose 3%, delivered product costs declined *"on a year over year basis during the second half of 2009"*, and the U.S. cleaning business's margin rose from 16.2% to 18.6% on *"Increased pricing, favorable raw material costs and cost savings actions"* (FY2009 annual report); the price was kept when the cost went away. In 2015-2016, with oil and its derivatives falling (the FY2014 annual report: *"lower oil prices will provide benefits across our segments in the form of lower raw material costs"*), every non-energy segment still took +1% to +2% and the adjusted gross margin rose (*"our 2016 adjusted gross margin was 48.0% compared against a 2015 adjusted gross margin of 47.3%. The increase was driven primarily by lower delivered product costs"*, FY2016 10-K). In 2020 the Institutional segment lost 21% of its volume and took +2% of price. A pass-through business gives the price back when the cost falls; this one did not in any filed year, outside the segment it has since split off. The 2021-2022 lag is recorded as the reversal test (Q6): a gross margin that does not recover after the next cost shock.
2. *"The chief competitor says entry is easy. JohnsonDiversey's 2009 10-K: 'Barriers to entry and expansion in the institutional and industrial cleaning, sanitation and hygiene industry are low', 'the numerous smaller competitors in our industry account for more than 75% of the global market', and 3M, Procter & Gamble, Clorox and Kimberly-Clark sell into the same customers. Ecolab's own 10-K: 'numerous smaller regional or local competitors'. That is not 'no close substitute'."* **Answer:** accepted as the competitor's statement, and it describes the industry; the row below describes what the two leaders earned in it. The same filing calls Ecolab *"the largest supplier in the global market for institutional and industrial cleaning, sanitation and hygiene products and related services"* and names it the *"primary global competitor"*; Diversey's successor's 10-K (FY2021) says *"the markets in which we operate are led by two large companies: Diversey and Ecolab Inc."*. Across 2019-2022 Diversey's gross margin ran 31.7-42.0% and its operating margin −4.0% to 2.6%; Ecolab's ran 37.8-43.9% and 11.0-14.7%. Low barriers to a small local competitor's entry are compatible with a customer who does not regard that competitor as a substitute for the chain-wide program; the price series is how the customer answered.
3. *"The price rises are small. Two points a year in ordinary years is roughly the general rise in prices; the only large increases (10% in 2022, 8% in 2023) came when every chemical seller raised prices. [E3-43] says 'aggressively'. And the filer's own risk factors say it may 'find it difficult to restore margins by maintaining pricing due to easing inflation from slowing economic growth', that 'multi-year contracts with some of our customers include terms affecting our pricing flexibility', and that customer consolidation 'could have a material adverse impact on ... our pricing'."* **Answer:** accepted: the ordinary-year increment is modest, about two points, and I do not claim [E3-33]'s untapped pricing power or [E5-28]'s near-monopoly. [E4-37]'s inverse metric reads the other way from agony: a price line that never turned negative in nineteen consolidated years, through two recessions and a pandemic, is the price of a supplier whose customers do not re-bid a detergent that is a small part of a restaurant's or a plant's cost. What the modest increment bounds is the class (NARROW, below), not the verdict.
4. *"Others earn more. Veralto's Water Quality segment, which includes ChemTreat, earned 24.0%, 24.5% and 25.4% operating margins in 2023-2025 against Ecolab's Global Water at 14.3%, 16.1% and 16.5%; Rollins earns 16-19% in pest control. On capital, Ecolab's pre-tax return including goodwill is 10-19%, ordinary, because it paid away the excess buying Nalco, Champion, Purolite, Ovivo and CoolIT."* **Answer:** accepted as position, and it is the second fact carried into the class. Veralto's segment sells *"leading brands including Hach, Trojan Technologies and ChemTreat"* (FY2025 10-K, `0001967680-26-000011`), analytical instruments and ultraviolet systems beside the chemistry, and ChemTreat's own margin is not filed; Ecolab's pest segment at 19.4% (2025) equals Rollins's 19.3%. Ecolab sits in the upper part of the row and is not its leader in water, which is why the class is NARROW. The return including goodwill measures what Ecolab paid for businesses, a capital-allocation question for Q3 ([E2-56]'s Pro-Am effect; [E5-24]); [E3-46] asks it of the business (*"Has it earned high returns on capital?"*), and on the net tangible capital the business uses the answer is 44-86% pre-tax in every year computed.
5. *"The part of Ecolab that sold to a commodity customer behaved like a commodity. Global Energy cut price in 2015, 2016 and 2017 while its volume fell 8-10%. Refining and petrochemical process chemistry, mining and pulp and paper are still in Global Water, and the 2026 purchases sell hardware to hyperscale data centers and chip fabs on projects."* **Answer:** accepted; the upstream business is outside the priced perimeter (split off 2020), and the consolidated and Global Industrial price lines, which still carried downstream refining, paper and mining, stayed positive in 2015-2017 and 2020. The 2026 perimeter (Ovivo, CoolIT; about $550M of CoolIT sales expected against $16bn) has no filed pricing record at all; recorded as the limit of this verdict and as a Q3 matter (a franchise buying a business outside it, [E3-40]).

None of the five shows Ecolab giving price back in a down year in the business that is priced today. The first and third are carried into the reversal condition.

### THE READING
- **[E2-44] (1), tested in the years it bites.** Volume fell in 2009 (−3%), was flat or down in 2015-2016 (0%, −1%) and fell sharply in 2020 (−9%, Institutional −21%); in each of those years consolidated price was +3%, 0% (every non-energy segment +1% to +2%), and +2%. *"We were able to offset a 3% decrease in volume with a 3% increase due to pricing"* (FY2009 annual report). No customer's capacity was fully used in 2020's restaurants and hotels, and the price rose. That is the characteristic [E2-44] names, shown in the conditions it names, and it is what OII and SANM could not show.
- **[E3-43], the returns half.** Pre-tax operating income on net tangible capital 44-86% in every year computed (FY2008-FY2017 and FY2020-FY2025), 44.3% at the 2022 cost-shock low. **[E3-46]: yes.**
- **The installed position and the cost of switching are in the filer's description, not only in its margin.** The dispensing systems are Ecolab's (*"Institutional develops various digital monitoring and chemical dispensing systems which are used by our customers to efficiently and safely dispense our cleaners and sanitizers"*), the lease program bundles the dishwasher with the chemistry and the maintenance (*"a lease program comprised of energy-efficient dishwashing machines, detergents, rinse additives and sanitizers, including full machine maintenance"*), and chains contract centrally (*"Corporate account sales personnel establish relationships and negotiate contracts with larger multi-unit or “chain” customers"*). The customer who switches replaces the equipment, the service call and the regulatory paperwork (several hundred EPA registrations) along with the detergent. That is why the price holds when the detergent's raw material falls.
- **The commodity doctrine [E2-58], and why it does not govern here.** The inputs are commodities; the product sold is a program at a site. The one Ecolab business that sold chemistry to a buyer who re-bid it on price (upstream oilfield) cut price with its customers' cycle and was split off. No administered price [E2-59].
- **[E2-44] (2), growth with minor capital: partly met.** Capital expenditure runs above depreciation (FY2025: capex $1,048.3M against depreciation $672.6M), much of it dispensing and monitoring equipment placed at customer sites; the net tangible capital base grew from $1.0bn (2008) to $4.4bn (2025) while operating income grew from $0.7bn to $2.7bn, most of the increase bought with the Nalco merger. Carried to Q4 (maintenance capital) rather than decided here.
