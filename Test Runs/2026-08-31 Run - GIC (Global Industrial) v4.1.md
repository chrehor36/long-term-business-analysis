## Q1 — CAN I UNDERSTAND HOW THIS MAKES MONEY? [E3-31]

- **Unit economics in my own words, no management language.** Global Industrial buys other
  companies' industrial junk — shelving, carts, fans, workbenches, hand trucks, safety gear,
  janitorial supplies, plumbing fittings, lighting, packaging, foodservice equipment,
  fasteners — in bulk, mostly imported from China and other Asian suppliers, holds some of it
  in **five primary US distribution centres and one in Canada** totalling **2.8 million square
  feet of leased space**, and sells it in small quantities to small and mid-sized businesses,
  schools and municipalities through a website, a phone bank, a periodic catalogue and a force
  of **relationship-marketer account managers** — the whole company is **1,980 people, of whom
  the 10-K says approximately 36% are customer-facing**. It never manufactures anything: the
  10-K says so in one sentence — *"These industrial and MRO products are **manufactured by
  other companies**."* Part of the range carries GIC's own labels (Global, Nexel, Paramount,
  Interion, Absocold) — the same third-party factories' goods with GIC's name on the box, and
  the filing calls this its *"higher margin private brand line"*. **The filing does not
  quantify the private-brand share of revenue and no figure is assumed here.** Out of every
  dollar of the **$1,379.1M** of 2025
  revenue, cost of goods takes **64.5 cents**, selling, general and administrative expense
  takes **28.5 cents**, and **7.1 cents** is operating income — of which tax takes 1.9,
  leaving **5.2 cents of net income**. The balance sheet is where the business lives:
  **$174.6M of inventories and $139.6M of receivables against only $18.5M of net plant**,
  because GIC leases every building it occupies and owns essentially no fixed assets. It
  needs about **16 cents of net working capital per dollar of revenue** and **0.22 cents a
  year of fixed capital expenditure** — a genuinely asset-light structure. It turns roughly
  **$68M of operating cash a year on a five-year mean**, spends $4M of it on equipment, pays
  out **$43M of dividends**, ~$9M of buybacks, and keeps the rest.
- **The scarce input this business controls.** Three candidates and I will name them honestly
  rather than reach: **(i)** the private-brand label estate, which is a real gross-margin
  lever and is the one thing GIC owns rather than resells; **(ii)** the embedded e-procurement
