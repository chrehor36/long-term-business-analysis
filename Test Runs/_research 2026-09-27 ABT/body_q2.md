## Q2 — IS IT A FRANCHISE? **[E3-03]**

*Every Abbott figure below is read from the filed 10-Ks for 2013-2025 and the 10-Q to 2026-06-30 (accessions in Step 0), or from SEC companyfacts checked against those statements. The price, volume and exchange columns are Abbott's own MD&A table (*"Components of % Change"*), parsed from all thirteen 10-Ks by `pv.py` (`pv_out.txt`); segment sales and operating earnings are the Note 16 tables as filed each year. Peer figures are each peer's own SEC companyfacts (10-K or 20-F annual facts, newest filed vintage) run through the MDT run's `row.py` unchanged: the device peers from the MDT run's outputs of 2026-09-26 (`Test Runs/_research 2026-09-26 MDT/peers/<TICKER>_row.txt`), the diagnostics and branded-generics peers and Abbott's own row fetched fresh here (`peers/<TICKER>_row.txt`, `peers/growth.py`).*

**The precedent, stated so it can be tested rather than copied.** The MDT run of 2026-09-26 closed Medtronic at Q2, OUT on the business, on [E3-03] criteria (2) and (3) in the filer's words and on a record of sales growing 2-3% a year while category leaders grew 8-16%; **Abbott's Medical Devices segment was one of the category leaders in that row** (12.5% a year 2023-2025, segment margin 31.4-33.7%). The ABBV run of today closed AbbVie at Q2 on criterion (3) and asked for a ruling on whether criterion (3) is read at the company or the product level. Abbott differs from both in ways the filings show, and each is tested below rather than assumed away: (i) **it files its price separately from its volume**, by segment, every year, so [E3-43]'s *"ability to regularly price"* can be measured rather than inferred; (ii) **its growth leader outgrows the focused rival** (Libre against Dexcom); (iii) **it is four businesses**, and one of them (Nutrition) is sold partly to consumers for cash.

**The hypotheses I formed while reading, both stated so they can be attacked [E4-26].** *(a) Abbott owns the largest continuous glucose monitor business in the world ($7.6bn in 2025, four times its 2019 size, growing faster than Dexcom every year from 2019 to 2025), cardiac and structural-heart franchises bought with St. Jude in 2017 that grew 10-17% a year in 2023-2025 at a rising segment margin (31.4% to 33.7%), an installed base of laboratory analyzers that binds customers to its reagents, and brands (Similac, Ensure, PediaSure) that took back their share after a recall; from 2023 to 2025 the whole company raised price every year; the business earns about 45% pre-tax on the assets its segments use; that is a set of franchises in one wrapper.* *(b) Abbott's own price column is negative for the whole company in every year from 2013 to 2022, negative in Diagnostics in twelve of thirteen years and in devices in every year filed until 2023 (*"pricing pressures on drug eluting stents (DES) as a result of market competition"*); the one segment that raised prices hard (Nutrition, +7.4%, +11.4%, +7.7% in 2022-2024) lost volume every year and then took *"new strategic price actions targeted to increase volume growth"* while its margin sat five points below its 2014-2021 level; the filer says *"price controls, competition, and rebates most impact the net selling prices of products"*; the device and diagnostic lines it leads were bought (St. Jude $23.6bn, Alere $4.5bn, Exact Sciences $20.6bn); its return on all the capital including what it paid is 12-13%.* The file does not get to pick. Each is tested on the filings below.

**[E3-03], criterion by criterion.** *"An economic franchise arises from a product or service that: (1) is needed or desired; (2) is thought by its customers to have no close substitute and; (3) is not subject to price regulation."*
- **(1) Needed or desired [x]: passes.** Glucose sensors for diabetics, heart implants and ablation catheters, laboratory tests hospitals run on every admission, infant formula and medical nutrition, and emerging-market medicines; net sales $44,328M in 2025 and $23,757M in H1 2026.
- **(3) Not subject to price regulation: FAILS IN PART, in the filer's own words, with measured effects in two places.** The MD&A's opening paragraph, every year from at least 2019 to the 10-Q of 2026: *"Patent protection and licenses, technological and performance features, and inclusion of Abbott’s products under a contract most impact which products are sold; price controls, competition, and rebates most impact the net selling prices of products"*. Item 1 (10-K 2025): *"Many countries control the price of healthcare products directly or indirectly, through reimbursement, payment, pricing, or coverage limitations"*; *"The government follows a diagnosis-related group (DRG) payment system ... DRG and PPS entitle a healthcare facility to a fixed reimbursement based on the diagnosis and/or procedure rather than actual costs incurred in patient treatment, thereby increasing the incentive for the facility to limit or control expenditures for many healthcare products"*; *"Medicare implemented a competitive bidding system for certain durable medical equipment (including diabetes products), enteral nutrition products, and supplies"* (a near-identical sentence in the 10-K for 2013: *"Medicare also implemented a competitive bidding system for durable medical equipment (including diabetes products), enteral nutrition products, and supplies"*); and for infant formula, *"under regulations governing the federally funded Special Supplemental Nutrition Program for Women, Infants, and Children (WIC), all states must have a cost containment program for infant formula. As a result, through competitive bidding states obtain rebates from manufacturers of infant formula whose products are used in the program."* Rebates: *"In 2025, 44 percent of Abbott’s consolidated gross revenues were subject to various forms of rebates and allowances ... Most of these rebates and allowances in 2025 are in the Nutritional Products and Diabetes Care businesses. Abbott provides rebates to state agencies, wholesalers, group purchasing organizations, and other government agencies ... Rebate amounts are usually based upon the volume of purchases using contractual or statutory prices"*; rebates and chargebacks rose from 17.4% to 21.1% of the gross sales subject to them in 2023-2025. Item 1A names *"government actions such as price controls"* abroad. **Measured**: the Chinese coronary tender, *"lower vascular sales in China in the fourth quarter of 2020 as a result of a new national tender program"* (10-K 2021), and the Chinese diagnostics procurement, *"challenging market conditions in China, including the impact of volume-based procurement programs"* (10-K 2025), with Diagnostics price −2.1% in 2025 and China sales falling from $2,253M (2023) to $1,907M (2025). **This is the regime [E2-59] describes (prices administered by the buyer), across the two segments that carry the growth (Diabetes Care through Medicare bidding and rebates; Diagnostics through procurement) and across US infant formula (WIC).** The exceptions are real: adult nutrition at retail, and cash-pay products in markets without reimbursement.
- **(2) Thought by its customers to have no close substitute: NOT SHOWN, and contradicted by the filer's own words for three of the four segments.** Item 1, nearly the same two sentences for Diagnostics and for Devices: *"subject to competition in technological innovation, price, convenience of use, service, ... long-term supply contracts, and product potential for overall cost-effectiveness and productivity gains. Some products in this segment can be subject to rapid product obsolescence or regulatory changes. Although Abbott has benefited from technological advantages of certain of its current products, these advantages may be reduced or eliminated as competitors introduce new products."* Nutrition: *"Competitive factors include consumer preferences, advertising, formulation, packaging, scientific innovation, price ... In addition, private label and local manufacturers’ products may increase competitive pressure."* Established Pharmaceuticals: *"the substitution of generic drugs for the brand prescribed and introduction of additional forms of already marketed established products by generic or branded competitors may increase competitive pressures."* Item 1A: *"Abbott’s products face intense competition from competitors' products and technological advances. Competitors’ products may be safer, more effective, more effectively marketed or sold, or have lower prices or superior performance features than Abbott’s products."* **Named substitution in the MD&A**: devices price declines *"primarily reflect pricing pressures on drug eluting stents (DES) as a result of market competition in the U.S. and other major markets"* (10-Ks 2013 to 2021); rhythm management's 2025 growth *"partially offset by lower traditional pacemaker and implantable cardioverter defibrillator sales"* (Abbott's own leadless device replacing its own traditional one); and the 2022 infant-formula recall, after which the filer reports *"progress in recovering market share"* (10-K 2023) and *"infant formula market share gains"* (10-K 2024): **share left for a substitute when Similac was absent, which is the substitution the criterion denies, and came back, which is evidence of brand preference that the criterion does not ask for.** **A limit, stated**: the Item 1 competition sentences are close to industry language (the MDT run found the same in Stryker's and Intuitive's 10-Ks), so their weight is on Abbott's own position only where the MD&A and the price column confirm them, which is below.
