### (d)(iii) THE ANSWER, PLAINLY: the boom **volume** has already washed out of the numbers; what is left is **price**, and the price is the exposure

1. **The WGO artifact is a boom *backlog* still sitting in the reported figures. Pentair has the

2. **The 2022 year inside the five-year window is a TROUGH, not a peak.** Owner earnings were
   **$232.8M** in 2022 against $506.3M in 2021 and $660.1M in 2025 — the destocking year, plus a

   number and it runs in Pentair's favour.**
3. **What IS boom-conditioned is the margin, and it is entirely price.** Pool's margin bridge, from

5. **The counter-case, stated fairly [E4-51].** Not all of the margin gain is boom price. Pentair
   divested lower-margin businesses (commercial services, 2025), bought higher-margin ones

   the burden of proof sits on the elevated level, not on the reversion**, and the four-year run of

## Q1 — CAN I UNDERSTAND HOW THIS MAKES MONEY? [E3-31]

- **Unit economics in my own words, no management language.** Pentair buys motors, plastic
  housings, sheet metal, copper, castings and electronics, and turns them into **three groups of
  boxes that move or clean water**. The first group — 38% of sales and 46% of profit — is the
  **pump, filter, heater, light, chlorinator and controller that sit in a concrete pad next to a
  backyard swimming pool**. The second — 25% of sales — is **the softener, the drinking-water filter
  cartridge, the pressure tank and the commercial ice machine**. The third — 37% of sales — is
  **pumps, valves and membranes for wells, farms, wastewater and industry**. It sells almost none of
  this to the person who uses it. It sells to distributors and dealers, and **one of those customers
  is 18% of the entire company**. Pentair converts raw material and labour at a **40.5% gross
  margin**, spends 17.6 points of revenue on selling and administration and 2.3 on research, and
