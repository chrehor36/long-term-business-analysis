# Competitor row — ROPER TECHNOLOGIES (ROP), Neptune Technology Group
Research date: 2026-09-05. For the BMI run competitor row (Q2). Roper owns Neptune (water meters / AMR / AMI).

All figures in **$ millions** as filed (Roper files in millions).

## Where Neptune sits
**Technology Enabled Products (TEP)** segment, one of three (Application Software, Network Software, Technology Enabled Products). TEP businesses as listed: "CIVCO Medical Solutions, FMI, Inovonics, IPA, **Neptune**, Northern Digital, rf IDEAS, and Verathon."

Neptune's product description (Part I): "Neptune – water meters, enabling water utilities to remotely monitor their customers utilizing Automatic Meter Reading (AMR), Advanced Metering Infrastructure (AMI) technologies, and cloud-based software supporting meter data management and utility billing."

## Neptune-specific language
**Neptune-only revenue and profit are not disclosed — a stated limit of this row entry.** TEP mixes Neptune with medical devices (Verathon, CIVCO, Northern Digital, IPA) and access/security products.

What the filing does say about Neptune:
1. FY2025 TEP organic growth "was broad-based across the segment, **led by our medical products businesses, highlighted by our precision measurement business, and growth in our access management businesses**" — **Neptune is not named as a growth driver** in the FY2025 discussion. Gross margin gains are likewise attributed to the precision measurement business and mix.
2. "On February 19, 2025, Roper acquired substantially all of the assets of **Muni-Link**, a leading provider of cloud-based utility management, billing, and customer communication software solutions for municipalities... integrated into our **Neptune** business and its results are reported in the Technology Enabled Products reportable segment." — Roper is bolting utility-billing SaaS onto Neptune, the same software-attach direction as BMI's BEACON/SmartCover strategy.
3. TEP software revenue (recurring) is tiny but growing: 46.1 (2025) vs 26.1 (2024) vs 17.3 (2023) — the only recurring-software line in TEP, plausibly mostly Neptune/Muni-Link meter-data-management, though not attributed.

## Pre-tax operating return on net tangible operating assets (FY2025, total company)
Neptune-only or TEP-only tangible capital is not disclosed (segment assets include allocated goodwill/intangibles; no segment split of cash or current liabilities). Total company, as filed (Dec 31, 2025):
- Income from operations: **2,235.4**
- Total assets: **34,577.0**
- Cash and cash equivalents: **297.4**
- Goodwill: **21,341.2**
- Other intangible assets, net: **9,764.2**
- Non-interest-bearing current liabilities = total current liabilities 3,725.6 − current portion of long-term debt 705.2 = **3,020.4** (accounts payable 150.3; accrued compensation 293.0; deferred revenue 1,906.8; other accrued 642.3; income taxes payable 28.0)
- Denominator: 34,577.0 − 297.4 − 21,341.2 − 9,764.2 − 3,020.4 = **153.8**
- **RONTA = 2,235.4 / 153.8 ≈ 1,453% pre-tax — effectively meaningless-high: Roper runs on almost no net tangible capital.** The remaining 153.8 still contains the non-operating Indicor equity investment of 796.3; excluding it the denominator is **negative (−642.5)** — customer deferred revenue more than funds the entire tangible asset base. The economically honest statement: Roper's operating businesses require negative net tangible capital, and the purchase price of that economics sits in $31.1bn of goodwill + intangibles.

## Row relevance
Neptune is BMI's most direct serious competitor in North American residential/commercial water metering, and it is invisible inside TEP: ~34.5% segment operating margins (above BMI's), but no Neptune revenue, growth, or margin disclosed since Roper bought it in 2003. The FY2025 filing names medical products, not Neptune, as the TEP growth engine, and TEP backlog was flat while BMI's utility segment grew. Held as a stated limit: the row records TEP as the disclosed unit and notes Neptune's figures are unknowable from filings.
