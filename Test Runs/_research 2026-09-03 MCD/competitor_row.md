Method note: figures were located via SEC XBRL companyfacts and cross-checked against
the filed statement text (the R-file rendering of the filed financial statements and/or
the primary 10-K document); at least one figure per company is cross-checked against the
filed statement text, as noted in each section. NTOA formula (same as ORLY/ULTA/DKS
runs): NTOA = total assets − cash and equivalents − short-term investments − goodwill −
other intangibles − operating-lease right-of-use assets − (total current liabilities −
current debt/current maturities − current operating-lease liabilities). Return =
operating income ÷ NTOA. Owner-earnings work belongs to the run file, not here.

### 4. Franchise-revenue composition — rent from franchisees
YES, small. "Property revenues" inside "Franchise and property revenues": FY2025 US $56M +
non-US $47M = ~$103M (Note 6 disaggregation). Policy quote (Note: Revenue Recognition):
"From time to time, we enter into rental agreements with franchisees for the lease or
sublease of restaurant locations. These rental agreements typically originate from
refranchising transactions and revenues related to the agreements are recognized as they
are earned." Item 2: YUM "owns land, building or both related to approximately 400
franchise restaurants that it leases to franchisees and leases land, building or both
related to approximately 225 franchise restaurants that it subleases to franchisees."
Ad-fund note: consolidated advertising cooperatives put $1,796M (FY2025) / $1,314M
(FY2020) of franchisee ad contributions into total revenues under ASC 606 (sales-based
royalty exception) — strip it for take-rate comparability (row above).

traffic, transactions, average check, guest count, ticket; only digital-sales share is

### 6. Price competition (Item 1, Competition)
"The retail food industry, in which our Concepts compete, is made up of supermarkets,
supercenters, warehouse stores, convenience stores, coffee shops, snack bars,
delicatessens and restaurants (including those in the QSR segment), and is intensely
competitive with respect to price and quality of food products, new product development,
digital engagement, advertising levels and promotional initiatives, customer service
reputation, restaurant location and attractiveness and maintenance of properties."

### 6. Price competition (Item 1A)
"The restaurant industry is intensely competitive and we compete with many
well-established food service companies on the basis of product choice, quality, value,
affordability, product innovation, delivery options, mobile ordering, brand reputation,
loyalty, service, facilities, and location." And on promotional intensity: "Also, to the
extent we use value offerings in our marketing and advertising programs to drive traffic
and/or respond to the competitive environment, the low price offerings may condition our
guests to resist higher prices in a more favorable economic environment."
