# Company Run — Synchrony Financial (SYF) — 2026-07-15
Framework v3.0. From the mechanical screen. Source: FY2025 10-K (CIK
1601712, filed 2026). **Financial business — Ruling 2's leverage-ceiling
track applies at Gate 4.** Not treated as a bank under the project's
standing "ignore banks" exclusion — Synchrony is a monoline private-label
consumer-credit-card lender/program manager, not a depository commercial
bank, and is analyzed in full.

## STEP 0 — RATE REFRESH
US 30-yr **5.10%**. Mkt cap $24.78B — no size premium.

## GATE 1 — CIRCLE OF COMPETENCE
Synchrony issues private-label and co-branded consumer credit cards on
behalf of retail partners (a two-sided business: retail-partner program
agreements on one side, cardholder lending/collections on the other).
Unit economics: interest/fee income on revolving balances minus funding
cost, credit losses, and program-partner revenue-share. Genuinely
understandable consumer-lending economics, though credit-cycle/underwriting
quality is a real specialist judgment this run does not independently
model loan-loss-reserve adequacy for. **VERDICT: PASS.**

## GATE 2 — MOAT
Franchise test [E3-03], from Synchrony's own 10-K: **"Our industry
continues to be highly competitive. We compete for relationships with
partners in connection with retaining existing or establishing new consumer
credit programs. Our primary competitors for partners include major
financial institutions such as American Express, Bread Financial, Capital
One/Discover, JPMorgan Chase, Citibank, TD Bank and Wells Fargo, as well as
financial technology companies, point-of-sale lending focused companies..."**
- **Needed/desired**: yes, for the retail partner (financing drives sales
  and loyalty) and for revolving-credit consumers.
- **No close substitute**: **partial**. Competing directly against Amex,
  Capital One, Chase, Citi, and a wave of point-of-sale/buy-now-pay-later
  fintechs (Affirm-style disruption is a real, live threat to the private-
  label-card model, not addressed head-on in the quoted section but a
  well-known industry dynamic) means new program wins are genuinely
  competitive. Existing, multi-year retail-partner program agreements
  (e.g., long-standing relationships with major national retailers) carry
  real switching costs for the partner (re-platforming a store-card
  program is operationally disruptive) — a real, if partial, moat.
**Class: NARROW** (embedded partner-program contracts), **Direction:
NARROWING** given the BNPL/fintech competitive pressure named in the
company's own competitor list.
**VERDICT: PASS with NARROW/NARROWING classification.**

points below the 10:1 fail line).

## GATE 5 — INVERSION
1. **Moat destruction** — BNPL/fintech disintermediation of point-of-sale
   financing, and/or loss of a major retail-partner program to a competitor
   at contract renewal (Synchrony has experienced high-profile partner
   losses in its history — a real, recurring risk category for this
   business model, not a hypothetical). Scored **[P]**.
2. **Management/key-person** — not independently verified. Scored **[U]**.
3. **Balance-sheet/credit-cycle stress** — 7.10:1 leverage is real (though
   under the ceiling); a severe consumer-credit downturn would compress
   both earnings AND capital simultaneously (unlike an industrial's
   PP&E-based stress test, a lender's stress case hits the OE base and the
   balance sheet together). Scored **[P]**, not [D] — this is a genuinely
   live risk for any consumer lender, not a remote one.
4. **Regulatory** (consumer-lending/interchange/late-fee rulemaking is an
   active, ongoing US regulatory area). Scored **[P]**.

**One [U], three [P]s, no [D]** — a real, higher-uncertainty profile than
the industrial/services names run today, consistent with lending being a
structurally riskier business model than most in this batch.
**Thesis-breaking metric:** loss of a top-5 retail-partner program, or
net charge-off rate rising meaningfully above the FY2023 trough level that
already anchors this valuation.
**VERDICT: PASS, with a documented [U] and three [P]s** — the most
qualitatively uncertain PASS of the S&P 500 names run today.
