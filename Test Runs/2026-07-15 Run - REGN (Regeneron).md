## GATE 2 — MOAT
Franchise test [E3-03], from Regeneron's own 10-K: **"We face substantial
competition from pharmaceutical and biotechnology companies. Our ability
to compete depends, to a great extent, on how fast we can develop safe and
effective product candidates, complete clinical testing and approval
processes, and supply commercial quantities of the product to the market.
Competition among products approved for sale is based on efficacy,
safety, reliability, ease of administration, dosing frequency,
availability, price, patent and other intellectual property position, and
other factors."**
- **No close substitute**: **real, but time-limited** — patent-protected
  biologics carry genuine exclusivity-period pricing power, a real moat
  mechanism distinct from a generic-drug or commodity business — but the
  company's own words name "patent... position" as a specific competitive
  factor, i.e., competition explicitly intensifies as patents expire.
  Eylea specifically has faced disclosed biosimilar competition in recent
  years (general public knowledge, not independently confirmed via this
  specific filing's financial detail this pass) — a real, not
  hypothetical, example of this exact risk materializing for this
  company's largest product.
**Class: NARROW-to-WIDE** (patent-protected during exclusivity, genuinely
eroding post-patent — Eylea's own biosimilar exposure is the concrete,
already-occurring example), **Direction: NARROWING** on the flagship
product specifically, though the broader pipeline provides some offset not
independently assessed in detail this pass.
**VERDICT: PASS with NARROW-to-WIDE classification**, using the
conservative NARROW end for Gate 6 sizing given the disclosed patent-
competition dynamic.

**VERDICT: PASS**, with the Eylea patent-cliff dynamic carried forward as
