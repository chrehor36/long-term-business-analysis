## GATE 2 — MOAT
Franchise test [E3-03], from Ameriprise's own 10-K: **"We operate in a
highly competitive global industry. As a diversified financial services
firm, we compete directly with a variety of financial institutions,
including registered investment advisers, securities brokers, asset
managers, banks and insurance companies. We directly compete for the
provision of products and services to clients, as well as for our
financial advisors and investment management personnel. Certain of our
competitors offer web-based or mobile-based financial services and
discount brokerage services... and we increasingly compete with various
financial technology companies..."**
- **No close substitute**: **partial** — the human financial-advisor
  relationship model carries real switching costs (clients are reluctant
  to leave a trusted advisor, and advisors themselves are recruited/
  retained competitively, per the filing's own language) — a more durable
  moat mechanism than T. Rowe Price's pure-AUM-fee model (run separately),
  but the company's own words concede intensifying fintech/robo-advisor
  competition.
**Class: NARROW** (advisor-relationship stickiness across a diversified
model), **Direction: NARROWING** given the named fintech competitive
pressure.
**VERDICT: PASS with NARROW/NARROWING classification.**

## GATE 4 — FINANCIAL QUALITY — MECHANICAL LEVERAGE CEILING FAIL
Full OE formula (P44 ASSET-LIGHT band, 25%, for the advisory/asset-
management operations) for the record:

| FY | Net income | D&A | Total capex | Maint. capex (25%) | **OE** |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2022 | $3,149M | $142M | $182M | $45.5M | **$3,245.5M** |
| 2023 | $2,556M | $151M | $184M | $46.0M | **$2,661.0M** |
| 2024 | $3,401M | $142M | $176M | $44.0M | **$3,499.0M** |
| 2025 | $3,563M | $151M | $162M | $40.5M | **$3,673.5M** |

The OE figures alone are healthy (lowest tier $2,661M, FY2023) — **but this
is a financial business per Ruling 2** (a real, balance-sheet-levered
annuity/insurance book via RiverSource, distinct from T. Rowe Price's pure
asset-management model run separately today), and the mechanical leverage
ceiling governs regardless of earnings quality:

**Total assets $190,904M ÷ total equity $6,549M (FY2025) = 29.15:1.**

This is **nearly triple** Ruling 2's 10:1 automatic-fail threshold — a
ratio this extreme is consistent with a large variable-annuity/separate-
account book (where policyholder-directed separate-account assets and
matching liabilities inflate both sides of the balance sheet substantially,
general insurance-accounting knowledge, not independently decomposed
against Ameriprise's specific separate-account disclosure this pass) rather
than operational insolvency risk — but **Ruling 2 is explicit that this
ceiling applies "no exception, no judgment call"** specifically because a
levered financial institution can always produce a qualitative explanation
("it's just separate-account accounting," "policyholders bear the
investment risk," etc.) right up until a crisis proves otherwise — the
exact reasoning the ruling documents for why Citigroup's 19:1 ratio in
2007 was allowed to pass on qualitative grounds in real time.
**VERDICT: FAIL — STOP**, mechanically, per Ruling 2. No judgment override
applied despite the plausible separate-account explanation, because
applying that kind of case-by-case exception is precisely what the ceiling
exists to prevent.
