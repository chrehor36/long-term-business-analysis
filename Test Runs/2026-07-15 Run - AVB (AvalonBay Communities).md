# Company Run — AvalonBay Communities (AVB) — 2026-07-15
Framework v3.0. From the REIT mechanical screen (user-supplied list). OE

## STEP 0 — RATE REFRESH
US 30-yr **5.10%** (unchanged, Jul 13–15 checks). Mkt cap $27.03B — no size
premium (well above the $2B large-cap threshold).

## GATE 1 — CIRCLE OF COMPETENCE
Owns, develops, redevelops, acquires, and operates apartment communities —
primarily upscale, mid/high-rise and garden-style — concentrated in supply-
constrained, high-barrier-to-entry coastal markets (New England, NY/NJ,
Mid-Atlantic, Pacific Northwest, Northern & Southern California, plus
expansion markets like Denver/Dallas/Austin). Unit economics are genuinely
simple: rent × occupancy − operating expenses − capital spend on existing
communities. Single business type, one segment in substance (some
management/development fee income is immaterial: $7.0M vs. $3.04B total
revenue). Read directly from the FY2025 10-K (Archives/edgar/data/915912).
**VERDICT: PASS.**

## GATE 2 — MOAT
Franchise test [E3-03]: needed/desired — yes, generically (housing always
needed); no close substitute — **weak on its face** (apartments are a
substitutable product), but AvalonBay's specific submarkets are
supply-constrained by zoning/land-cost/entitlement barriers, which is a real
(if narrow) structural cost-of-replication advantage, not a pricing-power
franchise; unregulated — **partial fail**, California and some Mid-Atlantic
jurisdictions carry rent-control/rent-stabilization exposure, a genuine and
disclosed regulatory risk (10-K risk factors, "Regulatory Matters" section).
Primary metric: total revenue $3,040.7M (2025) vs $2,913.8M (2024) vs
$2,767.9M (2023) — **+4.4% then +5.3%**, steady but unspectacular, consistent
with a mature, disciplined operator rather than a high-growth franchise.
**Class: NARROW** (location scarcity, not pricing power). **Direction:
STABLE-to-modestly-WIDENING.**
**VERDICT: PASS with NARROW classification carried into Gate 6 (30% MOS
floor, not the generous wide-moat treatment).**

## GATE 3 — MANAGEMENT
CEO Benjamin Schall since January 2022, following an orderly, disclosed
succession from Timothy Naughton (CEO 2012–2022, then non-executive Chairman
2023–2025 — a clean handoff, not an abrupt departure). Governance from the
2026 proxy (DEF 14A, filed 2026-04-06): stock ownership guidelines for
directors/officers, an insider trading policy with anti-hedging/
anti-speculation/no-pledging provisions, a related-party transaction policy
routed through the Nominating/Governance committee, and pay tied to TSR

found in this pass. **Limitation**: exact CEO compensation dollar figures
were not independently extracted this session (proxy is large; the
qualitative governance read was prioritized) — flagged, not filled with an
estimate.
**VERDICT: PASS**, with the compensation-detail gap noted as an open
follow-up rather than resolved.

closer to the FFO-style number.
**LOWEST VERIFIED TIER (anchors all buy prices): $1,567.478M (FY2023).**
ΔWC not separately extracted — REIT working capital (rent receivables,
payables) is typically small relative to these figures; treated as
immaterial pending a more precise pull.
Fortress check — REIT capital structure is balance-sheet-levered by nature,
so the **financial-business/leverage-ceiling track** applies (per Ruling 2's

explicit in the ruling's text, flagged for the record): Total assets
$22,192.137M ÷ Total equity $11,834.317M = **1.876:1** — far under the 10:1
ceiling.
**VERDICT: PASS.**

## GATE 5 — INVERSION
1. **Moat destruction** — oversupply in specific submarkets (multifamily
   construction cycles) or a durable shift away from renting in AVB's core
   coastal metros (remote-work-driven migration). Addressed structurally via
   geographic diversification across ~12 states + DC and a mix of urban/
   suburban product. Scored **[D]**.
2. **Management failure/key person** — succession already happened once,
   cleanly, on the record (Naughton → Schall). Scored **[D]**.
3. **Balance-sheet stress** — 1.88:1 assets/equity, investment-grade profile,
   nowhere near the 10:1 ceiling. Scored **[D]**.
4. **Regulatory risk** (rent control expansion in CA/NY/NJ-type markets) —
   real, disclosed, partially mitigated by geographic mix outside those
   jurisdictions, but not eliminated. Scored **[P]** — the one softer score
   in this set.

No [U]. **Thesis-breaking metric:** two consecutive years of negative
same-store NOI growth, or assets/equity rising past ~3:1.
**VERDICT: PASS** — one [P] (regulatory) carried forward as a real, not
hypothetical, risk.

## GATE 6 — VALUATION
**6-B Statute** (anchored to lowest tier, $1,567.478M): yield =
1,567.478 / 27,030 = **5.80%** vs. hurdle **5.10%** (large-cap, no premium)
→ **PASSES** with a 0.7pp cushion — thinner than Unit Corp's or Gamco's
margin, appropriately so given this is a much more efficiently-priced,
widely-covered large-cap.

**Book Two DCF (Ruling 4, Aesop certainty-spread method — built 2026-07-15
PM)**: Gate 3 was clean (no stated caveat) → discount rate = 5.10% sovereign
+ 2.5% NARROW-moat certainty spread = **7.60%**. Year-1 growth g1: recent OE
growth measured two ways — most-recent YoY (2024→2025) = **-1.88%** vs.
2-year CAGR (2023→2025) = +4.24% — used the LOWER (more conservative) of
the two, consistent with the framework's anchoring philosophy, so **g1 =
-1.88%** (AVB's OE actually declined in the most recent year; this is a real,
not smoothed-away, input). NARROW moat → 10yr fade to 2% terminal growth
[E2-22 growing-coupon reading]. Net cash/debt not separately added — OE is
already an NI-based (levered, post-interest) cash-flow measure, so the DCF
output is an equity-value figure directly, not an enterprise value needing a
net-debt bridge.
- **IV = $24,150.1M** | Max buy (20% MOS floor) = **$19,320.1M**
- Current mkt cap $27,030M → **price is ABOVE the Base-case IV itself**, not
  just above the MOS-floored max buy.

**Scream Test**: Book One (Statute) says PASS (5.80% yield vs 5.10% hurdle).
Book Two (build-up) says the current price already exceeds Base-case IV —
**the two books DISAGREE** → **WHISPER, pass.** Do not upgrade past
Statute-only on this disagreement.

**VERDICT: STATUTE-ONLY (starter size).** Book Two now built and it does NOT
confirm the Statute pass at full size — this is exactly the kind of case
Ruling 4/the dual-book design exists to catch (a name that clears the bare
comparison hurdle but isn't actually cheap on a real cash-flow basis).

## GATE 8 — REVERSE DCF
Implied g1 at current price (solving IV = mkt cap) = **+0.72%** — noticeably
above the -1.88% Base anchor, meaning the market is pricing in a return to
positive OE growth that the most recent year's actual results don't yet
support. Bear case (g1 = -5%, a moderate-recession OE decline): IV =
$21,068.3M — still below current price. Upside (Base IV − price) =
**-$2,879.9M** (negative — price exceeds Base IV already); downside (price −
Bear IV) = $5,961.7M. **Asymmetry ratio is negative (-0.48:1), far short of
the 2:1 minimum.**
**VERDICT: WAIT.** No entry — the reverse DCF confirms the Scream Test
disagreement: AVB is not offering the margin of safety the framework
requires, even though it clears the bare Statute yardstick.

## GATE 7 — SIZING
No position warranted at current price per Gate 8. If price fell to at or
below the $19,320.1M max-buy line (implying a mkt cap decline of roughly
28.5% from current, or a demonstrated OE recovery that raises the IV instead
— either would need to be re-checked, not just waited out on price alone), a
starter position would become newly justifiable subject to a fresh Gate 8
re-run.
