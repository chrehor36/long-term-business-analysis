## 2. RETURN ON EQUITY, FY2021-FY2025

**Method.** ROE = net income attributable to common shareholders / average common
shareholders' equity, where average = (beginning + ending) / 2 and common shareholders'
equity = total shareholders' equity attributable to the parent, less the carrying value of
preferred stock. XBRL tags `NetIncomeLoss` and `StockholdersEquity`, each traced to the 10-K
accession that filed it. Preferred carrying values: AIG $485m through FY2023 and **nil from
FY2024** (AIG redeemed its Series A during 2024; preferred dividends run 29 / 22 / 0 for
2023 / 2024 / 2025 per its FY2025 10-K EPS reconciliation); HIG $334m in every year (FY2025
10-K); CNA, TRV and WRB have **no preferred stock outstanding** in the window.

**Two restatement adjustments were made, both to the *beginning* equity of FY2023,** because
ASU 2018-12 (LDTI, long-duration targeted improvements) was adopted 1 January 2023 with a
1 January 2021 transition date and restated the comparatives:
- CNA beginning FY2023 equity taken as **8,548** (restated 31-Dec-2022, accession
  0000021175-24-000007), not the 8,825 originally filed.
- AIG beginning FY2023 equity taken as **40,970** (restated, 0000005272-24-000023), not 40,002.
- HIG beginning FY2023 equity taken as **13,676** (restated, 0000874766-24-000016), not 13,631.

All other years use the figures as filed in that year's own 10-K.

4. **CNA's 2023 relative bounce is a denominator artefact, not an earnings event.** Reported
   ROE jumped to 13.1% on net income of $1,205m against an average equity base that had
   collapsed by 28% from 2021. On the ex-AOCI basis the same year is 9.8%, in line with
   every other CNA year. There is no operating improvement in it.

## 5. LONG-TERM-CARE RUNOFF EXPOSURE: the single largest structural difference

### The finding, stated plainly

**CNA is the only one of the five that carries a long-term-care block. The other four carry
none.** A case-insensitive search of the full FY2025 10-K text of each registrant for
"long-term care", "long term care" and "LTC" returns:

|---|---|---|---|

| AIG | 0000005272-26-000023 | 0 / 0 / 0 | NO |

The peers' long-duration liabilities, where any exist, are not LTC:
- **AIG**: `LiabilityForFuturePolicyBenefits` = **$1,385m** at 31-Dec-2025 (vs $58,576m at
  31-Dec-2023, before the Corebridge deconsolidation). The residual is not LTC; AIG's FY2025
  10-K does not mention long-term care once.
- **HIG**: reserves for future policy benefits and other policyholder funds = **$444m**, of

undiscounted expected future benefit payments of $31,323m are **2.7x equity**. The block is
**entirely unreinsured**; ceded LTC reserves are nil.

**It runs at a loss and is not shrinking fast.** Life & Group produced a core loss of $(44)m in
2025 and $(23)m in 2024. Earned premium of $423m against interest accretion alone of $742m
means the block consumes investment income to stand still: net earned premiums 423 + net
investment income 914 = 1,337 against claims, benefits and expenses of 1,415. The reserve
*rose* from 13,158 to 13,448 over the year.

**The disclosed sensitivity is the number that matters for Q4 ("the named way it dies").**
CNA's own hypothetical-revision table, pre-tax reduction to income:

| Hypothetical revision | Pre-tax hit |
|---|---|
| 2.5% increase in morbidity | $300m |
| **5% increase in morbidity** | **$620m** |
| 5% decrease in active life mortality and lapse | $180m |
| **10% decrease in active life mortality and lapse** | **$350m** |
| 25% decrease in anticipated future premium rate increases | $30m |
| 50% decrease in anticipated future premium rate increases | $50m |

A 5% morbidity miss costs $620m pre-tax, roughly **half of CNA's entire FY2025 net income
($1,278m)**, and it would recur, not reverse. CNA states the revisions are "not currently
required or anticipated" but that it believes "they could occur based on past variances in
experience."

**The auditor agrees it is the hard number.** CNA's independent auditor designates
**"Future policy benefit reserves - Long-Term Care"** a **Critical Audit Matter**, on the
ground that estimating LTC future policy benefit reserves "requires significant judgment in
the selection of key assumptions, including the morbidity, specifically incidence,
assumption." None of the four peers carries a CAM of this kind because none of them carries
the liability.

### Why this differentiator drives the run

1. Every combined-ratio and ROE comparison in Sections 1 and 2 is measured on CNA's **P&C Operations**,
   which excludes Life & Group. On that flattering basis CNA is still last or next-to-last.
2. The LTC block sits behind that comparison as $13.4bn of unreinsured, 11-year-duration,
   morbidity-exposed liability standing against $11.6bn of equity, with a disclosed $620m
   pre-tax sensitivity to a 5% morbidity move.
3. **No peer carries anything comparable.** This is not a difference of degree. Four of the
   five have zero. It is the reason CNA cannot be valued as a clean P&C underwriter against
   this peer set, and it is the named candidate for how the business dies.
