# LOEWS CORPORATION (NYSE: L) — SHARE REPURCHASE RECORD
## Research file, built 2026-09-02. SEC EDGAR primary sources only.

**CIK** 0000060086 · **Fiscal year end** 31 December · **Single class of common stock**

**Purpose.** Assemble the buyback record so it can be tested against Buffett's conditions on
repurchase: that a company repurchase only when the shares sell **below a conservative
estimate of intrinsic value**, and only with funds surplus to the needs of the business.

**Status: COMPLETE, 2026-09-02.** Four blocks below.

**Headline.** FY2009 to FY2025: $11.276 billion spent, 52.7% of the shares retired, average
price $48.09, which was **0.81x the book value of what was bought**. The implied repurchase
price stayed **below** year-end book value per share in **every one of the seventeen years**,
range 0.63x to 1.00x. But the phrase **"intrinsic value" appears nowhere** in the FY2025 10-K or
the 2026 proxy, and Q4 2025 buying was done at $99 to $105 against year-end book of $90.71, the
first clear premium in the file.

**Reading the columns.**

- *Shares repurchased* is `TreasuryStockSharesAcquired` as Loews tags it: the **gross** count,
  which is what the cash was spent on. It is reported to one decimal place (0.1 M), so the
  implied price carries roughly 1% precision. This is the correct divisor for price paid.
- *Net change in o/s* is the decline in the share count, which is smaller than the gross
  repurchase in every year because shares are issued back out under compensation plans. It is
  the cross-check: it tracks the tagged repurchase count within a few hundred thousand shares
  in every one of the seventeen years, so the two independent series agree.
- *BVPS* uses **Loews-only** equity. Noncontrolling interests (the public minority in CNA and,
  through 2018, Boardwalk) are excluded, as they must be: the buyer of a Loews share does not
  own them.
- The equity statement tags a slightly different cost for the same purchases
  (`TreasuryStockValueAcquiredCostMethod`: e.g. $789M in 2025 vs $806M of cash) because of
  settlement timing. The **cash flow** figure is used throughout; using the equity-statement
  figure moves the average ratio by less than a hundredth.

### The one fact that matters

**Loews bought below book value in every one of the seventeen years. Not once, in 2009-2025,
did the implied average repurchase price reach year-end book value per share.**

The range runs from **0.63x** (2020, the COVID year, and the cheapest buying) to **1.00x** (2025,
the dearest). The discount has narrowed steadily: 0.63x-0.86x through 2023, then 0.99x in 2024
and 1.00x in 2025. On this measure the 2024-2025 purchases are the marginal ones.

Two cautions on the ratio, both against Loews:

1. **Year-end BVPS is not the book value at the moment of purchase.** Purchases are spread
   through the year; book value generally rose through the period. Measuring against
   *year-end* book therefore **flatters** the ratio in rising-book years. A mid-year book
   value would push several years closer to 1.0x.
2. **The 2022 ratio is an artifact.** Book value fell from $71.84 to $60.76 in 2022 not from
   losses but from a $3.5bn swing in accumulated other comprehensive loss as rates rose and
   CNA's bond portfolio marked down. Buying at 0.94x a temporarily depressed book is a
   different act from buying at 0.94x a stable one. The 2023 rebound to $70.65 confirms the
   mark was mostly rate-driven and reversed.

### The price-to-book at which the buying was done

Three ways of averaging, all of which agree:

| Method | Result |
|---|---|
| **Aggregate.** Total cash divided by the aggregate year-end book value of the shares bought (sum over years of shares repurchased x that year's BVPS) | **0.805x** |
| **Cash-weighted.** Each year's ratio weighted by that year's cash outlay | 0.818x |
| **Simple mean** of the seventeen annual ratios | 0.824x |

**The aggregate figure is the one to quote. Loews spent $11.28 billion buying back 52.7% of its
shares over seventeen years, at an average of about $48 per share, which was 81% of the book
value of what it bought.** It paid roughly 81 cents on the dollar of stated book, and never more
than 100 cents in any single year.

Year-by-year ratio, cheapest first: 

`2020 0.63x  2016 0.73x  2015 0.74x  2019 0.74x  2021 0.75x  2012 0.76x  2017 0.78x  2009 0.80x  2014 0.82x  2010 0.83x  2011 0.85x  2018 0.85x  2023 0.86x  2013 0.93x  2022 0.94x  2024 0.99x  2025 1.00x`

### What this does and does not establish

Buffett's second condition is that repurchase be made **below a conservative estimate of
intrinsic value**, not below book value. Book value is the proxy used here because it is the
only figure available consistently for seventeen years from primary filings. Whether book
value is a conservative floor for Loews's intrinsic value is a separate question and is **not**
settled by this table. It bears on it in two directions:

- **For book as a floor.** The largest single component of Loews's book is its majority stake in
  CNA, a listed insurer whose own equity is largely marked to market; Boardwalk Pipelines and
  Loews Hotels are carried at depreciated historical cost. Long-lived assets carried at
  depreciated cost are commonly worth more than carrying value, which argues intrinsic value
  sits above book. (The size of each component is not quantified here; that belongs in the
  sum-of-the-parts work, not in this file.)
- **Against book as a floor.** A holding company's book is not a claim on cash for the
  minority holder, and CNA's own book carries long-tail casualty reserves whose adequacy is a
  judgment. Book value here is stated, not conservative.

What the table does establish is the **factual** half of the test: the price paid, against the
only hard yardstick in the filings, never once exceeded stated book, and averaged about a 19%
discount to it. That is the evidence Q3 (are they rational?) has to weigh.

FY2007 10-K, verbatim:

> “During 2007, we purchased 14,789,949 shares of Loews common stock at an aggregate cost of
> $672 million. As a result of the proposed Separation and related proposed exchange offer of
> Loews common stock for Lorillard common stock referred to under ‘Overview-Lorillard

> Carolina Group stock until ten business days following completion or termination of the

That restriction is why 2008 open-market buying collapsed to $33 million. It was a legal
prohibition for most of the year, not a judgment that the stock was dear.

### Book value per share is NOT computable for 2006 and 2007

Total shareholders’ equity was $16,502 million at 2006-12-31 and $17,591 million at 2007-12-31
(FY2007 10-K balance sheet). Those totals stand behind **two classes of stock at once**: Loews
common stock and Carolina Group stock. Dividing either total by the Loews common share count
alone would credit the Loews common holder with equity that belonged to the tracking stock.
**No book value per Loews common share is stated for 2006 or 2007 in this file.** Allocating
the two classes would require the segment equity attribution and is out of scope here.

For 2008 the problem is gone, because the Carolina Group stock was eliminated in June 2008 and
the year-end balance sheet carries a single class. Equity attributable to Loews at 2008-12-31
was $13,133 million on 435,091,667 shares, or **$30.18** per share, against an implied 2008
repurchase price of $25.12, a ratio of **0.83x**. That is in line with the 2009 to 2025 record
but it rests on $33 million of buying, so it carries almost no weight.

### The 2008 split-off is NOT a repurchase

**This is the trap in the Loews share count and it must be stated plainly.** The share count
fell by 94,591,961 in 2008, from 529,683,628 to 435,091,667. Only 1,313,600 of that, about 1.4%
of the decline, was a repurchase. Substantially all of it was the Lorillard split-off.

FY2008 10-K, Note 2, verbatim (whitespace normalized):

> “On June 10, 2008, we distributed 108,478,429 shares, or approximately 62%, of the outstanding
> common stock of Lorillard in exchange for and in redemption of all of the 108,478,429
> outstanding shares of our former Carolina Group stock, in accordance with our Restated
> Certificate of Incorporation (the ‘Redemption’); and”
>
> “On June 16, 2008, we distributed the remaining 65,445,000 shares, or approximately 38%, of
> the outstanding common stock of Lorillard in exchange for 93,492,857 shares of Loews common
> stock, reflecting an exchange ratio of 0.70 (the ‘Exchange Offer’).”

And on the accounting:

> “The Loews common stock acquired by us in the Exchange Offer was recorded as a decrease in our
> Shareholders’ equity, reflecting the market value of the shares of Loews common stock
> delivered in the Exchange Offer. This decline was offset by a $4.3 billion gain to us from the
> Exchange Offer, which was reported as a gain on disposal of the discontinued business.”

**The reconciliation:**

| | Shares |
|---|---|
| Outstanding 2007-12-31 | 529,683,628 |
| Less: Exchange Offer, Loews shares tendered for Lorillard stock (**split-off, NOT a repurchase**) | (93,492,857) |
| Less: open-market repurchases for cash | (1,313,600) |
| Plus: shares issued, principally under compensation plans (residual) | 214,496 |
| **Outstanding 2008-12-31** | **435,091,667** |

**Why it is not a repurchase.** In a split-off the holder does not receive cash. Loews handed
over 65,445,000 shares of a **different company** and took back its own stock. No cash left the
business and no capital was returned; an asset was exchanged for shares. Buffett’s test asks
whether cash surplus to the business was used to buy stock below intrinsic value, and this
transaction spent no cash at all. Counting the 93.5 million shares as a repurchase would
overstate the 2008 buyback by a factor of about seventy and would corrupt any per-share
compounding figure that spans 2008.

**Consequence for the record.** The FY2009-FY2025 series in Block 1 starts after the split-off
is complete, so **Block 1 is clean of it**. Any longer series that starts in 2006 or 2007 must
break the 2008 share-count change into its two parts, as above, or it is wrong.

### THE CENTRAL FINDING: THE PHRASE "INTRINSIC VALUE" DOES NOT APPEAR

**"Intrinsic value" returns ZERO hits in the FY2025 10-K and ZERO hits in the 2026 proxy
statement.** Not in the repurchase disclosure, not in the MD&A, not in the compensation
discussion, not even in the option-award sense in which proxies commonly use the term.
`undervalued`, `discount to`, and `attractive price` likewise return nothing.

**Loews nowhere states that it repurchases stock because the stock is cheap.** The absence is
the finding, and it is recorded here as a finding rather than as a gap in the search.

### What the 10-K actually says. The full statement of policy, verbatim

Item 5, under the heading "Common Stock Repurchases", is the entire statement of why:

> "Our Board of Directors has authorized our management, as it deems appropriate, to purchase
> our outstanding common stock. Depending on market and other conditions, we may purchase
> shares of our common stock in the open market (including in open market transactions that may
> or may not satisfy all of the conditions of the Rule 10b-18 voluntary safe harbor), in
> privately negotiated transactions or otherwise."

That is all of it. It states an authorization and a set of mechanisms. It states no price
discipline, no valuation reference, no condition of any kind beyond "as it deems appropriate"
and "depending on market and other conditions." **This wording is essentially unchanged from
the FY2006 and FY2007 10-Ks quoted in Block 3**, twenty years earlier, which said Loews "may
purchase shares of our, and our subsidiaries', outstanding common stock in the open market or
otherwise." The company has said the same non-committal sentence for two decades.

**There is no announced program.** In the Item 5 issuer-purchases table for Q4 2025, column (c)
"Total number of shares purchased as part of publicly announced plans or programs" and column
(d) "Maximum number of shares (or approximate dollar value) of shares that may yet be purchased
under the plans or programs" both read **"N/A"** for all three months. Loews buys under a
standing board authorization with no announced dollar cap and no announced program. The Q4 2025
purchases as filed:

| Period | Shares purchased | Average price paid per share |
|---|---|---|
| October 1, 2025 - October 31, 2025 | 289,911 | $99.26 |
| November 1, 2025 - November 30, 2025 | 467,038 | $104.33 |
| December 1, 2025 - December 31, 2025 | 200,000 | $104.53 |

Those prices sit **above** the $90.71 year-end book value per share computed in Block 1, at
about 1.09x to 1.15x book. The full-year 2025 average of $90.56 is held to 1.00x only by the
cheaper buying earlier in the year. **The most recent quarter on record is the first stretch in
the whole twenty-year file in which Loews paid a clear premium to stated book.**

The rest of the 10-K treats repurchase purely as a cash movement. MD&A, verbatim:

> "Cash outflows in 2025 included the payment of $806 million to fund treasury stock purchases
> and $52 million of cash dividends to our shareholders."

And the equity note, verbatim:

> "Loews Corporation repurchased 8.9 million, 7.7 million and 14.0 million shares of its common
> stock at aggregate costs of $0.8 billion, $0.6 billion and $0.9 billion during the years ended
> December 31, 2025, 2024 and 2023. On December 31, 2025, 9.1 million shares of Loews
> Corporation common stock were retired. Upon retirement, treasury stock was eliminated through
> a reduction to common stock, APIC and retained earnings."

Quantities and dollars. No reason.

### What the proxy says

The proxy is where repurchase comes closest to being justified, and it still stops short. Under
"WHO WE ARE", verbatim:

> "Our primary function is to allocate our capital in ways that drive long-term value creation
> and returns for our shareholders. To do this we make decisions related to investments in our
> subsidiaries, repurchases of our shares, acquisitions and dispositions of subsidiaries and
> prudent investment of our cash and investment assets."

Repurchase is named as one of four capital-allocation levers. No price test is attached to it.

The Compensation Committee's list of the Chief Executive Officer's 2025 accomplishments,
verbatim:

> "Loews repurchased approximately 8.9 million shares, or 4.2%, of its common stock in 2025 and
> has repurchased approximately 64.4 million shares, or 23.9%, of its common stock from 2021
> through the end of 2025, while consistently maintaining a very strong liquidity position; and"

immediately followed by, verbatim:

> "the company's book value per share (excluding accumulated other comprehensive income)
> increased approximately 49.4% during the past five years."

**Read those two bullets together and the incentive is visible.** The buyback is credited to the
Chief Executive Officer by **volume** ("8.9 million shares, or 4.2%") and by **liquidity
preserved**, never by price paid. The yardstick placed next to it is growth in book value per
share excluding AOCI. Repurchase below book **mechanically** raises book value per share, so the
two bullets are not independent achievements: the first partly produces the second. Nothing in
the committee's language distinguishes buying at 0.63x book from buying at 1.15x book; both
count as shares repurchased, and both raise BVPS so long as the price is under book. The
framework's own warning applies: *"Never, ever, think about something else when you should be
thinking about the power of incentives"* **[E4-27]**.

For completeness, the pay metric itself is not a per-share or price-based measure at all.
Verbatim:

> "Performance-based income is defined in our Incentive Compensation Plan as our consolidated
> net income as adjusted by the Compensation Committee under the terms of our Incentive
> Compensation Plan to account for specific factors that may impact our business, but which the
> Compensation Committee deems reasonable and appropriate to exclude or include in determining
> performance for incentive compensation purposes."

Consolidated net income, committee-adjusted. Buying back stock cheaply does not raise it; if
anything, spending cash lowers investment income and therefore lowers it.

### An independent cross-check that the proxy hands us

The proxy's "approximately 64.4 million shares" for 2021 through 2025 can be checked against
the XBRL series in Block 1, which was assembled from a different tag in a different document:
21.1 + 12.7 + 14.0 + 7.7 + 8.9 = **64.4 million**. The proxy's "23.9%" against the 269,210,973
shares outstanding at 2020-12-31 gives 23.9%. The "4.2%" for 2025 against the 214,700,344
shares at 2024-12-31 gives 4.1%. **Block 1 is confirmed by the company's own arithmetic in a
separate filing.**

### Verdict on Buffett's second condition

| | |
|---|---|
| Did Loews in fact buy below book value? | **Yes, in every one of the seventeen years, at about 0.81x in aggregate.** Established in Blocks 1 and 2 from primary filings. |
| Does Loews **say** it buys below a conservative estimate of intrinsic value? | **No. No such statement exists in the FY2025 10-K or the 2026 proxy.** The phrase "intrinsic value" appears nowhere in either document. |
| Does Loews disclose any price discipline at all? | **No.** The sole stated policy is a standing board authorization exercised "as it deems appropriate" and "depending on market and other conditions," with no announced program and no dollar cap. |
| Is repurchase price part of how management is paid or judged? | **No.** The proxy credits repurchase by volume and pairs it with BVPS-ex-AOCI growth; the cash bonus metric is committee-adjusted consolidated net income. |

**The record and the rhetoric point the same way, but only the record is evidence.** Loews has
behaved for seventeen years exactly as a company buying below a conservative estimate of value
would behave, and it has never once said that is what it is doing. The behaviour therefore
cannot be credited to a stated and testable policy; it is an observed pattern that management
has left itself entirely free to abandon. The Q4 2025 prices of $99.26, $104.33 and $104.53,
all above year-end book of $90.71, are the first evidence in the file that it may be abandoning
it. That is the disconfirming evidence this section was built to hunt for **[E4-26]**, and it
should be carried forward, not set aside.
