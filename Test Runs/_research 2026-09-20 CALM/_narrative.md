## The finding: the filer wrote [E2-58] into its own risk factors

The corpus's commodity equation is *"persistent over-capacity without administered prices (or
costs) equals poor profitability"*, with long-term profitability set by *"the ratio of
supply-tight to supply-ample years"* and prosperity breeding the next glut. **Cal-Maine's Item
1A says it in the company's own voice:**

> *"During times when prices are high, the egg industry has typically produced more eggs,
> primarily by increasing the number of layers, which historically has ultimately resulted in an
> oversupply of eggs, leading to periods of lower prices."*

That is *"nothing fails like success"* filed with the Commission. Beside it: *"Our operating
results are materially impacted by market prices for eggs and feed grains (corn and soybean
meal), which are highly volatile, independent of each other, and out of our control"*; the
industry *"remains highly fragmented"* with the ten largest holding **57%** of table-egg layer
hens; and *"Competitive pressures may also restrict our ability to increase prices."*

**Criterion (2) of [E3-03] fails, and the mechanism is in the pricing disclosure rather than in
an impression.** *"Almost all of our conventional shell eggs are priced and sold under
market-based pricing frameworks"* keyed to *"one of the independently quoted regional wholesale
market prices for shell eggs"* - Urner Barry or the USDA. **A product priced off a published
quote for the identical product from anyone else has a close substitute by construction.** And
*"We do not sell eggs directly to consumers or set the prices at which eggs are sold to
consumers."*

## The [E2-58] exception is the whole question on a name like this, and it was tested

*"The one exception is a cost advantage that is both wide and sustainable … By definition such
exceptions are few."* **Cal-Maine has the strongest claim to that exception of any egg producer
in the United States** - the largest flock (50.0 million layers, 14.5 million pullets and
breeders), its own hatcheries, pullet farms and feed mills (2.1 of 2.2 million tons of finished
feed milled in-house), substantially all in-line processing, and 28 acquisitions since 1989. So
the run did not assert the commodity class; it built [E2-01]'s series and let the record answer.

| | |
|---|---|
| sixteen-year mean ROE | **16.6%** |
| **FY2017** | **-8.8%**, net loss $74.3M, **operating cash flow -$45.9M** |
| **FY2021** | **+0.2%**, net income $2.1M |
| the three supply-shock years (FY2016, FY2023, FY2025) | 34.5%, 47.1%, 47.6% |
| **the other thirteen years, mean** | **10.4%** |

**A cost advantage that is wide and sustainable does not produce a -8.8% year.** Ten point four
per cent is about where [E2-42] says *"red lights should start flashing."* The exception was
available, was tested against sixteen filed years, and was refused by the record rather than by
a prior.

## [E4-41] had a clean worked case here, and the flag was right

`level_shift` fired at **3.07** and `level_shift_oe` at **0.343**, both with *"STEP UP -
normalize down [E4-41]"*. Read against the filer's own variance table, the normalisation is owed
and the cause is named: conventional shell egg prices **+99.7% in FY2025** and **-50.9% in
FY2026**, with volumes *"relatively flat"* in both directions, because **40.2 million and 45.2
million commercial layer hens and pullets were depopulated due to HPAI** in 2024 and 2025.
**Those were overwhelmingly rivals' hens.** Cal-Maine lost about **352,000 pullets** to one
Maryland outbreak on 2026-03-14 and *"operations have fully resumed."* Conventional segment
income ran **$213.7M -> $1,290.0M -> $216.6M**.

**This is the cleanest [E4-41] case the project has had:** a favourable exogenous break whose
mechanism is a competitor's inventory being destroyed by a disease, disclosed in the filer's own
numbers on both the way up and the way down. **The flag has been wrong in other runs in this
project. It was checked, not assumed, and here it was right.**

## [E3-62]'s second step is answered by the contracts, not by inference

*"how much is going to stay home and how much is just going to flow through to the customer."*
Cal-Maine has written the answer into its own pricing frameworks: *"The majority of our specialty
shell eggs are priced and sold under frameworks that are based on cost of production"* -
**cost-plus passes every efficiency gain to the buyer by construction**, and specialty was
**36.8% of FY2026 net sales**. On the conventional half the price is a third-party quote. Even
the cost-based contracts leak the other way: *"customers may seek to renegotiate the terms of
their arrangements during periods of sustained low prices."* **Walmart with Sam's Club is 30.0%
of net sales and the top three are 43.1%**, so the counterparty has the weight to ask.

in court**, which is why the run recorded the litigation as Q2 class evidence and not as a Q3

## The uncomfortable part, written down rather than hedged

**This is not a bad business and the run should not be read as saying so.** Sixteen-year mean ROE
of 16.6% is respectable; there is no debt, the revolver is undrawn, equity is $2,640.5M, and the
company has compounded its flock by buying 28 businesses since 1989 without issuing paper.
**[E4-20]'s three savings accounts would very likely have called it *good*, and possibly better
than good, had Q4 been reached.** The file closed at Q2 for one reason and it is a structural one:
**the price of the product is a published number that anybody else's identical product also trades
at**, so whatever Cal-Maine earns above its cost of capital in a tight year is competed away in an
ample one, and the seventeen-year record is what that looks like. **[E2-38]'s line applies to the
industry rather than to these managers:** good jockeys, and the horse is a commodity.

**And the honest counterweight, because [E4-26] runs both ways.** If the prepared-foods and
owned-brand build actually crosses over - shell eggs were **84.6% to 94.3%** of net sales over
three years, and the announced projects add *"more than 30 percent"* of prepared-foods capacity
from mid-2027 through 2028 - **that is a different company and would need a new run, not a revision
of this one.** The reversal condition is recorded in words in the run file. **No price band is
armed and no `PORTFOLIO.md` row is added**, because the name failed on the business.
