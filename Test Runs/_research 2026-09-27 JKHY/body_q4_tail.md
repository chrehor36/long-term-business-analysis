### Name the specific way THIS business dies **[E2-27, E3-24]**
- **The mechanism: #11 THE PASS-THROUGH** (`Screens/SURVIVAL SHAPES - index.md`: *"the company survives but gains are passed to customers and suppliers, compressing the owner's return"*). The moat is the cost to a bank of moving its ledger; the moment that cost is paid anyway, the client re-chooses on price. Two filed forces make that moment more frequent: **consolidation** (every client bought by a bank on another core leaves: *"an early termination most commonly occurs due to the acquisition of one of our clients by another financial institution"*; the banks in the filer's market fell from about 9,000 in fiscal 2006 to 4,300) and **the company's own re-platforming** (moving clients to the public-cloud *"fully functional modern alternative for existing core functions"* is itself a conversion). At each renewal the filer already reports *"price compression"* and pays *"upfront incentive payments or credits"*. **Exposure, not experience [E4-40]:** nineteen years of stable margins are the experience; the exposure is that a six-year contract base re-prices one-sixth at a time.
- **Quantified from filed figures, and the resulting outcome:** cloud and processing revenue ($816.1M + $1,096.3M = $1,912.4M in fiscal 2026) sits mostly on six-year terms, so roughly one-sixth, about $319M, comes up each year. **The filer states no compression rate**; as an illustration only (my arithmetic, not a filed rate), a 10% cut on each year's renewals would take about $32M a year off revenue and, with costs unchanged, off operating income, against operating income growth of $66.3M in fiscal 2026 and total operating income of $635.0M. The client-incentive balance, the filed trace of the same force, grew $70.2M in three years (fiscal 2023-2026). The bank count fell by about 50 in fiscal 2026. **Outcome: the business lives, the returns drift down from 34-39% toward the rivals' levels, and owner earnings grow more slowly than revenue.** It does not reach insolvency on any filed figure: debt is $40M.
