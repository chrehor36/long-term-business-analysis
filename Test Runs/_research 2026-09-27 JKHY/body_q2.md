**The hypotheses I formed while reading, both stated so they can be attacked [E4-26].** *(a) The system of record for about one bank in five and one credit union in six in its market: once a bank's ledger is on a Jack Henry core, moving it is a multi-year project the client pays for, so clients stay for decades, leave mainly when they are bought, and pay a fee when they do; the company has earned 34-50% pre-tax on its operating capital (goodwill excluded) in every year from fiscal 2011 to 2026 and 20-28% with goodwill, far above every SEC-filing rival, and has taken share in both core markets while those markets halved. Customers act as though there is no close substitute [E3-43].* *(b) A software vendor in a four-to-six-way market whose own 10-K lists price among the grounds of competition, says renewals are a renegotiation and that "certain" of them have compressed price, and has begun paying clients upfront incentives that grow faster than revenue; the return is the return of an installed base that is being slowly repriced downward at each renewal, and switching costs slow the loss without setting the price (the FUL and sensor precedent).* The file does not get to pick. Each is tested on the filings below.

**[E3-03], criterion by criterion.** *"An economic franchise arises from a product or service that: (1) is needed or desired; (2) is thought by its customers to have no close substitute and; (3) is not subject to price regulation."*
- **(1) Needed or desired [x]: passes.** Every bank and credit union must keep its ledger on some system every day; the 10-K's own count of the market is *"approximately 4,300 commercial banks and savings institutions in the $55 billion and under asset range"* and *"approximately 4,400 domestic credit unions"*.
- **(3) Not subject to price regulation [x]: passes.** No fee schedule is set by a regulator. The regulator examines the company (*"Our private cloud services are subject to examination by FBA regulators under the Bank Service Company Act"*); it does not set its price. This is the difference from BR, whose core price is written in exchange rules.
- **(2) Thought by its customers to have no close substitute: the contested criterion, and where this run does its work.**
  - **The filer's words against it.** *"We operate in highly competitive and rapidly evolving markets and our business will be adversely affected if we fail to compete effectively."* *"We compete on the basis of product quality, reliability, performance, ease of use, quality of support and services, integration with other products, and pricing."* The named rivals: *"Our core solutions compete with large vendors that provide information and transaction processing solutions to banks and credit unions, including Fidelity National Information Services, Inc.; Fiserv, Inc.; Corelation, Inc.; and Finastra."* At renewal: *"Renewal time presents our clients with the opportunity to consider other providers or to renegotiate their contracts with us, including reducing the services we provide or negotiating the prices paid for our services. Certain of our renewals have resulted in price compression between the former and renegotiated contracts."* (the second sentence first appears in the **fiscal 2025** 10-K and is repeated in fiscal 2026; traced in all twenty-seven 10-Ks). Earlier, fiscal 2016 and 2017: *"Electronic payment services continued to show growth over the prior year, although that growth slowed due to some of our large customers being acquired and price compression on contract renewals in our card services offerings."* A standing risk factor since fiscal 2016: *"we may need to lower prices or offer other terms that negatively impact our results of operations in order to successfully compete."*
  - **The price the company pays to win and keep contracts, as filed.** Fiscal 2026: *"Contracts with our clients often include upfront incentive payments or credits provided to clients at or near the inception of an arrangement. These amounts are accounted for as a reduction to the transaction price"*. The contract-asset balance these create was **$108.3M at 2023-06-30, $136.9M (2024), $157.9M (2025), $178.5M (2026)**, from 5.2% to 7.0% of revenue, **up 65% in three years against revenue up 22%**. The description of the same balance changed three times in three filings: fiscal 2024, *"Contract assets primarily result from revenue being recognized when or as control of a solution or service is transferred to the client, but where invoicing is contingent upon the completion of other performance obligations"*; fiscal 2025, *"Contract assets primarily result from client discounts (contract incentives)"*; fiscal 2026, the incentive sentence above. (A candor point for Q3: the relabel moves toward plain English, not away from it.)

**The remaining Q2 tests.**
- **[E4-04], the durability question, in the ruling's form.** *"does a lapse in spending destroy the structure, or merely narrow it — and does the spending defend the same advantage, or buy its replacement?"* The installed base sits on six-year contracts with minimum payments, and has survived the move from on-premise to hosted delivery inside the filed record (the 10-K still offers both *"with identical functionality"*); software development stepped up from 2.8% of revenue (fiscal 2011, $27.0M) to 6.9-8.0% (fiscal 2020-2026; $184.2M on $2,544.3M in 2026) and has held there, a step recorded at Q4 for the capex band, not a rising spike. A lapse would narrow the lead, not dissolve the ledgers already on the cores. **The live exposure is the public-cloud rebuild**: the Jack Henry Platform is being developed *"into a fully functional modern alternative for existing core functions"*, and moving a client onto it is the conversion event at which a client can also choose a rival. That is recorded as the named death at Q4, not as a Q2 failure, because the filings show a lead **maintained** through one delivery generation already (on-premise to private cloud), which the ruling of 2026-09-20 says does not fail [E4-04]. **The perimeter close (UNKNOWABLE) was considered and refused in writing**: durability can be judged from nineteen years of unit counts and returns, which is the evidence the ruling asks for.
- **[E4-36], which cause of success.** An *"extreme of good performance over many factors"* is the nearest: service, a broad add-on catalogue sold into the installed base, and the installed base itself. Not wave-riding [E3-51]: the record runs through the 2009-2011 bank failures, the pandemic and the 2023 regional-bank stress with the operating margin never below 21%.

The attack on the digital layer is real and is where Jack Henry's own growth is being fought for.

**THE STRONGEST EVIDENCE AGAINST THE VERDICT BELOW, hunted hardest [E4-26, E3-41, E4-51].** The verdict below is IN (NARROW), so the case for OUT is set out first, as its holders would state it:
1. **The filer says price is contested.** "Pricing" is a ground of competition; renewals are where clients *"consider other providers or ... renegotiat[e]"*; *"Certain of our renewals have resulted in price compression"*. [E2-44]'s first characteristic is failed on the filer's words, which is how the BR run failed Broadridge's unregulated lines the same morning and how the PAY run failed Paymentus.
2. **The company is buying renewals.** Upfront incentives grew from $108.3M to $178.5M in three years, 65% against revenue's 22%, and the label on the balance was rewritten twice in the same period. A franchise does not pay its customers to stay.
3. **Switching costs are not a franchise.** They slow the loss of a customer; they do not set the price (the FUL and sensor-maker precedents). At renewal the customer has four named substitutes, two of them larger.
4. **The unit series has turned.** Fifty fewer banks in fiscal 2026, the first share dip in the series read.

**Answered.** (1) and (3) are the reason the class is NARROW and not WIDE, and the reason [E3-33]/[E5-28] is refused; they are accepted as facts. They do not carry OUT, because [E3-03] asks what the customers think about substitutes and [E3-43] names the evidence: the customers' behaviour over nineteen years (share held in banks, share up from about 8% to 16% in credit unions, exits attributed by the filer to acquisitions, a backlog of 3.3 years of revenue) and returns of 34-50% on capital ex goodwill that no filing rival approaches (the row) and that [E3-43] says a mere "business" earns only as the low-cost operator or in tight supply, neither of which applies. That distinguishes this file from BR, whose high returns were shared by a shrinking rival and whose core price is set by an exchange, and from FUL and the sensor-maker, whose own filings named price erosion as a standing annual rate and whose returns including goodwill sat at the bottom of their rows. It is the CRM precedent: criterion (2) passing on revealed behaviour as a switching-cost moat with no pricing power, class NARROW. (2) is the strongest single fact against, and it is carried forward: it is the first Q6 thesis-breaking metric, and it moves the direction to narrowing. (4) is one year and 50 banks against a market that lost about 140; it is recorded as direction, not as a verdict. **[E3-47]** (a wrongly closed file inside the circle is the costliest error) and its opposite, the risk of promoting a vendor on stability, were both weighed; the verdict does not rest on the manager or on the story, and no Q3 finding is used here.
