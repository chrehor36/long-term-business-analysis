## GATE 1 — CIRCLE OF COMPETENCE
APA Corporation is a holding company; its operating subsidiary Apache
explores for, develops, and produces crude oil, natural gas, and NGLs.
"APA Corporation (APA or the Company) is an independent energy company
that owns subsidiaries that explore for, develop, and produce crude oil,
natural gas, and NGLs. The Company's business has oil and gas operations
in three geographic areas: the U.S., Egypt, and offshore the U.K. in the
North Sea (North Sea)." U.S. operations concentrated in the Permian Basin
(largely acquired scale via the April 2024 Callon Petroleum all-stock deal,
~$4.5B including debt). APA also has pre-production development ongoing
offshore Suriname (targeting first production 2028) and early-stage
exploration in Uruguay and Alaska.

Unit economics in plain terms: drill/acquire wells → produce barrels of oil
and Mcf of gas → sell at whatever the prevailing market price is that day
→ revenue minus lease operating expense, G&A, taxes, and DD&A. There is no
management-language complexity here — it is a straightforward extraction-
and-sale business. The genuine complexity is geological/engineering
(reserve estimation, decline curves) and geopolitical (Egypt concession
terms, North Sea fiscal/decommissioning regime, offshore Suriname execution
risk) — all evaluable at a qualitative level without an informational edge,
though reserve engineering itself is specialist territory this run does not
independently verify (relies on the Company's disclosed reserve audit by
independent petroleum engineers, per the auditor's report).

Segments: three reporting geographies (US, Egypt, North Sea), each a
variation on the same core economics (find/produce/sell a fungible
commodity) — evaluable as a single business type rather than genuinely
distinct businesses. Informational edge: none — this is one of the most
widely covered, widely modeled sectors in public markets.
**VERDICT: PASS.**

## GATE 2 — MOAT (per segment)
Franchise test [E3-03], applied to the business as a whole (segments share
identical economics — a barrel of Permian oil, Egyptian oil, or North Sea
oil is priced off the same global benchmarks with quality/location
differentials, not off a company-specific price):

- **Needed/desired**: yes, generically — the world needs oil and gas. But
  the franchise test asks whether *this company's product* is needed/
  desired, not whether the commodity class is; APA's own barrel is
  indistinguishable from any competitor's barrel of the same grade.
- **No close substitute**: **FAILS**. Oil and gas are textbook fungible
  commodities — a barrel of WTI-grade crude from APA's Permian acreage is a
  perfect substitute for the same grade produced by any other operator.
  APA's own 10-K "Competitive Conditions" section states: "The oil and gas
  industry is highly competitive in the exploration for and acquisitions
  of reserves, the acquisition of oil and gas leases, equipment, and
  personnel required to find and produce reserves, and the gathering and
  marketing of oil, gas, and NGLs. The Company's competitors include
  national oil companies, major integrated oil and gas companies, other
  independent oil and gas companies, and participants in other industries
  supplying energy and fuel." The filing further concedes competitors "may
  possess financial or other resources substantially larger than the
  Company possesses" — a disadvantage, not an advantage, in bidding for the
  reserves the business depends on.
- **Unregulated**: **FAILS**. "The Company's U.S. operations are subject to
  federal, state, and local laws and regulations, including restrictions on
  production, changes in taxes and other amounts payable to governments,
  price or gathering rate controls, environmental protection laws and
  regulations, standards for drilling, completing, and equipping oil and
  gas wells, standards for plugging, abandonment, decommissioning, and site
  restoration." Egypt operations run under a foreign government concession
  (revenue shared with the Egyptian General Petroleum Corporation, a
  disclosed major customer); North Sea operations carry UK-specific
  decommissioning and windfall-tax-type exposure. This is a heavily
  regulated, politically exposed business on every axis.
- **Proven by pricing power + returns on capital**: **FAILS decisively**.
  Directly from the risk factors: "The market prices for crude oil, natural
  gas, and NGLs depend on factors beyond the Company's control, including:
  demand... worldwide and domestic supplies... actions taken by foreign oil
  and gas producing nations, including... OPEC+... political conditions...
  the price, competitiveness, availability of alternative fuels..." — a
  fourteen-bullet list of factors, none of which is "APA's own pricing
  decision." 2025 NYMEX oil ranged $55.44–$80.73/bbl (a 45% high-to-low
  swing) and gas $2.65–$9.86/MMBtu within the single year. Net income swung
  from $2,855M (2023) to $804M (2024) to $1,434M (2025) on commodity moves,
  not operational execution — the opposite signature of pricing power.

Primary moat metric: net income attributable to common stock, 3-yr trend:
2023 $2,855M → 2024 $804M (-72%) → 2025 $1,434M (+78%). This is commodity-
price whipsaw, not a franchise trend — there is no "moat metric" to track
here because the P&L is a leveraged bet on an external price the Company
explicitly disclaims control over.

**Class: NONE.** **Direction: N/A (no moat to widen or narrow).** APA is a
well-run operator (portfolio diversification across three continents,
successful cost reduction via the Callon integration, disciplined debt
paydown from divestiture proceeds) inside a structurally moat-less,
price-taking industry. Being a good operator in a bad-structure industry is
not a substitute for a franchise — Buffett's distinction between a wonderful
business and a business run wonderfully applies exactly here.

**VERDICT: FAIL — STOP.**
