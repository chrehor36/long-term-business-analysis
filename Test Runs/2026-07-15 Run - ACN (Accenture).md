## GATE 2 — MOAT
Franchise test [E3-03], from Accenture's own 10-K: **"Accenture operates in
a highly competitive and rapidly changing global marketplace. We compete
with a variety of organizations that offer solutions and services
competitive with those we offer but we believe no other company offers the
full range of services at scale that Accenture does, which uniquely
positions us in a highly competitive market. Our clients typically retain
us on a non-exclusive basis. Our competitors include large multinational IT
service providers... off-shore IT service providers in lower-cost
locations, particularly in India; accounting firms and consultancies..."**
- **No close substitute**: **mixed**. Accenture explicitly claims a
  scale/breadth advantage ("no other company offers the full range of
  services at scale") — a real, differentiated claim not made in
  Cognizant's competition section — but immediately concedes clients
  "typically retain us on a non-exclusive basis," meaning no lock-in at
  the relationship level, and names direct substitutes (large multinational
  and off-shore providers). The scale claim is credible given Accenture's
  size (far larger than named peers), but "uniquely positions us" is the
  company's own characterization, not an independently verified fact.
- Same disintermediation risk named in Cognizant's filing (client in-house
  capability/GCCs) is a reasonable read-across risk for the industry
  generally, though not independently confirmed as explicitly named in
  Accenture's own competition section this pass.
**Class: NARROW** (scale/breadth advantage, real but not exclusive-lock-in,
consistent with the non-exclusive-retention concession), **Direction:
STABLE-to-NARROWING** given the same industry-wide AI/insourcing pressure
already documented at Cognizant.
**VERDICT: PASS with NARROW classification** — a stronger scale claim than
Cognizant's, but not treated as a WIDE moat given the explicit non-
exclusivity concession.
