# Company Run — PayPal Holdings, Inc. (PYPL) — 2026-07-15
Framework v3.0. From the mechanical screen. Source: FY2025 10-K (CIK
1633917). GICS classifies PYPL under Financials, but it is a payments
network/processor, not a bank, insurer, or lender — **not excluded under
the standing "ignore banks" rule**, and the Ruling 2 financial-track
question is addressed explicitly below rather than defaulted either way.

## STEP 0 — RATE REFRESH
US 30-yr **5.10%**. Mkt cap $41.79B — no size premium.

## GATE 1 — CIRCLE OF COMPETENCE
Digital payments network (PayPal, Venmo) — a two-sided platform connecting
consumers and merchants, monetized via transaction take-rate.
**VERDICT: PASS.**

## GATE 2 — MOAT
Franchise test [E3-03], from PayPal's own 10-K: **"Competition also may
intensify as new competitors emerge, businesses combine or enter into new
partnerships, and established companies in other segments expand to
become competitive with various aspects of our business. Our business
faces competition from a wide range of businesses and from all forms of
physical and electronic payments. We face competition from banks and
financial institutions... payment networks... payment card processors..."**
- **No close substitute**: **weakening** — PayPal's historical moat is
  genuine two-sided network effects (merchants accept PayPal because
  consumers use it, and vice versa), a real mechanism, but the company's
  own words concede an intensifying, broadening competitive set. Apple
  Pay, Google Pay, and card networks' own digital-wallet offerings
  (general knowledge, not quoted in this filing this pass) have eroded
  PayPal's earlier checkout-button ubiquity advantage.
**Class: NARROW**, **Direction: NARROWING** — real, established network
effects, but a genuinely more contested position than PayPal held a
decade ago.
**VERDICT: PASS with NARROW/NARROWING classification.**

## GATE 4 — FINANCIAL QUALITY (full OE formula, P44 ASSET-LIGHT band)
**Ruling 2 financial-track judgment call, made explicitly**: PayPal holds
"funds payable and amounts due to customers" as a real balance-sheet
liability, but this is short-duration transaction float passing through
the platform — not underwritten insurance risk or a loan book the way a
bank or insurer holds risk-bearing assets/liabilities. **This run treats
PayPal under the non-financial survival track**, not Ruling 2's mechanical
leverage ceiling. As a sanity check regardless: Total assets $80,173M ÷
equity $20,256M (FY2025) = **3.96:1** — comfortably under 10:1 even had the
ceiling applied, supporting (not contradicting) the classification choice.
