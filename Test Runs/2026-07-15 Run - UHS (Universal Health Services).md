## GATE 2 — MOAT
Franchise test [E3-03], from UHS's own 10-K: **"The health care industry is
highly competitive. In recent years, competition among healthcare providers
for patients has intensified... In all of the geographical areas in which
we operate, there are other facilities that provide services comparable to
those offered by our facilities. In addition, some of our competitors
include hospitals that are owned by tax-supported governmental agencies or
by nonprofit corporations..."** — a direct concession that comparable
capacity exists in its own markets, and that some competitors have
structural funding advantages (tax support, endowments) UHS does not.
- **No close substitute**: **weak on this text alone**, though hospital/
  behavioral-health facilities carry real, general-knowledge structural
  entry barriers this run did not independently verify in this filing —
  certificate-of-need regulations in many states restrict new acute-care
  capacity, and behavioral health facility siting faces its own permitting
  hurdles — a plausible, but not filing-confirmed, local-market moat
  mechanism, flagged as general industry knowledge rather than a corpus- or
  filing-sourced claim.
- **Proven by pricing power**: **weak** — reimbursement rates are largely
  set by payers (government programs, insurers), not the operator.
**Class: NARROW** (local capacity-constrained geographic position,
plausible but not independently filing-verified this pass), **Direction:
STABLE**.
**VERDICT: PASS with NARROW classification**, explicitly flagging that the
strongest part of the moat case (regulatory entry barriers) rests on
general industry knowledge, not a verbatim citation from this filing.
