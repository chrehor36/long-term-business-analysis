**Lease cost — and this is the number that matters.**
> *"**Lease costs associated with fixed payments** on the Company's operating leases were **$2.1
> billion for 2025** and $2.0 billion for both 2024 and 2023. **Lease costs associated with
> variable payments** on the Company's leases were **$16.1 billion, $13.8 billion and $13.9
> billion** for 2025, 2024 and 2023, respectively."*

And the definition of those variable payments:
> *"Payments under the Company's lease arrangements may be fixed or variable, and **variable lease
> payments are primarily based on purchases of output of the underlying leased assets**."*

**Lease cost as % of revenue (net sales $416,161m FY2025):**
- Fixed operating lease cost: $2.1bn = **0.50% of revenue**
- **Variable lease cost: $16.1bn = 3.87% of revenue**
- **Total lease cost: $18.2bn = 4.37% of revenue**

**THIS IS THE LOAD-BEARING FINDING FOR MAINTENANCE CAPEX.** The $16.1bn variable line is
**supplier tooling and manufacturing equipment by another name.** Apple embeds production
equipment in supplier facilities under arrangements whose payments flex with output; because the
payments are variable they generate **no ROU asset and no lease liability**, and the cost lands in
cost of sales rather than in capex. It grew **+17% y/y** in FY2025 ($13.8bn → $16.1bn).

Consequence for the framework: **Apple's reported capex of $12,715m understates the annual capital
intensity of its manufacturing base by roughly the variable lease line.** A capex-based owner
earnings deduction of $12.7bn is *not* the whole maintenance requirement. The expense is already
charged through the income statement (so owner earnings built from net income are not
double-counting), but any analysis that reasons "Apple only spends $13bn a year on plant" is
wrong by more than an order of magnitude in economic substance. **Flag this explicitly in the
Q4 maintenance-capex judgment. `[E1-xx]`-style disclosed judgment required.**

Also disclosed: **$523 million** of fixed payment obligations under leases **not yet commenced**.
MD&A gives the on-a-different-basis figure: *"As of September 27, 2025, the Company had **fixed
lease payment obligations of $16.8 billion**, with $2.6 billion payable within 12 months."*

FY2025 maturity ladder: 2026 $4,752 · 2027 $3,708 · 2028 $1,981 · 2029 $1,306 · 2030 $788 ·
thereafter $773 · **total $13,308**.
Q3 FY2026 ladder: rest of 2026 $2,053 · 2027 $7,652 · 2028 $6,406 · 2029 $5,421 · 2030 $5,481 ·

**These long-dated commitments MORE THAN DOUBLED (+$14.3bn, +108%) in nine months.** The

non-current liabilities rising from $41,549m to $55,080m over the same nine months, this is a

**not** explain the increase in the 10-Q. **UNRESEARCHED — the resolving document is the FY2026
10-K (due ~Oct/Nov 2026) or an investor-day disclosure. Name it and go back for it.**

Verbatim (FY2025 10-K, MD&A):
> *"**Manufacturing Purchase Obligations** — The Company utilizes several outsourcing partners to
> manufacture subassemblies for the Company's products and to perform final assembly and testing of
> finished products. The Company also obtains individual components for its products from a wide
> variety of individual suppliers. As of September 27, 2025, the Company had **manufacturing
> purchase obligations of $56.2 billion, with $55.4 billion payable within 12 months.**"*

> *"**Other Purchase Obligations** — The Company's other purchase obligations primarily consist of
> **noncancelable obligations to acquire capital assets, including assets related to product
> manufacturing**, and noncancelable obligations related to supplier arrangements, licensed
> intellectual property and content, and distribution rights. As of September 27, 2025, the Company
> had other purchase obligations of **$14.8 billion, with $7.0 billion payable within 12 months.**"*

FY2023 added a cancellability statement that FY2024/FY2025 dropped: *"The Company's manufacturing
purchase obligations are **primarily noncancelable**."*

**Total off-balance-sheet purchase commitments at FY2025 year-end: $56.2bn + $14.8bn = $71.0bn**,
of which **$62.4bn falls due within twelve months**. Against net cash of $33.8bn at that date,
this is the single largest unrecorded claim on the balance sheet — though it is matched by
saleable product, and the risk is disclosed:
> *"Manufacturing purchase obligations cover the Company's forecasted component and manufacturing
> requirements, **typically for periods up to 150 days**. Because the Company's markets are
> volatile ... there is a risk the Company will forecast incorrectly and order or produce excess or
> insufficient amounts of components or products, **or not fully utilize purchase commitments.**"*

**No line item labelled "supplier tooling" appears anywhere.** The word **"tooling" returns 0 hits
in the FY2025 10-K.** Apple discloses the economics through the variable-lease line and the
"noncancelable obligations to acquire capital assets, including assets related to product
manufacturing" phrase in Other Purchase Obligations — it does **not** break out a tooling figure.
Record this as a **genuine disclosure gap**: the document that would resolve it does not exist,
which under the framework makes the precise tooling number **UNKNOWABLE**, not UNRESEARCHED.

## 4C. CONTINGENCIES — THE NOTE SAYS NOTHING; ITEM 3 SAYS EVERYTHING

**The contingencies note is one generic paragraph, identical in the FY2025 10-K and the Q3 FY2026
10-Q, naming NO matter and quantifying NOTHING:**
> *"**Contingencies** — The Company is subject to various legal proceedings and claims that have
> arisen in the ordinary course of business and that have not been fully resolved. The outcome of
> litigation is inherently uncertain. **In the opinion of management, there was not at least a
> reasonable possibility the Company may have incurred a material loss, or a material loss greater
> than a recorded accrual, concerning loss contingencies for asserted legal and other claims.**"*

**No accrual is disclosed. No reasonably-possible range is disclosed. Not one matter is named in
the financial statements.** Under ASC 450 this is a defensible position only if management truly
believes no material loss is reasonably possible — an assertion that sits uneasily beside an
Article 6(4) proceeding whose maximum sanction is **10% of worldwide net sales (~$42bn)**. Apple
resolves the tension by treating the fine exposure as not "reasonably possible" rather than as
"reasonably possible but not estimable." **This is the sharpest Q3 tension in the entire file.
It is not a misstatement — the named matters are fully described in Item 3 — but the
financial-statement note is doing the minimum the standard allows.**

### The named matters (FY2025 10-K Item 3, updated by Q3 FY2026 10-Q Part II Item 1)

**1. EU Digital Markets Act — QUANTIFIED, the only one.**
> *"On April 23, 2025, the Commission **fined the Company €500 million** in the Article 5(4)
> Investigation and issued a cease and desist order requiring the Company to remove technical and
> commercial restrictions that prevent developers from steering users to alternative distribution
> channels outside the App Store. **The Company has appealed** ... Also on April 23, 2025, the
> Commission issued preliminary findings in the Article 6(4) Investigation. **If the Commission
> makes a final determination ... it can issue a cease and desist order and may impose fines up to
> 10% of the Company's annual worldwide net sales.** The Commission may also seek to impose
> additional fines if it deems that the Company has violated a cease and desist order."*

10% of FY2025 net sales = **~$41.6bn**. Apple states it *"believes that it complies with the DMA."*

**2. DOJ antitrust suit (filed 2024-03-21) — NOT quantified.**
> *"On March 21, 2024, the DOJ and a number of state and district attorneys general filed a civil
> antitrust lawsuit in the U.S. District Court for the District of New Jersey against the Company
> alleging **monopolization or attempted monopolization in the markets for 'performance
> smartphones' and 'smartphones'** ... The DOJ is seeking **equitable relief** ... In addition,
> various civil litigation matters have been filed in state and federal courts in the U.S. alleging
> similar violations ... and **seeking monetary damages**. The Company believes it has substantial
> defenses and intends to vigorously defend itself."*
Unchanged word-for-word between the FY2025 10-K and the Q3 FY2026 10-Q — **no procedural progress
disclosed in ~10 months.**

**3. Epic v. Apple — MOVED SIGNIFICANTLY, and in Apple's favour.**
FY2025 10-K position: the California District Court on 2025-04-30 found Apple **in violation of
the 2021 Injunction**, barred *"any commission or any fee on purchases that consumers make outside
an app"*, and — the striking sentence — *"**referred the Company to the U.S. Attorney for the
Northern District of California for a determination whether criminal contempt proceedings are
appropriate.**"*

Q3 FY2026 10-Q update:
> *"On **December 11, 2025**, the Ninth Circuit Court issued an order **upholding the 2025
> Injunction in part and modifying certain aspects** to allow the Company to require parity in
> size, form and placement between the Company's in-app purchase and any links ... **The Ninth
> Circuit Court also held that the Company can charge some commission on link-out purchases**, and
> remanded to the California District Court ... On **May 21, 2026**, the Company filed a petition
> seeking Supreme Court review ... On **June 30, 2026, the Supreme Court granted the Company's
> petition to review the applicable legal standard for civil contempt.**"*

Note: the criminal-contempt referral language **disappears** from the Q3 FY2026 10-Q. Apple does
not say it was resolved; it simply is not repeated. **UNRESEARCHED — nameable document: the
docket in N.D. Cal. / the U.S. Attorney's office. Do not infer resolution from omission.**

**4. US v. Google search-distribution remedies — DISCLOSED IN RISK FACTORS, NOT IN ITEM 3 OR THE
CONTINGENCIES NOTE.** This is correct placement (Apple is not a party) but it is the biggest
economic exposure of the four:
> *"the Company **earns revenue from licensing arrangements with Google LLC** ... to offer their
> search services on the Company's platforms ... On August 5, 2024, Google was found to have
> violated U.S. antitrust laws. In connection with this finding, on **September 2, 2025**, the
> U.S. District Court for the District of Columbia **ordered certain remedies**. The court's order
> is subject to further proceedings ... **The court's order is also subject to appeal by both the
> DOJ and Google. A reversal of the order on appeal could result in imposition of certain remedies
> initially proposed by the DOJ, such as those prohibiting Google from offering the Company
> commercial terms for search distribution. If implemented, these remedies could materially
> adversely affect the Company's ability to earn revenue from such licensing arrangements.**"*

**Apple never discloses the dollar amount of the Google TAC payments anywhere in the 10-K.** This
is high-margin, near-pure-profit Services revenue widely estimated at $20bn+ annually. **The
document that would resolve it does not exist in Apple's filings → UNKNOWABLE from primary
sources.** It is, however, the single most concrete candidate for "the named way it dies" in Q4.

**5. State Aid / Ireland — RESOLVED AND PAID.** 11 mentions in the FY2025 10-K, all historical.
The €13bn-plus matter was closed by the ECJ on 2024-09-10 and charged in Q4 FY2024 as a **$10.2bn
net one-time income tax charge** ($15.8bn to Ireland, less a $4.8bn US foreign tax credit and
$823m of unrecognized tax benefits). See `q3_disclosure_flags.md` §1B. **No open state-aid
exposure.**

**Critical Audit Matter:** the E&Y CAM is **uncertain tax positions** — not litigation. The
auditor did not flag the legal contingencies as a CAM.

## 4D. CAPITALIZED SOFTWARE AND THE R&D POLICY

**Full-text counts, FY2025 10-K:**
| term | count |
|---|---|
| "capitalized software" | **0** |
| "software development costs" | **0** |
| "internal-use software" | **5** |

**Apple discloses NO capitalized software asset and NO capitalized software policy.** All five
"internal-use software" hits are accounted for:
- **Four** are the *future* accounting pronouncement, not current policy: *"In September 2025, the
  FASB issued ASU No. 2025-06, Intangibles—Goodwill and Other—**Internal-Use Software** (Subtopic
  350-40) ... **ASU 2025-06 will be effective for the Company in its first quarter of 2029**, and
  early adoption is permitted. The Company is currently evaluating the timing and method of its
  adoption."*
- **One** is a PP&E asset-class label: the Note 5 gross PP&E table line **"Machinery, equipment and
  internal-use software — $83,420m (FY2025), $80,205m (FY2024)"**. This line is **not
  disaggregated**; the software component is not separately stated. Depreciation on all PP&E was
  **$8.0bn (FY2025), $8.2bn (FY2024), $8.5bn (FY2023)**.

**R&D accounting: there is no capitalization policy because R&D is expensed.** Note 1's Summary of
Significant Accounting Policies contains no research-and-development paragraph at all — Apple's
policy list covers revenue, share-based compensation, cash equivalents, trade receivables,
marketable securities, inventories, PP&E, derivatives and income taxes, and stops. R&D runs
through the income statement in full: **$34,550m in FY2025** (8.3% of revenue), up from $31,370m
in FY2024.

**R&D is accelerating hard.** Q3 FY2026 10-Q: R&D of **$11,729m for the quarter** (vs $8,866m,
**+32% y/y**) and **$34,035m for nine months** (vs $25,684m, **+33% y/y**) — nine months of FY2026
has already nearly equalled all of FY2025. **This is the largest single change in Apple's cost
structure in the file and it is entirely expensed, entirely unfunded by capitalization.**

**Verdict for the framework: CONSERVATIVE.** Apple takes the most expense-forward treatment
available — no software capitalization, no R&D capitalization, no deferred cost asset. Under
[E5-08] this is the direction that understates current earnings and understates the asset base,
which is the honest direction. **When ASU 2025-06 becomes effective in Q1 FY2029 Apple will be
required to capitalize some internal-use software, which will mechanically raise reported
earnings. Pre-register that expectation now so it is not mistaken for operating improvement.**
