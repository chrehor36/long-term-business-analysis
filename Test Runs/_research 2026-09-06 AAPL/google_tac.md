## 1. THE HEADLINE FINDING — APPLE **DOES** NAME GOOGLE, AND **DOES** NAME THE REMEDIES ORDER

This corrects the premise that Apple is silent. Apple is not silent. It is **specific about
the counterparty and the litigation, and completely silent about the amount.**

### 1.1 VERBATIM — FY2025 10-K, Item 1A Risk Factors

> "Further, the Company has commercial relationships with other companies in the technology
> industry that are or may become subject to investigations and litigation that, if resolved
> against those other companies, could materially adversely affect the Company's commercial
> relationships with those business partners and materially adversely affect the Company's
> business, results of operations, financial condition and stock price. For example, the
> Company earns revenue from licensing arrangements with Google LLC ("Google") and other
> companies to offer their search services on the Company's platforms and applications, and
> certain of these arrangements are currently subject to government investigations and legal
> proceedings. On August 5, 2024, Google was found to have violated U.S. antitrust laws. In
> connection with this finding, on September 2, 2025, the U.S. District Court for the District
> of Columbia ("D.C. District Court") ordered certain remedies. The court's order is subject to
> further proceedings before the D.C. District Court, which may result in changes to the
> interpretation or application of the remedies ordered by the court, as well as new or changed
> remedies being ordered. The court's order is also subject to appeal by both the U.S.
> Department of Justice ("DOJ") and Google. A reversal of the order on appeal could result in
> imposition of certain remedies initially proposed by the DOJ, such as those prohibiting
> Google from offering the Company commercial terms for search distribution. If implemented,
> these remedies could materially adversely affect the Company's ability to earn revenue from
> such licensing arrangements."

*Source: FY2025 10-K, accession 0000320193-25-000079, Item 1A. This is the ONLY passage in the
entire 10-K that names Google.*

### 1.2 VERBATIM — Q3 FY2026 10-Q, same risk factor, ONE MATERIAL WORD CHANGED

The Q3 FY2026 10-Q repeats the paragraph nearly verbatim with one substantive update:

> "The court's order **was appealed by both the DOJ and Google.**"

(FY2025 10-K read: "The court's order **is also subject to appeal by** both the U.S. Department
of Justice ("DOJ") and Google.")

*Source: Q3 FY2026 10-Q, accession 0000320193-26-000020.*

**Reading:** as of the Q3 FY2026 filing the appeals are **filed, not merely available**. The
matter is live and unresolved. Apple's own framing is that the *downside* case is a reversal on
appeal reinstating the DOJ's original proposal — i.e. Apple treats the September 2, 2025 Mehta
remedy as the **favourable** outcome relative to what the DOJ asked for.

### 1.3 WHAT APPLE SAYS THE HARM WOULD BE

Apple names the mechanism precisely: remedies "**prohibiting Google from offering the Company
commercial terms for search distribution**." That is the payment itself. Apple concedes that if
implemented this "could **materially adversely affect** the Company's ability to earn revenue
from such licensing arrangements."

This is Apple, in its own filed risk factor, conceding that **a court, not Apple, controls
whether this revenue line continues to exist.**

| "no single customer" | 0 | 0 | **recorded absence** |

**Two absences carry the weight:**

1. **Apple never discloses the dollar size of the Google payment.** Not in the 10-K, not in the
   10-Q, not in MD&A, not in the revenue note, not in the segment note. It is not broken out
   anywhere in the filed financial statements.
2. **Apple never discloses Google as a revenue concentration.** There is no ">10% of revenue"
   customer disclosure anywhere in the filing.

### 2.1 The concentration disclosure Apple DOES make — and why it is not the Google payment

FY2025 10-K, Note 3 (Financial Instruments), VERBATIM:

> "As of September 27, 2025, the Company had one customer that represented 10% or more of total
> trade receivables, which accounted for 12%. The Company's third-party cellular network carriers
> accounted for 34% and 38% of total trade receivables as of September 27, 2025 and September 28,
> 2024, respectively. The Company requires third-party credit support or collateral from certain
> customers to limit credit risk."

And on the vendor side, VERBATIM:

> "As of September 27, 2025, the Company had two vendors that individually represented 10% or more
> of total vendor non-trade receivables, which accounted for 46% and 23%. As of September 28, 2024,
> the Company had two vendors that individually represented 10% or more of total vendor non-trade
> receivables, which accounted for 44% and 23%."

**Critical distinction for the run:** these are **RECEIVABLES** concentrations (a credit-risk
disclosure under ASC 825/275), **not REVENUE** concentrations. The unnamed "one customer" at 12%
of trade receivables is almost certainly a distribution/carrier counterparty, not Google — a
search-placement payment would not sit in trade receivables at that scale. **Apple makes no
revenue-concentration disclosure at all.** No customer is named at >10% of net sales.

## 3. THE ~$20BN FIGURE — WHERE IT ACTUALLY COMES FROM

**PLAIN STATEMENT: the ~$20bn figure is NOT filing-sourced. It appears in NEITHER Apple's nor
Alphabet's SEC filings. It is a COURT-RECORD figure.**

Rung of evidence, stated explicitly:

| Rung | Does the number appear? |
|---|---|
| Apple SEC filings (10-K, 10-Q) | **NO** — zero occurrences of any dollar figure for the arrangement |
| Alphabet SEC filings (10-K) | **NO** — Alphabet discloses TAC in aggregate only, never the Apple split (see `services_franchise.md` §Alphabet cross-check) |
| *US v. Google* court record (D.D.C., Judge Amit Mehta) | **YES** — the liability opinion of 2024-08-05 and the trial record contain the payment figures |
| Press / analyst estimates | YES, but derivative of the court record |

The figure entered the public record through the **findings of fact in the liability opinion in
*United States v. Google LLC*, No. 1:20-cv-03010 (D.D.C.), Judge Amit P. Mehta**, and through
trial exhibits and testimony in that case. That is a **primary court document**, and it is a
legitimate source — but it must be labelled as **a court document, not a company filing**, and it
is **historical** (trial record covers roughly 2021-2022 payments, with ~$20bn commonly cited for
2022).

**For the framework run this matters:** any owner-earnings or Q5 work that leans on "$20bn from
Google" is leaning on a court exhibit, not on an audited filed statement. Under operator rule 5
(STRICT INPUTS) and rule 4 (THE FILING GETS READ), that number cannot be treated as filed
financial data. It should be carried as a **court-record estimate, disclosed as such**, or the
analysis should be run on the range implied by Services revenue instead.

## 4. WHY THIS IS A Q2 CRITERION-3 PROBLEM

**[E3-03] criterion 3: "is not subject to price regulation."**

The evidence above establishes, from Apple's own filed words:

- The revenue exists by **contract with a single named counterparty** (Google LLC).
- That contract is **presently before a federal court** which has already ordered remedies
  against the counterparty (2025-09-02) and which retains jurisdiction ("subject to further
  proceedings before the D.C. District Court").
- Apple concedes remedies could **prohibit the counterparty from offering Apple commercial
  terms at all** — not merely regulate the price, but **forbid the transaction**.
- Both sides have appealed, so the terms are **not settled** as of Q3 FY2026.

**Apple does not set this price. A court does, or may.** On the plainest reading of criterion 3,
the most profitable identifiable line inside Services is not merely subject to price regulation —
it is subject to possible prohibition. This is a criterion-3 failure on the company's own
disclosure, and Apple states the effect would be "material."

**The mitigating read that must also be recorded:** the September 2, 2025 order did NOT ban the
payments. Apple's risk factor describes the *ban* as the DOJ's originally-proposed remedy that
could return only "a reversal of the order on appeal." So as filed, the payments continue — the
threat is contingent, not realised. The run should record this as a **live, court-controlled,
unquantified dependency**, not as a completed loss.

**[E4-04] — the moat must not need continuous rebuilding.** Note that this arrangement is
periodically renegotiated between the parties and now additionally supervised by a court. A moat
whose most profitable plank is a renewable bilateral contract with one counterparty, under active
antitrust supervision, is the definition of a plank that needs rebuilding.

## 5. OPEN ITEM FOR THE RUN

Apple gives the analyst **no way to size this from the filings.** The run cannot compute what
fraction of Services gross profit is at risk, because Apple does not disclose it and Alphabet does
not disclose it either. Under the four-verdict test:

- "Can I name the document that would resolve this?" — **Partly.** The *US v. Google* court record
  names historical amounts. It does not give current-year amounts, and neither company will file
  them.
- Therefore the precise exposure is **UNRESEARCHED as to history (go get the court opinion) and
  UNKNOWABLE as to the current and forward amount.** Either verdict closes the file at Q2; neither
  is a pass.
