Verbatim (FY2021 10-K): "Comparable restaurant sales include the benefit of menu price increases of
approximately 1.75% and 4.2% implemented in April 2021 and October 2021, respectively, as well as an
increase of 1.0% in October 2020."
Verbatim (FY2025 10-K): "Per person average check for 2025 includes the benefit of menu price
increases of approximately 1.4% and 1.7% implemented in Q2 2025 and Q4 2025, respectively. We
implemented menu price increases of approximately 2.2% and 0.9% in Q2 2024 and Q4 2024, respectively.
In addition, we plan to implement a menu price increase of approximately 1.9% in Q2 2026."

**The answer: guest counts did not merely hold, they grew every year while prices rose.**

Computed (compounding the filed annual figures; label: arithmetic on filed numbers):
- Cumulative FY2022-FY2025 guest traffic: 1.019 x 1.054 x 1.044 x 1.028 - 1 = **+15.3%**
- Cumulative FY2022-FY2025 per person average check: 1.078 x 1.047 x 1.041 x 1.021 - 1 = **+20.0%**
- Cumulative FY2022-FY2025 comp sales: 1.097 x 1.101 x 1.085 x 1.049 - 1 = **+37.5%**
- Cumulative menu pricing actions taken 2021-2025 (all ten actions compounded): **+25.7%**
- Cumulative menu pricing actions taken 2022-2025 (eight actions): **+18.5%**

Note: within each year the filed split is additive (traffic + check = comps), so the compounded
traffic and check series are each valid alone but their product slightly exceeds compounded comps.
Note also that average check rose LESS than pricing taken in most years (2023: check +4.7% vs ~4.9%
carried pricing; 2024: +4.1%; 2025: +2.1% vs ~3.1% in-year actions): filings attribute the gap to
mix. Old-regime verbatim (FY2017 10-K): "In all periods presented, average guest check did not
increase in line with the menu price increases implemented as guests shifted to lower menu price
items and/or purchased fewer beverages."

Contrast for the DRI row: TXRH priced ~25.7% cumulatively over 2021-2025 against CPI-era cost
inflation and still compounded traffic at +15.3% over FY2022-FY2025 (and +27.6% in FY2021 on
reopening). This is the value-position peer the DRI run must beat or explain.

- Adjusted current liabilities = 506,318 - 50,000 - 19,271 = 437,047

## 6. COMPETITION AND PRICING-PHILOSOPHY LANGUAGE (verbatim, FY2025 10-K unless noted)

Item 1, the pricing philosophy (operating strategy bullet "Offering everyday value"):
> "Our everyday value includes attractive price points, generous portions, and heaping sides. When we
> evaluate menu pricing, we focus on remaining disciplined as we balance short-term pressures with
> long-term growth while always keeping our guest top of mind. Prices are reviewed individually in
> each local market and are offered at moderate price points without sacrificing food quality. Within
> each menu category, we offer a choice of several price points with the goal of fulfilling each
> guest's budget and value expectations. Based on the results of our pricing evaluations, we will
> continue to take pricing actions we feel are needed while striving to maintain our value
> proposition."

MD&A restatement of the same discipline: "This includes a pricing strategy that balances the impacts
of inflationary pressures with our long-term value positioning."

Item 1, Competition:
> "Competition in the restaurant industry is intense. We compete with well-established food service
> companies on the basis of taste, quality, price and value of the food offered, service, atmosphere,
> location, take-out and delivery options, as well as the overall dining experience. Our competitors
> include a large and diverse group of restaurant chains and individual restaurants that range from
> independent local operators that have opened restaurants in various markets to well-capitalized
> national restaurant chains. We also face competition from meal kit delivery services as well as the
> supermarket industry."

Item 1A (risk factor version adds): "Our competitors may generate or more effectively implement
business strategies that improve the value and the relevance of their brands and reputation,
relative to ours."

Older price-point articulation with hard numbers (FY2020 10-K, Item 1 "Offering attractive price

> "We offer our food and beverages at moderate price points that we believe are as low as or lower

> for our 23-ounce Porterhouse T-Bone. The per guest average check for the Texas Roadhouse

## SUMMARY LINE FOR THE COMPETITOR ROW
Eleven of twelve years FY2014-FY2025 show POSITIVE guest traffic as filed (the twelfth, FY2020, is
undisclosed by the company's own statement); cumulative FY2022-FY2025 traffic +15.3% while check rose
+20.0% and cumulative menu pricing 2021-2025 was ~+25.7%. FY2025: 714 company / 102 franchise units,
TR AUV $8,687 thousand, restaurant margin 15.5%, zero debt, return on NTOA 34.0% (21.1% ROU-in,
28.6% incl. goodwill). Dividend suspended March 2020 after one $0.36 quarter, reinstated April 2021,
raised every year since. This is the traffic-first value operator against which DRI's check-led comp
growth must be judged.
