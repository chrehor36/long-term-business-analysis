## WHAT THE SOURCE SAYS

[E3-43] cites `Shareholder Letters/1991 Letter.txt`. The passage runs from line 262 to line 300, and its
subject is stated in its first sentence: *"The fact is that newspaper, television, and magazine properties
have begun to resemble businesses more than franchises in their economic behavior."* The sentence the runs
quote, at line 289, is a **general definition** inside that discussion: *"And a business, unlike a franchise,
can be killed by poor management."* **No bank appears anywhere in the passage.** The row is the definitional
test a bank is then measured against; it is not a classification of banks. Read against the corpus search of
2026-09-20 (read-only; its passages are being entered in the ledger the same day), the conclusion the three
runs reached is well supported, but by different text:

- the 2007 annual meeting, line 193, where Buffett names the product: banks are *"dealing in what is
  basically a commodity — money"*, and adds that the industry's high returns *"would be self-neutralizing"*;
- the 1982 letter, lines 470–478, which puts financial institutions inside the commodity-and-administered-prices
  frame by name (*"deposit costs for financial institutions"* among government-administered costs), in the same
  letter the framework already cites for [E2-58]'s low-cost exception;
- the 1990 letter's *"leverage of 20:1 magnifies the effects of managerial strengths and weaknesses"*
  **[E3-29]**, the 2008 meeting's *"that's hard to do for 99 percent of the banks"* (line 899), and the 2011
  meeting's *"Banking, if you just keep out of trouble on the asset side, is a very good business because you get
  your money so cheap and ... because of the implicit federal guarantee"* (line 307).

**So the correct statement is: a bank is on the "business" side of the 1991 dichotomy because its product is
a commodity and its returns are management-made and regulator-set, and the only door the corpus opens for it
is [E2-58]'s wide-and-sustainable cost advantage — in a bank's own terms, *"very cheap money on the deposit
side"* (2002 meeting, line 281) or the operating efficiency of the Bank of Granite (1996 meeting, lines
1337–1343).** That is what CCB, ACNB, SOFI, JPM and TFC actually tested, and their Q2 verdicts stand. Only
the sourcing sentence is wrong.
