### 1.3 The large performance-based option grant (PSOs) — size, date, price, conditions

**Grant date 7/20/2017 (fiscal 2018). Exercise price $51.13. Expiration 7/20/2025.
17,500,000 options, in seven equal tranches of 2,500,000 each. Identical grant to Safra Catz.**
(Outstanding Equity Awards at Fiscal 2025 Year-End Table, p. 60; tranche size stated repeatedly in
the CD&A and PVP footnotes.)

> "In fiscal 2018, the Compensation Committee granted each of Mr. Ellison and Ms. Catz an equity award
> consisting entirely of PSOs that could be earned in seven equal tranches, with one tranche vesting
> only upon the attainment of a stock price goal and each of the remaining six tranches vesting only
> upon the attainment of one of six market capitalization and operational performance goals, with
> achievement of both a market capitalization and matching operational performance goal required for a
> tranche to vest. Performance measured against the goals was evaluated annually. The eight-year
> performance period for the PSOs concluded at the end of fiscal 2025, with five of the seven tranches
> earned and vested."
> (2025 proxy, "Long-Term Incentive Compensation for Mr. Ellison and Ms. Catz", p. 51)

The extension:

> "In fiscal 2022, the Compensation Committee extended the end of the PSO performance period by three
> fiscal years from May 31, 2022 to May 31, 2025. After reviewing internal projections and forecasts,
> the Compensation Committee determined that the remaining operational performance goals were
> appropriately challenging. Furthermore, when designing the PSO program, the Compensation Committee
> required the matching of operational performance goals with sustained 30-day market capitalization
> goals because it wanted to ensure that our stockholders would also benefit from the achievement of
> these goals."

**Flag — an internal inconsistency in the filed document.** The CD&A on p. 40 states: "Oracle's stock
was up 130% from the end of fiscal 2022 to the end of fiscal 2025 / The S&P 500 was up 43% for the same
three-year period." The PVP table's own company TSR column runs 37.95 (FY2022) to 231.50 (FY2025), a
six-fold move, i.e. roughly +510% on the same span. The two disclosures in the same document cannot both
be describing the same three years. Separately, the proxy's own stated closing prices ($105.94 on
5/31/2023, $117.19 on 5/31/2024, $165.53 on 5/30/2025) imply a FY2023-to-FY2025 price ratio of 1.56,
while the PVP column implies 231.50/106.80 = 2.17. This arithmetic is mine, on figures taken verbatim
from the filing; I do not know the cause and the proxy offers no reconciliation. Anyone using these TSR
figures should verify them against the Item 5 Stock Performance Graph in the FY2025 Form 10-K before
relying on them.

**Why CAP swings so hard** (footnote 2 reconciliation, p. 66-67): CAP to the PEO is almost entirely the
change in fair value of the 2017 PSOs. For FY2025 the entire $460.7m of adjustment is
"Change in Fair Value of Outstanding Unvested Awards from Prior Years" of **$404,300,134** plus
"Change in Fair Value of Awards from Prior Years that Vested in the Covered Year" of **$56,392,122**;
FY2025 grants were zero. Footnote (a): "This amount includes the change in the fair value of 2,500,000
PSOs (representing the fourth tranche of PSOs) and 2,500,000 PSOs (representing the fifth tranche of
PSOs) which were earned in fiscal 2025 and vested in fiscal 2026 on June 24, 2025."

The same driver runs the non-PEO average, because Ellison is voluntarily included there: footnote (a)
to the non-PEO table, "This calculation includes the change in the fair value of 2,500,000 PSOs
(representing the fourth tranche of PSOs) and 2,500,000 PSOs (representing the fifth tranche of PSOs)
held by Mr. Ellison which were earned in fiscal 2025..."

**Most important financial measures** (Item 402(v)(6) tabular list, p. 69), verbatim:

### 8.2 The policy — Ellison is the sole carve-out

The named-person carve-out also appears twice in the highlights pages: "Anti-pledging policy applicable

> "The pledged shares secure personal term loans only used to fund outside personal business ventures."
> "None of Mr. Ellison's shares are pledged as collateral for margin accounts."
> "The pledged shares are not used to shift or hedge any economic risk in owning Oracle common stock."
> "Mr. Ellison is our Founder and largest stockholder. Mr. Ellison's stock ownership is more than 4,600

> "The Board believes that Mr. Ellison has the financial capacity to repay his personal term loans

Stockholders raised it again this year: the "What We Heard / The Board's Response" table on p. 6 lists

**NOT DISCLOSED:** the identity of the lenders, the loan principal, the loan-to-value or maintenance

## 10. WHAT AN OUTSIDE ANALYST SHOULD NOTICE

1. **The controlling shareholder writes his own equity story and it is currently blank.** Ellison holds
   40.6% and, after the PSOs expired 7/20/2025, has **no outstanding Oracle equity award at all**. The
   fiscal 2026 program for him is undisclosed: "The Compensation Committee continues to refine and
   discuss the elements of the fiscal 2026 equity program for the remaining NEOs" (p. 43). Whatever
   replaces the 2017 PSO will be set by a committee whose members he effectively elects, and it is not
   in this filing.

2. **The $1 salary is over.** "Mr. Ellison's base salary was set at $1 but it was subsequently increased
   to $950,000 starting in fiscal 2026" (p. 51). The symbolically-aligned salary that had held since
   fiscal 2011 ends without any stated rationale in the document.

3. **He is not an NEO.** Every FY2025 compensation table carries the footnote "Although Mr. Ellison is
   not an NEO for fiscal 2025 under applicable SEC rules, we have included his compensation in the
   presentation of the compensation tables as voluntary disclosure." A $1 salary and a zero bonus put
   the largest shareholder and de facto technology leader outside mandatory disclosure. The disclosure
   is a courtesy that can be withdrawn.

4. **The PSO scorecard is softer than it reads.** All six market-cap goals were cleared by FY2024, which
   means the binding constraint was only the operational leg. The two goals that failed are the two
   qualitative or hardest ones: "Become the largest enterprise SaaS company" and "Attain non-GAAP SaaS
   gross margin of 80%". Five of seven tranches (12.5m options at $51.13, against a $165.53 fiscal
   year-end price, about $1.43bn of intrinsic value each for Ellison and Catz) were earned. The
   performance period was **extended by three years mid-flight** in fiscal 2022, at a reported accounting
   cost of $129,275,000 for each of Ellison and Catz. A goalpost that moves is not a hurdle.

5. **Every operational goal is non-GAAP and self-defined.** The Item 402(v)(6) "most important measures"
   list is five non-GAAP metrics. The bonus metric is non-GAAP operating income. GAAP net income appears
   only where the SEC compels it. Non-GAAP operating income "reflects adjustments based on stock-based
   compensation expenses" — i.e. the metric that pays the executives is computed after adding back the
   cost of paying employees in stock.

6. **The bonus was earned and voluntarily surrendered.** "Prior to the reduction ... would have earned
   annual cash bonuses at 104% of the target amount", then reduced to $0 "in order to preserve capital".
   Read charitably this is discipline. Read sceptically, it converts a formula plan into a discretionary
   one, and the beneficiaries of the "preserved capital" (AI capex) are the same people whose equity is
   levered to the resulting stock price. The saving is trivial against the equity at stake: roughly
   $12.2m of forgone bonuses across the whole NEO group (5,207,393 + 5,207,393 + 520,739 + 750,000 +
   500,000 = $12,185,525, my arithmetic on the filed figures) against about $1.43bn of intrinsic value
   in Ellison's five earned PSO tranches alone (12,500,000 x ($165.53 - $51.13)).

7. **Pledging is the single largest governance exception and it grew.** 346,000,000 shares, 12.1% of the
   company, pledged by one man, up 69 million shares year over year, under a policy that names him as
   its sole carve-out. Loan terms, lenders and LTVs are not filed. This is the classic forced-sale
   channel: an equity holder whose personal balance sheet, not the company's, determines whether a block
   equal to an eighth of the shares outstanding stays put.

8. **Related-party dealing is pervasive, small in dollars, and structurally awkward.** Sales to Ellison
   entities in FY2025 were "approximately $10.8 million" and purchases from Ellison entities
   "approximately $10.5 million" (pp. 70-71). The items include a **three-year SailGP sponsorship** with
   Ellison's own F50 League LLC "valued at approximately $10 million per year" (Oracle paid $7.5m cash
   in FY2025 and SailGP took $2.5m in Oracle products), **$2.5 million to Glass Aviation**, an aircraft
   manager bought by **David Ellison, Mr. Ellison's son**, in 2024, and **approximately $500,000 to Wing
   and a Prayer, Inc., "a company owned by Mr. Ellison"**, for aircraft leases. Skydance Animation and
   Skydance Media, in which "Each of Mr. Ellison and his son, David Ellison, has a direct material
   interest", bought $2,479,332 of Oracle cloud between them. Oracle's own mitigant is that Ellison
   signed a **price protection agreement** under which "if we present Mr. Ellison with reasonable
   evidence of a lower price or rate for the same goods or services offered by the related company,
   which would have been available to us at the time we entered into the applicable transaction, then
   Mr. Ellison will reimburse us for the difference" (p. 70). The burden is on Oracle to find the better
   price and ask.

9. **Family on payroll.** "Steven Janicki, Vice President, Business Relationship Management, is
   Mr. Ellison's half-brother. In fiscal 2025, Mr. Janicki received a base salary of $301,962"; and
   "Robert Screven, Senior Applications Engineer, is Mr. Screven's son" at $143,179 (p. 72).

10. **Ampere.** Oracle held "approximately 29%" of Ampere Computing at 5/31/2025, carrying value
    $1.6 billion, chaired by "Renée J. James, a former Oracle director who left the Board in November
    2024", with put and call options "at prices of approximately $500 million to $1.5 billion" running to
    January 2027 and a pending SoftBank acquisition (pp. 71-72). Disclosed under related persons.

11. **The PVP TSR column does not reconcile to the CD&A or to the proxy's own share prices.** See §4.
    Do not use it without checking the 10-K performance graph.

12. **Say-on-pay support is low and the disclosure is minimal.** 67% (2022), 73% (2023), 78% (2024), all
    "of the votes cast", with Ellison's ~41% inside the numerator and no vote counts filed. The Board's
    characterisation, "ongoing year-over-year improvements", is accurate and also describes a company
    that has spent three years climbing back toward normal.

13. **No employment agreements, no severance, and no change-in-control acceleration of performance pay.**
    "Each of our current NEOs, other than Ms. Smith, is employed 'at will.' ... None of our NEOs has an
    employment agreement with Oracle that provides for payments or benefits in the event of a termination
    of employment or in connection with a change in control" (p. 55). This is genuinely shareholder
    friendly and should be scored as such. The change-in-control table nevertheless shows
    **$1,144,000,000** of intrinsic value for each of Ellison and Catz as of 5/30/2025, and
    **$572,000,000** each on death (pp. 63-64).

14. **Ellison recommends the pay of the people who work for him.** "In its assessment, the Compensation
    Committee considered the recommendations of Ms. Catz with respect to Mr. Levey and Ms. Smith, and
    Ms. Catz and Mr. Ellison with respect to Messrs. Henley and Screven" (p. 48). The stated safeguard is
    "None of our NEOs determines his or her own compensation."

15. **Board tenure and independence.** Stockholders raised that "Several independent directors are
    long-tenured and there is concern that there may be a risk of entrenchment and overreliance on
    tenured directors" (p. 7). The Compensation Committee that set all of the above is Conrades (Chair),
    Seligman (Vice Chair), Moorman and Panetta (Compensation Committee Report, p. 57). Board of 13
    nominees includes five executives: Ellison, Catz, Henley, Magouyrk and Sicilia.

16. **Peer group is aspirational.** Accenture, Adobe, Alphabet, Amazon, Apple, Cisco, HPE, Intel, IBM,
    Meta, Microsoft, Qualcomm, Salesforce, SAP (p. 56). Oracle benchmarks against the largest technology
    companies on earth, which raises every reference point. The proxy does note "the Compensation
    Committee did not use such information to tie any executive's individual compensation to specific
    target percentiles."

17. **Compensia is the independent consultant** and "did not determine or recommend any amounts or levels
    of our executive compensation for fiscal 2025" (p. 55).

18. **Dilution.** "our cumulative potential dilution since June 1, 2022 has been an annualized rate of
    1.7% per year" (p. 51). Equity plan overhang at 5/31/2025: 148 million shares issuable (about 31
    million from options at a $53.06 weighted-average strike and 1.04 years' weighted-average remaining
    life, the rest RSUs), 426 million available for future issuance, with full-value awards counting
    2.5:1 against the pool (pp. 64-65).

19. **Delinquent Section 16 filings:** one, minor. "a Form 4 reporting the acquisition of 1,682 shares of
    our common stock by Dr. Boskin was filed late on March 21, 2025" (p. 37).
