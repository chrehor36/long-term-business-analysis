**The hypothesis I formed while reading, stated so it can be attacked [E4-26]:** *Williams-Sonoma sells its own designs under its own names, which is closer to a brand than to a reseller, but a customer furnishing a room has close substitutes and the registrant says so; for twenty-three years (fiscal 1997-2019) the same brands earned ordinary retail margins, in line with the rest of the home-furnishings row and down to 1.2% before tax in fiscal 2008; the doubling since fiscal 2020 came with the pandemic wave and is explained in the filer's own MD&A by execution (fewer promotions, supply-chain efficiencies, freight costs falling back), not by a position the customer cannot route around.* The evidence against it is hunted and answered before the verdict, and it is strong.

**[E3-03], criterion by criterion.** *"An economic franchise arises from a product or service that: (1) is needed or desired; (2) is thought by its customers to have no close substitute and; (3) is not subject to price regulation."* The same 1991 passage: *"In contrast, "a business" earns exceptional profits only if it is the low-cost operator or if supply of its product or service is tight."* **[E3-43]**

- **(1) Needed or desired [x]: passes.** $7,806.8M of fiscal 2025 revenue for furniture, kitchenware, bedding and décor.
- **(3) Not subject to price regulation [x]: passes.** No filing read places its prices under a regulator; tariffs are a cost, not a price control.
- **(2) No close substitute [ ]: FAILS, in the registrant's own words, and in its own record across a full cycle.**
  - *The registrant names the substitutes.* Item 1A, fiscal 2025: *"We compete with other retailers that market lines of merchandise similar to ours. We compete with national, regional and local businesses that utilize a similar retail store strategy, as well as traditional furniture stores, department stores, direct-to-consumer businesses, and specialty stores. The continued sales growth in the e-commerce industry has encouraged the entry of many new competitors, including discount retailers selling similar products at reduced prices and new business models, as well as an increase in competition from established companies, many of whom are willing to spend significant funds and/or reduce pricing to gain market share."* Item 1: *"discount retailers selling undifferentiated products at reduced prices"*.
  - *It names price and promotion as the ground of competition.* The competitive challenges it lists include *"maintaining favorable brand recognition and achieving customer perception of value"*, *"marketing and competitively pricing our products to consumers"* and *"managing against increasingly competitive promotional activity"*. Among the factors that move comparable revenue: *"competition (including competitive promotional activity and discount retailers)"*.
  - *It says the product must be re-won each season.* Item 1A: *"in the specialty home products business, style and color trends are constantly evolving. As a result, consumer preferences cannot be predicted with certainty and may change between selling seasons."* Misjudging means that *"we may be required to mark down certain products to sell any excess inventory or to sell such inventory through our outlets or other liquidation channels at prices which are significantly lower than our retail prices"*. The fiscal 2025 MD&A credits growth to *"newness, collaborations, exclusive products"* and plans *"increased levels of newness, including new furniture collections and finishes"* and to *"create brand heat through collaborations, social and influencer partnerships"*. This is the language the ANF run of 2026-09-02 read as a moat *"that must be re-earned every season"* [E4-04]; the unit that persists is the name, and the name's pull is re-bought each season with design, collaborations and advertising ($552.6M in fiscal 2025, 7.1% of revenue; $293.6M, 9.5%, in fiscal 2010).
  - *The name did not hold the price when demand fell.* Fiscal 2008: revenue fell 14.8%, comparable store sales fell 17.2% (fiscal 2008 10-K `0001193125-09-071708`), and earnings before tax fell from $316.3M to $42.0M (1.2% of revenue, after $34.0M of impairment charges), with gross margin down from 38.9% to 33.8%; the fiscal 2009 10-K (`0001193125-10-075151`) gives the cause: *"the deleverage of fixed occupancy expenses primarily due to declining sales, an increase in cost of merchandise (including the impact of greater markdowns) and an increase in inventory related reserves of approximately $11,172,000"*. Fiscal 2009 operating margin 3.9%. In fiscal 2000-2001 the operating margin was 5.4-6.1%.

- **What the row shows.** Before 2020 Williams-Sonoma sat inside the row (9.3% against Bed Bath & Beyond's 9.1%, Ethan Allen's 6.8%, RH's 6.4%), below Bed Bath & Beyond in 2010-2012. In 2021-2022 the whole row stepped up together (RH 24.7% in 2021, Ethan Allen 16.9% and Arhaus 15.0% in 2022): **the step was the industry's, which is a wave [E3-51], not one company's position.** In 2023-2025 the others gave most of it back and Williams-Sonoma did not: 17.6% against RH 11.2%, Ethan Allen 13.5%, Arhaus 8.6%. And three home-goods specialty retailers that once earned double-digit margins, above or level with Williams-Sonoma's, are now bankrupt or loss-making: [E3-74] in the row itself, *"you are under attack all of the time ... you cannot coast in retailing."*

**The remaining Q2 tests.**
- **[E2-44], the two characteristics.** *First, pricing:* the filer's own record answers both ways, so both are stated. Against: fiscal 2008's markdowns; the Item 1A that lists *"competitively pricing our products"* and *"competitive promotional activity"*; tariffs absorbed rather than passed on (fiscal 2025 *"lower merchandise margins of 40 basis points as a result of the flow-through of tariffs"*; Q2 fiscal 2026 *"lower merchandise margins of -230bps primarily driven by tariff costs"*, release of 2026-08-26). For: fiscal 2023-2024, when revenue fell 10.6% and then 0.5% and the MD&A says the decline was driven partly by *"our strategy to reduce promotional activity"*, gross margin rose from 42.4% to 46.5%. **That is price held by giving up volume and share** (revenue 2022 to 2025: Williams-Sonoma −10.0%, RH −4.2%, Arhaus +12.2%, Wayfair +2.0%), not [E2-44]'s ability to raise prices *"rather easily ... without fear of significant loss of either market share or unit volume"*. *Second, capital:* growth has needed little capital beyond depreciation (capex $259.4M against D&A $231.4M in fiscal 2025), which passes; the stores are leased, so the capital that grows with the business is the lease book.
- **[E4-55], the physical series.** No units are disclosed. Real revenue (2025 dollars) was $7.43bn in fiscal 2019, $9.80bn at the fiscal 2021 peak and $7.81bn in fiscal 2025: **+5.1% in six years, −20.3% from the peak**; stores 614 to 506; store leased square feet 6,558,000 (fiscal 2019) to 5,762,000. Comparable brand revenue +6.5% (fiscal 2022), −9.9%, −1.6%, +3.5% (fiscal 2025), +6.2% in Q2 fiscal 2026. The margin has risen while the real business has shrunk from its peak; [E4-55]'s warning is about dollar revenue flattered by price, and here the dollar margin was held by price while volume fell.
- **[E3-33] / [E5-28], untapped pricing power.** The strongest reading of fiscal 2020 is that pricing power had been left unused (fiscal 2020 10-K: *"we were very deliberate in reducing promotions in all of our brands, resulting in product margin expansion"*). [E5-28] prices that claim: *"If you name some business that has incredible pricing power, you're talking about a business that's a monopoly or a near monopoly"*. Nine peers, private giants unmeasured, largest supplier 3% of purchases, the registrant's own list of competitors: not a near-monopoly. Recorded as the strongest reading against the verdict, not as the class.
- **[E4-37], the agony metric.** The agony is in the MD&A: price was held in fiscal 2023-2024 at the cost of a 10.6% revenue fall and share lost to RH, Arhaus and Wayfair. A yawn-price business does not choose between its price and its volume.
- **[E2-45], the attacker's test.** The corpus asked it of a furniture retailer and answered *"I'd rather wrestle grizzlies than compete with Mrs. B and her progeny"*; Nebraska Furniture Mart's defence was its cost and price, [E3-43]'s low-cost door. Asked of Williams-Sonoma, a premium-priced merchant, the answer runs the other way: an attacker with capital and skilled people needs designers, contract factories in Vietnam and India (the same ones: the largest supplier is 3%), a website and an advertising budget. That is how RH, Arhaus, Article and Wayfair entered; Arhaus grew from $495M (2019) to $1,379M (2025) while Williams-Sonoma's revenue fell from its peak. The registrant's own sentence: *"The continued sales growth in the e-commerce industry has encouraged the entry of many new competitors, including those with new business models"*.
- **[E4-36] / [E3-51], which cause of success.** The filer attributes the post-2020 margin to reduced promotions, supply-chain efficiencies (*"reductions in returns and damages, reduced out-of-market and multiple shipments"*), lower ocean freight (*"decreased ocean freight, detention and demurrage costs"*, fiscal 2023) and occupancy leverage as e-commerce rose to about 65% of revenue: execution on several variables [E4-36], plus a freight cycle, plus the pandemic wave the whole row rode [E3-51]. [E4-36]'s ownable example in this trade, *"our furniture and appliance store"*, is extreme minimisation (the low-cost door of [E3-43]); Williams-Sonoma is a premium-priced merchant, not the low-cost operator.
- **[E4-04] and [E4-23].** [E4-04] is applied as a competence limit (ruling of 2026-09-20), and it is not the ground of this verdict: the ground is criterion (2). The registrant's own words (*"newness"*, trends that *"may change between selling seasons"*) are recorded because they show why the substitutes stay close: the pull is re-bought every season. No single key person is named as essential; the 10-K's key-personnel risk is generic.
- **[E3-74] and [E3-38], the corpus on this trade.** *"retailing is a good case of a business where you have to stay smart"*; *"For a retailer, hiring that nephew would be an express ticket to bankruptcy."* The corpus puts retailing on the "a business" side of [E3-43] unless the low-cost door or a dominant position is shown. Neither is shown here.
- **The unit of the test [E4-08].** One reportable segment; Pottery Barn (38% of fiscal 2025 revenue), West Elm (24%), Williams Sonoma (17%) and Pottery Barn Kids and Teen (15%) are sold the same way, in the same channels, against the same substitutes. The kitchen brand (Williams Sonoma, comp +6.9% in fiscal 2025) and the furniture brands (Pottery Barn −6.2% in fiscal 2024) diverge in the cycle, not in kind.

**THE STRONGEST EVIDENCE AGAINST THIS VERDICT, hunted hardest [E4-26, E3-41, E4-51].**
1. **The post-wave separation.** In fiscal 2023-2025, with the housing market weak and comparable revenue negative in two of three years, Williams-Sonoma earned 17.6% while RH earned 11.2%, Ethan Allen 13.5% and Arhaus 8.6%; the row stepped up with it in 2021-2022 and fell back without it.
2. **Price held when volume fell.** Gross margin rose to a record 46.5% in fiscal 2024 while revenue fell; the MD&A says the company chose to cut promotions.
3. **Its own goods.** The products are designed in-house and sold nowhere else; the name is the product, the Kirkland case of [E5-57] and [E4-65] (*"the retailer would like his name to be the brand"*), not a reseller of others' brands.
4. **Returns.** Pre-tax earnings on average equity of 25-40% in most years since 1998 and 65-89% since fiscal 2020, with no funded debt.
5. **[E3-47]**: a wrongly closed file on an understood business is the costliest error, and this business is understood.

**Answered.** (1) Three years of separation after a wave the whole row rode are set against twenty-three years in which the same brands sat inside the row, earned 1.2% before tax in a downturn, and sat below Bed Bath & Beyond's margins in fiscal 2010-2012. The filer's MD&A explains the separation by what it did (fewer promotions, fewer damaged and split shipments, lower freight, more e-commerce), which is execution a competitor can copy or a manager can lose, [E3-43]'s *"a business, unlike a franchise, can be killed by poor management"*, not a position the customer cannot route around. The separation is recorded as the Q6-style reversal condition below, not dismissed. (2) The price was held by accepting a 10.6% fall in revenue and a loss of share to RH, Arhaus and Wayfair: [E2-44] asks for price held without that fear, and [E4-37] names the choice as agony. (3) Own designs make the substitute non-identical, not non-close: the registrant's own Item 1A says competitors *"market lines of merchandise similar to ours"* and *"discount retailers selling similar products at reduced prices"*, and Costco's Kirkland in [E5-57] is a brand that *"travels"* on the back of a low-cost operator, which Williams-Sonoma is not. (4) High returns on a lease-funded, capital-light merchant are what a well-run retailer earns in good years; Bed Bath & Beyond earned 14.7-16.5% operating margins in fiscal 2010-2012 and is gone. The return is [E3-43]'s second half; the first half, *"regularly price ... aggressively"*, is contradicted by fiscal 2008 and by the registrant's list of competitive challenges. (5) I weighed [E3-47]. The file closes on the product and the customer's substitutes, not on the manager or the price: **[E5-42] keeps the judgments apart**, and nothing here says Williams-Sonoma is badly run. The record says the reverse; it is well run, and that is the point of [E3-38]'s retailer.

**Why OUT and not the perimeter close (UNKNOWABLE at Q2).** The 2026-09-20 ruling reserves UNKNOWABLE for *"A name that passes [E3-03] and whose durability cannot be judged from filings"*. Williams-Sonoma does not pass [E3-03]: criterion (2) fails in its own Item 1 and Item 1A, in the downturn record of fiscal 2008-2009, and in a twenty-three-year margin series indistinguishable from a row that includes two bankrupt home retailers. The durability of the post-2020 margin is uncertain, but the question the ruling reserves for UNKNOWABLE (can a franchise's durability be judged?) does not arise when the franchise criterion itself fails. This follows the ANF (2026-09-02) and ULTA (2026-09-02) closes on the same criterion and the same kind of evidence.
