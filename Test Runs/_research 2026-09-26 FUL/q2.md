## Q2 — IS IT A FRANCHISE? **[E3-03]**

*Every FUL figure below is read from the filed 10-Ks for fiscal 2005 to 2025 (Items 1 and 1A, the MD&A variance tables and paragraphs, the income statements and the segment notes), or from SEC XBRL companyfacts checked against the filed statements (Step 0). Extractions in `Test Runs/_research 2026-09-26 FUL/`: `isparse.py` (`is_out.txt`), `pv.py` (`pv_out.txt`, from `pv_tables.txt` and `pv_words.txt`), and the peer script `peers/fulrow.py` (`peers/fulrow_out.txt`).*

**The hypothesis I formed while reading, stated so it can be attacked [E4-26]:** *FUL is a formulator that resells petrochemicals at a spread. Its price follows its raw-material costs up and down with a lag, its gross margin has stayed inside the same six-point band for twenty-one years, and when it has pushed price it has lost volume. It earns a respectable return on the tangible plant it runs but a thin one on the capital its owners have actually put in, because most of that capital is goodwill paid to buy other formulators. If that is right, it is [E3-43]'s "a business", not a franchise.* The evidence against, above all the qualification cost that keeps a customer on a formula, the widening gross margin since 2023, and a peer that earns less, is hunted and answered before the verdict.

**[E3-03], criterion by criterion.** *"An economic franchise arises from a product or service that: (1) is needed or desired; (2) is thought by its customers to have no close substitute and; (3) is not subject to price regulation."*

- **(1) Needed or desired [x]: passes.** Manufacturers cannot make a diaper, a carton, a label or a window without an adhesive; FUL's products *"help improve the performance of our customers’ products or improve their manufacturing processes"*.
- **(3) Not subject to price regulation [x]: passes.** Prices are set by FUL and its competitors; the regulation the filer names (EPA, REACH, medical-device approval) governs what may be sold, not its price.
- **(2) Thought by its customers to have no close substitute [ ]: FAILS in the registrant's own words.**
  - *The technology is not scarce.* Item 1: *"Much of the technology we use in our products and manufacturing processes is available in the public domain."*
  - *Price is a named basis of competition, against larger rivals.* Item 1: *"Our markets are highly competitive"*; *"Principal competitive factors in the sale of adhesives and other specialty chemicals are product performance, supply assurance, technical service, quality, price and customer service."* Item 1A: *"We experience substantial competition in each of the operating segments and geographic areas in which we operate"*; *"Many of our direct competitors are part of large multinational companies and may have more resources than we do. Any increase in competition may result in lost market share or reduced prices, which could result in reduced profit margins."*
  - *Price follows costs, with a lag, by the filer's own account.* Item 1A: *"If the prices of raw materials increase in a short period of time, we may be unable to pass these increases on to our customers in a timely manner and could experience reductions to our profit margins."* And the price of FUL's products is set in part outside FUL: *"supply and demand for certain of our products is driven by end-use markets and worldwide capacities which, in turn, impact demand for and pricing of our products."*
  - What there is of a substitute barrier is the customer's qualification cost, which the filer states from its own side (*"may require seeking re-approval from our customers"*). It is taken up as the first item of the evidence against.

**Checklist:** Needed or desired [x] · no close substitute [ ] **fails** (decided by the demonstration below) · not price-regulated [x]

- **The price rose 84% and the units fell 12% over twenty-one years, and the gross margin ended inside the band it began in.** A price that moved that far without widening the margin is a price that followed costs: raw materials are *"approximately 75 percent of our cost of sales"*. The margin troughs (26.2% in 2008, 25.3% in 2014, 26.2% in 2017, 25.7-25.8% in 2021-2022) are the raw-material spikes the filer names each year; the peaks (30.1% in 2009, 29.1% in 2016, 31.1% in 2025) follow falls in raw-material cost, before the price is given back (2014, 2016, 2020, 2024). **This is pass-through with a lag in both directions, the filer's own "unable to pass these increases on ... in a timely manner" drawn over two decades.**
- **[E2-44]'s first characteristic, tested directly: *"an ability to increase prices rather easily (even when product demand is flat and capacity is not fully utilized) without fear of significant loss of either market share or unit volume"*.** The one period in which FUL priced aggressively by its own description, 2005-2009, cost it about a quarter of its volume, and the filer called it repositioning. In 2023 it held +2.9% of price and lost 8.4% of volume. Fails.
- **[E4-55], units.** FUL publishes the split every year, which the RPM run of 2026-08-31 credited to it. The physical series is the honest one here and it says: organic volume in 2025 about 11.5% below 2004's, after twenty-one years of rising price. Dollar revenue grew from $1,512M to $3,474M, but by purchase and by price.
- **Returns (`peers/fulrow_out.txt`; EBIT = pre-tax income + interest expense; net tangible operating assets as in the GPC run):** EBIT margin **4.4-10.5% in 2009-2025** (10.0% in 2025); pre-tax return on net tangible operating assets **7.6-23.6%** (19.3% in 2025); **pre-tax return on operating capital including the goodwill and intangibles bought, 2.8-16.0%, and 7.3-9.7% in every year since 2018 (2.8% in 2017, the Royal Adhesives year)**; net income on average equity **5.5-16.9%**, and **7.3-8.6% in 2023-2025**. Goodwill and intangibles were 20-28% of assets before 2017 and **46.5-53.6% since** (Royal Adhesives). [E2-01] asks for a high return *"without undue leverage"*: FUL's equity return of 7-9% is earned on $2.0bn of equity carrying $2.05bn of debt.

**The remaining Q2 tests.**
- **[E2-44], the second characteristic** (*"an ability to accommodate large dollar volume increases in business ... with only minor additional investment of capital"*). Fails: dollar revenue rose from $1,512M (2005) to $3,474M (2025) while goodwill and intangibles rose from $0.25bn (2009, the first year in companyfacts) to $2.49bn and purchases of businesses ran to $3.74bn over 2005-2025 (Q4 table); the owners' capital grew with the dollars.
- **[E3-33] / [E5-28], untapped pricing power.** None: the class requires *"a monopoly or a near monopoly"*; FUL describes *"large multinational companies"* with *"more resources than we do"*.
- **[E4-37], the agony metric.** The filer's own 2014 sentence: raw-material cost rose *"as a result of increases in raw materials combined with price reductions"*; and every year's Item 1A: *"unable to pass these increases on to our customers in a timely manner"*.
- **[E2-53], the dominance test.** Not dominant: the filer calls itself *"the world’s largest pureplay adhesives company"* (the deal release), a title that excludes Henkel's larger adhesives unit only by excluding Henkel's other businesses.
- **[E2-45], the attacker's test.** A well-funded attacker would face qualification costs account by account, but the filer says the technology is largely public and the rivals are larger; the record of FUL itself attacking by acquisition (buying formulators rather than winning their customers) points the same way.
- **[E4-32], direction.** Gross margin up since 2022 (25.7% to 31.1%), and the filer's segment EBITDA margins up in Engineering (19.9% to 22.2%); returns including goodwill flat at 7-10% since 2018, equity return down from 11.2% (2022) to 7.9% (2025). The margin is recovering from a raw-material trough, not separating from the band.
- **[E4-36], which cause of success?** None of the four in the record: the gross margin band and the return on owners' capital describe an ordinary formulator.
- **The unit of the test [E4-08].** The company. Engineering Adhesives (30% of revenue, 22.2% segment adjusted EBITDA margin) is the strongest part; the filer reports segment adjusted EBITDA only, before its share of the $86.5M of amortisation of purchased intangibles, and reports no segment assets (only goodwill and intangibles by segment), so the segment cannot be tested on return; it is 30% of the company. AMS is to be a fourth, new segment (*"the transaction will establish a new global business unit"*).
- **[E4-04] and the 2026-09-20 ruling.** The perimeter close is for a name that passes [E3-03] and whose durability cannot be judged. FUL does not pass [E3-03]; the question does not arise.

**THE STRONGEST EVIDENCE AGAINST THIS VERDICT, hunted hardest [E4-26, E3-41, E4-51].**
1. **Qualification is a real switching cost.** An adhesive is a small part of the customer's cost, it is specified into the customer's line and product, and changing it means reformulating, re-testing and *"re-approval from our customers"*; that is the textbook reason specialty adhesives are said to have pricing power.
2. **The margin has widened three years running** (28.7%, 29.8%, 31.1%, the highest in the twenty-one years) with the filer crediting *"higher pricing, lower raw materials cost"*: price held while costs fell is [E2-44]'s sign.
3. **Return on the tangible business is respectable**: 19-23% pre-tax on net tangible operating assets in 2018-2025.
4. **A direct rival earns less**: Bostik's EBITDA margin (13.3%) sits below FUL's adjusted (17.9%).
5. **[E3-47]**: a wrongly closed file on an understood business is the costliest error.

**Answered.** (1) The qualification cost is real, and the record shows what it buys: it slows the loss of a customer, it does not let FUL set the price. Over twenty-one years the price followed the raw materials both ways, the margin stayed in its band, and the one period of aggressive pricing cost a quarter of the units. If qualification carried pricing, 2005-2009 would read differently and the Item 1A sentence about passing on increases would not have stood in every 10-K. (2) The 2023-2025 widening follows the 2021-2022 trough, when about +22% of price over two years still left the margin at 25.7%; it is the lag running the other way, and in 2024 FUL cut price 2.6% as raw materials fell. 31.1% is the top of a band that has held since 2005, not a new level, and three years is not a demonstration. (3) The tangible return is a good operating number, and on that denominator [E2-43] FUL still sits below every SEC filer in the row in 2025 (19.3% against 23.5-40.9%); and the owners' capital is not tangible: since 2017 about half of FUL's assets are goodwill and intangibles paid to buy other formulators, and on that capital the pre-tax return has been 7-10% in every year since 2018, the equity return 7-9% in 2023-2025 with debt equal to equity. [E3-03]'s *"high rates of return on capital"* is not met on the capital the owners have supplied, and [E2-73]'s rule that *"what we pay for a business does not affect the amount of capital its manager has to work with"* is for judging the managers, not for excusing the price of the franchise test. (4) One rival below FUL does not make FUL the price-setter; Henkel and Sika, the larger direct rivals, sit above it, and every SEC-filing adhesive seller sits above it on return. (5) I weighed [E3-47]. The file closes on the registrant's own account of how its prices are set, a twenty-one-year price and volume series the registrant publishes, a margin band that never broke, and a row that places it below the larger rivals; **[E5-42] keeps the judgments apart**, and nothing here says FUL is badly run.

**The analyst's own incentive, stated [operator rule 9, E4-27, E3-41].** This is the sixth Q2 OUT today (AYI, ST, OSK, KEX, GPC before it); the pull toward the pattern is a bias. It was tested by putting the qualification argument, the widening margin, the tangible return and the one rival that earns less at the head of the case against, and by resting the verdict on the filer's own price and volume series rather than on risk-factor wording that most chemical companies share.

**Why OUT and not the perimeter close (UNKNOWABLE at Q2).** The 2026-09-20 ruling reserves UNKNOWABLE for *"A name that passes [E3-03] and whose durability cannot be judged from filings"*. FUL does not pass [E3-03]: criterion (2) fails in its own Items 1 and 1A, and the demonstration is absent in twenty-one years of its own price, volume and margin figures. The evidence is in; it answers the class of the business, not its future.
