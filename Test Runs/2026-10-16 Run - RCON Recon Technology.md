## THE FOUNDATIONS (not a gate)
Two foundations bear. **A share is a business:** would I be content to own this "if the market closed for five years" **[M1997-109]**; the business has not produced cash in any of eleven years, so the owner's return would have to come from the next buyer, which is the focus the foundations rule out. **The market serves, it does not instruct:** the price fell from $60 a share (the PIPE price of April, restated) to $1.05 in six months, and "it just tells us prices" **[M2006-077]**; the fall is not evidence of value, and a market value below the net cash is not either. **Who is paid to tell you:** the company's own outlook passages ("we believe", "we expect", the plastic project's "purchase intentions") are a seller's projection; the rule is "don’t ask the barber whether you need a haircut" **[M2011-083]**. **Contrary evidence, written down as found** **[M1997-127]**: (i) the market value (about $2.31M) is far below the company's stated net cash after the ATM sale (about $9.8M) and its stated equity (¥566.3M, about $83.5M), the kind of figure that tempts a statistical buyer; (ii) FY2026 revenue rose 65.8% to ¥109.9M and gross margin rose to 33.2% on overseas oilfield projects; (iii) the long-term loans are stated to be covered by collateral with a fair value of about ¥800.0M. Each is weighed where it bears.

## THE STANDING RULE
A part-interest bought for cash, with no borrowing and no position size stated, puts the buyer at no risk of ruin from his own conduct: "Never risk permanent loss of capital." **[L2023-005]** binds how it is financed and sized, and a holding in a business that burns cash and issues shares is a case for a small size or none. No breach on the facts of the purchase.

## WHAT IN THE FRAMEWORK WAS WRONG OR UNCLEAR
Five things. (1) **The parts threshold breaks when every part loses money.** Q2's business-of-parts paragraph decides by "more than half of the company's five-year operating profit", but RCON reports only segment gross profit and an operating loss in every year, so no part has a share of a positive total; I used gross profit as the filer's only part-level measure and let the largest part decide. Worse, the loan book (¥330.0M, 52% of assets, the only line that earns anything, net interest ¥10.9M in FY2026) is not one of the filer's segments; read as a "part", it is the largest by assets and cannot be read from outside (borrowers unnamed), which on Q1's holding-company paragraph would close the file TOO HARD at Q1 rather than OUT at Q2. I followed the filer's own division into segments and say so; the framework should say whether a financial asset that the company holds outside its segments is a part.
