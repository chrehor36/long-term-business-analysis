### WHAT THE SHARE BUYS

### Q4: WHY (c) IS THE WHOLE STORY AT THIS FILER, AND TWO FINDINGS FOR THE TOOLING
1. **Content cash spend runs through OPERATING cash flow, not investing.** The *"Net change in
   produced and licensed content costs and advances"* add-back of $577M is the excess of $23,286M of
   content amortisation over $22,709M of cash spend. **So adding content to (c) double-counts
   $22.7bn a year.** And the library is being run down slightly - spend below amortisation in both
   filed years, production and programming assets $36,593M to $33,390M - so **recent operating cash
   flow is flattered by roughly $0.6-1.0bn a year**, which is a reason to distrust the top of the
   range.
2. **THE CASH-FLOW D&A LINE IS NOT THE (c) PROXY EITHER, AND THIS IS A TOOLING FINDING.** The
   $5,326M line is depreciation $3,859M **plus** amortisation of intangibles $1,467M, of which
   **$1,307M is TFCF and Hulu purchase-price amortisation - the amortisation of a 2019 cheque, which
   requires no replacement spending whatever.** Using $5,326M as (c) charges the shareholder for the
   acquisition a second time. **The correct D&A proxy at this filer is DEPRECIATION ALONE.** Any
   construction that reads a `DepreciationDepletionAndAmortization` tag as the (c) default overstates
   (c) by about **$1.5bn a year** for DIS. **This is the same class as the IBM finding (ROU
   amortisation plus acquired intangibles inside the add-backs) and the ELF finding**, and the same
   remedy applies: only a reader can see which class a filer is in.
3. **THE [E5-20] EXCEPTION CLASS WAS ASKED ON THE FILING AND ANSWERED *NO* - a different answer from
   AMZN, EQIX and DLR, and the evidence that settled it came from PEERS, not from reasoning.**

   recorded for EQIX and DLR; depreciation is 4.1% of revenue. And **the two listed theme-park
