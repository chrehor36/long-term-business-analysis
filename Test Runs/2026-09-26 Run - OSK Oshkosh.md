## Q1 — CAN I UNDERSTAND HOW THIS MAKES MONEY? **[E3-31]**
- **Unit economics in my own words, no management language.** Oshkosh builds heavy, specialised machines to order in its own plants (37 manufacturing facilities), mostly from steel, engines, axles and bought-in truck chassis, and sells them in three businesses. **Access (43% of 2025 sales, $4,494M)**: JLG boom lifts, scissor lifts and telehandlers, sold mainly to equipment rental companies that rent them to contractors; the rental companies buy when they replace ageing fleets and when construction grows, and stop buying when it falls (Access sales fell from $3,213M in FY2008 to $1,226M in FY2009, and from $4,080M in FY2019 to $2,515M in FY2020). **Vocational (36%, $3,727M)**: Pierce fire trucks ($1,516M), airport ground equipment, boarding bridges and airport rescue vehicles ($975M, most of it the 2023 AeroTech purchase), McNeilus refuse trucks ($744M) and smaller lines, sold to municipalities, airports, airlines and waste haulers, largely through dealers, from backlogs. **Transport (20%, $2,097M)**: Oshkosh Defense tactical trucks for the U.S. Department of Defense and allies, under contracts won in competitive bids (*"Most of our contracts with the DoD are multi-year firm, fixed-price contracts. These contracts typically contain annual sales price increases"*), and the Next Generation Delivery Vehicle for the Postal Service (*"up to 165,000 vehicles over a 10-year period"*; 51,500 ordered through 2025). The money is the gap between a price set months ahead and the cost of steel, components and labour when the machine is built: consolidated gross income $1,819.0M on $10,422.3M in 2025 (17.5%), segment operating margins of 11.2% (Access), 14.7% (Vocational) and 3.7% (Transport) before $187.4M of corporate costs, plus parts and service on a large installed base.
- **The scarce input this business controls.** **Not a resource; a set of incumbencies of different strength.** In fire apparatus, a national dealer network and custom products that municipal buyers specify (*"nationwide network of independent Pierce dealers"*, in *"a market characterized by a few large manufacturers and numerous small, regional competitors"*). In access equipment, a brand, a distribution and service network and residual values (*"product residual values that are generally higher than competitors’ units"*), in a market with at least five named global rivals. In defense, a qualified design and production line on programmes the government re-competes and whose design rights it increasingly buys (*"The U.S. government has become more aggressive in seeking to acquire the design rights to the Company’s current and potential future programs to facilitate competition for manufacturing our vehicles"*). Nothing is held by licence or site: *"The Company does not believe that any existing patent, trademark, copyright, trade secret or license is of such importance that its loss or termination would have a material adverse effect on the Company’s business taken as a whole."*
- **Will the fundamentals look broadly the same in ten years?** **The way it earns, yes; the mix does not stay put.** The FY2007 10-K describes the same four markets (*"access equipment, defense, fire & emergency and commercial"*), and the 2025 10-K describes them again under three segment names. What moves is the weight: defense was 73% of segment sales in FY2010 (the M-ATV year, $7,162M) and 20% in 2025; the JLTV, the programme that carried Defense from 2015, was lost at re-competition (*"in February 2023, the DoD awarded the JLTV Family of Vehicles follow on contract to another company based on, at least in part, a lower price"*) and its production ended in 2025; the Postal Service contract replaced part of it. Electrification is an engineering change inside each product line, not a replacement of the machine.
- **Relatively simple and stable in character [E3-31]?** The mechanism is simple (build to order, price ahead, recover costs); the earnings are not stable (consolidated operating income −$979.5M in FY2009 after $1,199.8M of impairments, $1,424.8M in FY2010, $364.0M in FY2016, $1,010.7M in 2024), and a fifth of the business depends on the government's procurement decisions. The instability is cyclical and contractual and its drivers are named in the filings (rental fleet replacement, municipal budgets, defense appropriations, steel). Not a business that would take months to understand [E4-46].
- **VERDICT: [x] IN**: the money is made visibly, as a margin between a price set in advance and the cost of building heavy specialised vehicles, in three businesses with named cycles and named customers. **IN is not a finding on the franchise.**

**The hypothesis I formed while reading, stated so it can be attacked [E4-26]:** *Oshkosh is three businesses with three different weaknesses against [E3-03]. Access is an oligopoly of global equipment makers selling to consolidating rental companies, where price follows cost and the cycle, and JLG earns what Genie earns. Transport sells to one buyer that re-competes its programmes, buys the designs to make them competable, and audits the price; that is criterion (2) and in part criterion (3). Vocational holds the best position (Pierce), but its recent pricing looks like the whole fire-apparatus industry repricing in a period of tight supply, not one firm's power, and its record before 2017 is thin. The company as a whole should show [E3-43]'s "business": margins of mid to high single digits, returns that move with the cycle, and prices that recover cost rather than lead it.* The evidence against, above all Pierce and JLG's premium positions and the tangible returns, is hunted and answered before the verdict.

**[E3-03], criterion by criterion.** *"An economic franchise arises from a product or service that: (1) is needed or desired; (2) is thought by its customers to have no close substitute and; (3) is not subject to price regulation. The existence of all three conditions will be demonstrated by a company's ability to regularly price its product or service aggressively and thereby to earn high rates of return on capital."*

- **(1) Needed or desired [x]: passes.** Fire departments, airports, waste haulers, rental fleets and the Army need these machines, and much of the demand is required by rule (airport rescue vehicles *"are required to be in service at most airports worldwide"*).

Costs and prices under such contracts may be subject to adjustment based upon the results of such audits and reviews."* The project's precedents read the same language as criterion (3) failing on the government half (HII, 2026-09-26; ICFI, 2026-09-25).
