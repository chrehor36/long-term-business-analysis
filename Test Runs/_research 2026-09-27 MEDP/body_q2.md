**[E3-03], criterion by criterion.** *"An economic franchise arises from a product or service that: (1) is needed or desired; (2) is thought by its customers to have no close substitute and; (3) is not subject to price regulation."*
- **(1) Needed or desired [x]: passes.** A sponsor cannot reach approval without the trial, and most small sponsors cannot run one themselves (82% of revenue from small biopharmaceutical companies).
- **(3) Not subject to price regulation [x]: passes.** The regulator governs how a trial is run (GCP, ICH, IRBs) and never what it costs; each contract is *"individually priced and negotiated based on the anticipated project scope"*.
- **(2) Thought by its customers to have no close substitute: the contested criterion, and where this file does its work.**
  - **The filer's words, traced through all ten 10-Ks and the prospectus (`pricing_sentences.txt`).** Every year FY2016-FY2025: *"Our major CRO competitors include IQVIA Holdings Inc., ICON plc, PPD, Inc. (now part of Thermo Fisher Scientific Inc.), Fortrea, Inc., and numerous specialty and regional CROs"* (the FY2025 form); *"We generally compete on the basis of a number of factors, including experience within specific therapeutic areas, quality of staff and services, reliability, range of provided services, ability to recruit principal investigators and patients into studies expeditiously, ability to organize and manage large-scale, global clinical trials, global presence with strategically located facilities, speed to completion, price and overall value"*; the market for its work is *"the amount of such R&D expenditures that is outsourced and subject to competitive bidding among CROs"*; *"biopharmaceutical companies continue to seek long-term strategic collaborations with global CROs with favorable pricing terms"*; and *"most of our customers for clinical trial services can terminate our contracts without cause upon 30 days’ notice."* In the prospectus and every 10-K FY2016-FY2019: *"The CRO industry is highly competitive"*; *"Increased competition has led to price and other forms of competition, such as acceptance of less favorable contract terms, which could adversely affect our operating results"*; *"there are few barriers to entry for smaller specialized companies considering entering the industry"*; *"Because of their size and focus, small CROs might compete effectively against larger companies such as us, especially in lower cost geographic areas"*. In the FY2019 10-K alone: *"We operate in a highly competitive environment and experience competitive pricing pressure. To achieve our operating margins over the last three years, we have implemented initiatives to control the rate of growth of our operating expenses. We will continue to utilize these initiatives in the future with a view to offsetting these pricing pressures"*. From FY2020 the risk-factor section was shortened and those four sentences do not reappear; the business they describe is the same, and the one that stayed says: *"Historically, we have been able to generate the operating margins that we do because of our disciplined, full-service operating model. However, we operate in a highly competitive environment, and, if we experience increased levels of competitive pricing pressure, or pricing pressure from the continued rise of inflation, our operating margins may decrease."* **Against these, one sentence every year: *"We believe there are significant barriers to others becoming a global provider offering a broad range of services and products"*; it is a barrier to becoming one of the global full-service firms, of which the same page names four.**
  - **Revealed behaviour for (a): share and margin together.** Service revenue grew 18.0% a year from FY2018 to FY2025 (16.7% from FY2016) while every filing rival grew more slowly (0.8-11.7% a year in the row below), and the margin on service revenue rose from 25.1% (FY2019) to 35.9% (FY2025). **A business buying share with price would show the margin falling; this one shows it rising.** Service revenue per employee rose from about $148K (FY2016) to $241K (FY2025), 5.6% a year; the filer never says how much of that is price and how much is productivity, and its only statement on price in these years is the inflation paragraph: *"Pricing actions and supply chain productivity initiatives have mitigated and are expected to continue to mitigate some inflationary pressures"* (FY2022-FY2025).
  - **Revealed behaviour for (a): the customers pay first, and on better terms every year.** *"The majority of our contracts are structured with an upfront fee that is collected at the time of contract signing"* (prospectus and every 10-K). Advanced billings rose from 14.2% of revenue (FY2015) to 33.8% (FY2025); receivables and unbilled less advances went from **+14 days of revenue (FY2015) to −65 days (FY2025)**. The filing rivals carry advances of 13.0% (IQVIA), 18.8% (ICON), 17.4% (Fortrea), 17.1% (Syneos, FY2022), 22.7% (PPD, FY2020) and 23.0% (PRA, FY2020) of revenue. **But the filer gives the reason itself, every year: *"in many cases we require advance payment from our customers for a portion of the study contract price upon the signing of a service contract which helps to mitigate credit risk."*** A credit term on sponsors who *"may have limited access to capital"* is what a careful lender to weak borrowers imposes; it is not evidence of what the customer thinks of the substitutes. And part of the balance is the sponsor's own money held for the sites: pass-through costs were 41% of FY2025 revenue, and the ASC 606 transition moved *"Pre-funded study costs"* of $57.4M into advanced billings (FY2018 10-K).
  - **Revealed behaviour for (b): the customer leaves.** *"We experience termination, cancellation and non-renewals of contracts by our customers in the ordinary course of business, and the number and dollar value of cancellations can vary significantly from year to year"*; the only cancellation rates ever filed are in the prospectus, *"our average quarterly cancellation rates as a percentage of the beginning of the period backlog were 4.2% and 2.7%"* (H1 2016 and H1 2015) and 3.2-3.7% a quarter in 2013-2015, about an eighth of backlog a year. Most cancellations follow the drug, not the rival (*"adverse patient reactions, lack of efficacy, or inadequate patient enrollment"*), so they show that the backlog is not a contract, not that customers defect. **No retention rate, repeat-customer share or sole-source share has been filed in any 10-K**; the prospectus claimed *"an increasingly significant, and growing, contribution from repeat business"* and gave no figure, and the claim left the 10-Ks after FY2017.

**[E3-43]'s demonstration, *"a company’s ability to regularly price its product or service aggressively and thereby to earn high rates of return on capital"*, and its other half: *"In contrast, “a business” earns exceptional profits only if it is the low-cost operator or if supply of its product or service is tight."***
- **The return half: passes, and beyond any measure.** Operating income of $534.9M on operating capital (goodwill included) that is now negative; 15.4% to 286.3% on the same construction FY2018-FY2024. **[E3-46]**: *"the best businesses, by definition, are going to be businesses that earn very high returns on capital employed over time"*; this one needs almost none.
- **The pricing half: not shown anywhere in the filings read, and the filer describes the opposite route.** No MD&A in FY2016-FY2025 or in any 10-Q read attributes revenue or margin to price; the filer says its margins come *"because of our disciplined, full-service operating model"*, that it has offset *"competitive pricing pressure"* with *"initiatives to control the rate of growth of our operating expenses"* (FY2019), and that price is one of the grounds on which each trial is won. **That is [E3-43]'s own description of "a business": exceptional profits as the low-cost operator, which "With superior management, a company may maintain ... for a much longer time, but even then unceasingly faces the possibility of competitive attack. And a business, unlike a franchise, can be killed by poor management."** The customer's price for the filer's service is set trial by trial in a bid, and the margin is made on the cost side of the bid: bid well, deliver in fewer hours than estimated.
- **[E2-44], the two characteristics.** The first, *"an ability to increase prices rather easily (even when product demand is flat and capacity is not fully utilized) without fear of significant loss of either market share or unit volume"*: **not shown**; the risk factor says the opposite (*"if we experience increased levels of competitive pricing pressure ... our operating margins may decrease"*), and demand has not been flat in the filed record to test it. The second, *"an ability to accommodate large dollar volume increases in business ... with only minor additional investment of capital"*: **met in full**; capital spending was 1.2-3.4% of revenue FY2016-FY2025 and working capital is negative and becoming more so as the business grows.

### THE STRONGEST EVIDENCE AGAINST THIS VERDICT, stated as its holders would state it [E4-51, E4-26, E3-47]
The verdict below is OUT, so the case for IN is set out first, at full strength:
1. **The returns are the demonstration [E3-43, E3-46].** A business that earns $535M on negative capital, with margins rising for six years while growing faster than every rival, most of them several times over, is not a commodity contractor; the corpus asks for high returns on capital and this is the highest the project has recorded.
2. **Customers behave as if there is no close substitute.** They hand Medpace a third of a year's revenue in advance and accept net terms 65 days in its favour when every rival gives them 13-23% advances; a customer with a close substitute would take the better terms elsewhere.
3. **Price is not shown to be given back.** Unlike AYI and CSCO, whose MD&As quantified price given back year after year, Medpace's filings contain no negative price figure; the pricing-pressure sentences are generic risk factors, and the only positive statement (*"Pricing actions ... have mitigated ... inflationary pressures"*) says price kept up with cost inflation.
4. **Switching mid-trial is costly and rare, and the JKHY and CRM precedents passed switching-cost moats as NARROW** without demonstrated pricing power.

**Answered.** (1) is accepted as fact and is why the file is recorded as an excellent business: but [E3-43] itself says returns of this kind are earned two ways, and the filer names its way: *"because of our disciplined, full-service operating model"*, offsetting *"competitive pricing pressure"* by cost *"initiatives"*. That is the low-cost-operator route, which the passage calls "a business" and says *"can be killed by poor management"*; the high return does not decide the class, the route does (the AYI file of 2026-09-26, whose register entry reads "the best operator, not a price-setter"). (2) is weakened by the filer's own reason (*"which helps to mitigate credit risk"*), by the customer mix (82% small sponsors *"which may have limited access to capital"*) and by the pass-through share of the balance; better payment terms from weaker counterparties are a credit fact, and the file cannot show from them what the customer thinks of the substitutes. (3) is correct as far as it goes: the price half is **absent**, not negative; but [E3-43] requires it to be **present** (*"regularly price its product or service aggressively"*), and the filer's statements of what sets its price (bid, *"competitive bidding among CROs"*, *"price and overall value"*, FY2019 *"experience competitive pricing pressure"*) are the evidence that it is not. (4) fails on the unit of the purchase: JKHY's customer re-chooses once every six years at the cost of converting its ledger, and the filings show it staying; Medpace's customer re-chooses at every trial, can leave a running trial on 30 days' notice, and the filings show no retention figure at all. The switching cost here lasts one trial. **[E3-47]** (a wrongly closed file inside the circle is the costliest error) was weighed, and is the reason the reversal conditions below are written in the customer's terms, so that the file reopens on evidence rather than on price. **[E4-23]** is carried as a separate defect: even a reader who grants the franchise must grant that its stated source is one founder's operating model.

**The analyst's own incentive, stated [operator rule 9, E4-27, E3-41].** OUT ends the run and saves three gates of work, which is a pull toward OUT; the record of the returns is the most striking the project has seen, which is a pull toward IN by story; and the AYI and III files of the last two days pull toward OUT by analogy.
