## Q1 — CAN I UNDERSTAND HOW THIS MAKES MONEY? **[E3-31]**
- **Unit economics in my own words, no management language.** A rancher, feedlot, packer, grower or food brand wants to put a claim on an animal or a package that a buyer will pay more for: raised without hormones (the condition for the EU's duty-free high-quality beef quota), source- and age-verified (once the condition for beef exports to Japan), organic, non-GMO, gluten-free, humanely handled, a retailer's or a program's own standard. The claim is only worth the premium if an independent party has checked it. Where Food Comes From sends its own auditors to the farm or plant, or reviews records at a desk, against the standard, and charges **by the audit day** or **a fixed annual certification fee** (*"We recognize revenue utilizing an input method to measure over-time progress of each verification audit based on the number of audit days performed"*; *"For certain of our third-party crop and other processed product audits, we assess a fixed fee for the annual certification period"*). Travel is billed through at cost and booked gross. That is **80.8%** of 2025 revenue ($20.1M). It also resells **cattle ear tags** it buys from manufacturers (*"an ancillary part of our verification and certification services"*, 14.5%, $3.6M) and does **consulting and data work** billed by the hour off a rate card (4.7%, $1.2M, *"predominantly project based and not recurring"*). Total 2025 revenue **$24.9M**, gross margin **38.2%**, operating income **$1.2M** (4.8%), net income $1.5M after a $0.9M gain on selling the Progressive Beef stake. The costs are people: auditors, their travel, and a head office. Volume moves with the number of cattle and farms enrolled in verified programs, which moves with the cattle cycle and with the premium buyers pay for verified animals.
- **The scarce input this business controls.** Its standing with the standard owners: the accreditations and approvals that let it verify to a program (*"We are recognized and utilized by numerous standard-setting bodies as an accredited verification or certification service provider"*), a customer book of *"more than approximately 17,500"* producers and food businesses, a few programs where it is the designated verifier (*"Where Food Comes From is the primary certifier for Progressive Beef"*), its own label and standard (*"Where Food Comes From Source Verified"*, *"Raisewell Certified Standard"*), and, by the filer's own account, two people: *"Both John and Leann Saunders' reputation and prominence in the field provide us with a strong competitive advantage."* Whether any of this is scarce is Q2's question.
- **Will the fundamentals look broadly the same in ten years?** The need for an independent check on a label claim will exist as long as label claims earn premiums; which claims earn premiums changes with trade rules and buyer fashion (the filer's own example: *"In January 2013, the Japanese government announced a change to its import requirements on U.S. beef ... it negatively impacted the premiums typically seen in the marketplace for source and age verified cattle"*). The mechanism, auditor days sold to producers who want a claim, is the same in every 10-K from fiscal 2008 to 2025; the mix of claims is not. That is a question for Q2 and Q4, not a failure of understanding.
- **Relatively simple and stable in character [E3-31]?** Yes: a labour business that sells inspection days and annual certificates, plus a pass-through of ear tags. I can state how it makes money in under five minutes **[E4-46]**; there is no technology to judge, despite the EDGAR "software" code, and nothing in the accounts depends on a model I cannot follow.
