(New York, founded 1982 by Jean Madar and Philippe Benacin, who still run it; "Inter Parfums, Inc." until a rename voted on 2024-09-17) rents the right to put famous fashion and jewellery names on perfume, owns a few such names outright, and has the perfume composed, bottled and filled by outside firms before selling it to department stores, duty-free operators and distributors in over 120 countries; 68% of sales go through Interparfums SA in Paris, 72% owned and listed on Euronext. $1,488.5M of net sales in FY2025, gross margin 63.6%, operating income $270.3M (18.2%), $168.4M of net income to IPAR's owners after the 28% outside holders of the Paris company. - **What the filings show at Q2.** The best-run licensee that files, renting its product. *"All of our rights relating to prestige fragrance brands, other than Off-White, Lanvin, Goutal and Rochas, are derived from licenses"*; the seven largest brands (Jimmy Choo, Coach, Montblanc, GUESS, Lacoste, Donna Karan/DKNY, Ferragamo), 77% of FY2025 sales, are all licensed, expire 2030-2048, and renew at the licensor's option or on sales targets. The company has lived the outcome once: **Burberry was 62% of sales in FY2004 and 46% in FY2012 when its owner exercised a contractual buy-back** (*"the greater of the then fair market value of the unexpired term of the license or 70% of 2010 net wholesale sales"*, €181M), and **sales fell 28% over the next three years** (FY2012 $654.1M to FY2015 $468.5M), not passing the old level until FY2018. Balmain bought its licence back too (2016, €5.4M); Dunhill was not renewed (2023). Coty's own 10-K shows the licences moving in both directions (Lacoste to Interparfums, Beckham and Nautica to follow, Gucci back to Kering). About a fifth of sales goes on advertising names the company mostly does not own and 8.2% on royalties; price follows cost (gross margin 61.4-63.9% since FY2015; *"we may not be able to continue increasing our prices indefinitely"*); capital grows about 75 cents per added dollar of sales. Returns are good (27-31% on operating capital), not a franchise's: [E3-43]'s "a business", the best tenant; [E3-79]'s lease with See's defence (the customers' attachment) missing, because the customers are attached to the owner's name.
