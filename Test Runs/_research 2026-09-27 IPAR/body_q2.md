*Every Interparfums figure below is read from its filed 10-Ks FY2004-FY2025 and the 10-Q for the quarter to 2026-06-30 (accessions in Step 0), or from SEC companyfacts checked against those statements. **The question this file has to answer first: whose franchise is it?** The consumer who buys a bottle of Jimmy Choo I Want Choo buys the Jimmy Choo name. The brief asked whether that preference belongs to Interparfums or to the licensors, and told the run not to assume either answer. **The two hypotheses, stated so they can be attacked [E4-26].** *(a) The licensee of choice: brand owners pick Interparfums over the larger houses, renew it for decades "without any material changes in operating conditions", and it earns the best margins in its row; a brand owner acts as though there is no close substitute for it, so the franchise is the capability, and it is Interparfums'.* *(b) A tenant on other people's names: the consumer's attachment is to trademarks Interparfums does not own, the owners can decline to renew, reset terms, or buy the licence back, and licences move between licensees in both directions; Interparfums is [E3-43]'s best operator, "a business", whose profits last as long as its contracts and its execution.* Each is tested on the filings below.

**What is owned and what is rented, in the filer's own words.** *"All of our rights relating to prestige fragrance brands, other than Off-White, Lanvin, Goutal and Rochas, are derived from licenses or other agreements from unaffiliated third parties, and our business is dependent upon the continuation and renewal of such licenses and other agreements on terms favorable to us. Each license or agreement is for a specific term and may have additional optional terms. Generally, each license is subject to us making required royalty payments (which are subject to certain minimums), minimum advertising and promotional expenditures and meeting minimum sales requirements. ... Just as the loss of a license or other significant agreement may have a material adverse effect on us, a renewal on less favorable terms may also negatively impact us"* (10-K FY2025, Item 1A). **The seven brands the filer names as its largest, 77% of FY2025 sales, are all licensed**: Jimmy Choo 17% (to 2031), Coach 15% (to 2031), Montblanc 15% (to 2030), GUESS 12% (to 2048), Lacoste 7% (to 2038), Donna Karan/DKNY 7% (to 2032, *"plus a 5 -year optional term if certain sales targets are met"*), Ferragamo 4% (to 2031, the same condition). Of the owned names, **Lanvin** carries a call held by the seller: *"we granted the seller the right to repurchase the brand names and trademarks on July 1, 2027 for € 70 million (approximately $82 million)"*; **Rochas** has been impaired three times ($2.4M 2021, $6.8M 2022, $4.0M 2024, on the fashion trademark); **Off-White** and **Goutal** began commercial use on 2026-01-01; **Solférino** was launched in 2025. None of the owned names is among the seven.

**[E3-03], criterion by criterion.** *"An economic franchise arises from a product or service that: (1) is needed or desired; (2) is thought by its customers to have no close substitute and; (3) is not subject to price regulation."*
- **(1) Needed or desired [x]: passes.** Prestige fragrance is desired; the filer calls it *"the extremely buoyant high-end fragrance market"*.
- **(3) Not subject to price regulation [x]: passes.** Fragrance is regulated as a cosmetic for safety and labelling (MoCRA, EU Regulation 1223/2009), never for price.
- **(2) Thought by its customers to have no close substitute: the contested criterion.**
  - **The consumer's belief attaches to the name, and the name is the licensor's.** Where the consumer's preference is strong enough to count, the filings show it following the trademark, not the licensee: the Burberry buy-out took 46% of sales with it and the licensee's sales fell 28% over three years. The filer says the same of the market: *"The market for our products depends to a significant extent upon the value associated with our trademarks and brand names"*, and 24 names stand in its FY2025 licence table (two not yet in force) against five owned fragrance names.
  - **For the licensors, who buy Interparfums' service, licensees are close substitutes, and the record shows them substituted.** Licences have moved from Interparfums to a rival (Burberry to Coty), from rivals to Interparfums (Lacoste, and by 2028-2030 David Beckham and Nautica, from Coty), and back to their owners (Burberry, Balmain, Gucci at Coty). Renewal is, by contract, *"plus a 5 -year optional term if certain sales targets are met"* or subject to *"agreement of the licensor"*.
  - **The competition the filer describes.** *"The market for prestige fragrance products is highly competitive and sensitive to changing preferences and demands. The prestige fragrance industry is highly concentrated around certain major players with resources far greater than ours"* (Item 1); *"We are subject to extreme competition in the fragrance industry. ... Competitive factors include pricing, marketing, advertising and promotional activities ... and most importantly, consumer brand recognition ... or if we are unsuccessful in competing on price terms"* (Item 1A).

**[E3-43]'s demonstration, *"a company’s ability to regularly price its product or service aggressively and thereby to earn high rates of return on capital"*, and its other half: *"In contrast, “a business” earns exceptional profits only if it is the low-cost operator or if supply of its product or service is tight."***
- **The return half: passes, at a good level, not an exceptional one.** Operating income on operating capital including the bought licences and trademarks: 16.3-36.1% FY2009-FY2025, 26.5-31.5% FY2021-FY2025. **[E3-46]** is satisfied as a number, at a level far below the MEDP file's 107-286% and consistent with a well-run distributor's return on stock and receivables.
- **The pricing half: not shown, and the filer describes the opposite.** *"we were able to offset the effects of inflation during 2022 by increasing the prices of our products ... we increased our sales prices to mitigate its impact to some degree in prior years and implemented cost saving efforts to mitigate these impacts in the current year. With the inflation moderating in 2025, we did increase our prices, predominately in the US, to partially offset the impact of tariffs. However, we may not be able to continue increasing our prices indefinitely without causing a reduction in the number of consumers with sufficient disposable income to buy certain of our fragrance products"* (Item 1A, FY2025); FY2025 tariffs *"resulted in $12.8 million in higher costs ... We have been able to partially mitigate these impacts through favorable segment and brand mix ... as well as pricing, leaving us with a gross margin erosion of 0.3% of sales"*. Gross margin has held 61.4-63.9% since FY2015: **price follows cost; it does not lead it.** Where pricing power exists in this trade it is the brand owner's, who sets the royalty and the advertising minimum and can decline to renew.
- **[E2-44], the two characteristics.** The first (*"an ability to increase prices rather easily (even when product demand is flat and capacity is not fully utilized) without fear of significant loss of either market share or unit volume"*): **not shown** (above). The second (*"an ability to accommodate large dollar volume increases in business ... with only minor additional investment of capital"*): **not met**: about 75 cents of operating capital for each added dollar of sales since FY2009, plus entry fees to add licences.

### THE STRONGEST EVIDENCE AGAINST THIS VERDICT, stated as its holders would state it [E4-51, E4-26, E3-47]
The verdict below is OUT, so the case for IN is set out first, at full strength:
1. **The licensors behave as though there is no close substitute for Interparfums.** They renew it for decades on unchanged terms (GUESS to 2048, Cavalli to 2046), hand it licences from the largest house (Lacoste, Beckham, Nautica from Coty), and say so (*"an exceptional steward"*). The customer of a licensee is the licensor, and that customer's revealed preference is the [E3-03] test.
2. **The returns and the row.** 27-31% on capital including the bought licences, and the best margins and growth of every licensee that files.
3. **The Burberry payment proves the licence is an owned asset.** €181M was paid for it; value was compensated, not lost; and sales passed the Burberry peak within six years.
4. **Interparfums increasingly owns its names** (Lanvin, Rochas, Off-White, Goutal, Solférino).
5. **The COKE precedent**: a licensee passed Q2 NARROW in this register.

**Answered.** (1) is accepted as fact and is the finding that Interparfums is an excellent operator; but the same customers have also left it (Burberry and Balmain by buy-back, Dunhill by non-renewal, Celine by agreement, Gap and Banana Republic by expiry), renew on conditions (*"if certain sales targets are met"*), and move licences among licensees in both directions; a customer that re-chooses at every term and has exercised a buy-back twice is choosing the best operator in a market of substitutes, [E3-43]'s *"a business"*, which *"can be killed by poor management"* (the MEDP and AYI class). (2) is accepted and weighed at [E3-43]: the return half passes, the pricing half is absent (price follows cost), and [E2-44]'s second characteristic fails (75 cents of capital per added dollar of sales). (3) is the evidence for (b), not (a): the payment was fixed by a contract formula for *"the unexpired term of the license"*, the licensee was paid for years of rent and nothing for the brand equity it had spent a fifth of its sales building, and the consumer followed the owner (sales −28%). (4) is too small and too new to carry the file: none of the owned names is among the seven that make 77% of sales, Lanvin is callable by its seller on 2027-07-01 at €70M, and Off-White, Goutal and Solférino began selling in 2025-2026; it is written into the reversal conditions below. (5) fails on the unit of the contract: the COKE entry records that its bottling agreement *"is renewable BY THE BOTTLER indefinitely ... the licence dies by covenant default, not expiry"*; Interparfums' licences expire on dates in its own table and renew at the licensor's option or on sales targets, and two of its licensors have bought their licences back. **[E3-47]** (a wrongly closed file inside the circle is the costliest error) was weighed, and is why the reversal conditions below are written in terms of ownership and renewal, so the file reopens on evidence rather than on price. **[E4-23]** is carried as a separate defect.
