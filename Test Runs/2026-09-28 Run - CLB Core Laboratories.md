## Q1 — CAN I UNDERSTAND HOW THIS MAKES MONEY? **[E3-31]**
- Unit economics in my own words, no management language: **Core Lab is paid to measure things and to sell one consumable, in two segments.** **Reservoir Description (FY2025 revenue $347.7M, 66%; operating income $43.9M, 12.6%)** runs a network of laboratories (*"over 70 offices in more than 50 countries"*, five large technology centres in Aberdeen, Abu Dhabi, Houston, Kuala Lumpur and Rotterdam) to which oil companies send rock cores and fluid samples from their wells; Core Lab measures porosity, permeability and how the oil, gas and water behave at reservoir pressure and temperature, and bills per test or per study. About two-thirds of that segment is fluids work, which includes testing crude oil and refined products for quality and quantity when cargoes move (*"Fluid analyses account for approximately two-thirds of Reservoir Description revenue."*). It also sells multi-client studies whose data it owns and resells, and a small line of laboratory instruments ($13.0M of product sales in FY2025). Costs are the scientists, the buildings and the equipment, which run whether or not samples arrive, so margin swings with volume. **Production Enhancement ($178.8M, 34%; operating income $12.1M, 6.7%)** is mainly a manufacturer: it makes perforating charges and guns (the explosives lowered into a cased well to open it to the rock) in Texas, Alberta and Wales, and sells them per well ($114.1M of product sales), plus a diagnostic service ($64.8M) that puts chemical tracers into fracturing fluid and reads which stages of a horizontal well are flowing. Services were 76% of consolidated revenue and products 24% in FY2025 (*"Services accounted for 76%, 74% and 73% of our revenue for the years ended December 31, 2025, 2024 and 2023, respectively."*). Total FY2025 revenue $526.5M; segment operating income $56.0M; net income attributable to the company $29.7M after $10.6M of interest.
- **The brief's Q1 prior checked against the segment note: right in outline, incomplete in three places.** It omits (1) the crude-oil and refined-products assay and inspection work inside Reservoir Description's fluids line (the FY2025 MD&A names *"laboratory assay services and laboratory instrument sales, which yield lower margins"*), (2) that Production Enhancement is mostly a product business (64% products) whose product is perforating hardware, and (3) the multi-client studies Core Lab owns and resells (*"Core Lab retains rights of ownership to complete joint industry project studies, which can be resold at a later date."*).
- The scarce input this business controls: **the global laboratory network and its staff, the accumulated measurement databases and multi-client studies (*"Core Lab’s proprietary World Wide Rock Catalog™ provides a database and analog reference set for predicting properties when physical measurements are unavailable."*), patented perforating-charge designs and tracer chemistry, and relationships with national and international oil companies across fifty countries.** Whether any of this is scarce in [E3-03]'s sense is Q2's question, not Q1's.
- Will the fundamentals look broadly the same in ten years? **The way the money is made, yes; the volume, no one can say from the filings.** The company was established in 1936, and the FY2007 10-K describes the same measurement and completion businesses in three segments. What changes is demand: Reservoir Description follows international and offshore field development and Production Enhancement follows North American well completions, and the filer names a technology risk in its own words (*"As genAI technologies continue to improve, we may not be able to replicate or compete with potential applications relevant to our business, including, for example, enhanced computer modeling of reservoir rock and fluid properties, which could displace some of our current products or services."*). That bears on the moat (Q2) and on survival (Q4); it does not change the unit economics, which are a fee per measurement on a fixed-cost laboratory network and a margin per perforating system sold.
- **Simple and stable in character [E3-31]; a decision that can be made on the filings [E4-46].** Testing samples for a fee and making an explosive consumable require no technology forecast to understand. The science inside a test is specialist, but the business question (how many samples arrive and what each one pays) is not.

### THE STRONGEST EVIDENCE AGAINST THIS VERDICT, stated as its holders would state it [E4-51, E4-26, E3-47]
The verdict below is OUT, so the case for IN is set out first, at full strength.
1. *"For six years this business earned more than its whole capital every year, pre-tax: 123% to 162% on net tangible capital from 2009 to 2014, operating margins of 27% to 32% at a time when Schlumberger earned 21% and Halliburton 11% to 19%. That is [E3-43]'s 'high rates of return on capital' at a level almost nothing in the oilfield reaches, and it was earned on a laboratory network, proprietary databases and patented charges, which is a scarce-input business, not a rented rig."* **Answer:** accepted as the fact of 2009-2014, and it is the best fact in the file. It is also the record that has not been repeated. The same network, databases and patents earned 17.5% to 23.5% in FY2021-FY2025, a fifth to a sixth of the earlier level, on a capital base that did not grow (net tangible capital $140-269M in every year). Operating income fell from $346.5M (FY2014) to $56.5M (FY2025) on revenue down 51%. [E3-43] asks for the ability to price *"regularly"*; the record shows it in one cycle's up-leg and not in the eleven years since, which include the 2022-2023 years of high oil prices. And in no filing read, boom or bust, does the filer attribute a margin gain to price: its explanations are demand for proprietary diagnostics (2014), activity levels, fixed-cost absorption, cost restoration and mix.
2. *"2009 was a real test and Core Lab passed it: revenue fell 11% and the operating margin rose from 26.5% to 26.9%; Reservoir Description's margin rose from 23.4% to 25.6% on revenue down 5%. A business without pricing power loses margin when volume falls."* **Answer:** accepted as the 2009 record. Two cautions: the downturn was short (revenue was above 2008 by 2010), and Production Enhancement's margin fell that year with the filer naming price in its own words (*"Additionally, reduced demand in North America has decreased margins due to pressure on pricing"*, FY2009 10-K). The longer downturn that followed is the better test, and there Reservoir Description's margin fell in every year from 27.7% (FY2014) to 13.1% (FY2019), the filer giving *"lower activity levels"* as the cause, and it has not been back above 15.5% since. A margin that falls with volume for five straight years and never recovers is the fixed-cost laboratory meeting a customer who will not pay more per test to cover the idle capacity.
3. *"The returns today are still respectable: 20% to 23% pre-tax on tangible capital. A commodity business in [E2-58]'s sense earns 'poor profitability'; this one does not."* **Answer:** accepted that 20% is not ruin, and it is carried into the reversal condition. But [E2-58]'s test is relative and over the cycle: a laboratory business that earned 130% and now earns 21% has moved from the exception to the middle of its peers, and the row below shows where the middle is. Core Lab's FY2021-FY2025 operating margin (mean 10.1%) is below Schlumberger's (17.4%, three years filed), Halliburton's (13.9%) and Weatherford's (12.2%), and above only the smaller completion-tool filers.
4. *"The laboratory side is not the oilfield cycle. It is 80% international, it follows long-cycle offshore development, and the 2024 MD&A credits 'continued improvement in utilization of our global laboratory network'. The recovery is under way: Reservoir Description's margin went 7.4% → 12.3% → 14.9% from 2022 to 2024."* **Answer:** accepted as the direction of 2022-2024. It reversed at once: 12.6% in FY2025, which the filer explains by mix, not price (*"The changes in operating income and operating margins were primarily attributable to a different mix of revenue with a lower level of revenue from reservoir rock and reservoir fluid analysis in 2025, which yields a higher margin, and higher level of laboratory assay services and laboratory instrument sales, which yield lower margins."*), and 4.7% in the June 2026 quarter, with the Middle East conflict named. A 14.9% peak is about half the margin of 2008-2014.
5. *"The measurements are hard to replicate: a World Wide Rock Catalog, decades of proprietary studies, reservoir-condition flow systems Core Lab builds itself. A customer does not easily switch the lab that holds its field's history."* **Answer:** accepted that switching is slow.
