### 5b. RAPID7: filed TWO retention metrics, and withdrew them BOTH, in stages, two years apart

Rapid7 historically filed a **pair** of rates in the same sentence:

- **"renewal rate"**, a dollar-based *net* retention rate. Its definition includes upsells
  and cross-sells in the numerator, which is why it printed above 100%. This is the metric
  directly comparable to Tenable's dollar-based net expansion rate and to CrowdStrike's
  dollar-based net retention rate.
- **"expiring revenue renewal rate"** (later shortened to **"expiring renewal rate"**), the
  same denominator *without* the upsell and cross-sell credit, so the closest thing Rapid7
  ever filed to a gross-dollar retention rate. It printed in the **high 80s**.

**Verbatim, FY2020 10-K (filed 2021-02-26), THE LAST FILING THAT CARRIES THE NUMBER, and note
the measurement window has already been quietly narrowed from the full year to a single

agreements, including upsells and cross-sells of additional products, but excluding
professional services and Logentries, in a trailing 12-month period by the dollar value of

**The second and final withdrawal is dated to the 10-K for FY2021, accession
`0001560327-22-000026`, filed 2022-02-24.** In that filing the renewal-rate paragraph, the
definition and the number are
all absent from the business section and from MD&A. What remains in that vintage and in
every vintage since is (i) a strategy bullet with no number, verbatim, still present in the
FY2025 10-K: *"Strengthen our customer renewal rate: We intend to continue to drive customer
satisfaction and renewals by offering professional services, support, and strong investments
in customer success functions"*; (ii) a risk-factor mention, verbatim: *"customers have no
obligation to renew their subscriptions with us and we may not be able to accurately predict
customer renewal rates"*; and (iii) a passing reference inside the ARR definition, verbatim:
*"ARR is not a forecast of future revenue, which can be impacted by contract start and end
dates and renewal rates."* **The heading survived, the number did not.**

**No explanatory note accompanied the removal.** Searching the FY2021 10-K for "no longer"
returns only three hits, none of which concerns the renewal rate: two are third-party-vendor
risk-factor boilerplate and one is the ASU 2020-06 convertible-note accounting note.

**What replaced it.** The FY2021 10-K introduced a revised customer-count definition and
kept ARR; the FY2022 10-K (filed 2023-02-24) added **"ARR per customer"**, defined verbatim
as *"ARR per customer is defined as ARR divided by the number of customers at the end of the
period."* This is not a retention metric: it is a ratio of two stock quantities that cannot
separate expansion within the retained base from mix change in gross adds and churn. **Since
2022-02-24 Rapid7 has filed no measure of what a retained customer cohort does to its
spend.**
