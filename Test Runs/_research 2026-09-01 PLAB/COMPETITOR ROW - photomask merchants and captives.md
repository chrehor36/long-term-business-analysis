Source: DNP Consolidated Financial Statements, Note 23 Segment Information, pp.43-46 of the FY2025 file and p.44 of the FY2024 file (URLs in §1). Consolidated
operating income for context: JPY 101,039m (FY2026/3) and JPY 93,612m (FY2025/3) on net sales of JPY 1,512,571m and JPY 1,457,609m.

**MIXED SEGMENT WARNING.** DNP's own definition of Electronics (FY2025 FS, p.41): "Optical films for displays, metal masks for organic EL displays, large photomasks
for liquid crystal displays, electric shades, **photomasks for semiconductor products**, lead frames for small semiconductor packages, LSI design, hard disk drive
suspensions, camera module components for smartphones, etc." Nine product lines; semiconductor photomasks are one. **Not a photomask margin.**

**How much of Electronics is actually photomask?** DNP Group IR-Day 2025 (2025-07-10), slide 9, "Details of the Focus Business Areas": business area
**Semiconductors**, main product "Photomasks for semiconductors production (Top-level market share in photomasks for external sales)", **FY2024 sales JPY 66bn**, ROE
approx. 10%, market CAGR 2024-2029 approx. 8%. Against Electronics sales of JPY 247,777m that year, the semiconductor business is roughly **27% of the segment**.
Caveat: the IR-Day agenda lists Semiconductors as "Photomasks for Semiconductors, Glass Core", so JPY 66bn is not photomask-only either. **A photomask-only DNP
revenue or margin is NOT OBTAINABLE.** Forward plan (slide 56): "Semiconductor Photomask Sales Plan" indexed FY2024 = 100 runs FY2025 113, FY2026 122, FY2027 138,
FY2028 155, a stated **CAGR of 11.6% (FY2024-FY2028)** vs a semiconductor market at 8.13%; FY2026-FY2028 capex plan JPY 30bn; EUV masks "expected to be 10% of
overall sales as planned"; "start of shipment of masks for EUV with the launch of a pilot line at Rapidus."

**DNP's stake in Photronics' Xiamen JV (PDMCX).** Documented from the Photronics side only. Photronics FY2025 10-K, Note 6: in January 2018 Photronics (via
Photronics Singapore PTE. LTD.) and DNP (via DNP Asia Pacific PTE, Ltd.) formed a JV "under which **DNP obtained a 49.99% interest in the Company's IC business in
Xiamen, China**". Photronics holds the 50.01% variable interest, appoints the board majority, is the primary beneficiary, and **consolidates PDMCX**. Net income
recorded from PDMCX: USD 19,462k (FY2025), USD 20,074k (FY2024), USD 25,098k (FY2023). Put/call: if either party's interest falls below 20.0% for more than six
consecutive months, either side may force a sale at the exiting party's share of net book value.

**DNP's own disclosure of the PDMCX stake: NOT FOUND.** "Photronics" appears zero times in DNP's FY2025 consolidated financial statements and zero times in the
IR-Day 2025 deck. Neither carrying value nor earnings contribution is disclosed by DNP. **Competitive note:** Photronics names DNP a competitor **"(outside of Taiwan
and China)"**, reflecting the two Photronics/DNP joint ventures. DNP is partner and competitor, separated by geography.

IT segment operating profit is NOT DISCLOSED**; the FY25 full-year slide gives consolidated operating profit only (JPY 285.2bn on JPY 947.7bn revenue, FY25 Q4
presentation p.5).

**MIXED SEGMENT WARNING.** Hoya's product list for Information Technology (AR FY2025, p.31): "Electronics related products: **Photomasks and Maskblanks for
semiconductors, Photomasks for FPD, Glass disks for hard disk drives (HDDs)**. Imaging related products: Optical lenses, Optical glass material, Light source." HDD
glass substrates are large and currently the fastest-growing line (FY26 Q1 HDD substrate sales +29% YoY on nearline/HAMR data-centre demand, FY26 Q1 presentation
p.13). The 50%+ margin blends mask blanks, FPD masks, HDD glass and optics. It is **not** a photomask margin and materially overstates what a merchant mask shop
earns.

**IS HOYA A SUPPLIER TO PHOTRONICS AS WELL AS A COMPETITOR? Yes as to the industry role; the specific Photronics-Hoya relationship is NOT DOCUMENTED in any primary
filing.**

**Competitor:** Photronics FY2025 10-K, p.7, names "Hoya Corporation"; Hoya makes FPD and semiconductor photomasks in its own right. **Supplier, from Hoya's own
words:** HOYA REPORT 2022, IT review, says mask blank customers are "Semiconductor manufacturers, foundries, and other HOYA customers" and "**In some cases, product
goes via photomask manufacturer**" (https://www.hoya.com/ir/2022/en/review/it.html); HOYA REPORT 2025 restates customers as "Semiconductor foundries and
semiconductor manufacturers" (https://www.hoya.com/ir/2025/en/), and Hoya claims to be "the only manufacturer that has rolled out both EUV and optical mask blanks"
with "an exceptionally high market share". **Photronics' side:** blanks are described generically, "high precision quartz substrates ... used as photomask starting
blanks and **primarily obtained from Japanese and South Korean suppliers**", with "a limited number of suppliers of these raw materials, and we do not have long-term
contracts with these suppliers. Any delays or quality problems ... **particularly photomask blanks**, could cause delays in the shipments of photomasks."
**Photronics never names Hoya.** The link is an industry-structure inference.

**Analytic consequence:** Hoya sits one layer upstream of Photronics on the blanks it buys and alongside it on the masks it sells. Photronics' key input is supplied
by a near-monopolist earning 50%+ segment margins while Photronics earns 24.5%, and §7 shows the largest merchant in the industry can pass through only about half of
blank price increases. That is the shape of the value chain and the most important thing on this page for the moat question.

## 5. CAPTIVE MASK SHOPS: do any of them report photomasks separately?

**No. Confirmed definitively from the filings themselves.**

**Intel (INTC).** FY2025 Form 10-K, FY ended 2025-12-27, filed 2026-01-23. Full text searched: **"photomask", "mask shop" and "reticle" each appear ZERO times.**
Intel reports "our business results in three reportable segments: **CCG, DCAI and Intel Foundry**", plus a non-reportable "all other". Photomask cost sits
unidentified inside Intel Foundry's cost of sales. **TSMC (TSM).** FY2025 Form 20-F, FY ended 2025-12-31, filed 2026-04-16. Full text searched: **"photomask" ZERO;
"reticle" ZERO.** Note 38: "TSMC's chief operating decision makers periodically review operating results, focusing on operating income generated by foundry segment
... the Company **has only one operating segment, the foundry segment**." **Samsung (005930.KS):** see §6, zero "mask" hits in 358 pages, segments DX / DS / SDC /
Harman.

**What this means.** The captive 63% of the market publishes **no revenue, no cost, no capex and no margin for photomasks anywhere**. The competitor row cannot be
completed for the larger half of the industry. That is a structural **UNKNOWABLE**, not an UNRESEARCHED gap: no document exists that would resolve it, because the
captives do not measure it for external reporting.

**Direction of travel: the captives are outsourcing more.** Tekscend FY2026/3 tanshin MD&A: 「外販フォトマスク市場においては、フォトマスクを内製しないファウンダリからの外注需要拡大に加え、フォトマスクを内製する
半導体メーカーにおいても、内部リソース不足を背景に外注需要が高まりました。」 ("In the merchant photomask market, in addition to expanding outsourcing demand from foundries that do not produce photomasks
in-house, **outsourcing demand also rose among semiconductor makers that DO produce photomasks in-house**, against a background of internal resource shortage.")
Forward look, same document: memory makers that have historically self-supplied are expected to outsource, being focused internally on leading-edge memory for AI.
The EDINET prospectus adds that in-house shops will likely concentrate capital and people on High-NA EUV and buy merchant supply for existing nodes. DNP IR-Day 2025,
slide 53: "We plan to expand our business into cutting-edge areas currently dominated by in-house photomasks." See §6 for the Korea-specific evidence, which is the
strongest of the set.

Photronics' own framing of the oscillation, FY2025 10-K, p.7:
> "...a trend toward the divestiture or closing of captive photomask operations ... and to an increase in the share
> of the market served by independent merchant manufacturers ... That period was followed by a period during which
> ... some captive mask facilities invested at faster rates than independent manufacturers, and the revenue share of
> market transitioned back to photomasks being majority captive-supplied. **More recently, there has been a tendency
> of more production being directed to the independent merchant manufacturers, with market share moving toward the
> independents.** Nevertheless, most captive manufacturers maintain business and technology relationships with
> independent photomask manufacturers for ongoing support."

Photronics does **not** quantify the split in either the FY2025 or the FY2024 10-K. SEMI does, via Tekscend: 63% captive in 2024 (§1), against 65% in 2016, 65% in
2017 and 64% in 2018 per SEMI's own free press releases ([2019-04-15](https://www.semi.org/en/semiconductor-photomask-sales-reach-record-high-4-billion)). **The
captive share has drifted down about two points in eight years: the shift is real but slow.**

growth in the U.S., **75% in South Korea**, and 18% in Japan", driven by "significantly higher outsourcing volumes from existing customers" as AI/HBM pulls internal
engineers to leading-edge work. 1H Q&A (2025-11-21, Q1): "the **captive players have been shifting their focus to EUV and memory, HBM** ... we see a continuous trend
of outsourcing optical masks." Photronics is investing behind the same thesis: Korea was **20% of Q3 FY2026 revenue** and USD 158.5m in FY2025, and the Q3 FY2026
release says "Our investments in the U.S. and Korea remain on track."

## 7. PHOTOMASK PRICING

**Headline: mainstream/legacy prices are under pressure, not rising. Mask-SET value is rising at the leading edge, but through layer count and node mix, not through
like-for-like price increases. No issuer publishes a like-for-like price index, and no primary document gives a dollar or yen figure for a mask set at any node.**

### Mainstream / legacy: under pressure (primary)

**Tekscend Annual Securities Report, 5th FY, 2026-06-24**, competition risk factor: "if **price competition among merchant photomask vendors intensifies** ... **a
growing number of emerging photomask vendors have appeared in China in recent years, raising concerns about intensified price and technology competition.**" **FY2025
full-year briefing (filed 2026-06-04):** "In China, **intensified price competition with local photomask vendors** led to a year-on-year decline in our Chinese sales
ratio ... the downward trend has recently begun to bottom out." **FY2025 1H Q&A (2025-11-21, Q7):** legacy tools are at end-of-life, and because Tekscend
cannibalises parts to extend equipment life, "**we are able to supply legacy products at the lower price which customers appreciate.**" IPO prospectus p.35 calls
this industry-wide: EOL tools force over-specified replacements, so "**cost competitiveness has become an industry-wide problem**." **Photronics:** IC mainstream
fell 8.2% in FY2025 (USD -33.4m) and a further 9% YoY in Q3 FY2026 (8-K Ex-99.2, 2026-08-26, slide 15: IC mainstream USD 86.2m; IC high-end USD 68.5m, a record 44%
of IC revenue). CEO, Q3 FY2026 call: "**China market is competitive** ... primarily on the mainstream. We have some local mask houses as competitors" **[Motley Fool
transcript of the company's call; release and deck are primary via 8-K acc. 0000810136-26-000006]**.

### Input costs rising, only half passed through (the key margin fact)

**Tekscend FY2025 Q3 Q&A (2026-02-24, Q7):** "we have also **received price increase requests from blank suppliers** and some of this impact remains our burden. On
the other hand, since our company handles the largest volume of photomasks among competitors, **we have been able to pass on approximately half of the price
increases to our customers.**" Full-year Q&A (2026-06-04, Q6): "**Material costs have been increasing year by year** ... it is not always possible to fully pass
through all costs." That is the Hoya-upstream squeeze of §3 quantified by the industry's largest merchant: roughly **50% pass-through on blanks**, by the player with
the most buying power. Read Photronics' gross margin slide from 37.7% to 36.4% to 35.3% across FY2023-FY2025 against it.

### Advanced node and EUV: set value rises, but no price is disclosed

- **Masks per set by node**, Tekscend full-year Q&A (2026-06-04, Q15): "as nodes advance from 28nm to 14nm to 7nm,
  **mask count per set increases from approximately 50 to 70 to 90**".
- **Tekscend IPO prospectus, EDINET S100WN47, 2025-09-04, p.37:** at the leading edge, "in addition to an increase in
  the number of photomasks required per set, **the ASP per set is also on a rising trend**". English annual report
  (2026-06-24, p.9): "more photomasks in a single photomask set, which typically leads to a **higher unit price for
  the photomask set**", ordered "on a set basis".
- **EUV is expensive but still small:** "**EUV blanks are extremely expensive compared to conventional photomasks**,
  so profitability can be significantly affected if yields are not sufficient ... due to high technological value and
  higher mask prices accompanying scaling, margins are generally higher" (full-year Q&A, Q12). Scale: "**the EUV
  ratio is around 5%**" of Tekscend revenue in FY2026/3, rising to "around **8-9% by 2027-2028**" (Q2). DNP plans EUV
  at 10% of its photomask sales (§2).
- **Photronics 8-K Ex-99.2, 2026-08-26, slide 6, "Node Migration Drives Mask Set Value":** plots "Indicative Mask
  Value" from 130nm to 2/3nm, footlined "Drives higher individual mask ASP, more layers per mask set, and higher
  barriers of entry for competitors". **The y-axis carries no units and no dollar values.**
- The **only** dollar figure from any officer: Photronics chairman/CEO, Q3 FY2026 call, "**1 mask set on the high end
  is a big dollar amount that can swing quite a bit of, a couple of million plus dollars in a quarter easily**"
  **[Motley Fool transcript]**. He is describing revenue variability from set timing, not quoting a price. Implies a
  high-end set on the order of USD 1m+, but **it is not a price disclosure.**

### A derived ASP series does exist, from the Chinese listed mask makers

Two of Photronics' named competitors are listed on the Shanghai STAR board and file sales **volume in square metres** alongside revenue by product, so ASP per m² is
arithmetic on filed numbers. Shenzhen QingYi (688138) FY2025, https://static.cninfo.com.cn/finalpage/2026-04-27/1225197584.PDF (2026-04-27); Shenzhen Newway (688401)
FY2025, https://static.cninfo.com.cn/finalpage/2026-04-17/1225113244.PDF (2026-04-17). Derived **CNY/m²**:

| | 2023 | 2024 | 2025 |
|---|---|---|---|
| QingYi quartz mask | 220,825 | 234,952 (+6.4%) | 232,443 (**-1.1%**) |
| QingYi soda-lime mask | 27,398 | 26,625 (-2.8%) | 26,704 (+0.3%) |
| Newway quartz mask | 230,847 | 236,767 (+2.6%) | 253,568 (+7.1%) |
| Newway soda-lime mask | 29,959 | 38,738 (+29.3%) | 50,662 (+30.8%) |

**Carry the caveat:** ASP per m² blends price with mix (generation, precision class, substrate), and both issuers attribute growth to volume and mix, not price.
Newway's FY2025 report says quartz revenue rose 主要系石英掩膜版销售量增长所致 (mainly because volume grew). Both are mostly **FPD** mask makers: QingYi FY2025 is CNY
977.98m FPD vs CNY 204.15m semiconductor. Cleanest read is the quartz line: **flat to slightly down at QingYi in
2025, up at Newway on mix.** QingYi's competition risk factor names Photronics, TOPPAN, SKE, HOYA, DNP, LG-IT and Taiwan Mask, and warns of intensification.

### Photronics' own mix (primary, FY2025 10-K revenue disaggregation note, USD thousands)

|---|---|---|---|
| IC High-end | 238,865 | 228,469 | 194,939 |

Definitions, same 10-K: "High-end" = 28nm and smaller for ICs, and Generation 10.5+, AMOLED and LTPS for FPDs; 32nm and above and Generation 8 and below are
mainstream. The only ASP statement in the whole filing, a footnote to the MD&A revenue tables: **"High-end photomasks typically have higher ASPs than mainstream
photomasks."** Two structural facts: photomasks are "individually unique ... consequently, **the Company's photomasks do not have standard or 'list' prices**"; and
"We expect to face continued competition which, in the past, **has led to pressure to reduce prices** ... together with the significant investment required in
capital equipment to manufacture high-end photomasks [this] will continue in the future." **Photronics discloses no ASP figure, no price index and no per-mask price,
and never separates price from volume.**

**Volume is not the problem.** Tekscend FY2026/3 tanshin MD&A: demand was solid "across all nodes from advanced to mature" (「先端から成熟まですべてのノードにおいて需要が堅調に推移」), and for
mature processes "demand was maintained across a wide range of applications and held firm"; the EDINET prospectus adds that IoT and automotive keep legacy-node
demand expanding alongside advanced. DNP IR-Day 2025, slide 53: "the current photomask merchant market remains strong, primarily due to ongoing demand in product
development." **So the margin compression at both Photronics and Tekscend is a price-and-input-cost story, not a volume story.**
