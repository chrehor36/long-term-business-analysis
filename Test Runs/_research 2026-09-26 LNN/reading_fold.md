### The finding
- **[E3-03] criterion (2) fails for irrigation (84% of revenue) in the registrant's own words**: *"Four manufacturers
  control a substantial majority of the U.S. center pivot irrigation system market"*, competition *"in areas of price
  and seasonal programs"*, and in every 10-K from FY2010 to FY2025 the risk factor that *"Due to price competition [...]
  the Company may not be able to recoup increases in these costs through price increases"*. **The market leader says the
  same**: Valmont, *"Pricing in the industry can become highly competitive, particularly during periods of low demand"*.
- **Price follows steel, in the MD&A's own year-by-year explanation**: *"reduced market pricing from passing through lower
  steel costs"* (FY2016), *"higher average selling prices from passing through higher raw material costs"* (FY2017), *"the
  pass through of higher raw material and other costs to customers"* (FY2023), *"slightly lower average selling prices"*
  in the soft years FY2024 and FY2025. Irrigation revenue $626.0M in FY2013, $568.0M in FY2025. No unit series is filed.
- **The returns are good and they are the industry's.** Pretax operating income on capital net of cash 12.9-46.8% in
  FY2011-FY2025 except FY2019 (2.4%), because a pivot needs little plant; the filer's own after-tax ROIC, with its cash in
  the base, was 5.9-7.2% in FY2015-FY2018. **The row**: Lindsay's irrigation margin 8.5-20.8%, Valmont's 12.4-20.6% on the
  same basis, both peaking in 2013 and troughing in 2019; Lindsay below Valmont in every year 2015-2020 and above in
  2022-2025. A lead that alternates is not a low-cost position [E2-58].
- **The Road Zipper is a real narrow position** (*"limited competition in its moveable barrier line"*) inside a segment
  that is 16% of revenue and sells in lumpy public projects; the FY2024 10-K dropped the clause *"there is not another
  moveable barrier product today comparable"*. The unit of the test is the company [E4-08].
