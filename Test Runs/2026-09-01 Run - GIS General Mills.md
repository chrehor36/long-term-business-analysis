## Q1 — CAN I UNDERSTAND HOW THIS MAKES MONEY? **[E3-31]**

- **Unit economics in my own words, without management's language.** General Mills buys
  grain, meat, oils, sugar, fruit, nuts and packaging, runs them through owned plants, puts
  a trademark on the box, and sells $18.4bn a year of it to a small number of very large
  retailers, of which **one takes 22%**. The profit is the gap between what a shopper will
  pay for the name and what the input plus the plant plus the retailer's shelf cost, less
  about **4.7% of sales spent on advertising and media** ($873.6M in FY2026) and **1.4% on
  R&D** ($256.0M) to keep the name worth paying for. Four businesses sit inside that
  description: **North America Retail** (57% of sales, 68% of segment profit, 20.7% margin,
  covering cereal, snacks, meals and baking), **International** (17% / 6%, 6.2% margin),
  **North America Pet** (14% / 16%, 19.1% margin, essentially Blue Buffalo), and
  **North America Foodservice** (12% / 10%, 15.3% margin). Two joint ventures sit outside:
  Cereal Partners Worldwide with Nestlé and Häagen-Dazs Japan.
- **The scarce input this business controls:** shelf position for a #1 or #2 trademark in a
  category a shopper buys without thinking. It does **not** control grain, meat or oil
  prices, and it does not control the shelf. It rents the shelf, from Walmart above all.
- **Will the fundamentals look broadly the same in ten years?** Yes as to mechanism. People
  will eat cereal, frozen pizza, snack bars and pet food, and the manufacturing and
  distribution arrangement will look much as it does now. The doubts are about the *level*
  and about **who captures the margin between the farm and the shopper**, which are Q2 and
  Q4 questions, not intelligibility failures.
- **VERDICT: [x] IN.** "Relatively simple and stable in character" [E3-31]. Nothing here
  needs five months of study [E4-46] and "the math is not complicated" [E3-27].

### THE SINGLE STRONGEST DISCONFIRMING FACT
