## Q1 — CAN I UNDERSTAND IT? Where its economics will be in ten years, inside my perimeter. STOP.
**The test.** Understanding is "a reasonable fix on about what the earning power and competitive position will look like in five or 10 years" **[M2012-065]**, "a reasonable probability of being able to asses where the business will be in 10 years" **[M2000-037]**; the product can be understood while the economics cannot: "We just don’t know the economics of it 10 years from now." **[M2000-104]**. The product is understood: a zinc-halide battery module and its management software, sold as direct-current blocks to developers and power producers for three to twelve hour storage (10-K, Item 1). The question is the economics.

**The key variables, and how predictable they are** **[M1998-044]**:
1. **Can the product be made at a profit?** Cost of goods has exceeded revenue in every year the filings report: revenue / cost of goods, USD millions, 2019 0.5 / 8.3, 2020 0.2 / 5.5, 2021 4.6 / 46.5, 2022 17.9 / 153.3, 2023 16.4 / 89.8, 2024 15.6 / 98.9, 2025 114.2 / 258.0 (gross margin -126%), first half 2026 125.7 / 219.0 (-74%) (10-K XBRL as filed, accessions above; 10-Q `0001628280-26-052906`). Those costs are after production credits: $21.3M of 45X credit reduced 2025 cost of goods, $22.8M the first half of 2026. The filer describes itself, in its own MD&A, as "a nascent technology with a new manufacturing process that is early in its product lifecycle" and "expects its cost of goods sold to exceed revenues in the near term" (10-K, Item 7, Cost of goods sold). No year shows what a unit earns at scale, because no year has been at scale: "The output achieved to date is a fraction of what the Company expects will be necessary for full commercialization" (10-K, Item 1A). Not predictable from the record.
2. **What the rival technology will cost.** The filer names its rivals as lithium-ion makers (Fluence, Panasonic, Samsung, LG Chem, Tesla, BYD, Sungrow, CATL) and long-duration makers (ESS Inc., Enervenue, Ambri, Form Energy), and says "global lithium available supply exceeds demand. Decreases in the price of lithium [...] may increase the competitiveness of the traditional Li-ion batteries relative to the zinc-based energy storage solutions we provide" and that its modules "have less power density and may be considered inferior to competitors’ products" (10-K, Item 1A). Tesla's own 10-K for FY2025 reports its storage revenue up 27% "partially offset by a decrease in average selling price of Megapack" and cost per unit down "from lower raw material costs" (`0001628280-26-003952`). The price the product must beat is set by rivals hundreds of times its size and is falling; its path over ten years is not something the filer or I can state.
3. **What the subsidies will be.** The 45X credits run "through 2029, after which the PTC will begin to gradually phase down through 2032"; the 2025 tax act added prohibited-foreign-entity limits whose "full impact [...] cannot be known with certainty"; customers' demand depends in part on investment credits (10-K, Items 1 and 1A). A large part of the business's cost position and of its customers' economics is a policy variable with a stated end date inside the ten years.
4. **Will customers adopt it?** The filer's own words: "Our inability to predict the extent of customer adoption of our proprietary technologies in the already-established traditional energy storage market makes it difficult to evaluate our profitability and future prospects." (10-K, Item 1A). Two customers took 51.5% and 18.8% of 2025 revenue (Item 1); two customers took 97.7% of revenue in the second quarter of 2026, and $55.0M of the quarter's $68.8M came from "a revenue contract with a related party" entered in June 2026 (10-Q, Note 11).
5. **Will the product stay the product?** Gen 2.3 was replaced by Z3 (shipping since the third quarter of 2023), Z3 Phase 1 tooling was written off for Phase 2 ($9.1M in 2024, $1.8M in 2025), a new software platform was launched in 2025 and is being retrofitted to "legacy customer systems", and a new architecture (Indensity) in 2026 (10-K Items 1 and 7; 10-Q MD&A). The filer reports "quality issues in the field" and warranty accruals "based on a short operating history" (Item 1A).

**The tests, applied.**
- Test 3, do the past statements tell me the future ones **[M2008-033]**: no. Every past statement is of a business below scale with negative gross profit; the filer itself says the record "makes it difficult to evaluate our prospects".
- Test 4, important and knowable **[M2006-076]**: the deciding variable, the gross margin at scale against a falling lithium-ion price under a subsidy regime that phases down inside ten years, is important and is not knowable from any filing; "If something’s important but unknowable, forget it." **[M2006-076]**.
- Test 5, would the insiders write it down **[M2000-105]**: the insider here is the filer, and in its own risk factors it says it cannot predict customer adoption and that this "makes it difficult to evaluate our profitability and future prospects". That is the industry's own insider declining to write the forecast down.
- Test 6, can I name the winner **[M2012-067]**: the 10-K names twelve rivals across two technologies, most of them far larger; the growth of storage demand that the filer describes is visible, but "Just because Charlie and I can clearly see dramatic growth ahead for an industry does not mean we can judge what its profit margins and returns on capital will be as a host of competitors battle for supremacy." **[L2009-005]**; "we don’t know who the winners will be" **[M2014-097]**.
- Test 7, customers or technology **[M2017-019]**: the forecast is about technology (cell chemistry, manufacturing yield, the rival chemistry's cost curve), not about how a consumer behaves.
- Test 9, do I doubt it is inside **[M2002-092]**: yes, so it is not.
- Rapid change: the filer calls energy storage "a rapidly evolving sector" (10-K, Item 1, Industry Overview); a business where "future technology could hurt the business as it presently exists [...] won’t make it through the filter" **[M1998-008]**; "a business that must deal with fast-moving technology is not going to lend itself to reliable evaluations of its long-term economics" **[L1993-023]**; "whenever we look at a business and we see lots of change coming, 9 times out of 10, we’re going to pass on that." **[M1999-063]**.

**The contrary evidence, weighed.** The revenue growth, the second line, the DOE advance and the narrowing gross loss (all written down under the foundations) show the company moving; none of them shows the economics at scale or the cost of the rival product in ten years. A gross margin of -74% after production credits, in the best half-year the company has reported, is still a product sold for less than it costs to make. The movement makes the forecast more interesting; it does not make it knowable.

**The cause of the box, WORK or NATURE.** The deciding question (what this product will earn, at scale, against lithium-ion, after the credits phase down) is not answerable by reading another filing: the filer has read all of its own and says it cannot predict adoption, and its profitability depends on a rival cost curve and a policy schedule outside any filing. That is the second cause the rows name, "the nature of the industry would be the roadblock" **[L1993-023]**, with "Our problem -- which we can't solve by studying up" **[L1999-018]**, and the five-month rule: "if we can’t make a decision in five minutes, we can’t make it in five months." **[M2008-086]**. NATURE.

**The OUT reading considered.** Q1's list of what it rules OUT begins "Start-ups are not our game." **[L2007-015]**; "We don’t do startups." **[M2008-088]**. The filer calls itself "a growth company in the early commercialization stage of its lifecycle" (10-K, Item 1A), has never sold a unit at a gross profit, and expects to rely on outside capital "for the foreseeable future". The framework does not say what a start-up is, and this company has sold product since 2018, been listed since 2020 and reported $114.2M of revenue in 2025. I read the framework's fixed routing as governing: "A business whose ten-year economics cannot be foreseen because its industry changes fast closes here, at Q1, in TOO HARD" (Q1, The routing, fixed), and the rows give ignorance, not a finding against the business, as the reason: "It doesn’t mean it isn’t a good buy. [...] It just means that we don’t know how to evaluate it." **[M2000-038]**. Either reading closes the file at Q1, and under either a lower price does not reopen it. The choice is reported under What in the framework was wrong or unclear.

**VERDICT: TOO HARD (NATURE)** **[M2006-013]**. The economics of a battery that has never been made at a gross profit, against a lithium-ion cost curve the filer says is falling and a production credit that phases down by 2032, cannot be foreseen ten years out, and the filer itself says it cannot predict adoption. The file closes here. Q2 to Q12: NOT REACHED.
