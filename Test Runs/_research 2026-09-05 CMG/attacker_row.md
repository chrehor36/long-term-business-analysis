## SYNTHESIS — the answer the row gives

**Is CAVA (and SG) taking the marginal fast-casual customer while CMG's traffic
declines?** CAVA: yes, on filed evidence — +10.4%, +8.7%, +1.6% annual traffic FY2023-25
and re-accelerating to +6.1% traffic YTD FY2026, with AUV climbing toward CMG's ($3.09M
trailing vs CMG ~$3.2M) at 1/8th the unit count and restaurant-level margins already
inside CMG's band (25.7% Q2 FY2026 vs CMG ~25-27%). SG: no — its traffic decline is
worse than CMG's, and it is impairing stores. WING: unit machine intact but demand
cracked (-3.3% FY2025, -8.1% YTD FY2026, AUV falling) — the soft fast-casual consumer is
real and industry-wide in 2025, which makes CAVA's 2026 traffic re-acceleration the
standout fact: the one brand growing traffic in a traffic-starved category, directly in
CMG's demographic.

**Trajectory-at-same-store-count comparison** (CMG milestones per operator's frame; CMG
figures to be verified in the defender row): CAVA at 439-476 units already posts
$2.9-3.1M AUV and ~24-26% restaurant-level margins — numbers CMG only reached at
multiples of that unit count and two decades of pricing later. CAVA's own filings put
its 2025 new-unit class "trending above $3.0 million in AUV" (10-K line 782), i.e., new
CAVA units open at roughly CMG's system AUV. The mitigants, from CAVA's own filings: its
traffic went ~flat in H2 2025 (Q3 FY2025: "approximately flat"), its operating margin is
still thin (4.7% vs CMG's corporate margin several times that), and its base is 1/8th
CMG's — the attacker is small, but on every filed unit-economics measure it is the real
one.
