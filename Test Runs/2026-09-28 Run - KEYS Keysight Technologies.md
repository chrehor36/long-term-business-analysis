## Q1 — CAN I UNDERSTAND HOW THIS MAKES MONEY? **[E3-31]**
- Unit economics in my own words, no management language: **Keysight sells engineers the instruments and software they use to prove that an electronic design works before it is built in volume, and then services those instruments.** A customer designing a 5G handset chip, a satellite radio, an optical transceiver for a data centre, a radar or an electric-vehicle power stage buys oscilloscopes, signal and network analyzers, signal generators, power supplies and protocol analyzers (hardware sold once, priced by performance: bandwidth, frequency range, noise floor), measurement applications and design and simulation software that run on or beside them (sold as licences or subscriptions), and network-emulation systems that load a network the way real traffic would. **Revenue FY2025 $5,375M: products $4,063M (76%), services and other $1,312M (24%)**; by timing, $4,337M recognised at a point in time and $1,038M over time (the repair and calibration contracts, extended warranties, support and subscriptions, Note 3). **Two segments** (Note 16): the **Communications Solutions Group** (CSG, $3,726M, 69%: commercial communications $2,488M and aerospace, defense and government $1,238M; segment operating income $986M, 26.5%) and the **Electronic Industrial Solutions Group** (EISG, $1,649M, 31%: automotive and energy, semiconductor, general electronics, and ESI Group's simulation software; $407M, 24.7%). About 40,000 end customers a year, none above 10% of revenue; more than 80% of sales through its own direct sales force. The costs are mainly people: R&D $1,007M (18.7% of revenue), selling and administration $1,474M (27.4%), and cost of sales $2,038M (37.9%); manufacturing is final assembly and calibration in Penang, Malaysia, with the high-frequency chips, probes and modules made in its own technology centres in California, Colorado and Germany. Most of the money is made on the gross margin of hardware and software sold to R&D budgets (62.1% consolidated in FY2025), less a heavy fixed R&D and sales cost, so operating margin swings with volume (24.9% in FY2023 to 16.3% in FY2025 on revenue 1.6% lower).
- **The brief's Q1 prior checked against the segment note: right, and incomplete in three places.** It omits (1) the network-emulation and network-visibility systems that came with Ixia and Spirent (a large part of CSG's commercial communications revenue), (2) the computer-aided engineering and simulation software that came with ESI Group and OSG inside EISG, and (3) that the recurring part is small and defined by the filer: over-time revenue is $1,038M (19%), and the remaining performance obligations beyond one year were *"approximately $ 629 million as of October 31, 2025"*; hardware sold at a point in time is most of the business.
- The scarce input this business controls: **measurement know-how and proprietary components built up since the Hewlett-Packard instrument business began (the filer: *"We trace our heritage back over 80 years"*), made in its own fab and technology centres (*"microwave monolithic integrated circuits, thick- and thin-film circuits, optical components, high-speed probes ... These capabilities provide a competitive advantage by delivering performance levels not commercially available"*), a large installed base of instruments that needs calibration and repair, and early access to customers' next-generation designs through standards bodies and R&D relationships.** Whether any of this makes the product one customers think has no close substitute is Q2's question, not Q1's.
- Will the fundamentals look broadly the same in ten years? **The way the money is made, yes; which products earn it, no, and the filer says so.** The FY2003 Agilent 10-K describes the same business (general-purpose electronic test, communications test, design software) against the same named competitors (Tektronix, Rohde & Schwarz, Anritsu, National Instruments, Spirent, Ixia); twenty-two years later Keysight sells to the same kinds of engineers the same kinds of measurement and has bought two of those competitors. What turns over is each product generation: *"Without the timely introduction of new solutions, services and enhancements, our solutions and services will become technologically obsolete over time"* (FY2025 10-K), which is why R&D runs at 17-19% of revenue. That bears on the durability of any moat (Q2, [E4-04]), not on the unit economics, which are a margin on measurement equipment and software sold to R&D budgets that rise and fall with the electronics cycle.
- **Simple in character, and a decision that can be made on the filings [E3-31, E4-46].** The electronics inside an instrument are specialist; the business question (how many engineers' budgets buy how many instruments at what margin, and does the margin hold when budgets fall) is not, and twenty-five years of filed segment data answer it. The complexity the filer describes (*"a highly competitive and rapidly changing global marketplace"*) is carried to Q2, where [E4-04]'s competence limit is the test for it, not closed here: [E4-46] closes Q1 on a business that would take months to understand, and this one is understood from its own and its peers' filings.

### THE STRONGEST EVIDENCE AGAINST THIS VERDICT, stated as its holders would state it [E4-51, E4-26, E3-47]
