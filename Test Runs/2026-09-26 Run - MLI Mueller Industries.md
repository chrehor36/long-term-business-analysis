## Q1 — CAN I UNDERSTAND HOW THIS MAKES MONEY? **[E3-31]**
- **Unit economics in my own words, no management language.** Mueller buys copper (refined cathode on short-term contracts and scrap on the open market), plus brass scrap, zinc and aluminium, melts and extrudes it, and draws it into copper tube for plumbing water lines and air-conditioning and refrigeration lines, copper fittings, line sets (pre-cut, insulated copper pairs that join an outdoor AC unit to the indoor coil), and brass rod that other manufacturers machine into valves and fittings. It sells to plumbing and HVAC wholesalers, building-material retailers and air-conditioner makers. **The selling price is the metal plus a charge for turning it into a product**: *"Net selling prices generally fluctuate with changes in raw material costs. Changes in raw material costs are generally passed through to customers by adjustments to selling prices"* (MD&A, FY2025 10-K). So the revenue line moves with the copper price and tells little; the money is the gap between the metal cost and the selling price, times the pounds shipped, less the cost of the mills. The registrant says so itself: *"Profitability of certain of our product lines depends upon the "spreads" between the cost of raw material and the selling prices of our products [...] Our earnings and cash flow are dependent upon these spreads that fluctuate based upon market conditions."* FY2025 by segment (Note 3): **Piping Systems** (tube, fittings, line sets; plus resold valves and plumbing specialties) $2,708.7M of sales, $772.3M of operating income (28.5%); **Industrial Metals** (brass rod, forgings, impact extrusions, and since 2024 Nehring's wire and cable) $1,023.6M, $105.0M (10.3%); **Climate** (refrigeration valves, HVAC components, flexible duct) $497.9M, $145.1M (29.1%). Consolidated: sales $4,178.5M, gross margin 29.0%, operating income $958.5M. No customer above 10% of sales; order backlog *"not significant"*; demand follows housing starts, commercial building, and repair and remodelling.
- **The scarce input this business controls.** **Copper-tube and brass-rod mill capacity inside North America**, in a market the registrant says is *"still predominantly supplied by U.S. manufacturers"*, with two named domestic tube rivals (Cerro Flow Products and Cambridge-Lee) and one named brass-rod rival (Wieland Chase), and the ability to run scrap as feedstock (*"Our copper tube facilities can accommodate both refined copper and certain grades of copper scrap"*). **Not** a patent, brand or licence: *"Our business is not materially dependent on patents, trademarks, licenses, franchises, or concessions held."* Whether a few North American mills are scarce enough to be a franchise is Q2's question, and the step in earnings since 2021 makes it the whole question.
- **Will the fundamentals look broadly the same in ten years?** **The mechanism, yes; the level, not on the filings.** The FY2007 and FY2025 10-Ks describe the same products, customers, plants (Fulton, Mississippi; Wynne, Arkansas; Port Huron, Michigan) and pass-through pricing, and the same two substitution threats: *"For plumbing systems, plastics are the primary substitute product; these products represent an increasing share of consumption. For certain air-conditioning and refrigeration applications, aluminum-based systems are the primary substitution threat. We cannot predict the acceptance or the rate of switching that may occur."* What has not been stable is the size of the spread: operating income ran $126-245M a year in FY2008-FY2020 and $656-959M in FY2021-FY2025. Understanding how the money is made does not require predicting that level; Q2 and Q4 do.
- **Relatively simple and stable in character [E3-31]?** Simple: a metal fabricator paid a conversion charge over the metal it passes through. Stable in its mechanism, cyclical in its level, with no technology the business turns on. **Not [E4-46]'s months-of-study case.**
- **VERDICT: [x] IN**: the money is made visibly, as a conversion spread over passed-through copper and brass, times pounds, earned in a North American market served mainly by North American mills. **IN is not a finding on the franchise, and not a finding on which level of earnings is the real one.**
