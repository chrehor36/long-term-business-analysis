## Q1 — CAN I UNDERSTAND IT? Where its economics will be in ten years, inside my perimeter. STOP.
**Lines of evidence chosen from the FY2025 10-K** (Item 1 business and segments, Item 1A risks, Item 7 MD&A), stated as the brief's second departure requires: (1) the Gas Utility's regulated economics, rate base, allowed returns and the rate mechanisms (ISRS, RSE, RSA, and from 2026 Tennessee's ARM), Items 1 and 7 and Note 15; (2) customer and volume trends and the competition the 10-K names (electric companies, propane, bypass, renewables), Items 1 and 1A; (3) the capital programme and its funding (capital expenditures planned at $809M for FY2026; debt, the ATM programme, the equity units), Item 7 Liquidity; (4) the portfolio changes: the Piedmont Tennessee purchase ($2.48 billion), the sales of Spire Marketing and Spire Storage and the planned sale of Spire Mississippi, Item 7 Pending Acquisition and the FY2026 10-Qs; (5) the non-utility segments, Gas Marketing and Midstream, their commodity and storage-spread exposure, Item 1A; (6) regulatory and legislative risk on gas use, Item 1A.

- **The test applied.** Understanding means "a reasonable fix on about what the earning power and competitive position will look like in five or 10 years" **[M2012-065]**; the product may stay opaque if "I understand the economic dynamics of the industry" **[M2011-014]**. A gas distribution utility's economics are written into its rate orders: earnings are an allowed return on a rate base that grows with the capital the regulator approves. The key variables **[M1998-044]** are four: the rate base (plant in service; Spire Missouri's rate case is "based on a total rate base plant in service of $ 4,379.6", Note 15), the allowed return and the lag in getting it, customer numbers and weather-normalized usage, and the share of the capital programme funded by equity. Each is stated in the filings and each moves slowly: weather is cushioned by riders ("Spire Missouri and Spire Mississippi each have a Weather Normalization Adjustment rider, Spire Alabama has a Temperature Adjustment Rider", Item 1A), and gas cost is passed through ("allow the Utilities to flow through to customers ... the cost of purchased gas supplies", Item 1). The past statements tell the future ones, in the sense of Q1's third test **[M2008-033]**: the FY2021 to FY2025 statements show a rate base growing on spending, earnings that follow it with a regulatory lag, and cash swings that are gas-cost timing.
- **Is it important and knowable** **[M2006-076]**: the deciding facts (allowed returns, rate base, the capital programme, the regulators' recent orders) are knowable from filings. The one forecast the insiders would hesitate to write is gas demand twenty years out under climate policy; it is a Q2 threat, not a fog over the next ten years' economics, because the 10-K's own reading is that "any such resulting impacts would occur over a long period of time" (Item 1A).
- **Routing.** Not a fast-changing industry. Not a bank. **A business of parts** (Q1's holding-company paragraph, through Q2's threshold): the Gas Utility segment earned 237.2, 198.6, 200.5, 217.0 and 231.4 of GAAP net income in FY2021 to FY2025 (segment reconciliations, 10-Ks FY2022, FY2023 and FY2025), against consolidated net income of 271.7, 220.8, 217.5, 250.9 and 271.7: 1,084.7 of 1,232.6, or 88%. Gas Marketing (a commodity marketing book, 5-year GAAP net income 44.8 + 35.6 + 39.1 + 32.7 + 33.7) and Midstream do not decide; Marketing and Spire Storage were sold in 2026 (10-Q `0001193125-26-334335`, Note 3). The parts that matter can be understood; doubt about the sold commodity book does not reach the whole **[M2002-092]**.

## Q2 — WHY IS THE CASTLE STILL STANDING, and what keeps it standing ten to twenty years? STOP.
The question: "why is that castle still standing? And what’s going to keep it standing or cause it not to be standing five, 10, 20 years from now" **[M1995-038]**. Read on the deciding part, the Gas Utility (the parts table below).

- **The attacker with money** **[M2011-015]**. "Spire Missouri is the only distributor of natural gas within its franchised service areas, while Spire Alabama is the main distributor of natural gas in its service areas" (10-K FY2025, Item 1). A rival with a hundred million or ten billion dollars cannot lay a second set of mains under St. Louis or Birmingham: the franchise is a municipal permit and the rates are set by the commission. The attack the 10-K names is not a second gas network but substitution and bypass: "The principal competition for the Utilities comes from the local electric companies", with propane, fuel oil, district steam in downtown St. Louis and Kansas City, municipal gas systems beside the Alabama territories, and large customers who "bypass gas distribution services by directly connecting with interstate pipelines" (Items 1 and 1A). The castle holds against the money test; the threat is the substitute.
- **Pricing power and the agony before a rise** **[M2005-020]**. The price is set by regulators in rate cases, and the agony is institutional: Spire Missouri's case GR-2025-0107 settled for a $210.0M base increase effective 2025-10-24 (Note 15); Spire Alabama's allowed range was cut to 9.50%-9.90% from 10.15%-10.65% in 2022, its rate year 2025 budget raised the return "offset by cost reductions due to customer affordability efforts", and its October 2025 filing projects 8.24%, below the range (Note 15). The power is the regulator's allowance, not the business's own; the rows read a return set by a regulator as fair, not rich: "there’s no way we get rich on our utility investments. But there’s no way we get poor, either" **[M2009-104]**.
- **Unit volume and share of mind** **[M1999-054]**. Total system volumes 3,107.8 / 2,960.4 / 3,113.9 million CCF (FY2023 to FY2025) and average customers 1,735,633 / 1,740,928 / 1,744,537 (Item 1): flat volume, customer growth of about a quarter of a percent a year. No decline; no growth.

For heat in a cold-winter territory gas is the low-cost delivered energy on the 10-K's own reading: "the Utilities believe that the relative comparison of natural gas equipment and operating costs with those of competitive fuels will not change significantly in the foreseeable future" (Item 1).
