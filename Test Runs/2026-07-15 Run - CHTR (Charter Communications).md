## STEP 0 — RATE REFRESH
US 30-yr **5.10%** (unchanged, Jul 13–15 checks this week). No size premium —

## GATE 1 — CIRCLE OF COMPETENCE
Broadband/cable connectivity company (Spectrum brand): sells Internet, mobile,
video and voice, primarily bundled, to 58M passings across 41 states. Unit
economics are simple in substance: monthly recurring revenue per customer
relationship × customer count − network operating costs − content/programming
costs − capital spend to build and maintain the network. Effectively one
reportable business (residential connectivity is ~85%+ of revenue; small
business, mid-market/large business and advertising are adjacent, evaluable
extensions of the same network asset, not separate circles of competence).
**Complication flagged, not disqualifying:** Charter has two large pending
transactions — the Cox Communications transactions (~$4.0B cash + assumption
of ~$12.6B of Cox net debt/finance leases) and the Liberty Broadband
Combination (all-stock, plus assumption of ~$1.8B of Liberty Broadband debt)
— both still pending as of the FY2025 10-K. The pro forma entity is
same-industry and understandable in kind, so this does not fail Gate 1, but
it means every financial figure in this run describes the company **before**
these deals close, not the entity an investor would actually own in 12
months. Carried forward as a live risk into Gates 4 and 5.
**VERDICT: PASS**

## GATE 2 — MOAT
Franchise test [E3-03]: needed/desired — yes, broadband is close to essential
infrastructure; no close substitute — **fails on its face**: the 10-K's own
Competition section (Item 1) discloses fiber-to-the-home overbuild from AT&T
(27% of footprint) and Verizon (16% of footprint), fixed-wireless 5G
substitution from all three national mobile carriers, DBS, and virtual MVPD
video substitutes — "intense competition" language appears repeatedly and is
not boilerplate given the customer trend below; unregulated — fails, cable
franchises are local-government-granted, non-exclusive, and subject to
extensive FCC/state regulation. What Charter actually has is a **structural
cost-of-replication barrier** (duplicate last-mile wireline overbuild is
rarely economic), which is real but narrow, not a pricing-power franchise.

Primary metric (filing-sourced, FY2025 vs FY2024, Item 1 customer table):
total customer relationships 31,846K vs 32,214K (**−1.1%**); video customers
12,605K vs 12,892K (**−2.2%**, following a longer, well-known decline —
video revenue itself fell **−9.4%** in 2025 per the MD&A revenue-by-service
table); total revenue $54,774M vs $55,085M (**−0.6%**). Internet and mobile
are still growing (Internet revenue +1.7%, Mobile service revenue +22.0%)
and are the genuine offsets keeping the top line roughly flat, but the
headline picture is a mature/eroding legacy product (video, voice) being
substituted faster than the connectivity products are growing share.
**Class: NARROW** (infrastructure cost-of-replication, not a pricing-power
franchise). **Direction: NARROWING** — this is not a single soft year; it is
recorded, filing-sourced, multi-metric erosion (customers, video revenue,
total revenue) against explicitly disclosed structural substitution
(FTTH overbuild, 5G FWA, streaming).
**VERDICT: PASS with NARROW/NARROWING carried into Gates 5 and 6 as a live,
not hypothetical, risk** — consistent with how AVB's regulatory exposure was
handled: a real caveat carried forward, not grounds by itself to fail Gate 2.

## GATE 3 — MANAGEMENT
CEO Christopher L. Winfrey (President & CEO); Non-Executive Chairman Craig
Zinterhofer since the 2024 governance change (clean separation of Chair/CEO).
2025 salary $1,823,846 (Summary Compensation Table, 2026 DEF 14A) — full
equity award detail not itemized in this pass, but the proxy's own Pay vs.
Performance section is unusually candid: "Compensation Actually Paid"
relative to Summary Compensation Table Total came in at **−7.1x for the CEO
in 2025** (worse than −4.3x in 2024) because Charter's stock price declined
sharply while net income still grew (+... modest range disclosed) — i.e.,
the pay program is genuinely linked to the (declining) stock, not insulated
from it. That is a positive alignment signal on its own terms.
**Governance complexity, disclosed and policy-governed, not an integrity
flag:** Liberty Broadband (~29.22% of voting stock) and Advance/Newhouse
Partnership ("A/N," ~13.12% of voting stock) each hold contractual board
designation rights (3 and 2 directors respectively, out of 13), preemptive
rights, and standing share-repurchase participation agreements under a 2015
Stockholders Agreement. Related-party transactions (tax distributions to
A/N of ~$130M in 2025, programming relationships tied to a former director's
other board seat) are disclosed and routed through the Audit Committee under
a written policy. This is real influence by two large, informed insiders —
not hidden, not a candor failure — but it means minority-shareholder
interests are structurally counterbalanced by two parties who are also
transaction counterparties in the pending Cox and Liberty Broadband deals.
No litigation, restatement, or accounting-integrity flag found this pass.
**VERDICT: PASS**, with the A/N/Liberty Broadband dual-insider governance
structure flagged as a real, ongoing complexity rather than resolved away.

## GATE 4 — FINANCIAL QUALITY (full formula, P44 convention)
Cap-intensive business (network infrastructure) → P44 band 65–70% of total
capex as maintenance capex. Used **65%** (low end of the band): the 10-K
explicitly identifies a meaningful growth component within total capex —
the "subsidized rural construction initiative" ($7.7B cumulative since
2022, expansion-only spend) and the DOCSIS 4.0/network-evolution upgrade
program (expected substantially complete by end of 2027) — so a smaller
share of total capex than the band's high end is treated as pure upkeep.
SBC check: $673M (2025) vs. OE below — **11.0%, under the 15% P44
threshold, no adjusted-OE deduction required.**

| FY | NI (attrib. to Charter) | D&A | Total capex (XBRL) | Maint capex (65%) | **OE = NI+D&A−maint capex** |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2021 | $4,654M | $9,345M | $7,635M | $4,962.8M | **$9,036.3M** |
| 2022 | $5,055M | $8,903M | $9,376M | $6,094.4M | **$7,863.6M** |
| 2023 | $4,557M | $8,696M | $11,115M | $7,224.8M | **$6,028.3M** |
| 2024 | $5,083M | $8,673M | $11,269M | $7,324.9M | **$6,431.2M** |
| 2025 | $4,987M | $8,711M | $11,659M | $7,578.4M | **$6,119.7M** |

ΔWC not separately extracted this pass (subscription-billing working capital
is typically modest relative to these figures for a cable operator, but this
is an assumption, not a verified figure — flagged as a gap, per AVB's
precedent of flagging rather than estimating).
Note the **trend**: OE fell every year from 2021 to 2023 (−33%) as capex
intensity rose (capex nearly doubled, 2021→2025, while D&A was roughly
flat) — the network-upgrade and rural-expansion investment cycle has been
consuming a rising share of the cash the business throws off, even as
NI/D&A held up. This is the balance-sheet-quality echo of the Gate 2
NARROWING finding, not a separate issue.
**LOWEST VERIFIED TIER (would anchor Statute if reached): $6,028.3M
(FY2023).**

**Fortress check — two tracks (Ruling 2):** non-financial business →
SURVIVAL TRACK.
- **Credit rating: sub-investment-grade.** S&P BB+ (issuer), Moody's Ba2
  (corporate family; senior secured notes one notch better at Ba1) — this is
  high-yield, not investment-grade, credit.
- **Leverage: 4.15x** consolidated net-debt-to-Adjusted-EBITDA as of
  12/31/2025 (10-K, Risk Factors — "Risks Related to Our Indebtedness"),
  which the company states is within its own 4.0–4.5x target range, i.e.
  Charter is running leverage it considers normal but which sits at the
  high-yield end of the ratings spectrum by design, not by accident.
- **Total debt $94,617M principal** (Item 7/Note 9 debt table) against
  **$4.4B available under credit facilities** — undrawn liquidity is
  **~4.7% of total debt outstanding**, a thin cushion in absolute terms even
  though the maturity ladder itself is reasonably termed-out (issues dated
  out to 2055; the company proactively refinanced 2025/2026 maturities in
  Sept. 2025 and Jan. 2026 rather than letting them cliff).
- **Two simultaneous, debt-increasing acquisitions pending, not yet closed:**
  Cox Transactions add ~$12.6B of assumed net debt plus $4.0B of new
  cash-funded debt; Liberty Broadband Combination adds ~$1.8B of assumed
  debt. Pro forma total debt approaches ~$113B. The 10-K's own risk factor
  language flags that a **ratings downgrade below investment grade on the
  Cox notes would trigger a mandatory 101% change-of-control repurchase
  offer** — i.e., the company itself identifies a credit-downgrade scenario
  as a cash-drain trigger, not a remote hypothetical.
- **Stress test:** current net interest expense is already **$5,042M/year**
  (2025 income statement). A 50% OE decline from the lowest verified tier
  ($6,028M → ~$3,014M) would not cover net interest expense alone, let
  alone maintenance capex and scheduled amortization, without drawing
  heavily on a revolver that covers under 5% of the existing debt stock —
  during a period when, per the ratings-trigger language above, a stressed
  Charter would likely be facing tightening (not loosening) credit access.
  This is not a remote, theoretical tail scenario given where leverage
  already sits today, pre-Cox-closing.
**Judgment call (this is a real one, not mechanical):** ordinary
investment-grade corporate leverage is explicitly NOT a Gate 4 disqualifier
under Ruling 2 — but Charter's leverage is not investment-grade, sits at the
top of the company's own target range, is about to increase further via two
concurrent acquisitions, and is layered on a moat already scored NARROWING
in Gate 2. The one mitigating factor (a termed-out, actively-managed
maturity ladder) does not outweigh the combination of sub-investment-grade
rating + thin undrawn-liquidity coverage + imminent leverage increase +
eroding underlying franchise. This does not read as a business that
survives a sustained 50% OE decline without existential risk — it reads as
a business already priced and structured for a scenario where the growth
engine (Internet/mobile) keeps outrunning the legacy decline (video/voice),
which is precisely the assumption a 50%/2-year stress test is designed to
question.
**VERDICT: FAIL — STOP.**
