## Q1 — CAN I UNDERSTAND IT? Where its economics will be in ten years, inside my perimeter. STOP.
**The lines of evidence this run chose, from the FY2025 10-K's Items 1, 1A and 7 and the filings since** (the brief's departure 1): (1) what the business sells and why the customer must buy it (Item 1: aftermarket parts distribution and component and engine-accessory MRO; the FAA rule that aircraft be "continuously maintained"); (2) the OEM distribution agreements and the inventory committed to them (Item 7's "inventory purchases to support new distribution programs"; the 2021 provision); (3) competition and price (Item 1, Item 1A); (4) customer concentration (Item 1A, the 20% customer group); (5) the acquisition strategy and its funding (Item 1A, "A key element of the Company's business strategy is growth through the acquisition of additional companies"; the offerings; PAG); (6) the cash the segment has turned its profit into (Item 7 cash flows, the H1 2026 10-Q); (7) the debt taken for PAG (8-K of 2026-05-07). Rivals: see Q2.

- **What is to be understood.** Since FY2024 VSE has been a single reportable segment, Aviation, "a leading provider of aftermarket parts distribution and MRO services for components and engine accessories supporting commercial, business and general aviation" (FY2025 10-K, Item 1). It earns in two ways: it distributes parts made by others, largely under agreements with OEMs (2025 products revenue $703.9M), and it repairs components and engine accessories in certificated shops (services revenue $408.4M). PAG, closed 2026-05-05, adds the same two activities for business and general aviation, rotorcraft and defense; in Q2 2026 repair revenue rose 149.4% and distribution 17.2% on PAG's arrival (earnings release, `0000102752-26-000055`). The customer's need is set by regulation: the FAA's rules "are designed to ensure that all aircraft and aviation equipment are continuously maintained in proper condition", and repairs "can be performed only at certified repair facilities utilizing certified technicians" (Item 1).
  The product (a pump, a starter, a wheel and brake overhaul) need not be understood technically if the economics are **[M2011-014]**.
- **The key variables and how predictable they are** **[M1998-044]**: (a) the flying done by the installed fleet, which sets how many parts wear out and how many components come in for repair; it is cyclical (the Aviation segment's revenue fell from $224.5M in 2019 to $165.1M in 2020, FY2020 10-K) but the ten-year direction of an installed, regulated fleet can be pictured; (b) whether VSE keeps and adds the OEM programs it distributes and the repair approvals it holds; this is the company's place in the industry, which is Q2's question; (c) how much capital the inventory for those programs ties up, which the filings record year by year (Q3, Q4). Test 7 of Q1: the forecast is about fleet use and regulation, not about fast-moving technology **[M2017-019]**; the 10-K's paragraph on AI and new parts is a general risk, not a description of a changing product.
- **Do the past statements tell me the future ones?** **[M2008-033]** In part. The statements of the business as a whole do not, because the business was rebuilt by purchase every year from 2021 to 2026 and no year of the window describes it as it now stands (Step 0). But the two activities have their own records: the Aviation segment's revenue and operating income from FY2018 to FY2025, through the 2020 trough, and PAG's audited 2024 and 2025 statements. Those are enough to judge "where the company will stand within the industry" **[M2012-065]** at the next question.
- **The doubt rule** **[M2002-092]**. The doubt this run holds is about the company's position and its cash (Q2, Q4), not about what the business does or what drives it. Seeing the industry is not seeing the winner **[M2012-067]**; that is the question Q2 owns, and Q1 does not close on it.
- **Routing.** Not a bank or lender; not a holding company of unlike parts (one reportable segment, two revenue lines in one industry); not a business of rapid technological change.
- **VERDICT: IN.** The economics of a regulated aftermarket for parts and repairs can be pictured ten years out **[M2012-065]**, **[M2000-104]**; the company's place in it passes to Q2.

## Q2 — WHY IS THE CASTLE STILL STANDING, and what keeps it standing ten to twenty years? STOP.
The question is the 1995 one: "why is that castle still standing?", "What are the key factors? And how permanent are they?" **[M1995-038]**.

**The castle VSE claims.** Management's words: an "OEM-centric strategy", and organic growth from "new business wins, execution on new distribution agreements, expanded product and repair capabilities, market share gains and increased share of wallet on existing programs" (Q2 2026 release, `0000102752-26-000055`). The key factors are therefore (i) agreements under which OEMs let VSE distribute their parts, (ii) FAA-certificated repair capabilities, (iii) inventory on the shelf when a grounded aircraft needs a part. The 10-K itself states the competitive factors as "customer knowledge, product availability, technical and financial qualifications, past performance, repair turnaround time, government budgetary priorities, sales force initiatives and price" (Item 1).

**The tests, each with its filing fact.**
1. **How permanent are the key factors?** **[M1995-038]** The distribution agreements are the OEMs' to grant and to move. The 10-K warns that "OEMs, aircraft maintenance providers, leasing companies and U.S. FAA certificated repair facilities may attempt to bundle their services and product offerings in the supply industry" and that "Some of the Company's original equipment manufacturer ("OEM") competitors have greater name recognition" (Item 1A): the party that owns the part is also a competitor. The 2021 provision of $24.4M on inventory for "international region distribution programs entered into prior to 2019" shows a program going wrong and the distributor carrying the stock (FY2021 10-K).
2. **The money test** **[M2011-015]**, **[M2000-077]**. Could a well-funded attacker build this? The filings answer yes, by example. VSE built its present Aviation business with money in five years: about $896M of cash for acquisitions in FY2023 to FY2025 (cash-flow statements, FY2025 10-K: $218.6M, $283.1M, $394.1M) and $1,766.8M more in H1 2026 (10-Q), funded by five public share offerings. The sellers were themselves builders with money: PAG and Aero 3 were portfolio companies of GenNx360 Capital Partners (FY2025 10-K, Item 7; the Aero 3 parent was GenNx/AeroRepair IntermediateCo Inc.), and PAG had itself spent $476.4M on acquisitions in 2024 and $88.5M in 2025 (PAG's audited cash-flow statements, Exhibit 99.2). A field in which a private-equity sponsor can assemble a platform and sell it on, and a buyer can assemble a larger one in five years, is a field where "a dozen people want to go into it" **[M2000-077]**; "If the answer had been yes, we wouldn’t have done it." **[M2011-015]**. The speakers' own phrase for how they treat a barrier runs the other way: "We sort of buy barriers; we don’t build them." **[M2012-105]**
3. **The low bid** **[M2017-009]**. The 10-K lists "price" among the principal competitive factors and calls the parts industry "highly fragmented" with "intense competition" (Item 1, Item 1A). The customer for a distributed OEM part buys the OEM's part; what VSE adds is having it and shipping it. No filing read shows customers paying VSE more than a rival for the same part.
4. **Pricing power and the agony before a rise** **[M2005-020]**. The FY2025 10-K says inflation was "mitigated with improved efficiency in the Company's underlying business through productivity improvements and pass-through price increases" (Item 7) and, in the same filing, that "the Company may be unable to raise prices enough to keep up with the rate of inflation" (Item 1A). Neither shows power over price; the second is the agony in the filer's own words.
5. **One customer group, 20% of revenue** (Item 1A). The rows' warning that "one competitor is frequently enough to ruin a business" **[M2012-108]** has its mirror here: one customer group leaving would remove a fifth of the revenue.
6. **Maintained or rebuilt?** **[L2007-005]** Organic growth is reported as the product of "new business wins" and "new distribution agreements" each year (releases of 2025-02-27, 2026-02-26 and 2026-08-05).
   A lead won program by program, from OEMs who can give the next program to a rival, is the moat "that must be continuously rebuilt" **[L2007-005]**.
7. **Ask the competitors** **[M1999-130]**: no instance found on the public record read (the filings of VSE and of the three rivals below name no rival).
8. **Widening or narrowing** **[M1999-108]**: the segment margin widened from 8.9% (2022) to about 13% (2023, 2024); the full-cycle record below says what that widening is worth.

**The competitor row.** The FY2025 10-K names no competitor (Item 1 and Item 1A read in full; the FY2019 to FY2024 10-Ks searched for named rivals: no instance found). **CONVENTION of this run:** with no rival named, the three SEC filers whose businesses are closest to VSE's two activities were chosen, AAR Corp (parts supply and MRO), AerSale Corporation (used serviceable material and MRO) and HEICO Corporation (aftermarket replacement parts and repair, the PMA maker); rationale: the brief asks for up to three rivals from their own filings and the 10-K supplies none, so the choice is the analyst's and could be argued.
