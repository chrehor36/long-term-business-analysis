## Q2 — IS IT A FRANCHISE? **[E3-03]**

*Every GPC figure below is read from the filed 10-Ks for 2006 to 2025 (Items 1 and 1A, the MD&A, the segment notes) and the 10-Q to 2026-06-30, or from SEC XBRL companyfacts checked against the filed statements (Step 0). Extractions in `Test Runs/_research 2026-09-26 GPC/`: `seg.py`, `segtable.py` (`segtable_out.txt`), and the peer script `peers/row.py` (`peers/row_out.txt`, `peers/row_summary.txt`).*

**The hypothesis I formed while reading, stated so it can be attacked [E4-26]:** *GPC is a large, old, well-run distributor in two fragmented trades whose customers can buy the same part from several other distributors. It earns a thin margin (6-9% before corporate cost for twenty years) and turns it into a respectable return only because its suppliers finance its inventory. Inside its own competitor row it is the weaker operator in automotive (ORLY and AZO earn about three times its margin on the same parts to the same repair shops) and an ordinary one in industrial (Motion earns what Applied Industrial earns). If that is right, it is [E3-43]'s "a business", not a franchise.* The evidence against, above all the long record of steady returns and the NAPA name, is hunted and answered before the verdict.

**[E3-03], criterion by criterion.** *"An economic franchise arises from a product or service that: (1) is needed or desired; (2) is thought by its customers to have no close substitute and; (3) is not subject to price regulation."*

- **(1) Needed or desired [x]: passes.** A car off the road or a plant line down needs the part now; the filer: *"Most orders are fulfilled quickly from well-stocked inventories, reducing vehicle downtime for our end customers"*; Motion's products are *"designed for direct use by the customer in plant and facility maintenance, ensuring they are able to keep their operations running with limited disruption"*.
- **(3) Not subject to price regulation [x]: passes.** Prices are set by GPC and its rivals; the 1954 consent decree forbids collusive practices among NAPA members, it does not set prices.
- **(2) Thought by its customers to have no close substitute [ ]: FAILS in the registrant's own words, and the failure is stronger than the generic "highly competitive" that every retailer writes.**
  - *Both trades, Item 1:* *"Our Global Automotive and Industrial businesses operate in highly competitive markets, with a wide range of national, regional and local businesses."* The filer names the substitutes itself: *"Key competitors in North America include AutoZone, Inc., O'Reilly Auto Parts, Inc., Advance Auto Parts, Inc., and LKQ Corporation"*, plus *"automobile manufacturers ... automobile dealers, warehouse clubs, mass merchandisers and online retailers"*; *"In industrial distribution, our main competitor is Applied Industrial Technologies, Inc., but we also compete against Fastenal Company, and W.W. Grainger, Inc"*.
  - *Price is named as a basis of the advantage, and the filer says it follows its competitors' prices down.* 2025 Item 1: *"Our competitive advantages are based on broad product availability, quality service, strong brand recognition, and competitive pricing."* Item 1A, the same sentence in every 10-K from 2022 to 2025: *"Because we seek to offer competitive prices, we may be forced to reduce our prices if our competitors reduce their prices or increase promotional spending, which could result in a material decline in our revenues and earnings."* And: *"We anticipate no decline in competition in any of our business segments in the foreseeable future."* Industrial, 2022-2024 10-Ks: *"The industrial distribution business is highly competitive and fragmented. ... We compete primarily on the breadth of product offerings, quality service and competitive pricing."* O'Reilly's 2025 10-K, read for contrast, says its trade is *"highly competitive in many areas, including customer service, product availability, store location, brand recognition, and price"*, but does not name competitive pricing as its own advantage or say it must follow rivals' prices; **the textual failure here is GPC's own, not the industry's boilerplate**, and the verdict does not rest on it alone (the demonstration below decides).
  - *The price the filer describes is inflation passed through, not price set.* 2022: *"We deployed strategic pricing increases throughout 2022 to offset the dynamic product cost increases we faced"*; 2023, U.S. Automotive sales *"negatively impacted by moderating inflation levels, which adversely impacted the pricing environment year-over-year, and higher interest rates, which reduced sales to our independent owners"*; 2025, *"We estimate that comparable sales benefited from approximately 2.0% of price inflation, including tariff related impacts."* When supplier costs stop rising, GPC's price stops rising: [E2-44]'s first characteristic (*"an ability to increase prices rather easily (even when product demand is flat and capacity is not fully utilized)"*) is the thing the 2023 10-K reports it did not have.

**Checklist:** Needed or desired [x] · no close substitute [ ] **fails** (both segments, in the filer's own words; decided by the demonstration below) · not price-regulated [x]

Net income on average equity 17.4-32.5% (2011-2024). **The inventory is paid for by the suppliers**: trade payables equal inventory ($6,279.9M against $6,287.9M at 2026-06-30), and $3,171.5M of those payables are *"Confirmed obligations"* under a bank supply-chain finance programme; with those counted as the debt they functionally are (the 2023 10-K's $3,034.1M at year end), the 2023 return falls from 36% to about 22%.

- **Peers named: eight**, every competitor the 10-K names by name (AZO, ORLY, AAP and LKQ in automotive; Applied, Fastenal and Grainger in industrial) plus MSC Industrial; Bapcor (Australia, the named Australasian rival) is not an SEC filer, and the European rivals (LKQ Europe aside) are private or unsegmented. **The row is sufficient to place GPC and the verdict does not rest on the missing ones**, so the class is not held PROVISIONAL (the KEX, OSK and HII precedent).
- **Segment against segment.** GPC's *segment* figures sit in the same places: North America Automotive EBITDA margin **7.1%** in 2025 against O'Reilly's and AutoZone's EBIT margins of 19-20% (EBIT, not EBITDA, so the gap is understated); Industrial EBITDA margin 12.6-12.9% (2024-2025) against Applied's EBIT margin of 11.4-11.6%, the filer's own *"main competitor"*, doing the same thing at the same margin; Fastenal earns 20%.
- **Relative position is moving against GPC in automotive.** O'Reilly's 10-K for 2025: *"Comparable store sales increased 4.7% and 2.9% for the year ended December 31, 2025 and 2024, respectively."* GPC: *"flat Automotive comparable sales"* in 2024; in 2025 North America Automotive sales rose 3.3%, *"primarily driven by a 2.6% contribution from acquisitions"*, so about 0.7% came from comparable sales, with about 2% of price inflation in the company's comparable sales. The same repair shops, the same parts, and share moving to the higher-margin rival.
- **What the row shows.** In automotive GPC sits with LKQ and above the collapsed Advance, **at a third of the margin and roughly half to a third of the return on tangible operating assets of the two leaders**; in industrial it earns what Applied earns and below Fastenal and Grainger. Nowhere in the row is it the operator that sets the price.
- **What the row cannot show [E3-61]**: *"I think you’d have to know the people involved to fully understand what was happening."*

**The remaining Q2 tests.**
- **[E2-44], the two characteristics.** (1) Fails (the 2023 10-K, above). (2) Fails: dollar growth has come with capital, much of it bought: acquisitions of $1.6bn (2022), $306.9M (2023), $1,080.2M (2024), $318.3M (2025), and before that Alliance Automotive Group (2017) and many others; capital expenditure rose from $102.0M (2012) to $469.8-567.3M (2023-2025).
- **[E3-33] / [E5-28], untapped pricing power.** None: the class requires *"a monopoly or a near monopoly"*; GPC names seven substantial competitors and a market that is *"large and fragmented"*.
- **[E4-37], the agony metric.** The filer's own sentence is the answer: *"we may be forced to reduce our prices if our competitors reduce their prices"*.
- **[E2-53], the dominance test.** Not dominant anywhere it reports; the North American leaders by margin and growth are AZO and ORLY.
- **[E2-45], the attacker's test.** The attack is being run now, from inside the row: O'Reilly's 10-K for 2025 attributes its comparable-store growth partly to *"an increase in transaction counts for professional service provider customers"*, NAPA's core (DIFM) customer, in the same two years GPC's automotive comparable sales were flat to barely positive; GPC's answer in 2024-2025 was to buy its own independent owners' stores (*"more than 500 stores in the U.S., mostly from our independent owners"*), buying margin it used to share rather than widening a moat.
- **[E4-32], direction.** Down in automotive (margin 9.1% in 2015 to 6.2% in 2025; North America EBITDA down three years; comparable sales behind O'Reilly's); up in industrial (8% to 12%) on the KDG purchase and the inflation years, with comparable sales down 2.1% in 2024.
- **[E4-55], units.** GPC publishes no unit series (no parts, tickets or transactions); O'Reilly's run of 2026-09-02 recorded that GPC *"split neither"* ticket nor transactions, which this run confirms in the 2025 10-K (no such figure found). Store counts are not units.
- **[E4-36], which cause of success?** Breadth of stock near the customer, which is scale; the row shows scale earning an ordinary distribution return here, and a much higher one at two rivals with the same scale.
- **The unit of the test [E4-08].** The company, and after the separation each half. Automotive (63%) fails on the demonstration and the row; Industrial (37%) passes the return half of the demonstration but not the pricing half (*"We compete primarily on ... competitive pricing"*, its main competitor at the same margin). Neither half is a franchise on the filings.
- **[E4-04] and the 2026-09-20 ruling.** The perimeter close is reserved for a name that passes [E3-03] and whose durability cannot be judged. GPC does not pass [E3-03]; the question does not arise. The separation does not change it: both halves are read above.

**THE STRONGEST EVIDENCE AGAINST THIS VERDICT, hunted hardest [E4-26, E3-41, E4-51].**
1. **The record.** Net income on average equity 17.4-32.5% in every year 2011-2024 except the 2020 charge year; pre-tax return on net tangible operating assets 22-43%; a dividend *"every year since going public in 1948"*, raised for *"69 consecutive years through 2025"* (70 in 2026). A business that cannot price does not usually do that for decades.
2. **Motion's returns.** 26-44% pre-tax on segment assets in every year shown, rising to 40.5% in 2025; about Fastenal's return on tangible operating assets.
3. **NAPA.** A century-old brand, 4,317 independently owned stores, over 20,000 affiliated repair shops, and a distribution system that supports stores competitors do not own; the filer calls the brand *"a key competitive advantage"*.
4. **The ORLY precedent.** O'Reilly passed Q2 (NARROW) on 2026-09-02 in the same trade under 10-K language that is also "highly competitive".
5. **[E3-47]**: a wrongly closed file on an understood business is the costliest error.

**Answered.** (1) The steady return is real, and it is made of a thin margin multiplied by suppliers' money, not of price: the margin has sat at 6-9% for twenty years, and the equity return rests on inventory that the suppliers and a bank programme finance ($6.28bn of payables against $6.29bn of inventory; $3.17bn of it bank-confirmed), plus $4.98bn of debt and $1.25bn of receivables sold. [E2-01] asks for a high return *"without undue leverage"*; on capital that counts the supply-chain finance as the debt it is, 2023's 36% becomes about 22%, a good distributor's number, not a franchise's. The dividend record is a record of steadiness, which [E3-43]'s *"a business"* can have *"With superior management"*. (2) Motion's return is turnover, not price: its margin was 5.6-8.5% from 2006 to 2020, its filer says it competes *"primarily on ... competitive pricing"*, and Applied, its *"main competitor"*, earns the same margin; its 2024 comparable sales fell 2.1% when industrial demand softened. High return on a light asset base in a fragmented trade is [E3-43]'s low-cost operator with *"superior management"*, which *"unceasingly faces the possibility of competitive attack"*, not a franchise. (3) The NAPA brand is real, but the customers the filer describes choose on *"product availability, customer service, changing customer preferences, store location, and pricing pressures"*, and the rival chains without the brand earn three times GPC's margin selling to the same shops: the brand does not carry pricing. (4) O'Reilly's pass rested on its demonstration, a 19-22% margin and 53-106% return on tangible operating assets with comparable sales rising, and the attacker's test won; GPC stands on the other side of that same row. Consistency across runs is kept by applying the same test, not by reaching the same answer for every name in a trade [E4-08]. (5) I weighed [E3-47]. The file closes on the registrant's own account of how it prices, twenty years of a flat thin margin, and a competitor row that places it below the price-setters in both of its trades; **[E5-42] keeps the judgments apart**, and nothing here says GPC is badly run.

**The analyst's own incentive, stated [operator rule 9, E4-27, E3-41].** This is the fifth Q2 OUT today (AYI, ST, OSK, KEX before it); the pull toward the pattern is a bias, and so is the opposite pull toward passing a well-known, steady dividend payer. Both were tested by putting the equity return, Motion's segment return, NAPA and the ORLY precedent at the head of the case against, by computing every peer by the same construction from its own filings, and by resting the verdict on the demonstration and the row, not on risk-factor wording that O'Reilly's filing shares in part.

**Why OUT and not the perimeter close (UNKNOWABLE at Q2).** The 2026-09-20 ruling reserves UNKNOWABLE for *"A name that passes [E3-03] and whose durability cannot be judged from filings"*. GPC does not pass [E3-03]: criterion (2) fails in its own Items 1 and 1A, and the demonstration is absent in the half that is most of the company and half-present in the other. The evidence is in; it answers the class of the business, not its future.
