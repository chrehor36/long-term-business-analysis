### Name the specific way THIS business dies **[E2-27, E3-24]**
- The mechanism: **#17 THE PERMIT** (`Screens/SURVIVAL SHAPES - index.md`; rows [E2-76, E2-77, E3-03]: political repricing spreading market by market, below-cost pricing ending in government provision), **with #11 THE PASS-THROUGH as a feature** (the gains of the network passed to the issuers who choose it, through the rebates). The product is a fee on other people's payments that governments now price market by market: interchange is capped in the US (debit), the EU, Australia, New Zealand (cross-border, May 2026) and by settlement in US credit for five years; when interchange is capped, *"issuers ... [may] seek a fee reduction from us to decrease the expense of their payment programs"* (the 10-K's own sequence), so the cap migrates into the rebates line; the network fees themselves are the next target (*"could in the future lead to regulation relating to our network fees"*, the EC's acquirer-fee request, merchants' petitions); and governments provide the substitute directly (*"schemes that offer increasingly lower or subsidized P2M pricing"*; domestic switching mandates). The company lives in every version; **the owner's return dies if the growth the price assumes is regulated or bid away**, which is Q5's question, while the franchise shrinks from a toll to a utility.
- Quantified from filed figures, and the resulting outcome: **the [E3-24] arithmetic.** Payment-network net revenue was $19,476M in FY2025 (18.3bp of GDV). **Case A, the permit tightens:** net take back to the FY2020-FY2021 level (15.5bp) on today's volume cuts payment-network revenue by about $3.0bn; with operating costs unchanged and tax at 20%, **owner earnings fall about $2.4bn, to about $13.4bn (FY2024's level)**: a lost two years, not a death. **Case B, the permit and the substitute together:** Case A plus half of the value-added services' growth (those services are sold to the same banks on the same network) and GDV growth falling from 9% to 4% a year: owner earnings flat for a decade near $13-14bn, which the business survives with room (interest covered about fifteen times) and the buyer at $568 does not (Q5). **The settlement guarantee, modelled on exposure, not experience [E4-40]:** net exposure $76,699M at 2026-06-30; an unmitigated failure of customers holding 1% of it would cost about $0.8bn, 5% of one year's owner earnings; the 10-K: *"Historically, the Company has experienced a low level of losses from customer settlement failures."* **Litigation, on exposure:** Block and Intuit claim over $5bn of single damages (trebled under the Sherman Act if they win), the new 2026 class challenges the 2018 release, UK claims over £1.5bn; a $15bn adverse outcome would cost one year's owner earnings, paid over several, with the omnibus agreement sharing US MDL costs (*"Mastercard would pay 12 % of the monetary portion of the settlement"* in a global settlement with Visa and the banks).
