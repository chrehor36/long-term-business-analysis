## GATE 1 — CIRCLE OF COMPETENCE
Assisted and DIY tax preparation (22.8M returns filed in FY2025 across
US/Canada/Australia), plus small adjacent fintech (Spruce) and
small-business services. Seasonal, recurring, fee-per-return economics —
among the simplest business models run in this project. **VERDICT: PASS.**

## GATE 2 — MOAT
Franchise test [E3-03], from HRB's own 10-K: **"...competition in and
across each category, including from tax return preparation firms,
software providers, accounting firms, independent tax preparers,
certified public accountants and governmental organizations, including
the IRS, state and foreign tax authorities. We are one of the largest
providers of tax return preparation solutions and electronic filing
services in the U.S., Canada, and Australia with 22.8 million returns
filed..."**
- Real moat mechanism: the dominant retail assisted-tax brand + a
  ~9,000-office seasonal network + return-client habit (tax prep is
  high-consequence, annual, and trust-based — clients default to last
  year's preparer). Scale leadership is disclosed, not asserted.
- Real, named erosion vectors: DIY software (Intuit/TurboTax — the
  decades-long secular share shift), and **the IRS itself** (Direct File)
  named in the company's own filing as a competitor — a genuinely unusual
  disclosed risk: the referee can enter the game.
**Class: NARROW**, **Direction: STABLE-to-NARROWING** — but note the
5-year NI band ($554–606M, no year outside it) is direct evidence the
erosion has been slow enough for pricing to offset it so far.
**VERDICT: PASS with NARROW classification.**
