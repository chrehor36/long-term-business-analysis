### The finding that decided it, and it came from the FDIC rather than from EDGAR

The ACNB run of the same morning left a standing ruling for the banks still queued: *"the competitor
row for a bank should be built from the FDIC Call Report FIRST."* For ACNB that ruling existed
because 56 insured institutions compete in its nine counties and most are private or mutual, so the
Call Report was the only instrument that could see the row at all. **For a $556 billion bank with
1,927 branches in fourteen states the ruling does something different, and it turned out to be worth
more: every real competitor is an SEC registrant, so what the Call Report adds is a SECOND, uniform,
independent measurement on definitions no filer can choose.**

Two rows were built, nine banks and five years each — PNC, US Bancorp, Fifth Third, KeyCorp,
Regions, Citizens, and M&T and Huntington added because they are the same size class and the same
business and their exclusion would leave the row without the two banks that grew.

**Row A**, computed by this session on one specification from each filer's tagged annual statement
values and **validated against the subject's own filed figure** — this run's 2025 ROTCE of 12.28%
against Truist's published 12.7%, the gap being exactly the $290M of intangible amortisation and the
$416M deferred-tax adjustment Truist adds — put Truist **8th of 9** on the five-year mean return on
tangible common equity (11.43%, or 11.50% on the as-filed correction) and **8th of 9** on net income
available to common over average assets (0.745%).

**Row B, the FDIC Call Report, put it last.** On the regulator's own uniform definitions, **Truist
Bank (CERT 9846) has the WORST 2021-2025 mean pre-tax return on assets of the nine largest regional
banks in the United States — 0.991% against Regions Bank's 1.875% — and the WORST mean return on
equity, 7.03% against 13.86%.** It funds itself at the **4th cheapest** cost of the nine (1.341%),
runs the **5th best** net interest margin (3.113%), and carries mid-pack leverage (13.3× tangible
common equity). **The inputs are average. The output is last.** That is the whole Q2 verdict in three
lines, and it is not a judgment — it is the regulator's arithmetic.

**Spent:** **$27.7 billion of AFS securities sold at a $6,651M pre-tax / $5.1bn after-tax loss**,
reinvested from a 2.80% book yield into 5.27%. **And the honest reading cuts against the obvious

retained earnings — **and the tax benefit was real cash.** Reinvesting adds roughly **$680M a year**

**What survives is that the repositioning was the REMEDY and the disease was the decision that

### Q4: it survives, and the run says so without hedging

### The (c) convention: CCB's form adopted, ACNB's split applied, and a third form added because Truist's filings make the first one wrong

The brief asked the run to follow the prior banks' construction *"unless Truist's filings make it
wrong, and say which."* **They do, and the failure is specific and fixable.** Truist's total assets
**fell** $19,906M in 2023 and $4,173M in 2024, and neither was a contraction of the business —
**$7,655M of it was the Insurance Holdings segment leaving the balance sheet.** So CCB's
`Δassets × Tier 1 leverage` form prints **$5,278M of owner earnings in 2024 — the highest of the six
years — in the year the company realised a $6,651M securities loss.** The form used instead is
**`Δ(risk-weighted assets) × the CET1 ratio`**, which is the binding constraint, is what the
regulator measures, and is the one the company itself uses in public (*"Creates $945MM or 22 bps of
CET1 capital"*).

**Bank owner earnings: $2,616M (RWA form, the single clean year) to $3,690M (CCB form, five-year
mean) — a 41% spread whose every dollar is traceable to whether a disposal that released regulatory
capital counts as earnings. Truist earned $21,773M over five years and had to retain $6,036M of it,
27.7%, simply to hold its CET1 ratio while risk-weighted assets grew.** And the equity account
confirms it independently, which is the check ACNB's ruling asks for: **common equity FELL from
$62,864M to $60,273M over five years while $19,951M was earned and $18,850M was paid out.**

**Recommendation carried to the operator: the convention has now passed three independent tests
(CCB, ACNB, SOFI) and been refined by a fourth. Give it a ledger row in the RWA/CET1 form with the
Δassets form as the fallback for filers that do not publish risk-weighted assets, or refuse it —
rather than let a sixth run re-invent it.**

### Tooling and brief defects

1. **An FDIC API trap, found and fixed.** `banks.data.fdic.gov/api/institutions` with
   `search=NAME:"..."` is a **full-text** match. A first pass that searched `"TRUIST BANK"` and took
   the largest match by assets returned **JPMorgan Chase Bank, CERT 628**; `"CITIZENS BANK"` returned
   **Wells Fargo Bank, CERT 3511**; `"KEYBANK"` and `"MANUFACTURERS AND TRADERS TRUST"` returned
   **nothing at all**. A row built that way is wrong and looks entirely plausible. The correct call
   is `filters=NAME:"<exact legal name>" AND ACTIVE:1`, verified against city and state, and nine
   CERTs are saved for reuse in `_research 2026-09-19 TFC/fdic_certs.json`. **The ACNB run asked for
   an FDIC helper in `tools/`; this run seconds that request and supplies a second recipe.**
2. **A data trap that caught this run's OWN Q2 table.** `companyfacts` returns the **latest** vintage
   of a restated balance-sheet date. Truist's goodwill at 2022-12-31 is **$23,233M** in the tagged
   data and **$27,013M** on the FY2022 10-K's own balance sheet — the $3,780M difference being the
   Insurance Holdings goodwill later moved to discontinued operations. **Any ROTCE or tangible-book
   computation for a filer with a discontinued-operations recast must use the as-filed capital table,
   not the tagged data.** Corrected in a dated addendum; the ranking did not change.
3. **The brief said TFC is not in the WAVE 6 table and is in the excluded-bank subsection. The exact
   opposite is true on both counts** — and this is the **fifth consecutive fold** to correct a brief
   about where a name lives in the queue, and a verbatim repeat of JPM's. **The two names that ARE
   rows in the eleven-name table are JPM and TFC; the three in the excluded subsection are CCB, ACNB
   and SOFI.**
4. **The brief said "the fourth bank".** JPM folded as register entry 130 claiming the fourth while
   this file was being written, so **TFC is the fifth** — the third consecutive bank whose brief
   mis-stated the count, corrected by counting the register rather than trusting the brief.
5. **`sources.py:_get()` still defaults to a Mozilla user agent and `sec.gov/Archives` answers 403.**
   The brief warned of it (*"three runs have now hit this"*) and every fetch in this run passed
   `headers=SEC_UA` explicitly. It is now four runs, and the default should change.
