## Q2 — IS IT A FRANCHISE? **[E3-03]**
*The analyst's incentives, stated before the reading (operator rule 9, [E4-27], [E4-26], [E3-41]): the largest rival, Sysco, closed at Q2 OUT in this register on 2026-09-26, and PFG earns less per sales dollar than Sysco, so the easy prior is "a fortiori OUT", which would close the file in an hour. A prior that saves work is the one to hunt hardest against. **So the case for IN is written first and at full strength** [E4-51], from PFG's own filings, and the reading below must answer each item on the filings, not on the Sysco file.*

### THE STRONGEST EVIDENCE FOR IN (the case against this file's verdict), stated as its holders would state it [E4-51, E4-26]
1. **Customers keep choosing PFG over the leader, every year.** Organic independent case growth, read in each 10-K: *"independent case growth of approximately 4.9%"* (FY2019), *"independent cases declined 5.1%"* excluding Reinhart (FY2020, the pandemic), *"independent cases grew 12.6%"* excluding Reinhart (FY2021), *"organic independent case growth of 11.8%"* (FY2022), *"6.2%"* (FY2023), *"6.0%"* (FY2024), *"4.6%"* (FY2025), *"5.9%"* (FY2026). Against Sysco's U.S. local cases (the SYY run's table, from Sysco's 10-Ks) PFG was ahead in all eight years, and ahead of US Foods' independent cases in five of the eight pairs as that table aligns them. A business gaining the most valuable customers from a larger rival for eight years is doing something those customers value.
2. **The mix is moving toward the profitable end.** The filer's own mechanism: independents *"generate higher gross profit per case that more than offsets the generally higher supply chain costs"* and buy more own-label *"Performance Brands, which are our higher margin products"*; independents were *"42.1%"* of Foodservice sales in FY2026. The Foodservice segment's profit margin (EBITDA, then Adjusted EBITDA, as each 10-K defines it) rose from **2.8% (FY2019: 428.0 on 15,095.1)** and 2.0% (FY2020) to **3.3% (FY2023), 3.4% (FY2024), 3.6% (FY2025) and 3.5% (FY2026: 1,293.0 on 36,560.3)**.
3. **Scale is a cost advantage and the filer says it will widen.** *"We believe we have a competitive advantage through economies of scale in purchasing and procurement"*; *"We believe that the relative growth of larger foodservice distributors will continue to outpace that of smaller, independent players in our industry."* [E2-58] allows the one exception, *"a cost advantage that is both wide and sustainable"*.
4. **One segment earns twice the others.** Specialty (Vistar), *"a leading national distributor"* to vending and office-coffee operators and theatres (FY2019 10-K: *"Vending operators comprise Vistar’s largest channel"*), earned **7.1% of sales in each of FY2023-FY2026** (325.3 on 4,549.3; 340.6 on 4,789.8; 348.2 on 4,905.0; 360.4 on 5,056.6), against 3.2-4.1% in FY2016-FY2021, and about 22% pre-tax before depreciation on all its segment assets (360.4 on 1,623.6 in FY2026).
5. **The spread has survived thirty years.** The broadline business earned 1.8-1.9% operating margins in 1998-2000 and 1.2-1.6% in 2003-2007 (the old registrant's five-year tables), 0.8-1.1% under private-equity ownership in FY2011-FY2015 and 1.3-1.4% in FY2023-FY2026. A thin spread that never disappears might be a defended one.

### THE CASE AGAINST (the reading)
1. **Criterion (2) fails in the registrant's own words, in every 10-K FY2016-FY2026.** *"We often do not have exclusive service agreements with our customers, and our customers may switch to other distributors if those distributors can offer lower prices, differentiated products, or customer service that is perceived to be superior."* (the sentence is in all eleven, `grep` over `cache/k2016`-`k2026`); *"We believe that most purchasing decisions in the foodservice business are based on the quality and price of the product and a distributor’s ability to fill orders completely and accurately and to provide timely deliveries"*; *"Many of our customers buy from us pursuant to individual purchase orders, and we often do not enter into long-term agreements with these customers"*; *"Distribution is a relatively low margin industry"*; and, of its larger customers, *"purchase their products from us through group purchasing organizations (“GPOs”) in an effort to lower the prices paid by these customers on their foodservice orders, and we have in the past experienced some pricing pressure from these purchasers"*. The two rivals that file say the same of the same industry: US Foods, *"The cost of switching distributors is very low, as are the barriers to entry into the U.S. foodservice distribution industry"* (10-K FY2025, `0001665918-26-000008`, quoted in the SYY run and read in `USFD_10K_2025-12-27.txt`); Sysco, *"Since switching costs are very low, customers can make supplier and channel changes very quickly"* (every 10-K FY2014-FY2026, the SYY run).
2. **The price is cost plus a mark-up; no filed year credits a price increase to margin.** *"Net sales growth is primarily a function of case growth, pricing (which is primarily based on product inflation/deflation), and a changing mix"* (FY2018-FY2022; FY2016-FY2017 and FY2022-FY2024 carry the same parenthesis with *"acquisitions"* or without *"primarily"* at the start, and FY2025-FY2026 read *"pricing, including product inflation/deflation"*); every year's MD&A attributes the rise in selling price per case to *"inflation"* (FY2018, FY2019, FY2021-FY2026 read); the Convenience segment's price rise in FY2024 was *"cigarette manufacturers’ price increases"*, the manufacturer's price, not PFG's. The risk factor has said since FY2018 that *"increased competition may result in price reductions, reduced gross margins, and loss of market share"*, and since FY2025 *"price reductions or other concessions"*. [E2-44]'s first characteristic (*"an ability to increase prices rather easily (even when product demand is flat and capacity is not fully utilized)"*) is **not shown in any year**; the filer's mechanism is the opposite: *"if one or more of our competitors in the food-away-from-home industry adopted a lower cost structure, we would potentially be pressured to lower prices to our customers"* (FY2026).
3. **The share gains are bought with cost, and they are the attacker's evidence, not the moat's.** The customers who moved to PFG moved from Sysco, which [E2-45]'s test reads the other way round: the leader's customers could be taken, by a smaller rival, every year for eight years. PFG takes them with salespeople and service: FY2026 *"a $227.3 million increase in personnel expenses related to salaries and wages, commissions, and benefits"*, and operating expenses rose 9.1% in step with gross profit's 9.1%. The share gained did not widen the company's margin: operating margin **1.26% (FY2016), 1.26%, 1.44%, 1.43% (FY2019), −0.39% (FY2020), 0.66%, 0.64%, 1.34%, 1.42%, 1.29%, 1.31% (FY2026)** (`peers_out.txt`). Customers who can be won by service from the leader can be won back the same way.
4. **The scale advantage belongs to the two larger rivals, on the filer's own statement and on the row.** For FY2016-FY2023 the 10-Ks said *"there are two larger broadline distributors, Sysco and US Foods, with national footprints"* (a comma after "Sysco" in FY2020-FY2023); *"Certain of our competitors have greater financial and other resources than we do"* (FY2016-FY2023), sharpened from FY2024 to *"Certain of our competitors may have greater scale and greater financial and other resources than we do in certain markets"*. [E2-58]'s exception is a cost advantage *"wide and sustainable"*: the competitor row below shows PFG with the lowest operating margin and the lowest return on tangible operating capital of the three national broadliners, so the low-cost door, if it exists in this industry, is not PFG's.
5. **Specialty's step is procurement, mix and inflation, not price the customer cannot refuse.** The filer's own explanations: FY2022, *"a favorable shift in the channel mix primarily related to the recovery in the theater channel, and an increase in procurement gains"*; FY2023, *"a favorable shift in the mix of cases sold, growth in cases sold, and procurement related gains"*; FY2024, *"a recent acquisition and pricing improvement from procurement efficiencies, partially offset by expected decreases in inventory holding gains"* (inflation's windfall on inventory, [E4-41]'s class to be normalized down); FY2026, *"favorable channel sales mix and vendor rebates and promotional incentives"*, with Specialty cases *"decreased 0.6%"*. Procurement gains are what the maker concedes to a large buyer, not what the customer pays for having no substitute; and Specialty is 7.5% of segment sales and 16.9% of segment profit (360.4 of 2,128.8). The company priced at $91.97 is chiefly Foodservice (60.7% of segment profit) and Convenience (22.3%).
6. **Convenience is a pass-through of a declining product.** Core-Mark as an independent filer earned **0.35-0.78% operating margins and 5.2-12.6% pre-tax on tangible operating capital in 2010-2020** (`peers_out.txt`); cigarettes are *"sales of which are generally declining"*; the excise tax sits in both sales and cost; and the segment's growth in FY2026 came from *"the addition of new chain customers"*, won by bid. The segment earned 1.8% of sales before depreciation in FY2026.
7. **Thirty years of thin margins is [E2-58]'s equation at rest, not a moat.** The long record in item 5 above never shows a year of pricing: the margin moves with mix, purchases and inflation (FY2020's loss and FY2021-FY2022's 0.6% came with the pandemic and with Core-Mark's lower-margin sales). The corpus's franchise produces *"high rates of return on capital"* [E3-43] from pricing, *"thereby"*; a spread held at 1-2% by cost control is the other class the same passage names: *"a business ... can be killed by poor management"*.

**The case for IN, answered.** (1) The eight years of share gains are real, and they are the attacker's evidence: they show the leader's customers can be taken, and they were taken with salespeople at a cost that kept PFG's margin flat. (2) The mix shift to independents raised the Foodservice segment's margin by about three-quarters of a point over seven years, from 2.8% to 3.5% before depreciation, which is still below Sysco's consolidated operating margin after depreciation (3.66%).
