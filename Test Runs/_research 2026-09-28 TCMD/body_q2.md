## Q2 — IS IT A FRANCHISE? **[E3-03]**

*Every Tactile figure below is read from its filed annual reports FY2016-FY2025 (Item 1, Item 1A, the MD&A and the notes), from the 10-Q for the quarter to 2026-06-30, or from SEC companyfacts checked against the 2025 statements (Step 0). Scripts and outputs, all in `Test Runs/_research 2026-09-28 TCMD/`: `margins.py` (`margins_out.txt`, gross, operating, selling and billing margins 2014-2025), `peers.py` (`peers_out.txt`, the competitor row), `sgrep.py` (sentence search across the ten 10-Ks), `cfs2.py` and `cond.py` (`cfs_cond.txt`, every filed cash-flow statement).*

**The question this file has to answer: who sets Tactile's price?** A maker of a device whose gross margin has sat at 71-76% in every year for twelve years looks like a business with something the customer cannot get elsewhere. A supplier that bills insurers at rates the insurers publish, and whose own filing says so, looks like a business whose price belongs to the payer. **The two hypotheses, stated so they can be attacked [E4-26].** *(a) A narrow franchise in advanced home compression: the only pump cleared for the head and neck, the advanced reimbursement code, a clinical literature no rival matches, a direct channel no rival has built, and returns on tangible capital of 33-46% since 2023.* *(b) A durable-medical-equipment supplier in an administered-price trade, whose allowance per device is set by fee schedules, whose rivals bill the same codes, whose gross margin is consumed by the cost of finding and billing each patient, and whose operating margin has never exceeded 8.9% of revenue.* Each is tested on the filings below.

**How the filer describes its price, in its own words.**
- **Who sets it, every 10-K FY2022-FY2025 (Item 1A):** *"Reimbursement rates are established by fee schedules mandated by private payers, Medicare, the Veterans Administration, and certain Medicaid programs."* And every 10-K FY2018-FY2025: *"Since our prices are often fixed due to the reimbursement policies of, and arrangements with, third-party payers, this could result in lower margins on our products."* The 2025 sentence goes on: *"Although Medicare and certain private payers index their reimbursement rate off of a subset of cost of living, the Veterans Administration, certain Medicaid programs and other private payers are more likely to remain constant or slightly decrease their reimbursement rates. As a result, we may not be able to offset the effects of general inflation on our operating costs through increases in prices for our products."*
- **Which way it moves, every 10-K FY2016-FY2025 (MD&A):** *"We continue to work to reduce product manufacturing costs through enhanced product design efforts, supply chain initiatives in an effort to offset anticipated price erosion"*; and every 10-K FY2019-FY2025: *"However, we also anticipate pricing pressure from private insurers, which will result in continued downward pressure on our revenue growth rate."* Item 1A, 2025: *"Any decline in the amount payers are willing to pay for our products could create pricing pressure for us. If we are forced to lower the price we charge for our products, our gross margins will decrease"*; *"Consolidation in the healthcare industry could lead to demands for price concessions"*.
- **What the premium rests on:** *"Our Flexitouch Plus system controller is reimbursed under HCPCS code E0652, and our Entre Plus and Nimbl system controllers are each reimbursed under HCPCS code E0651"*; *"HCPCS codes for durable medical equipment are assigned and managed by CMS and a Medicare contractor responsible for Pricing, Data Analysis and Coding, or PDAC. [...] If the PDAC contractor determines one of our products does not meet the current HCPCS definition, it could remove all coding or assign a different HCPCS code with a lesser payment rate."* The head-and-neck garment *"does not currently have a billing code assigned; it is currently billed using the miscellaneous DMEPOS HCPCS code E1399."* AffloVest is *"reimbursed under HCPCS code E0483"*, billed by the dealer.
- **What decides volume, 2024-2026:** the Medicare coverage rule for pumps was retired on 2024-11-14 so that *"the NCD will apply to all PCDs"*; *"beginning April 13, 2026, CMS will require prior authorization requests (“PARs”) for pneumatic compression devices billed under the HCPCS codes for our PCD products"*; and *"While our products are not currently subject to the competitive bidding process under Medicare, if our products were to become subject to such process in the future, it could negatively affect our business"*.
- **The rivals:** every 10-K FY2019-FY2025, *"Our most significant manufacturing competitors are Bio Compression Systems, Inc. and Lympha Press USA"* (FY2016-FY2017: *"Our most significant competitors are Bio Compression Systems, Inc. and Lympha Press USA"*); *"Manufacturing companies compete for sales to patients primarily based on product features and service"*; and every 10-K FY2016-FY2025, that the industry *"will become increasingly competitive"*. Each clinical comparison carries the caveat *"The study does not reflect a comparison of our Flexitouch system to a product that is billed under the same Healthcare Common Procedure Coding System, or HCPCS, Code as our Flexitouch system"*: other advanced pumps bill E0652. In airway clearance: *"Competitors within the airway clearance market consist largely of three other HFCWO vest manufacturers: Baxter (formerly Hill-Rom), Philips Medical and Electromed"*, and Electromed's own 10-K (FY to 2026-06-30, filed 2026-08-25, `0001437749-26-028914`) names it back: *"The AffloVest® from Tactile Systems Technology Inc. (“Tactile Medical”) also participates in the same market as our SmartVest System."*

**Who pays, by channel (10-K Item 1 each year; 10-Q June 2026 channel table).** Private insurers and other payers 74% (2015-2016), 71% (2018), 72% (2019), 71% (2020), 60% (2024), **51% (2025)**; Medicare 13%, 12%, 8%, 9%, 11%, 16%, 18%, **24%**; the Veterans Administration (on the Federal Supply Schedule, *"established by Section 603 of the Veterans Health Care Act of 1992"*) 18-20% (2017-2018), 17% (2019), 13% (2020), 11% (2024), **9% (2025)**; DME distributors (AffloVest) **16% (2025)**. **Every dollar is paid at a rate set by the payer, by the government, or by a dealer who is himself paid at the E0483 allowance.** In the first quarter under the Medicare prior-authorization requirement (April-June 2026), Medicare revenue was **$17,427K against $19,422K a year earlier (−10.3%)** while private-payer revenue rose 25% ($48,791K against $39,041K).

**[E3-03], criterion by criterion, the unit being the company [E4-08].** *"An economic franchise arises from a product or service that: (1) is needed or desired; (2) is thought by its customers to have no close substitute and; (3) is not subject to price regulation."*
- **(1) Needed or desired [x]: passes.** Over 84,000 patients a year; payers covering *"over 278 million lives"* contract for it.
- **(3) Not subject to price regulation [ ]: FAILS, in the filer's own words.** *"Reimbursement rates are established by fee schedules mandated by private payers, Medicare, the Veterans Administration, and certain Medicaid programs"*; *"our prices are often fixed due to the reimbursement policies of, and arrangements with, third-party payers"*. A third of 2025 revenue is paid on government schedules directly (Medicare 24%, the VA's Federal Supply Schedule 9%, Medicaid inside *"other payers"*); half at private payers' fee schedules; a sixth through dealers paid on the E0483 allowance. The premium that makes Flexitouch worth more than a basic pump is a **code** (E0652 against E0651), assigned by CMS's coding contractor, who *"could remove all coding or assign a different HCPCS code with a lesser payment rate"*. **This is [E2-59]'s administered price** (*"if prices or costs are administered in some manner and thereby insulated at least partially from normal market forces. This administration can be carried out (a) legally through government intervention"*): the allowance is generous today, and the moat that generosity makes belongs to the regime, which in 2024-2026 retired a coverage rule, imposed prior authorization, and keeps competitive bidding in reserve. **The USPH file of 2026-09-26 closed a fee-schedule provider OUT on this criterion with 36% of revenue on the government's schedule**; here the filer says every rate is set by a schedule.
- **(2) Thought by its customers to have no close substitute [ ]: NOT SHOWN.** For Flexitouch, the head-and-neck and trunk indications are a real distinction the filer claims for itself, and the clinical literature is Tactile's; but the payer, who sets the price, pays the same E0652 allowance for other advanced pumps (the filer's own caveat on every comparison study), and the filer names two manufacturing rivals who compete *"primarily based on product features and service"*. For AffloVest, the filer names three rival vests and the largest listed rival names AffloVest; all four bill E0483. For the basic pumps (Entre Plus, Nimbl), the product is by definition the simple one.

**[E3-43]'s demonstration, *"a company’s ability to regularly price its product or service aggressively and thereby to earn high rates of return on capital"*, and its other doors.**
- **The pricing half: NOT MET.** No year in ten 10-Ks records a price increase taken; every one records *"anticipated price erosion"*, and seven record *"pricing pressure from private insurers"*. Revenue per patient $3,139-3,314 in 2022-2025 through a period of general inflation that the filer says it *"may not be able to offset"*.
- **The return half: MET ONLY IN 2023-2025, and not by price.** 13.6-21.6% pre-tax on all operating capital and 33-46% on tangible capital in the last three years; 4-12% and losses in the six before. The recent returns come from patient volume and cost (gross margin 71.1% in 2023 to 75.9% in 2025; selling cost 43.1% of revenue in 2022 to 36.8% in 2025), which is a well-run business, not a price set aggressively.
- **[E3-43]'s "a business" doors:** *"“a business” earns exceptional profits only if it is the low-cost operator or if supply of its product or service is tight."* **The low-cost door [E2-58]: not claimed**; the direct model is the high-cost model (selling and billing 64% of 2025 revenue; the filer argues it captures *"both the manufacturer and distributor margins"*, which is the same allowance counted once). **Tight supply: no**; *"we expect that the industry will become increasingly competitive in the future"*.

**[E2-44], the two characteristics.** (1) *"an ability to increase prices rather easily (even when product demand is flat and capacity is not fully utilized)"*: **not shown**; the price is the allowance. (2) *"an ability to accommodate large dollar volume increases in business ... with only minor additional investment of capital"*: **met in the plant** (capital expenditures $1.8-5.4M a year on revenue of $109-330M) **and not in the payroll or the receivables**: each added patient needs an account manager's referral, a reimbursement case and, for Medicare, an appeal that ran more than a year (*"The appeal process can be lengthy, lasting more than a year in most cases"*; the non-current Medicare receivable reached $23.1M at 2022-12-31).

### THE STRONGEST EVIDENCE AGAINST THIS VERDICT, stated as its holders would state it [E4-51, E4-26, E3-47]
The verdict below is OUT, so the case for IN is set out first, at full strength:
1. **A 71-76% gross margin for twelve straight years is not what a regulated commodity earns.** Payers pay Tactile three to four times what the device costs to make, year after year, through a pandemic and an inflation. Whatever sets the price, it is set high for this product, and the product has held it.
2. **The advanced code is earned, not granted.** Flexitouch Plus is the only pump cleared for head-and-neck lymphedema and the trunk and chest; Tactile paid for the clinical trials, including a 236-patient randomised trial reported in 2025; payers approve 80-90% of its claims. A rival's pump billed under E0652 is not the same pump, and doctors refer to Tactile by name.
3. **The direct channel is the moat.** No pump rival has national accreditation, state licences, Medicare billing privileges and a 332-person field force together; the filer's own attacker's test says what it would cost to copy. The channel captures the distributor's margin that every rival gives away.
4. **The returns are here now.** 33-46% pre-tax on tangible capital in 2023-2025, rising; operating margin up from −5.2% to 8.9% in three years; units up 29%. A franchise in its early years looks exactly like this.
5. **Criterion (3) is about a regulator fixing a firm's price, like a utility commission; a fee schedule for a category of device is the market's price, and Tactile's product earns the top of the category.**

**Answered.** (1) is accepted as fact and answered by the rest of the income statement: the allowance is high and **it is spent**, 59-70% of revenue on selling and billing in every year, so that operating income has never reached 9% of revenue; a margin set by a payer and consumed by the cost of collecting it is [E2-59]'s administered price, not [E3-43]'s price set aggressively, and it lasts exactly as long as the regime keeps it (the filer: *"a large negative payment adjustment could adversely affect our business"*). (2) is accepted as the best evidence for criterion (2) and does not reach criterion (3): the head-and-neck garment has no code of its own and bills as *"miscellaneous"*, and the premium between E0651 and E0652 is a coding contractor's decision the filer says can be reversed; in the first quarter of Medicare prior authorization, Medicare revenue fell 10.3%, which is the regime setting volume as well as price. (3) is accepted as real and is a moat around **volume and collection**, not price, of the kind [E3-43] classes as *"a business"*; the attacker's cost is Tactile's cost too (selling and billing $210M in 2025 against $29M of operating income). (4) is accepted as the last three years and set against the twelve: returns earned from volume and cost inside a flat price per patient are the well-run operator's, and three good years after six poor ones is not [E3-46]'s *"over time"*. (5) is the argument the verdict weighs most, and it is answered on the filer's own words, which name Medicare, the VA and Medicaid (a third of revenue, government schedules by statute) alongside private schedules, and say the prices are *"often fixed"*; and it is answered by precedent: USPH closed OUT on 36% of revenue on the government's schedule and the rest at contracted rates, the same construction.

**For the operator (not decided here; PRIME RULE 5):** the ABBV file of 2026-09-27 asked whether criterion (3) is read at the company level or at the level of the products that carry the sales. **Here the two readings agree**: every product line is paid at an administered rate (lymphedema through payer schedules, 84% of revenue; AffloVest through dealers paid on E0483, 16%). The open question does not move this verdict, and it is recorded so the ruling, when made, can confirm that without a re-run.

**The analyst's own incentive, stated [operator rule 9, E4-27, E3-41].** The USPH precedent two days old pulls toward OUT by consistency, and the run of recent wave 7 files closing at Q2 pulls toward habit (the ABBV file counted fifteen of sixteen from LNN to MDT); against that, a 76% gross margin, a stock that rose from $10.14 to $29.00 in six months of 2025 and a management that bought back 2.1M shares at $12.48 in 2025 pull toward admiration. The verdict rests on the filer's own sentences about who sets its price (ten 10-Ks), the margin series of twelve years, the revenue per patient of four, and a row of five SEC filers; the case for IN was written first and at full strength.
