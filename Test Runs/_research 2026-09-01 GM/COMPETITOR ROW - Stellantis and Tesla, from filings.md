### CAPTIVE FINANCE — CONFIRMED ABSENT
Note 16 discloses exactly **two operating and reportable segments** (automotive; energy
generation and storage). Leasing sits **inside** the automotive segment. **There is no captive
finance segment and no separate financial-services disclosure.** Risk factors: *"We offer
financing arrangements for our vehicles in North America, Europe and Asia primarily ourselves
and through various financial institutions."* Direct operating leases run through consolidated
VIEs (Note 14) but are not a reportable segment.

**Tesla's cash-flow statement has NO industrial/financial-services split — confirmed absent.**
So Tesla is the clean control in this row: an automaker whose consolidated OCF is not
contaminated by a lending book. **Segment operating income is NOT disclosed** — the CODM
measure is gross profit.

## WHAT THIS ROW ESTABLISHES BEFORE THE GM AND FORD RUNS OPEN

1. **The captive-finance contamination is real and material, and one peer proves it by
   arithmetic**: Stellantis reports **negative €4,650M consolidated operating cash flow and
   positive €5,050M industrial operating cash flow.** The sign of the number depends on which
   company you look at.
2. **Toyota is the disclosure benchmark** — a full two-way income statement, cash-flow
   statement and balance sheet, non-financial vs financial services, and a finance arm
   contributing **16.6% of consolidated pre-tax income**. GM's and Ford's candour should be
   judged against that, not against nothing [E2-49].
3. **Tesla is the control**: no captive finance segment at all, so its consolidated OCF is
   uncontaminated.
4. **Stellantis and Honda both took catastrophic EV write-offs in the same year** — €25.4bn of
   adjustments and ¥1.58 trillion respectively — and **both attribute part of it to the same
   US policy reversal**. That is a shared, dated, external cause and it bears on Q4's named
   way it dies for every name in the row.
5. **Warranty is being re-estimated upward across the industry**: Stellantis +€5.4bn catch-up,
   Toyota recall provisions +844,899 against 317,414, Tesla +1,127 of pre-existing-warranty
   revisions. Test whether GM and Ford are doing the same or are the outliers.
