## GATE 1 — CIRCLE OF COMPETENCE
Diversified industrial conglomerate across Aerospace Technologies, Building
Automation, Energy and Sustainability Solutions, and Industrial Automation.
Genuinely a multi-segment business (four distinct end markets, each with
its own competitive dynamics) — the honest circle-of-competence claim is
structural (a well-run diversified industrial holding company), similar in
kind to how this project has treated other diversified conglomerates
(e.g., the sogo shosha, Otsuka Holdings), rather than claiming independent
mastery of jet-engine-component metallurgy and building-control software
alike. **VERDICT: PASS**, on the structural/diversified-holding-company
basis, not segment-by-segment mastery.

## GATE 2 — MOAT
Franchise test [E3-03], applied per segment given the genuinely different
competitive dynamics. From Honeywell's own 10-K competitor list: **"Some of
our key competitors include but are not limited to: AEROSPACE TECHNOLOGIES
[Garmin, L3Harris, Rolls Royce, RTX Corporation, Safran, Thales] BUILDING
AUTOMATION [Johnson Controls, Schneider Electric, Siemens] ENERGY AND
SUSTAINABILITY SOLUTIONS [Clariant, Flowserve, Topsoe] INDUSTRIAL
AUTOMATION [Dematic, Emerson Electric, Itron, MSA Safety Incorporated,
Rockwell Automation, TE Connectivity, Zebra Technologies]. Our businesses
compete on a variety of factors such as performance, applied technology,
product innovation, product recognition, quality, reliability, customer
service, delivery, and price."**
- **Aerospace Technologies**: real switching-cost moat mechanism —
  aerospace components require FAA/regulatory certification once designed
  into an airframe, making supplier substitution costly and slow (general
  industry knowledge about aerospace-supplier economics, not independently
  confirmed as stated in this specific filing this pass). **Narrower/
  stronger** than the other three segments.
- **Building Automation / Energy & Sustainability / Industrial Automation**:
  competing against large, capable, well-resourced peers (Siemens, Emerson,
  Rockwell, Schneider) on the generic factors named above — genuinely more
  competitive, commodity-adjacent territory.
**Class: NARROW overall, segment-dependent** (aerospace materially
stronger than the other three) — the same "segment-dependent moat" pattern
this project has already named at other diversified names (e.g., Otsuka
Holdings, Canon). **Direction: STABLE.**
**VERDICT: PASS with NARROW/segment-dependent classification**, not
inflated into a uniform WIDE-moat read across all four businesses.
