## GATE 1 — CIRCLE OF COMPETENCE
Unit economics in plain terms: Skyworks designs and manufactures analog and
mixed-signal RF semiconductor components — power amplifiers, filters (SAW/
TC-SAW/BAW), switches, front-end modules — sold mainly to smartphone OEMs,
plus a smaller and growing non-mobile book (automotive, industrial,
infrastructure, aerospace/defense, IoT). Revenue is units × negotiated ASP
per design win; cost base is a hybrid of owned "internal manufacturing
capabilities" (10-K, p.6: assembly/test facilities it owns and operates,
plus wafer fabrication capacity) and external foundry/subcontractor
"alliances and strategic relationships for leading-edge technologies" — a
fab-lite/hybrid model, not pure fabless and not a full IDM. Segment count:
**1** — "the Company considers its product offerings to be similar in
nature and therefore not segregated for reporting purposes" (10-K Note,
Segment/Revenue disclosure); one reporting unit for goodwill testing.
Informational edge: none special, but none needed — a widely covered
large-cap semiconductor supplier with risks (cyclicality, customer
concentration) disclosed in plain language.

Material fact carried forward rather than pre-judged: on October 27, 2025,
Skyworks entered a definitive Merger Agreement to combine with Qorvo, Inc.
in an integrated two-step merger, expected to close "early in calendar year
2027," subject to HSR/antitrust clearance in multiple jurisdictions and both
companies' shareholder votes (10-K, Risk Factors, "Proposed Transaction with
Qorvo," pp.12–14). This is not yet closed. The entity evaluated here is
standalone Skyworks as reported in the FY2025 10-K — the pending combination
is treated as a Gate-5-relevant risk/catalyst, not folded into the
circle-of-competence read of the business as it exists today.
**VERDICT: PASS** (single, plainly understandable segment; no informational
edge but no informational deficit).

## GATE 2 — MOAT
Franchise test [E3-03]:
- **Needed/desired** — yes. RF front-end and analog content is structurally
  required in every smartphone and is growing with 5G and AI-phone
  complexity (10-K, "Strategy" section).
- **No close substitute** — **weak**. Real technology differentiation exists
  (approximately 5,200 issued patents; BAW/TC-SAW filter expertise; system-
  level integration the company calls out as a strength), which matters
  against smaller entrants. But against its actual customer base the
  substitute risk is live: Qorvo and Broadcom are credible alternative
  merchant RF suppliers, Apple has publicly pursued in-house silicon
  capability for years, and — critically — **there is no disclosed
  long-term supply contract**. The 10-K's own risk-factor language is
  explicit: "the achievement or loss of design wins with such customers" —
  business is re-competed each product cycle, not locked in.
- **Unregulated** — yes.
- **Proven by pricing power + returns on capital** — **fails on the
  evidence**, not marginally:
  - Gross margin: 44.2% (FY2023) → 41.2% (FY2024) → 41.2% (FY2025); FY2025
    MD&A attributes the decline explicitly to "unfavorable product mix,
    lower average selling prices" (10-K, MD&A, Results of Operations) — a
    direct, filed admission of eroding pricing power, not an inference.
  - Revenue declined two consecutive years: $4,772.4M (FY2023) → $4,178.0M
    (FY2024, −12.5%) → $4,086.9M (FY2025, −2.2%) (10-K income statement).
  - Return on equity fell every year for four consecutive years (NI/period-
    end equity, XBRL-sourced net income and stockholders' equity, cross-
    checked to the 10-K balance sheet/income statement): 28.3% (FY2021) →
    23.3% (FY2022) → 16.2% (FY2023) → 9.4% (FY2024) → 8.3% (FY2025).

Primary moat metric: gross margin trend. Only 3 fiscal years are available
from a single 10-K; a true 8-quarter trend would require 10-Qs not in hand —
flagged as a data limitation, not filled with an estimate. The 4-year ROE
series (drawn from annual 10-K/XBRL data, not quarterly) corroborates the
same direction and is not similarly limited.
**Direction: NARROWING.** **Class: at most NARROW** — and the specific prong
that defines a franchise (pricing power proven by returns on capital) is not
weak, it is actively moving the wrong way, for four straight years running.

**Customer-concentration finding (the specific question this run was asked
to check):** Apple accounted for 67%, 69%, and 66% of net revenue in fiscal
2025, 2024, and 2023 respectively (10-K Note 14, Customer Concentration —
verbatim figures). This is not simply "customer concentration risk" in the
generic sense the template's risk register would carry into Gate 5 — at this
magnitude, with a customer that (a) has viable alternative suppliers, (b)
has growing in-house design ambitions, (c) re-competes the relationship each
design cycle with no contractual lock-in, and (d) is the disclosed proximate
cause of the margin compression above, the relationship is closer to
monopsony than franchise. The bargaining power sits with the customer, not
the supplier — the opposite of what the franchise test is checking for.
**VERDICT: FAIL — STOP.** Four consecutive years of ROE decline and two
consecutive years of revenue decline are not a single bad year absorbed by
an otherwise-provable moat; they are the moat-metric evidence itself, and it
fails the pricing-power/returns-on-capital prong of the franchise test on
its own filed numbers. Real technology and IP exist, but they have not
translated into pricing power against Skyworks' actual customer base — the
test the framework asks for is proof, not potential.

## ELIMINATED REGISTER
**Skyworks Solutions (SWKS) — eliminated at Gate 2 (Moat), 2026-07-15.**
Reason: customer concentration (67% Apple, FY2025) without contractual lock-
in, against a customer with viable alternative suppliers and growing in-
house design ambitions, has produced four consecutive years of ROE decline
(28.3%→8.3%, FY2021–FY2025) and two consecutive years of revenue decline —
direct, filed evidence against the pricing-power/returns-on-capital prong of
the franchise test, not a hypothetical risk.

**Note for future re-underwriting, not an entry signal:** Skyworks has a
pending stock-for-stock merger with Qorvo (announced 2025-10-27, expected
close early calendar 2027, subject to antitrust and shareholder approval).
A combined SWKS+Qorvo would reduce head-to-head merchant RF competition and
could change the bargaining dynamic with OEM customers described above. That
is a reason to re-run this company from Gate 1 after the merger closes (or
is abandoned) — it is not a reason to treat today's elimination as
provisional or to carry any valuation language forward.
