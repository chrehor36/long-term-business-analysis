## THE FINDING OF THE RUN: a competent operator of three substitutable businesses, and its own auditors said so

**Q2 closes on [E3-03] criterion 2, and the evidence is the registrant's own prose.** Superior's
FY2025 10-K describes Branded Products as competing *"with a multitude of national and regional
companies"* plus *"local firms in most major metropolitan areas"*; the Contact Centers market as
*"highly competitive and fragmented"*, where *"Nearshore operators can provide comparable service
to their U.S. counterparts at a fraction of the price"*; and its own inputs as *"widely available"*
with substitutable alternatives. The Q2 2026 earnings release calls all three markets *"large,
fragmented and growing"*. **"Fragmented" is the company's own word for every market it is in.**

**And its own accounting wrote the purchased advantage to zero.** Goodwill ran $4.1M (2015) to
$39.4M (2021) to **$0.0M at 2022-12-31** on a *"non-cash goodwill impairment charge of $45.9
million"*, with $5.6M of indefinite-lived trade names going the same year and **a further $2.6M
tradename impairment in Healthcare Apparel in Q2 2026**. Over the sixteen filed years the company
spent **$172.9M of cash on acquisitions - 88% of today's market capitalisation**. There is no
goodwill left to show for it.

**[E4-04] did NOT decide this file and was not used to.** Under the ruling of 2026-09-20 it is a
competence limit, not a fourth franchise criterion, and durability here is judgeable from the
filings. This is an OUT on the business with the evidence in hand, not the perimeter close.

## The uncomfortable part, written down rather than hedged

**[E4-51] demands the best argument against my own verdict, and here it is.** Superior's **Branded
Products segment - 64% of revenue - earned a 7.66% operating margin in FY2025, above Cimpress's
6.72% and more than double Lands' End's 3.32%.** Its Contact Centers segment earned 7.13% while
Concentrix earned -9.34% and TTEC -5.48%. **On a segment view Superior is a middling-to-good
operator in both markets, not a bad one.** My bear case rests on the consolidated line, and the
consolidated line is dragged below every peer by a **$23.3M corporate centre** - that is, by a
holding-company structure, not by a competitive failure in the operating businesses. A reader who
thought that cost could be halved, or the three businesses separated, would be looking at very
different numbers.

**Why it does not move the verdict.** [E3-03] criterion 2 asks about the product, not the
operator's relative skill, and the registrant answers it against itself in three places. A good
operator inside a substitutable market is **[E2-37]**'s *"remarkable textile company - but not a
remarkable business"*. And the corporate cost is not an accident to be modelled away: it ran
$17.1M, $19.6M, $23.5M, $23.3M across the four filed years since the re-segmentation - **up 36%
while revenue fell 2.2%.**

**A second uncomfortable item, recorded and not scored.** The 2026 proxy shows the annual incentive
bonus tied to *"earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation and amortization (EBITDA) as adjusted
for certain items"*, and the **Earnings Target moved from $39,656,000 for 2025 to $31,106,000 for
2026** - a 21.6% reduction, set after FY2025 EBITDA came in at $25.7M. **[E2-49]** asks exactly
this question about a moved bullseye. **It is recorded beneath the close and it is NOT scored**,
because Q3 never opened and because a strong or weak Q3 could not have changed Q2 anyway
**[E2-37, E2-38, E3-39]**.
