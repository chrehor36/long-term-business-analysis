## Q1 — CAN I UNDERSTAND HOW THIS MAKES MONEY? [E3-31]

- **Unit economics in my own words, no management language.** FTI Consulting employs **8,118
  people, of whom 6,421 bill**, and sells their hours. Revenue is, near enough, **billable heads ×
  available hours × utilisation × hourly rate**, plus three tails: success fees on restructuring
  and litigation outcomes, pass-through costs re-billed at no margin, and per-gigabyte hosting
  revenue in the Technology segment. **The filed arithmetic for 2025: 6,421 billable professionals
  at utilisation of 60% (Corporate Finance), 57% (FLC) and 59% (Economic Consulting), at average
  realised rates of $529, $442 and $583 per hour, produced $3,788.9M of revenue.** Of every revenue
  dollar, **67.9 cents is direct cost of revenues** — overwhelmingly the salaries, bonuses,
  share-based compensation and forgivable-loan amortisation of the people who did the work —
  leaving a **32.1% gross margin**. Selling and administration takes **21.1 points**, special
  charges 0.7 and intangible amortisation 0.1, leaving **10.3 cents of operating income**. Interest
  takes 0.5 and tax 2.6, leaving **7.2 cents of net income**. It does this on **$169.3M of net
  property and equipment** — 4.5% of revenue — and it is not a capital-light business in the way
  that number suggests, because the real capital is **$1,037.7M of receivables at 88 days sales
  outstanding** and **$338.5M of forgivable loans lent to its own senior staff to stop them
  leaving**. There is no float, no subscription, no installed base and no backlog disclosure. **The
  cash cycle is savagely seasonal**: prior-year bonuses are paid in Q1, so operating cash flow is
  **negative in the first half of every year** (H1 2026 −$157.7M; H1 2025 −$409.5M) and is earned
  back in the second.
- **The scarce input this business controls: none that it owns.** The scarce input is **the named
  expert whom a court, a tribunal, a bankruptcy judge or a general counsel will accept** — and
  FTI's own Item 1 says it does not control that input. Verbatim, FY2025 10-K, Competition:
  *"Since our businesses depend in large part on professional relationships, **there are low
  barriers of entry for professionals, including our professionals, electing to work
  independently, start their own firms or change employers.**"* And in Item 1A: *"**Our people are
  our primary assets and account for the majority of our expenses.**"* **The scarce input walks,
  and FTI says so.** What FTI does own is the *aggregation*: the ability to staff a large
  cross-border matter across five disciplines at once, the conflict-clearance apparatus, the
  balance sheet that can carry 88 days of receivables and a $338.5M loan book, and the brand that
  gets it onto panels. That is a real asset and it is weighed at Q2.
- **Will the fundamentals look broadly the same in ten years?** **Yes — the MECHANISM will.**
  Companies will still fail, still merge, still be sued, still be investigated; somebody will still
  charge $500 an hour to testify about it. The unit of account — an hour, priced — is the same unit
  it was in 2008, and the 2008 and 2010 filings describe the same five segments doing the same five
  things. **What will NOT be stable is the level**, and the run says so here rather than hiding it:
  Economic Consulting's segment operating income was **$109.8M in 2023, $18.8M in 2025 and $0.1M in
  the first half of 2026**; owner earnings ranged 4.85× across eight years. FTI's own Item 1A names
  the technological threat in plain words: *"some non-expert services and tasks currently performed
  by our professionals **have been and will continue to be replaced by automation, including
