## Q2 — WHY IS THE CASTLE STILL STANDING, and what keeps it standing ten to twenty years? STOP.
The question: "why is that castle still standing?", and "How much do they depend on the genius of the lord in the castle?" **[M1995-038]**; a moat is what "protects excellent returns on invested capital" **[L2007-004]**. Read for the deciding part, Design Automation; Design IP is noted where it bears.

- **The key factors, and how permanent.** **[M1995-038]** From the filing: a customer's design flow is built on one supplier's tools and data formats, its engineers are trained on them, and the leading-edge flows are qualified with the foundries' processes (10-K Item 1, Support for Industry Standards; Digital Design Family); the licences are network licences sold on two-to-three-year terms and renewed (10-K MD&A); and the scale of the research, $2,479M in FY2025, 35% of revenue, and 33% to 36% of revenue in every year FY2016 to FY2025 (XBRL, first-filed). The first two are habits that persist. The third is not a fixed asset: the filer says the licences and the updates form "a single, combined offering" because "timely, relevant software updates are integral to maintaining the utility of the software licenses" (10-K MD&A, Revenue). In the filer's own words, then, the product loses its use without continuing invention.
- **Without the lord.** The business passed from its founder-chief executive (de Geus, now executive chair) to Ghazi in January 2024 with the margins still widening (Design Automation 37%, 39%, 42%, FY2023 to FY2025); no superstar dependence found **[M1995-038]**.
- **The attacker with money.** **[M2011-015]** asks "could I do it?". The filing says the field is "highly competitive", with "new entrants to the market" and customers who "internally develop design tools" (10-K Item 1A), and that China "has formed government-backed investment funds as it seeks to build independent EDA capabilities" (same). **The judgment.** On the record the castle has stood for the whole span: two filers, the same growth, wide margins, and a rival that names Synopsys first. The question the STOP asks is what keeps it standing ten to twenty years, and here the evidence runs to one place. This is a business that, in the words of the row, is "quite dependent on the technology continuing to gallop": "if they had never invented any more pharmaceuticals, it would be a terrible business" **[M1999-075]**. The filer says the same of its own licences (updates "integral to maintaining the utility of the software licenses"); it spends a third of its revenue every year to keep them current; it describes its own industry as one where "Technology ... evolves rapidly and is characterized by frequent product introductions" and where AI brings "disruption to both our business models and existing technology offerings" (10-K Item 1A). The framework sends such a business to TOO HARD, not OUT, because its ten-year economics cannot be judged: "those are the businesses of rapid change" **[M2001-069]**; "A moat that must be continuously rebuilt will eventually be no moat at all." **[L2007-005]**. The widening of the Design Automation margin is the strongest evidence the other way, the daily work the rows call "widening the moat" **[L2005-010]**; the framework carries that pull as OPEN (section VI, Q2: **[L2005-010]** and **[L2004-007]** against **[L2007-005]**) and gives no test that separates widening from rebuilding. What the filing does give is one part where a year's roadmap decisions helped cut operating profit by 43% (Design IP, FY2025), and a market (China, 16% of revenue two years ago, under 10% now) whose access the filer says it cannot forecast. Design IP's year is what the row means by "if something can gain competitive advantage very quickly, you have to worry about them losing it quickly, too" **[M2002-050]**, seen in this company's own accounts.
