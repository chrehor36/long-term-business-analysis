## Row 1 — Grupo Bimbo (BMV: BIMBO), THE competitor
**Not an SEC filer.** Source rung: company IR site, English (evidence-ladder rung 3):
- "Grupo Bimbo Reports 4Q25 Results" (grupobimbo.com quarterly-reports page; results for
  the three and twelve months ended 2025-12-31; IFRS, MXN).
- "Grupo Bimbo Reports 2Q26 Results," July 23, 2026 (three months ended 2026-06-30).
Bimbo's North America segment = US + Canada, ~43-45% of group revenue (~MXN 190bn FY2025
≈ US$10bn — roughly 2x FLO's whole company).

| Metric | FY2025 (cal.) | 2Q26 (Apr-Jun 2026) |
|---|---|---|
| NA net sales, currency-neutral | **−4.0%** | **+1.3%** (2nd consecutive quarter of growth) |
| NA adj EBITDA margin | **9.0%** (+60bp) | **10.0%** (+100bp) |
| NA operating margin | n/d in release | 4.6% (+130bp) |
| US market share, company statement | Q4: "market share improvements across **all branded categories** in the U.S." | "**gained share in all categories in the U.S.** — for the first time since 2020 — most notably in breakfast, buns & rolls, **mainstream bread** and salty snacks" |
| Group net debt / adj EBITDA | 2.7x | **2.5x**, avg maturity **9.5 yrs**, avg cost 6.5% |

FLO same windows, from FLO's filings: FY2025 organic (ex-53rd week, ex-Simple Mills)
**−2.8%** (price/mix −0.8, volume −2.0); Q2 FY2026 (12 wks to 2026-07-18) net sales
**−4.0%**, volume **−5.8%**, branded-retail volume **−7.6%**; adj EBITDA margin FY2025
10.2% → Q2 FY2026 **9.3%** (−180bp). Net debt/adj EBITDA ≈ **3.4-3.6x** (judgment:
$1,633M net debt ÷ FY2026 guide $453-481M), covenant holiday in effect to Oct 2027.

**What the row says:** through FY2025 both melted (Bimbo NA worse on sales); in H1 2026
the two series **crossed** — Bimbo NA turned to growth and share gains in FLO's core
category (mainstream bread) with margins expanding through FLO's level, while FLO's
volume decline accelerated to −7.6% branded and its margin fell through Bimbo's. Bimbo
carries under 2.6x leverage with 9.5-year maturities against FLO's ~3.5x on a covenant
holiday. The attacker's test [E2-45] is not hypothetical: the best-capitalized attacker
is taking the share now, in the filed/issued record of both companies.

## Row 2 — J.M. Smucker (NYSE: SJM), Sweet Baked Snacks segment (Hostess) — category context
Source: SJM Form 10-K FY2026 (year ended 2026-04-30), filed 2026-06-09, acc.
0000091419-26-000050.
- Segment organic net sales FY2026: **−7%** ex-divestitures ("Volume/mix decreased net
  sales by 8 percentage points... snack cakes, private label, and breakfast"; price +1pp).
- Q3 FY2026: **$961.7M pre-tax impairment** — $507.5M goodwill (the **full remaining
  goodwill** of the Sweet Baked Snacks reporting unit) + $454.2M Hostess trademark, which
  was then **reclassified from indefinite- to finite-lived**. Filing language: "sustained
  underperformance of the sweet baked goods category."
- Context: SJM acquired Hostess in November 2023 for ~$5.6B; within three fiscal years
  the goodwill is fully written off.

**What the row says:** the adjacent packaged sweet-baked category is melting at ~−7-8%
volume with the acquirer's capital destroyed — the FY2025-26 base rate for "buy a
packaged-bakery brand platform with debt" (the shape of FLO's Simple Mills bet, though
Simple Mills itself is still growing).

## Row 3 — Private label (the retailer, [E5-35])
Measured through FLO's own 10-K (quoted above): **26% of dollars, 35% of units** of US
retail fresh packaged bread, expanding since FY2022. The ledger's own Kirkland passage
[E5-35 source] observes private label reaching "35 percent, even, maybe 40" of some
markets — **US bread units are already at the top of that observed band.** The branded
side's answer must be differentiation (DKB organic, Canyon gluten-free, Simple Mills);
the mainstream loaf competes directly with a retailer product at roughly half the price,
and FLO's own filing says consumers are trading down to it.
