# VISA COMPETITOR ROW - Mastercard (MA) and American Express (AXP), Discover appendix
**Research note for Q2 (franchise). A moat is a relative claim; this is the relative row.**
Compiled 2026-08-28 under work order dated 2026-08-26. Every figure is from an SEC filing and
carries its accession number in the source register below. Nothing is adjusted or annualized;
window mismatches are stated, not repaired.

**Fiscal window caveat, stated once.** Visa's fiscal year ends September 30 (FY2025 = Oct 2024
to Sep 2025). Mastercard and American Express report calendar years ended December 31, so
"FY2025" columns are offset by one quarter. A second Visa-internal offset: Visa's payments
volume table is measured over the twelve months ended June 30 (its service revenue in a fiscal
year is assessed on volume through the prior June 30); its processed transactions and all P&L
lines are the fiscal year ended September 30. Both offsets are carried as-is below.

**Who Visa names.** Visa 10-K FY2025, Item 1, Competition: "Our electronic payment competitors
principally include the ones listed below." Under Global or Multi-regional Networks: "Examples
include American Express, Diners Club/Discover (now owned by Capital One), JCB, Mastercard and
UnionPay." The section also names local/regional networks (NYCE, Pulse, STAR, Interac, eftpos),
RTP networks (FedNow, PIX, UPI), digital wallet providers, alternative payments providers
(BNPL, stablecoins), payment processors and value-added-service providers. The filed-network
row below covers the named networks that file with the SEC: Mastercard and American Express in
full, Discover to its final standalone filing. JCB and UnionPay do not file with the SEC (see
section 4).

Visa's 10-K names "Diners Club/Discover (now owned by Capital One)". Capital One's acquisition

## 6. WHAT THE ROW SHOWS (report, not a verdict)

On every line the filings permit, Visa is the largest named network by a wide multiple:
roughly 1.5x Mastercard on cash-inclusive volume ($16.4T vs $10.6T), transactions (257.5B
processed vs 175.5B switched) and cards (~5B vs 3.4B), 1.2x on net revenue ($40.0B vs
$32.8B), with the two four-party networks' filed margins within a few points of each other
(60.0% vs 57.6% in the 2025 windows, Visa's after a $2.6B litigation provision) and everyone
else either an order of magnitude smaller (AmEx $1.9T network volume, Discover $0.6T) or a
different business model whose filings do not produce the same lines. The row also shows
Mastercard compounding each scale metric slightly faster from the smaller base (net revenue
+16% vs +11%, GDV +9% local vs Visa payments volume +8% constant, in the 2025 windows), and
it says nothing about pricing power, interchange regulation or the RTP/wallet flank that
Visa's own competition section spends most of its words on.
