## 2. Dollars against units, the Precision Steel pattern

HDMC segment revenue divided by worldwide retail units:

| year | HDMC revenue ($M) | retail units | revenue per unit |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 2019 | 4,572.7 (Motorcycles segment) | 218,273 | $20,949 |
| 2021 | 4,504.4 | 194,256 | $23,188 |
| 2022 | 4,887.7 | 178,451 | $27,389 |
| 2023 | 4,844.6 | 162,771 | **$29,763** |
| 2024 | 4,121.9 | 151,229 | $27,256 |
| 2025 | 3,578.3 | 132,535 | $26,999 |

**2019-2023: units -25%, dollars per unit +42%, so dollar revenue rose 6%.** That is
the price lever holding the dollar line up while the physical base fell, exactly
[E4-55]'s "a serious reverse, not likely to disappear in some 'bounce back' effect."

**2023-2025: units -19%, dollars per unit -9%. The price lever stopped working**, and
dollar revenue fell 26% in two years. HDMC segment operating income went from
$661.2M (2023) to **-$28.7M (2025)**.

count for 2024 (25,860) exceeds the whole EMEA *retail unit* figure for 2024
(24,082), and the 2025 Europe registration drop (-44%) does not reconcile to the
2025 EMEA retail drop (-10.9%). The two series are differently defined (registration
country set vs. retail-sales region) and the filing does not bridge them. **The
direction finding in this run rests on the U.S. series and the EMEA retail series,
which agree with each other**; the Europe registration series is reported as filed
and not leaned on.

## 5. Owner demographics: NOT DISCLOSED

HOG published customer-demographic language through the FY2015 10-K ("U.S. core
customers as Caucasian men over the age of 35"; outreach segments defined by age,
sex and ethnicity). **No median or average owner age appears in any 10-K read for
this run (FY2015, FY2019, FY2021, FY2023, FY2024, FY2025).** The word "aging" in
those filings refers to employee-wellbeing programs and patent portfolios.

**Verdict: UNRESEARCHED as to a filing; UNKNOWABLE from filings alone.** The
document that would resolve it is a third-party owner survey (Motorcycle Industry
Council owner-demographics study, or S&P Global Mobility / Polk new-registration
demographic cuts), **not a filing, and paid**. The 10-K discloses the *consequence*
instead, and that is a filed fact worth more than the age number: the used-Harley
market is named by the company as a substitute that reduces demand for new bikes:

> "when the supply of used motorcycles increases or the prices for used
> Harley-Davidson motorcycles decline, there can be reduced demand among retail
> purchasers for new Harley-Davidson motorcycles at or near manufacturer's suggested
> retail prices" (FY2025 10-K, Item 1A)
