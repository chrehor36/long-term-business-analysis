Support for `Test Runs/2026-08-30 Run - CCS (Century Communities) v4.1.md`, Q2.
Metric: **return on equity (NI ÷ average total stockholders' equity), latest fiscal year
and 5-year mean (FY2021-FY2025)**, the [E2-01] primary test run as the same-metric peer
row [E3-28]. Chosen because homebuilding is the commodity class [E2-58]: the only moat
the class admits is a cost advantage "wide and sustainable," and a persistent superior
return on capital across the supply-ample years is the only filing-visible evidence such
an advantage can leave.

data is transcription and screening; it decides no verdict. The SUBJECT's figures were

Step 0 of the run file). Fiscal windows differ and are stated per row: DHI ends Sep,

## What the row gives and takes

- **Gives:** CCS's 5-yr mean (18.0%) is mid-pack; its boom years were real. Its balance
  sheet (34.2% homebuilding debt/capital at Q2-2026) is ordinary for the set.
- **Takes:** in the supply-ample year, the year that reveals cost position in a
  commodity class [E2-58], CCS earns **8th of 9**, ahead of only LGI Homes, the
  pure-play entry-level spec comparable. The entry-level spec END of the industry is
  the WORST-performing end of the row in the down year (CCS 5.7%, LGIH 3.5%), while
  the structural-advantage names the July 6-pack passed on disclosed claims (NVR's
  lot-option model, DHI's scale) hold 14.5-33.2%. The "entry-level scale" claim
  therefore reads as segment exposure, not cost advantage: when affordability is the
  binding constraint, the affordability-segment builder discounts first and deepest.
- Same-window subject margin series (filed, FY2025 10-K and FY2019 10-K): homebuilding
  gross margin 17.5% (2018) · 17.7% (2019) · ~24% (2021 peak; "580 basis point
  increase" over 2020, FY2021 10-K) · 21.5% (2024) · 17.6% (2025) · 18.0% (H1-2026).
  The boom margin round-tripped to the pre-boom level; peers' trough ROEs above show
  who kept a cushion.
- Row limit [E3-61]: the row shows position, not conduct. It cannot say whether the
  industry prices like a demented Kellogg next cycle.
