## THE AMAZON GAP, recorded honestly per the operator's instruction

**Amazon's FY2025 10-K contains zero occurrences of "pet food", "pet supplies",
"pet products", " pets" or "Pet "** (checked mechanically over the full 324,891-character
converted text of `amzn-20251231.htm`; the same file returns 10 hits for "Prime" and 2
for "grocery", so the conversion is sound). Amazon reports three segments (North America,
International, AWS) and a product/service disaggregation: Online stores $269,287M,
Third-party seller services $172,162M, Subscription services $49,619M, Advertising,
Physical stores. **Pet is not separable at any level of Amazon's disclosure, and no
filing will ever make it separable.**

Per the absence-claim rule: *no pet-category disclosure found* in Amazon's, Walmart's or
Target's filed reports. This is **not UNRESEARCHED**. There is no document to fetch and
there never will be. It is a permanent measurement limit on the most important cell in
the row, and the run states it as such.

What can be said from filings: Amazon's North America segment did $426,305M of net sales
at a 6.95% operating margin in FY2025, which is 34x Chewy's revenue at 3.4x Chewy's
operating margin, inside a fulfilment network that also carries third-party sellers,
advertising and Prime. Chewy's own 10-K names "other online retailers", "online sales for
omnichannel retailers" and "our suppliers' own websites" as competitors, and lists
**price first** among "the principal competitive factors influencing our business."

## THE UNITS SERIES [E4-55]: the honest one

> Precision Steel's pounds fell 69M to 46M while price rises held dollar revenue level:
> *"a serious reverse, not likely to disappear in some 'bounce back' effect."* **[E4-55]**

Chewy's physical series is **active customers**. The dollar series is **net sales per
active customer (NSPAC)**. Both are defined in the 10-K and filed every quarter.

Sources: FY2022 10-K (0001766502-23-000011) for FY2020 to FY2022; FY2024 10-K
(0001766502-25-000014) for FY2022 to FY2024; FY2025 10-K (0001766502-26-000034) for
FY2023 to FY2025; Q1 FY2026 10-Q (0001628280-26-042060).

**What the two series say together.** The customer count peaked in FY2021 at 20.663M,
fell for **two consecutive years**, and did not exceed the COVID-cohort peak until
**FY2025, four years later**. Across those same four years net sales never fell, because
NSPAC rose 15.6%, then 15.1%, then 11.9%. The dollar line was carried by price and mix
while the physical line went backwards, which is the exact pattern [E4-55] tells you to
read as the honest one. It has since turned: units +4.0% and +3.6% while NSPAC growth has
fallen to roughly 2%. **The engine has swapped from price and mix to units, and the price
and mix half is now nearly exhausted.**

The COVID distortion [E4-41] is named. FY2020 to FY2021 carried a pet-adoption boom
(19.2M to 20.7M actives, +7.6%); FY2022 and FY2023 are its normalisation. Any growth rate
drawn across FY2020 to FY2023 is a calculated-endpoint artifact [E4-38].

## THE PRICING TEST [E2-44, E4-37, E3-33, E5-28]

- **Can it raise price with demand flat? No filed evidence.** Gross margin did widen
  (28.4%, 29.2%, 29.8%, then 30.1% in Q1 FY2026), but the 10-K attributes it to
  *"growth in sponsored ads and margin growth across our consumables business"*, that is,
  to **advertising revenue charged back to the brands** and to mix, not to a price rise.
  Sponsored-ads revenue is **not separately quantified** in the filing, which is itself a
  disclosure gap.
- **[E4-37] agony test.** Chewy's own competition paragraph puts price first among
  competitive factors and names suppliers' own websites as competitors. That is the agony
  end of the scale, not the yawn end.
- **[E3-33] untapped pricing power, scoped by [E5-28].** Claiming that class claims
  near-monopoly. Chewy is not one, and the row shows why: identical third-party goods are
  available at Amazon, Walmart, Target, Petco and the brands' own sites. **The class is
  not claimed.**

## THE SUPPLIER SIDE [E5-35]: Kirkland, in reverse

Buffett's Kirkland passage is about the *retailer's* brand taking power from the national
brands. Run in reverse on Chewy:

- **Chewy's three largest vendors were 39% of net sales in FY2025, FY2024 and FY2023.**
  10-K Note 2, verbatim: *"Sales of products from the Company's three largest vendors
  represented approximately 39%, 39%, and 39% of the Company's net sales."* Flat for three
  years. The concentration is on the *supplier* side.
- Chewy has run private brands since 2016 (Frisco, American Journey, Tylee's, Vibeful in
  2022, Get Real in 2025) and **discloses no private-brand share of sales, in any year**.
  Costco names Kirkland and quantifies it. Petco lists its owned brands (WholeHearted,
  Reddy, Well & Good, So Phresh, You & Me, Vetco) across roughly 1,400 stores. After a
  decade, Chewy's non-disclosure is itself the reading: **the retailer's own brand is not
  yet the source of the economics.**
- Freshpet is the brand-side control in this row. It earns **40.8% gross margin and 6.87%
  operating margin on $1.1bn**, under a tenth of Chewy's sales at three times Chewy's
  operating margin, bought at a capex burden (13.45% of sales) Chewy does not carry.
  **The premium margin in this category sits with the manufacturer, not the retailer.**

## THE ATTACKER'S TEST [E2-45]

*"how I would like, assuming I had ample capital and skilled personnel, to compete with
it."*

I would be Amazon, and Amazon already is. It has more customers, a denser network, an
existing subscribe-and-save mechanic, and it sells the identical Purina, Hill's and Blue
Buffalo bags. The one part of Chewy that is genuinely hard to copy is the **regulated
pharmacy and vet workflow**: state pharmacy licensure, compounding, and PracticeHub at
*"approximately 20,000 veterinary practices … an estimated 50% of all U.S. vet clinics"*
(FY2025 10-K). Pet health and specialty was $1,980.5M, **15.7% of sales, +11.8%**. Growing,
but not yet the business.

The row's verdict on the attack so far: Chewy has **won share**. It grew 6.2% while Petco
shrank 2.5% and closed stores. That is real and the run states it. What the row also shows
is the **price** of winning it. After fifteen years and $12.6bn of revenue, Chewy's
operating margin is 2.02%, identical to two decimal places to that of the shrinking,
leveraged, store-based competitor it is beating. The share gain has not converted into an
owner's return. [E3-62]'s second step asks *"how much is going to stay home and how much
is just going to flow through to the customer"*, and the filed answer, for fifteen years,
is **flow through**.
