## THE HEADLINE FINDING, BEFORE ANY NUMBER

**Both public peers have effectively exited the heavyweight motorcycle business Harley
competes in, and one of them paid to leave.**

> **Polaris Inc., the disclosed number two in North American 900cc+ motorcycles, sold
> Indian Motorcycle to Carolwood LP for "a nominal sales price," took $342.5M of charges,
> wrote On Road segment goodwill to zero, and recorded a NET CASH OUTFLOW of $79.3M on the
> disposal.** Announced 2025-10-10; closed **2026-02-02**.
> Source: Polaris FY2025 10-K, acc. 0001628280-26-008033, Note 4; 8-K acc. 0001628280-25-044839;
> Q2 2026 10-Q, acc. 0001628280-26-050104

That is a **market-clearing price observation on a heavyweight motorcycle franchise**, made
by a rational, well-capitalised operator, at arm's length, ten months ago. It is the single
most important fact in this row and it belongs at Q2, Q4 and Q5 of the HOG run.

## 3. THE 601+cc QUESTION, and a caution on Harley's own series

**Neither Polaris nor BRP cites the Motorcycle Industry Council or any 601+cc registration
series anywhere.** Both state that industry figures are internal management estimates.
Polaris verbatim: *"There are limited sources that report on our markets and industries…
much of the market and industry data presented in this Annual Report is based on
internally-generated management estimates."* An EDGAR full-text search 2023-2026 finds
601+cc filings are **essentially Harley's alone** (HOG, its LiveWire subsidiary, and H
Partners' proxy materials).

**Consequence, recorded honestly:** the 601+cc series that anchors the market-share finding
in the HOG run is **single-sourced from Harley's own filings** (MIC for the US, Management
Services Helwig Schmitt GmbH for Europe) and is not independently corroborated at the same
definition by any peer filing. Polaris's independently constructed "NA 900cc+
cruiser/touring/standard" series shows **the same direction**, 190,000 (2020) to roughly
156,000 (2025), which corroborates the **trend** but on a different definition and
geography. The two series are not interchangeable, and the run does not treat them as such.

## 4. WHAT THE ROW ESTABLISHES FOR THE HOG RUN

1. **The category is shrinking on every independently constructed measure.** Polaris's NA
   900cc+ estimate: −18% 2021→2024 and down again in 2025. BRP's global 3WV: **−29% in two
   years**. BRP's global electric motorcycle: ~6,000 units, −18%. Harley's own MIC-sourced
   US 601+cc: −7.4% in two years. **Four independent estimates, one direction.**
2. **A rational, well-capitalised competitor concluded a heavyweight motorcycle franchise
   was worth approximately nothing.** Polaris, the disclosed **#2** in North American
   900cc+, accepted a *nominal* price, absorbed $342.5M of charges, wrote the segment
   goodwill to zero, and **paid $79.3M of net cash** to hand the business to private equity.
   **[E2-45]**'s attacker's test is normally hypothetical; here the incumbent attacker
   answered it by leaving.
3. **Neither peer is a franchise in motorcycles.** Polaris has left. BRP's overlap is a
   shrinking 3WV niche plus a sub-scale EV line it has just impaired by CAD 229.8M and
   explicitly slowed investment in.
4. **The peers' non-motorcycle powersports businesses are recovering while motorcycles are
   not.** Polaris Q2 2026 gross margin 23.7% and operating margin 7.4%; BRP Q1 FY2027
   revenue +29.5% with gross margin 23.5%; both cut inventories ~17% and are seeing retail
   stabilise. **The distress is specific to heavyweight two-wheel, not to discretionary
   powersports generally.** That materially weakens any "it is only the cycle" framing for
   Harley. **[E3-61]** limits what a row can show, but this is the one thing a row shows
   best: whether the trouble is the industry or the company.
5. **Financing structures differ fundamentally**, which is why Harley's consolidated margins
   are not comparable to either peer's without the two-business separation the run performs:
   Harley **consolidates** HDFS; Polaris uses an **equity-method JV**; BRP has **no captive
   at all**.
6. **On the dividend**, the contrast is stark and it bears on Stage 0: through the same
   industry collapse, **Polaris preserved a 31-year increase streak** (paying $150.3M into a
   $465.5M loss year) while **Harley cut 94.7% and remains at half its 2019 rate.**

**Peers taken: 2 of roughly 6 real competitors.** BMW Motorrad, Honda, Yamaha and
Kawasaki/Suzuki report motorcycles inside conglomerate segments and are **not retrievable at
a comparable metric**, that shortfall is a named work order (annual reports at
bmwgroup.com, global.honda, global.yamaha-motor.com, khi.co.jp; English, free, but the
segment definitions do not align). The row is therefore supplemented by the industry
registration series, which captures all of them at once in the only metric that matters
here: **units, and share of units.**

**Row limit stated [E3-61]:** *"In some businesses, the participants behave like a demented
Kellogg. In other businesses, they don't … I think you'd have to know the people involved."*
The row shows position; it cannot show conduct.
