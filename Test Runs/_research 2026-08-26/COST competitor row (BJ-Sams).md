## What the row shows
1. **Productivity per box is the moat metric, and the gap is enormous.** Costco moves
   $272M through an average warehouse — ~1.8x Sam's ($155M) and ~3.4x BJ's ($80M). Volume
   per SKU is what lets an 11.1% gross margin cover costs; BJ's needs 16.7%. A member
   sees the gap as price.
2. **The fee pool concentrates at Costco.** Costco collects $5.3B of membership fees —
   more than 2x Sam's "membership and other" and 10.6x BJ's — at a **higher** sticker
   ($65/$130 vs $60/$120) and a **higher** renewal rate (92.3% US/Can vs BJ's 90%; Sam's
   silent). The costlier club keeps its members better.
3. **Both fee increases landed, at both filers that disclose them.** Costco raised
   Sept 2024 (renewal 92.9% → 92.3% US/Can, with the filing attributing the drift to
   online-sold membership mix, not the price); BJ's raised Jan 2025 (renewal held at 90%).
   Fee increases in this industry do not break retention — evidence for the [E3-33]
   untapped-pricing-power reading and Buffett's no-agony test [E4-37].
4. **Growth is not being bought with margin.** Costco's comps (+8% ex gas/FX) lead both
   peers (+2.6% BJ merch, +2.9% Sam's incl fuel) while its operating margin holds ~3.9%.
5. **Same-model contrast in conduct** (noted, not scored here): BJ's summary table leads
   with Adjusted EBITDA and Adjusted EPS; Costco's 10-K contains **zero** occurrences of
   EBITDA (checked mechanically across the full document text).

**The row's limit [E3-61]:** identical structures produce different outcomes; the row
shows *position*, not conduct or destiny. Sam's Club sits inside Walmart's balance sheet
— the best-capitalized attacker imaginable has run this attack for four decades and holds
57% of Costco's per-club volume at a lower operating margin.
