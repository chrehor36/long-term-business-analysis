# TABLE TRAC COMPETITOR ROW - casino management systems (LNW, IGT, Konami; Aristocrat appendix)
**Research note for Q2 (franchise). A moat is a relative claim; this is the relative row.**
Compiled 2026-08-28 under work order dated 2026-08-26. Every figure carries its source in the
register below. Nothing is adjusted, annualized or currency-converted; window mismatches are
stated, not repaired.

**Fiscal window caveat, stated once.** TBTC and Light & Wonder report calendar years ended
December 31. IGT's last filing containing the casino-systems business is the FY2024 20-F, so
its three-year window (2024/2023/2022) trails the others by one year. Konami's fiscal year
ends March 31 (its "FY2026" = Apr 2025 to Mar 2026, offset one quarter forward). Aristocrat's
ends September 30. All carried as-is.

**Who TBTC names: nobody.** The FY2025 10-K's entire competition section, verbatim: "There is
intense competition in the gaming management and gaming products industry which is
characterized by dynamic customer demand and rapid technological advances. Today, there are
many systems providers in the U.S. and abroad offering casinos and gaming operators total
solution casino management and table games management systems." The risk factors add, without
names: "Several of our competitors are larger, have greater name recognition, longer operating
histories, larger marketing budgets and significantly greater resources than we do, and are
able to devote greater resources to the development, promotion and sale of their products and
services." No competitor is named anywhere in the filing (full-text check: IGT, Light &
Wonder, Konami, Everi, Aristocrat, Agilysys, Scientific Games, Bally, Oracle all absent).

**So the peer set is taken from the peer side's filings.** Light & Wonder's FY2025 10-K, Item
1, names the CMS field: "Our principal competitors in CMSs include Aristocrat, IGT and
Konami." The row below is therefore LNW's filed list plus LNW itself: the four firms a filed
document identifies as the casino-management-systems competitive set. This is a documented
substitution, not TBTC's own list, because TBTC publishes none.

**Structural note on IGT.** On July 1, 2025 Apollo funds acquired IGT's Gaming & Digital
business (which contains the Advantage casino management system) and Everi Holdings
simultaneously; the combined private company operates under the IGT name, and the public
filer IGT PLC, retaining only lottery, was renamed Brightstar Lottery PLC. After calendar
2025, LNW is the only SEC-filing CMS competitor left; IGT's figures below are its last filed
years, and no continuation of that series will exist.

Aggregation, stated honestly:
- The **LNW line is the only CMS-only comparable** in any filing. It bundles nothing: LNW
  defines it as installation and support of CMSs plus related hardware (iVIEW) and
  maintenance. At $285M it is **26x TBTC's entire company**.
- The **IGT line is a whole division**: gaming terminals, game sales, iGaming and sports
  betting, with the Advantage CMS inside and never broken out. Cost lines are as allocated in
  the discontinued-operations presentation.
- The **Konami segment** is "Production, manufacture, sale and service of gaming machines and
  casino management systems" (their definition, verbatim) - slots plus SYNKROS, CMS share not
  broken out.
- The **Aristocrat Gaming segment** is the land-based business (slots plus systems); CMS
  revenue is not separately disclosed anywhere in the profit announcement.

## 6. WHAT THE ROW SHOWS (report, not a verdict)

TBTC sells casino management systems against three named giants and one direct filer whose
CMS-only revenue line alone is 26x TBTC's entire company ($285M vs $11.0M), while the other
peers' relevant divisions run from roughly ¥43B to A$4.0B with the CMS piece never broken
out, and TBTC's own filed installed base sits flat at 115 operators across all three years
even as it delivers eight systems a year. What the filings equally make plain is that no
peer files a number against TBTC's two best lines - a 70-74% company gross margin and a
maintenance-revenue base now at 56% of sales - and that the field's structure just changed
underneath the row: the largest competitor's systems business went private mid-2025, so from
2026 on this comparison can be rebuilt from filings for only one of the four named peers.
