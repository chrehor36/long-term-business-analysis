## 2. THE COMPETITOR ROW [E3-28]

Three peers taken. **Federal Signal** is the closest comparable in ALG's largest segment
(street sweepers, vacuum trucks, safety/vocational truck equipment). **Toro** is the closest
in professional turf and grounds equipment sold through dealers to grounds, golf and
municipal buyers. **Douglas Dynamics** is the closest in snow and ice control and municipal
work-truck attachments. No peer filing was unavailable, so the moat class is **not
provisional**.

Denominator definitions, identical on all four sides:
- **Unleveraged net tangible operating assets [E2-43]** = net PP&E + rental equipment, net +
  inventories + trade receivables − trade payables.
- **Total invested capital** = the above **plus goodwill and intangible assets, net**, i.e.
  including the price paid for the acquisitions. Reported separately, never hidden.

### What the row says
1. **ALG has the lowest operating margin of the four** — 9.45%, against Federal Signal's
   15.63% and Douglas Dynamics' 11.22%. Toro's 9.09% is lower only because it carries an
   $81.1M trade-name impairment; ex-impairment Toro is 10.89%.
2. **ALG has the lowest return on unleveraged net tangible operating capital of the four**,
   by nine to ten points: **19.9% against 27.3% to 29.8%.**
3. **ALG has the lowest return on total invested capital including goodwill of the four**:
   13.5% pre-tax against 15.1% to 17.5%.
4. **ALG has the lowest return on equity of the four, by roughly half** — 9.6% against 17.2%
   to 21.0% — and it is **not** a leverage artifact, because ALG also carries by far the
   **least** debt relative to equity (0.18x against 0.41x to 0.63x). ALG loses the row on
   the unleveraged measure too.
5. **The one place ALG leads is acquisition restraint**: goodwill and intangibles are 22.4%
   of assets against 37.2% and 41.9% at the two acquisitive peers. ALG has paid less for
   what it bought. It still earns less on the total.
6. **The row's limit [E3-61]:** it shows position, not conduct. Federal Signal made a large
   acquisition in the window (Trackless, C$56.3M initial cash) and grew net sales 17% to a
   record $2.18bn while ALG's fell 1.5%. Whether that is Federal Signal executing better or
   Federal Signal buying growth is a judgment about people the row cannot make.

**Note on rental equipment.** ALG puts rental-fleet **additions** inside operating activities
(the "Rental equipment" working-capital line) and adds rental depreciation back there too.
Operating cash flow is therefore already net of rental capex, and (c) needs to cover **PP&E
only**. Over 2021-2025 the rental line was a net **−$65.6M** use of cash.

**Working capital is the dominant swing factor.** Net working capital (receivables +
inventories − payables) as a share of revenue:

| 2019 | 2020 | 2021 | 2022 | 2023 | 2024 | 2025 | 2026-06-30 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 37.8% | 32.4% | 34.3% | 37.8% | 37.9% | 34.7% | 33.4% | **37.8%** |

Roughly **35 cents of working capital per incremental dollar of revenue**, structurally.
2021-2022 absorbed $215.5M as the boom was funded; 2024 released $33.4M as sales fell;
H1 2026 absorbed $94.2M again (receivables +$62.9M, inventories +$31.3M), which is why
H1 2026 operating cash flow was only **$22.7M** against $36.9M a year earlier.

### 3.4 Owner earnings [E2-23] — CONVENTION: mean OCF − mean SBC − (c)

(c) is a **disclosed judgment**. The band runs from the corpus default — **D&A, here PP&E
depreciation only [E3-44, E2-41]** — to **total PP&E capex plus patents**. ALG is **not** in
the [E5-20] capital-intensive exception class: capex is 1.9% of revenue and averages 1.24x
depreciation, and the filing nowhere says depreciation understates renewal. Both ends of the
band are therefore legitimate.
