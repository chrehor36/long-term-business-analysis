## 4. STAGE 0(c): BOOM/BUST WINDOW ARTIFACTS [E4-41]

The five-year window FY2021–FY2025 contains **both** a favourable exogenous break and an
unfavourable one. Both are named and quantified from the filings.

**Favourable (must be normalised out per [E4-41]):**
1. **COVID-suppressed utilisation.** MCR 82.5% (2019) → **79.1% (2020)** → 82.6% (2021).
   The 2020 figure is 340bp below the 2019 baseline on $201,478M of premiums = roughly
   **$6.8bn of pre-tax earnings that deferred care handed the company**. 2020 is outside
   the five-year window but inside the seven-year one, and the deferral tail runs into 2021.
2. **Prior-year reserve releases.** Favourable development, filed each year: $580M (2019),
   $880M (2020), **$1,700M (2021)**, $410M (2022), $840M (2023), $700M (2024),
   **$140M (2025)**, and **$1,250M in H1 2026 alone**. The 2021 release is the largest in
   the series and is a direct COVID artifact.
3. **2026's recovery is partly the same mechanism running again.** H1 2026 prior-year
   favourable development $1,250M against $320M in H1 2025; Q2 2026 net favourable
   development $860M (the release says *"the majority related to 2026 dates of service"*).
   The Q2 2026 release also attributes the $11.1bn quarterly operating cash flow to
   *"the timing of a substantial government payment."*
4. **The Q4 2025 loss-contract reserve is releasing into 2026 earnings.** $623M established
   in Q4 2025 for *"anticipated future losses in 2026"*; H1 2026 shows a *"net decrease in
   loss contract reserves of $50 million and $187 million"* for the quarter and half.

**Unfavourable (must NOT be treated as permanent without saying so):**
5. **FY2025 itself.** MCR 89.1%, operating margin 4.2% against 8.7% in 2023, plus a
   $2,878M pre-tax fourth-quarter charge ($1.78/share).

**Conclusion of leg (c): the window is distorted at both ends.** The 2021–23 earnings level
was flattered by deferred care and outsized reserve releases; the 2025 level was depressed
by a pricing error and a one-time charge; and the 2026 recovery contains identifiable
reserve release and payment-timing benefit. **No single window is trustworthy, which is why
five are displayed in §5.**

## 5. OWNER EARNINGS: METHOD AND SERIES

**Convention (framework):** multi-year mean of (operating cash flow − share-based
compensation) − (c), where (c) is a disclosed judgment.

**(c) judged = total capital expenditure.** UNH is the [E2-41] 95% class, not the [E5-20]
capital-intensive exception (net PP&E and capitalised software $10,762M against $447,567M
of revenue; capex 0.81% of revenue). But the **D&A end of the band is refused**, because
$1.7bn of the $4,361M D&A line is acquired-intangible amortisation that renews nothing
(Note 6: *"Amortization expense relating to intangible assets … was $1.7 billion"*). Cash
depreciation ex-intangibles is roughly $2.7bn, **below** capex of $3,622M. Using D&A would
import a charge that buys nothing and would flatter owner earnings. **(c) = capex.**

**Float strip, and why.** [E2-61] and [E3-69] scope the insurer's read: cash-based scoring
is invalid, and float is borrowed money, not earnings. UNH's operating cash flow is inflated
each year by growth in the claims-payable pool. The two float lines that can be isolated
from the filed cash-flow statement are **medical costs payable** and **unearned revenues**.
They are stripped. **What cannot be isolated is disclosed:** "accounts payable and other
liabilities" (+$1,665M in 2025) contains customer funds, rebates payable and CMS-related
accruals that are also float-like, and "other current receivables" (−$3,608M in 2025)
contains CMS risk-adjustment and Part D receivables that run the other way. **Neither is
separable from the filed lines; the net of the two would reduce owner earnings further in
most years, so the disclosed error runs in the conservative direction.**

**Cost of float [E3-69], the corpus's own sector number, computed and reported.** UnitedHealthcare's
earnings from operations were $16,415M (2023), $15,584M (2024) and $9,425M (2025) against a
medical-costs-payable pool of $32,395M, $34,224M and $39,337M. **UNH's cost of float is
deeply negative, it is paid 24% to 48% a year to hold it.** But the reading that matters is
the opposite of Berkshire's: **UNH does not earn its return by investing float.** Investment
and other income was $3,920M of $447,567M of revenue (0.88%) and 13–21% of pre-tax income.
The float is a 39-day claim lag on a $314bn cost base, not the business model. **The
sector method therefore governs the liquidity read (§7) but not the valuation.**
