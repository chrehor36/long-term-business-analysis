## WHAT THE ROW ESTABLISHES

**1. This is a category verdict, not a company verdict. Every one of the five is losing
units and buying dollars with price.** Not one of the five grew volume in its latest
fiscal year. Four of the five took a GAAP operating-margin collapse in the latest year, and
in four of five it is impairment-driven. **The branded centre-store franchise is narrowing
across the industry** — which is [E4-32]'s direction test answered at the category level.
SJM's own 10-K names the mechanism: *"The packaged foods industry has been challenged by a
general long-term decline in sales volume in the center of the store."*

**2. SJM took more price than anyone and gave up more volume than most.** +9 pp of price
against −4 pp of volume in FY2026, versus GIS −1/−1, CAG +1.0/−1.4, HSY +6/−1, KHC
+0.7/−4.1. The pricing power is real and is at the top of the peer set — **and the
adjusted operating margin still fell 240 bp**, because in coffee the price went to the
green-coffee market, not home [E3-62]. Hershey is the mirror image and the confirming
case: +6% price, gross margin down **1,380 bp** on cocoa, Q2 2026 confection volume
**−10 pts** against **+14 pts** of price, US CMG takeaway −8.6% with share loss. **Two
branded converters of a traded soft commodity, both of whom own the trademark and neither
of whom owns the economics.**

**3. SJM's profitability is at the top of the peer set; its customer concentration is the
worst.** Adjusted operating margin 18.5% ranks second of five (KHC 19.02%, SJM 18.5%, GIS
15.3%, CAG 11.3%). Walmart at **34% of net sales** is the highest concentration in the row
(CAG 29%, HSY/McLane 27%, GIS 22%, KHC 21%), with **no long-term supply contracts with any
major customer** — the [E5-35] Kirkland passage applied to the most exposed supplier in the
set.

**4. Private label is a rising, named competitor for everyone, and only SJM quantifies
it.** SJM: 14.0% dollar share (from 13.7%) in US Retail + Sweet Baked Snacks, 22.2% in Away
From Home. GIS, CAG and KHC carry it as a risk factor only — KHC's wording is a near-twin
of SJM's. **Hershey is the outlier: "store brand", "value-seeking" and "trade down" do not
appear in its filings at all,** and where private label appears Hershey is the *supplier*.
For context, the FLO run's subject faced private label at **26% of dollars / 35% of units**
— SJM's exposure is materially lower.

**5. The write-down cycle has a leader and a laggard.** Hershey's three-year impairment
total is **$6.4M**; Kraft Heinz's is **$13.6bn plus $7.4bn in H1 2026**, with **$18.2bn of
its remaining $19.7bn of goodwill (92%) carrying under 5% headroom** and $9.3bn of brands
at **zero** headroom (Kraft, Oscar Mayer, Miracle Whip, Cool Whip, Lunchables, Kool Aid,
Claussen, ABC, Wattie's, Bagel Bites). Kraft written down twice in thirteen months ($1.9bn
then $3.4bn); Lunchables in three consecutive years. Conagra's Refrigerated & Frozen unit
and several indefinite-lived brands now carry **zero excess fair value** and the filing
concedes *"a heightened risk of future impairments."* SJM sits in the middle: $2,944.2M
gone, but **all of it in one segment (11% of sales) which now carries no goodwill at all**,
and *"the estimated fair value exceeded the carrying value by greater than 10 percent for
all of our reporting units and indefinite-lived intangible assets, with the exception of
the Pup-Peroni brand."* **SJM's remaining balance sheet is better cushioned than KHC's or
CAG's.**

**6. The corpus's own worked case is live and got worse.** Kraft Heinz's 2018 $15.4bn
write-down was **restated upward to $15.9bn** with disclosed material weaknesses in
internal control (KHC 10-K FY2018, acc. 0001637459-19-000049). Accumulated goodwill
impairment: $11.8bn (2023) → $13.5bn (2024) → $20.2bn (2025) → **$22.6bn (Q2 2026)**. The
separation announced 2025-09-02 was **paused 2026-02-11**, one day before the 10-K was
filed, after $126M of spend. Management's own stated cause of the impairment: *"the market
remains uncertain as to the Company's ability to achieve the plan."* **This is the base
rate for a levered branded-food roll-up that pays up, and it is the reason SJM's Hostess
arithmetic is read the way it is at Q3.**

**7. The FLO pattern is arriving in this peer group.** **Conagra cut its quarterly dividend
50%** (from $0.35 to $0.175) on 2026-07-15, announced by a new CEO alongside *"enhancing
our financial flexibility"*, with net leverage 3.83× guided to ~4.0×. SJM at 3.31×,
deleveraging $720.0M in FY2026 with no leverage covenant and a $2.0bn undrawn revolver to
March 2030, is **the better-financed member of a deteriorating set** — which is a
statement about relative position, not about whether the class is worth owning.

**8. GLP-1 cannot be separated from elasticity in any of these filings.** The literal
string "GLP-1" appears in **none** of the four competitors' filings. GIS carries *"eating
habits **(including the impact of weight loss drugs)**"* and *"weight loss trends"* in its
FY2026 and FY2025 risk factors — unchanged language. HSY carries *"weight management (i.e.,
use of medications, dieting)"* in FY2025 and FY2024, and **drops it from the Q2 2026 10-Q**,
attributing the −10 pt confection volume entirely to *"price elasticity and normal
quarter-to-quarter shipment variability."* **UNKNOWABLE as a magnitude from primary
filings; a real possibility as an accelerant.** [E4-40]: model the exposure, not the
experience.
