## What the row shows, and what it does not

**Shows — the category did not shrink; Columbia's share of it did.** Two direct
competitors selling technical outdoor/active product to the same consumer through the
same channels under the same U.S. tariff regime grew revenue 74% and 114% respectively
over the same window, *with gross margins expanding 670bp and 850bp*, while Columbia grew
8.7% with gross margin flat and operating margin cut by more than half. The [E2-45]
attacker's test — *"how I would like, assuming I had ample capital and skilled personnel,
to compete with it"* — does not need to be imagined here; the attackers filed their
answers.

**Shows — Columbia is not the worst house on the street.** VFC and UAA are both smaller
and less profitable than they were, and UAA is loss-making at the operating line. COLM
still earns a positive 6.1% operating margin, 10.2% ROE, and carries no debt against
$790.8M of cash and short-term investments (2025-12-31). Recorded fairly per **[E4-26]**:
the mid-tier of this category is melting generally, and COLM is melting more slowly than
two of its four comparables.

**Does not show — conduct [E3-61].** *"In some businesses, the participants behave like a
demented Kellogg. In other businesses, they don't … I think you'd have to know the people
involved."* The row places Columbia in the middle of a five-name field with two winners
above it and two losers below. It cannot tell you whether the ACCELERATE strategy works.

**Named gap:** Patagonia — private, employee/trust-owned, no filed financial statements
located on EDGAR. Route if ever needed: none in the U.S. system; the company publishes
