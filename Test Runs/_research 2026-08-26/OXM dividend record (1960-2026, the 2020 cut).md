## The tasked question: did the lens's cut flag miss a suspension in 2020?
**Answer: there was never a suspension. There WAS a cut — 32.4%, spring 2020 — and the
company itself confirms continuous payment:** *"we have paid dividends each quarter since
we became a public company in July 1960"* (FY2025 10-K, Item 5 and MD&A, twice).

## What the growth number really is
- The lens's "~16.6% 5y dividend CAGR" is measured off the **cut-year base**. From FY2020's
  $1.00 to FY2025's $2.76 the 5-year CAGR is 22.5%; from the **pre-cut FY2019 rate ($1.48)**
  the 6-year CAGR is **10.9%**. Real growth, but roughly a third of the headline CAGR is
  recovery from the cut, not new ground.
- Trailing four declared quarters (0.69×3 + 0.70) = $2.77 → **7.2% trailing yield** at
  $38.31; forward run-rate $2.80 → **7.3%**. The lens's yield figure is confirmed.

## The coverage arithmetic the record now sits on (filed figures)
- FY2025: OCF $119.6M vs capex $108.3M + dividends $42.1M + open-market buybacks $55.2M
  (+$2.3M vesting withholding) — the gap went on the revolver: borrowings **$31M → $116M**
  (10-K: the increase in long-term debt "was the result of (1) lower net earnings,
  (2) capital expenditures ... (3) share repurchases, (4) payments of dividends and
  (5) working capital requirements exceeding cash flow from operations").
- Q1 FY2026: OCF $8M vs capex $23M + dividends $11M — borrowings **$116M → $143M**
  (Q1 press release, 8-K acc. 0000075288-26-000055).
- 10-K, in terms: *"We may borrow to fund dividends or repurchase shares in the short
  term."*
- Precedent: the one time OCF fell hard with obligations rising (spring 2020), the board
  cut within one quarter. The 1960-2026 never-missed record is a record of *payment*, not
  of *rate*.
