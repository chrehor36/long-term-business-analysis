## THE GAP THAT CANNOT BE FILLED AT THE SEC RUNG: name it [E3-28]

**Forest River, Inc.** is the #2 or #1 North American RV manufacturer by units depending on
the year and the category. It has been a wholly-owned **Berkshire Hathaway** subsidiary since
2005. Berkshire does not segment-report it: Forest River is consolidated inside Berkshire's
**Manufacturing** grouping with dozens of other businesses, and no revenue, margin, unit or
backlog figure for it is retrievable from any SEC filing. **Ladder rung: SEC EDGAR primary
documents. Blocked, permanently, by the parent's reporting structure.** No aggregator
substitute is admitted [operator rule 5].

The consequence is stated rather than papered over: **roughly a third of this industry's
capacity is invisible to this row**, and it is the third owned by a permanent-capital parent
with no quarterly guidance, no buyback programme and no stock price to defend. That is a
structural competitive fact about WGO's market that the filings cannot quantify. It does not
soften the row's findings, every one of which comes from the visible competitors. It means
the row is a floor on the competitive pressure, not a measure of it.

*(Secondary, smaller: REV Group (REVG) makes some recreational vehicles inside a mostly
specialty-vehicle business; not pulled, and its RV exposure is too small and too commingled to
carry a same-metric comparison. Recorded as a minor work order.)*

## 2. WHAT THE ROW SHOWS: THE VALUE CHAIN, NOT THE COMPANY

**The single most important number in this file: the component suppliers earn three times the
assemblers' margin, and they earn it in the same demand year.**

| | operating margin, latest FY | latest interim |
|---|---|---|
| **Assemblers**, WGO | **2.0%** | 2.4% |
| **Assemblers**, THO | 3.1% (derived) | 2.6% pretax |
| **Suppliers**, PATK | **7.0%** | 7.0% |
| **Suppliers**, LCII | **6.8%** | **9.3%** |

And the suppliers' revenue fell only ~20% from the 2022 peak against the assemblers' ~42%.
Two mechanisms, both filed:

1. **The suppliers pass the [E2-44] first test that WGO fails.** LCI discloses **content per
   RV**: **$5,097 → $5,670 per travel trailer / fifth wheel** between 2024 and 2025, **+11.2%**,
   and $3,742 → $3,993 per motorhome, **+6.7%**, in a flat-to-weak demand year. Over the same
   window WGO's towable ASP fell 5.4% and its 10-Ks record *"targeted price reductions"* and
   *"higher discounts and allowances."* **The supplier raised its price into the OEM's product
   while the OEM cut its price to the dealer.** That is the value migrating one step upstream,
   measured in the filings of both parties.
2. **The suppliers diversified out of RV and it worked.** Patrick's 2025 revenue splits RV
   $1,776.1M / Marine $606.4M / Powersports $384.1M / Manufactured Housing $681.5M / Industrial
   $502.7M. RV is 45% of the business. LCI runs an **Aftermarket** segment at $932.4M with
   $95.8M of segment operating profit. WGO's diversification (Barletta, Chris-Craft) took
   marine to 13% of revenue and one of the two was impaired.

**The Q2 consequence, stated plainly:** WGO occupies the low-margin assembly step of a value
chain in which the durable economics sit with a components oligopoly it does not control and
cannot bypass. This is [E3-62]'s second step run backwards, *"how much is going to stay home
and how much is just going to flow through to the customer"*, and in an assembly business
with over-capacity the answer is that it flows through, in both directions: to the supplier on
the way in and to the dealer on the way out.

## 3. THE UNIT TEST [E4-55]: WGO IS LOSING SHARE INTO A RECOVERING TOWABLE MARKET

**Only Thor discloses units among the four peers.** Patrick, LCI and Brunswick disclose none.
Patrick and LCI quote RVIA industry units instead, and **Brunswick's 2025 10-K contains no
unit table, no market-share statement and no NMMA reference at all** (the 2022 10-K mentioned NMMA
once and "market share" once in narrative; both are gone by 2025).

**RVIA calendar-year wholesale shipments, as quoted inside filed 10-Ks** (Patrick's MD&A, all-
category total; LCI's category split, which uses a different base and must not be summed to

| Motorhomes (LCII) | n/a | n/a | 56,200 | 58,400 | 45,900 | 34,900 | 36,000 |

|---|---|---|---|

**The towable market recovered 15% off the bottom and Winnebago did not participate.** Implied

capturing, and the largest candidate is the one this row cannot see.

## 4. THE BACKLOG WITHDRAWAL: TESTED AGAINST THE PEERS, AND IT IS COMPANY-SPECIFIC

This row was built partly to answer one question: **is WGO's removal of backlog from the
FY2025 10-K an industry-wide disclosure change, or WGO's own choice?**

| company | backlog disclosure |
|---|---|
| **WGO** | Disclosed by segment, units **and** dollars, in the FY2022, FY2023 and FY2024 10-Ks, with a dedicated "Backlog" heading in Item 1. **Removed entirely from the FY2025 10-K**, all three segments, and the Item 1 heading. Last figures published (FY2024): Towable 4,850u / $137.1M; Motorhome 897u / $234.4M; Marine 3,403u / $260.0M. |
| **THO** | **Continues to disclose, at full segment granularity, in every 10-K and every 10-Q**, through FY2025 and into the Q3 FY2026 10-Q. |
| **PATK** | Never disclosed; states affirmatively *"as a result, the Company has no material backlog of orders"*, identical language in the 2022 and 2025 10-Ks. |
| **LCII** | Never disclosed; states *"neither segment has any significant backlog"*, identical language 2022 and 2025. |
| **BC** | The word "backlog" appears **zero times** in either the 2022 or the 2025 10-K. |

**Thor's backlog, still published while it fell 82% ($000):**

| as of 31 July | 2021 | 2022 | 2023 | 2024 | 2025 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| NA Towable | 9,284,229 | 2,571,009 | 756,047 | 552,379 | **525,014** |
| NA Motorized | 4,014,738 | 3,436,629 | 1,242,936 | 776,903 | **1,004,620** |
| European | 3,559,097 | 2,753,602 | 3,549,660 | 1,950,793 | **1,525,592** |
| **Total** | **16,858,064** | 8,761,240 | 5,548,643 | 3,280,075 | **3,055,226** |

**The verdict on the test: the withdrawal is WGO's own.** The one peer that had a backlog to
disclose kept disclosing it through a comparable collapse, in both its annual and its quarterly
filings. Two peers never had one and say so in unchanged language. This is not a change in
industry practice; it is [E2-49], *"disposition of the yardstick"*, at one company.

*(The same test on market share: Thor publishes a Stat Surveys retail-share series in its
10-K every year. WGO published a share series in its FY2021 10-K MD&A and does not publish one
in FY2025. Same direction, same company.)*

## 7. WHAT THE ROW GIVES WGO, AND WHAT IT TAKES AWAY

**Gives:**
- WGO's headline revenue decline (−43.6%) is **in line with Thor's** (−41.3%), so the collapse
  is the industry's, not a company-specific failure.
- In the **current** nine months WGO's towable unit decline (−9.9%) is **half** of Thor's
  (−20.9%), and its motorized recovery (+9.0%) is real, if smaller than Thor's (+21.2%).
- Brunswick, the marine comparator, posted an **operating loss** in 2025 on a $305.8M Navico
  goodwill impairment, while WGO's Marine segment earned a **7.5% operating margin with units
  +11.7%**. **Barletta is outperforming the closest listed marine comparable.** This is the
  best thing in the row for WGO and it is a real, filed fact.
- WGO's leverage is middling for the row, not extreme: net debt ÷ equity 32% against PATK's
  123% and BC's 108%.

**Takes away:**
- The **lowest operating margin in the row**, in both the latest full year (2.0%) and the
  latest interim (2.4%), against suppliers earning 7–9% in the same demand environment.
- **Share loss into a recovering towable market**: industry +15.1%, Thor +12.5%, WGO +0.4%.
- **The largest share loss in motorhomes**: industry −21.6%, Thor −30.9%, WGO −31.6%.
- **The heaviest dealer guarantee relative to revenue, equity and tangible book**, on the
  thinnest cash balance.
- **The only company in the row that withdrew a disclosure yardstick** while the one peer that
  had the same yardstick kept publishing it through the same collapse.

## THE ROW'S LIMIT, STATED [E3-61]
*"In some businesses, the participants behave like a demented Kellogg. In other businesses,
they don't … I think you'd have to know the people involved."* This row shows **position**: margin, units, share, exposure, disclosure. It cannot show **conduct**: whether the industry's
discounting is a temporary response to a destock or a permanent equilibrium depends on how
Thor, Forest River and the private builders choose to behave, and the corpus says even Munger
had no model for predicting that. It is also a floor rather than a measure of the competitive
pressure, because **Forest River is invisible** (section above).
