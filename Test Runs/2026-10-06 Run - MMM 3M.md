## THE FOUNDATIONS (not a gate)
Two foundations bear on this name. **A share is a business:** the owner of 3M stock owns, with the tape and the
abrasives, a share of what the business owes on its legacy claims, and the test "would I be happy buying this stock if
the market closed for five years?" **[M1997-109]** is a question about how much of the coming cash belongs to claimants
rather than owners. **The analyst's habits:** "What do I not know that I need to know?" **[M1999-129]**, and look for
"what’s wrong" and "what you’re missing" **[M2025-013]**; a name as familiar as this one invites the worst anchor, "your
previous conclusion" **[M2016-054]**, here a lifetime of hearing that 3M is a great industrial franchise. **Who is paid to
tell you:** the company's own headline numbers are "adjusted"; the Q2 2026 release leads with "Adjusted EPS of $2.40, up
11% YoY" and an "Adjusted free cash flow of $1.3 billion" against "Cash from operations of $1.0 billion" (EX-99.1,
`0000066740-26-000242`), the adjustment taking out "Net costs for significant litigation and PFAS exit". Who prepares a
figure that excludes the claims is weighed at Q4, which this run does not reach.

**Contrary evidence, written down as found** **[M1997-127]**:
- Against the close below: the PWS and CAE settlements show that the two largest claims were, in the end, turned into
  fixed, scheduled sums: "3M will pay $10.5 billion to $12.5 billion in total ... with payments to be made from 2024
  through 2036", and "3M will contribute up to a total amount of $6.0 billion between 2023 and 2029" with "more than 99%
  of claimants" participating (10-K FY2025, Item 1A and Note 17). A claim that becomes a schedule is knowable.
- Against the close below: 3M "completed its exit of PFAS manufacturing at the end of 2025" (10-K MD&A), so the source
  of new PFAS claims from its own production has stopped; what remains is the tail of what was made before.
- Against the close below: the operating business earns well on the filer's own adjusted figures, an "Adjusted operating
  income margin of 24.9%" in Q2 2026 and adjusted organic sales growth of 5.4% (EX-99.1), and 3M "has paid dividend
  continuously since 1916" (10-K MD&A).
- For the close: the company states, quarter after quarter, that it "is unable to estimate a possible loss or range of
  possible loss in excess of the amounts accrued" for PFAS and for CAE (10-Q Q2 2026, `0000066740-26-000246`).
- For the close, found by accident: 3M carries a respirator mask and asbestos accrual of $473 million at 2025-12-31 with
  "an estimation period for future claims ... approaching the year 2050", and "cannot estimate the amount or upper end of
  the range of amounts by which the liability may exceed the accrual" (10-K Note 17).

## Q1 — CAN I UNDERSTAND IT? Where its economics will be in ten years, inside my perimeter. STOP.
**The test.** Understanding is "a reasonable fix on about what the earning power and competitive position will look like
in five or 10 years" **[M2012-065]**, "where the business will be in 10 years" **[M2000-037]**; the first step is "trying
to identify the key variables in that particular business, and evaluating how predictable they were first ... If
something is not very predictable, forget it." **[M1998-044]**.

**What the business is** (10-K FY2025, `0000066740-26-000014`, Item 1 and MD&A). Three segments on 2025 sales of
$24,948M: Safety and Industrial, $11,384M, 45.6% of sales, segment margin 24.9% (abrasives, industrial adhesives and
tapes, electrical markets, personal safety, roofing granules, automotive aftermarket); Transportation and Electronics,
$8,272M, 33.2%, margin 17.4% (advanced materials, automotive and aerospace, commercial branding and transportation,
display materials, electronics materials, "Semiconductor production materials", "Solutions for data centers");
Consumer, $4,920M, 19.7%, margin 20.2% (Command, Filtrete, Scotch-Brite, Scotch, Post-it, Meguiar's). Organic sales
change was −0.2% in 2024 and +0.9% in 2025 on the GAAP basis.

**Key variable 1, the operating economics.** Most of the sales are tapes, abrasives, protective equipment, films and
household brands, products whose chemistry I need not know so long as I "understand the economic dynamics of the
industry" **[M2011-014]**. The company itself writes this part down: it gives 2026 "adjusted EPS guidance increased from
$8.50 - $8.70 to $8.80 - $8.95" (EX-99.1, `0000066740-26-000242`). One part carries a "technological component that’s
of significance" **[M1998-008]**: the electronics, display and semiconductor lines inside Transportation and
Electronics, a segment whose GAAP organic sales fell in 2024 and 2025. On this variable alone I would have carried the
name forward with that doubt named; it is not what closes the run.

**Key variable 2, the claims on the cash.** For an owner, "where the business will be in 10 years" includes who is
paid first out of its cash. The filings:
- PFAS: "other environmental liabilities" accrued of **$7.4 billion** at 2026-06-30 (10-Q Q2 2026,
  `0000066740-26-000246`), the larger part the public water system settlement, "$10.5 billion to $12.5 billion in total
  ... with payments to be made from 2024 through 2036" (10-K Item 1A), which runs through the whole ten-year horizon.
- Combat Arms earplugs: accrued **$1.9 billion** at 2026-06-30 (10-Q), payable "between 2023 and 2029" (10-K Note 17).
- The two accruals together are $9.3 billion, about 11% of the $84,161M market cap. They are the knowable part.
- The unaccrued part: "approximately 15,200 cases" pending in the AFFF MDL "As of July 1, 2026", and "the number of
  plaintiffs asserting AFFF-related claims is substantially higher than the number of cases" (10-Q); the first personal
  injury bellwether trial, set for October 2025, was vacated in August 2025 and "No new trial date has been set", while
  "general causation expert discovery" for liver and thyroid cancers was completed in April 2026 (10-Q). State attorneys
  general suits in the MDL on behalf of thirty-two states, the District of Columbia and three territories, seeking
  "natural resource damages, consumer protection civil penalties ... and, where available, punitive damages", with more
  "proceeding outside the AFFF MDL" (10-K Note 17); eighteen actions pending against 3M Belgium (10-K Note 17). The one
  state suit settled so far, New Jersey's, costs "up to $450 million" (10-K Note 17).
- The company's own statement on the unaccrued part, for PFAS and for CAE alike: "The Company is unable to estimate a
  possible loss or range of possible loss in excess of the amounts accrued at this time." (10-Q Q2 2026). For the
  respirator mask and asbestos claims, accrued at $473 million with future claims "approaching the year 2050", it
  "cannot estimate the amount or upper end of the range" (10-K Note 17).

**The tests applied.**
1. *Key variables and how predictable* **[M1998-044]**: the second variable is not predictable by anyone who has filed
   on it.
2. *Do the past statements tell me the future ones* **[M2008-033]**: no. The statements of 2023 carry a pre-tax
   operating loss of $10,689M made by the two settlements (10-K, income statement); those of 2025 and 2026 still carry
   "Net costs for significant litigation and PFAS exit" each quarter (EX-99.1), and the filing warns of "future charges
   that could have a material adverse effect" (10-K Item 1A). What the next statements will charge is the very number
   the company declines to estimate.
3. *Important and knowable* **[M2006-076]**: "If something’s important but unknowable, forget it." It is important: the
   settled part alone, the PWS settlement, is up to $12.5 billion, and the unsettled part reaches claimants the
   settled part did not release: personal-injury plaintiffs and the sovereigns. It is unknowable from where I stand, for the
   reason in test 4.
4. *Would the insiders write it down* **[M2000-105]**: here the insiders are the defendant itself, with its counsel and
   its auditors, and they have written it down in the negative, every quarter, in its own periodic filings. The same
   company writes down its operating forecast (the adjusted EPS guidance above) and leaves this one out. That is the
   speakers' test answered by the people who know most.
5. *Do I doubt it is inside* **[M2002-092]**: yes; and "if you have doubts about something being into your circle of
   competence, it isn’t."
6. *What the speakers did with this shape*: "we have passed up some businesses, because we were worried about the
   product liability potential" **[M2001-008]**; "We’ve walked away from several deals that were quite attractive in
   every respect except asbestos. ... So we don’t go near asbestos." **[M2001-032]**; and the deep pocket draws the
   claim: "it gets more interesting if Berkshire ... is involved" **[M2002-009]**. Business risk is what over five to
   fifteen years could "destroy, or modify, or reduce the economic strengths", and "In some businesses ... it’s
   impossible for us to figure ... and then we just — we don’t even think about it then." **[M2000-014]**.

**Routing, stated because the framework does not name this case.** The framework routes rapid change to Q1 TOO HARD
and carries product liability at Q9 as a WEIGHING (test 7, **[M2001-008]**), with the asbestos row as a candidate stop
only. I close here, at Q1, on Q1's own tests 3, 4 and 5 and the definition, because the claim is not a risk of loss to
be weighed against a valued business: it is a share of the ten-year cash whose size cannot be stated, so that the
earning power that belongs to the owner cannot be fixed. Had the run gone on, Q7 would have had to value a cash stream
net of a claim its issuer "is unable to estimate", and "if we don’t have the faintest idea what the future stream is
going to look like, we don’t have the faintest idea what it’s worth, now" **[M1994-077]**; "If, however, we lack the
ability to estimate future earnings ... we simply move on" **[L2013-012]**. That route reaches the same box; it is
recorded as reasoning, not as a computation (operator rule 3).

**Which cause.** NATURE, not WORK. The deciding question is the size of the unaccrued PFAS, CAE and asbestos claims; the
answer turns on courts that have not set a trial date, on general causation science still in expert discovery, and on
the claims of three dozen sovereigns, and the party with the best information declines in writing to estimate it. "We
couldn't solve this problem, moreover, even if we were to spend years intensely studying" **[L1993-023]**; this is not
"I haven’t done the work" **[M1994-026]**, since no reading of public documents can know more than the defendant's own
filing says it knows. No research pass is opened. A lower price does not reopen it: "It doesn’t mean it isn’t a good buy.
It doesn’t mean it isn’t selling for a fraction of its worth. It just means that we don’t know how to evaluate it."
**[M2000-038]**.

**What would reopen it (a filed fact, not a price):** a global resolution of the AFFF personal-injury and state
attorneys general claims into a fixed, scheduled sum, as the PWS and CAE settlements turned their claims into
schedules, or a 3M filing that states a range of reasonably possible loss in excess of the accruals. Then the run starts
again at Q1 with the claims as a known deduction.
