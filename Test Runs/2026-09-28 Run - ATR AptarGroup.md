## Q1 — CAN I UNDERSTAND HOW THIS MAKES MONEY? **[E3-31]**
- **Unit economics in my own words, no management language.** Aptar molds plastic, formulates rubber and silicone, and assembles the result at high speed into small parts that go on or into other companies' containers: the spray pump on a nasal spray bottle, the metering valve inside an asthma inhaler, the rubber stopper and plunger in a vial or pre-filled syringe, the pump on a perfume or lotion bottle, the aerosol valve on a deodorant can, the flip-top cap on a ketchup or shampoo bottle. It sells them by the million to about 5,000 makers of medicines, cosmetics, foods and household goods (*"no single customer or group of affiliated customers accounting for greater than 4% of our 2025 Net Sales"*), mostly from 57 plants near where the customer fills (*"Our philosophy is to produce as much as possible in the region where it will be sold"*). It buys resin, rubber, silicone and metal, and pays for machines, moulds and clean rooms (capital expenditures $270.4M in 2025 against depreciation of $242.3M; *"Our business is capital intensive"*). It keeps the difference between the price per part and the materials, labour and machine time in it. **The three segments earn very different spreads (10-K 2025, Note 18; segment Adjusted EBITDA less the segment's own depreciation and amortization, $M): Pharma $471.5M on $1,737.5M of net sales (27.1%) and $2,283.6M of segment assets; Beauty $67.7M on $1,309.4M (5.2%) and $1,691.9M; Closures $59.8M on $730.3M (8.2%) and $799.4M.** Pharma is 46% of sales and 79% of the three segments' operating profit ($471.5M of $599.0M).
- **The scarce input this business controls.** The candidates, stated before Q2 tests them: (a) **in Pharma, the place of the device inside a customer's drug approval**: *"Characteristics of this market include (i) governmental regulation of our pharmaceutical customers, (ii) clean room manufacturing environments and (iii) a significant amount of time and research from initially working with pharmaceutical companies at the molecular development stage of a medication through the eventual distribution to the market"*, and *"Pharma regulatory agencies in the United States and EU have developed and introduced Combination Products specific guidelines ... which now is required to be appropriately designed, developed and documented"*; the filer calls itself *"a leading supplier of nasal drug delivery spray pumps and metered dose inhaler valves"*; (b) **process know-how in moulding and elastomers** (*"We believe that we possess certain technical capabilities and know-how that make our products difficult for a competitor to duplicate"*; tolerances *"as small as one one-thousandth of an inch"*); (c) **global plant footprint near customers**.
