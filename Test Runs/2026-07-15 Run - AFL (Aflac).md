## GATE 1 — CIRCLE OF COMPETENCE

Aflac Japan (historically the larger profit contributor — general
knowledge, not independently re-confirmed for the current fiscal year this
pass). Unit economics: premium revenue minus claims and distribution
costs, distributed largely through payroll-deduction worksite sales and
third-party distribution partners. **VERDICT: PASS**, with the two-country

real complexity, not glossed over.

## GATE 2 — MOAT
Franchise test [E3-03], from Aflac's own 10-K: **"...competition exists for
sales associates and brokers with demonstrated ability. Further, low rates
of unemployment... tend to make it more difficult for Aflac U.S. to
maintain its network of sales associates. In Japan, the Company's sales
results are dependent upon its relationship with sales associates and
other distribution partners, such as Japan Post Group, which in recent
periods has accounted for a significant portion of Aflac Japan's total
sales."** — a direct, disclosed concession that a meaningful share of
Japan sales flows through a **single named distribution partner**.
- **No close substitute**: Aflac's supplemental-insurance brand (cancer/
  accident/critical-illness coverage) carries a long operating history and
  real brand recognition, particularly in Japan (general public knowledge)
  — but the moat is materially **distribution-dependent**, not purely
  brand/product-based, given the Japan Post concentration named above.
**Class: NARROW** (long-standing brand plus distribution relationships,
but concentrated distribution risk), **Direction: STABLE-to-slightly-
NARROWING** given the named Japan Post dependency and the disclosed US
sales-associate recruitment difficulty in a tight labor market.
**VERDICT: PASS with NARROW classification**, the Japan Post concentration
risk carried forward explicitly rather than treated as a footnote.

## ADDENDUM — FY2025 NI-DROP CAUSE RESOLVED, 2026-07-16
The run's single biggest open item is closed via SEC XBRL (CIK 4977):
**the drop was mark-to-market investment noise, not operating decay.**
- `GainLossOnInvestments`: +$590M (2023) → **+$1,271M (2024)** →
  **−$572M (2025)** — a $1,843M pre-tax swing between 2024 and 2025 that
  almost exactly matches the $1,797M reported NI decline.
- The operating engine was stable throughout: net investment income
  $3,811M → $4,116M → $4,076M; gross investment income essentially flat.
- Underlying NI (ex-gains/losses at ~17% effective tax): **~$4,169M (2023)
  → ~$4,388M (2024) → ~$4,121M (2025)** — a steady ~$4.1-4.4B engine with
  GAAP swinging ±$1.5B around it on marks. FY2024's reported $5,443M was
  INFLATED by the same mechanism that depressed FY2025.

**Framework consequences:**
- The $3,682M anchor stays unadjusted (VZ/CMCSA precedent — no smoothing);
  it now sits visibly BELOW the ~$4.1B underlying level, i.e., conservative.
- g1 corrects from the artifact −32.85% to the underlying trajectory:
  lower of underlying YoY (−6.1%) vs 2-yr CAGR (−0.6%) → **−6.1%**.
- Recomputed Book Two (caveat rate 8.35%): IV $41.8B at g1 −6.1% (cap at
  147% of IV); even at a +2% ceiling construction IV is $59.1B vs the
  $61.6B cap. **Verdict stays WAIT at current price under every
  construction** — but now with a clean explanation and a computed
  trigger: **max-buy ≈ $43.5B cap at g1=0 (−29% from current) to $33.5B
  (−46%) on the strict construction.** Radar entry: a ~30% decline makes
  AFL genuinely interesting; until then it is priced-and-understood, not
  unresolved.
- The Japan Post distribution-concentration flag and Ruling 2 ceiling
  result (3.95:1, clears) are unchanged by this addendum.
