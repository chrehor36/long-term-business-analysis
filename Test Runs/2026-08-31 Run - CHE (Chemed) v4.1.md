## (c) Boom-window artifact check **[E4-41]**: **PASS, and the finding is that the two halves boomed in OPPOSITE years**

[E4-41]'s instruction is to normalise the mean **down** for favourable exogenous breaks. Chemed's

**1. The CARES Act payroll-tax deferral, a pure timing artifact, filed on the face of the cash-flow

run normalises for it and displays both, and notes that any window containing all three years

**2. VITAS boomed in 2020. Roto-Rooter boomed in 2023. They have never peaked together.**

**VITAS's peak year is 2020 and its trough is 2022, a 45% swing. Roto-Rooter's peak year is 2023
and its trough is 2020, a 36% swing. Consolidated net income moved in a band of $250M to $319M,
a 28% swing, and looked far calmer than either business actually was.** The pandemic raised

consolidated earnings across 2020 to 2025 is a composition artifact of two uncorrelated shocks
cancelling, not evidence that either business is stable.** That is recorded here and it is the
substance of the [E5-11] staying-power read below.

**3. The 2025 VITAS Medicare-cap charge and its 2026 non-repeat, which is the artifact running

revenue charge.** Any forward figure that annualises H1 2026 is trusting that comparison.

**Leg (c) passes.** The conclusion is not "the earnings are a boom artifact." It is that **no

## Q1: CAN I UNDERSTAND HOW THIS MAKES MONEY? **[E3-31]**

- **Unit economics in my own words, no management language. Chemed is a holding company that owns
  two businesses which have nothing to do with each other.**

  **VITAS (64% of revenue, $1,630.1M) is paid a fixed daily rate by the United States government
  to look after people who are dying, and it keeps whatever it does not spend on their care.**
  The government sets the rate. On any given day in 2025 VITAS was looking after **22,338 people**
  (that is the average daily census), it admitted **70,817** during the year, and it collected
  **$206.92 per patient per day** before the cap adjustments. Ninety-four per cent of that money
  came from Medicare. Against it, VITAS spent $1,257.7M on wages, patient care and other direct
  costs, which is **77.2 cents of every revenue dollar**, and another 6.2 cents on selling and
  administration, leaving **15.1 cents of operating margin**. There are four payment rates
  (routine home care at a two-tier rate that steps down after day sixty, continuous care billed
  in fifteen-minute increments, general inpatient care, and respite), and the mix moves the
  average. Nothing about that description is optional or complicated: the price is published, the
  volume is measured in patient-days, and the cost is nurses and aides driving to houses.

  **Roto-Rooter (36% of revenue, $899.9M) answers a phone when a drain blocks and sends a van.**
  The customer has an emergency, does not shop, and pays whatever the technician quotes. Roto-
  Rooter did that through company-owned branches in the big cities, through Independent
  Contractors who pay a percentage of weekly cash collections for the right to use the name, and
  through franchisees who pay a flat monthly fee scaled to territory population and indexed to
  CPI. **Franchise fees are $5.7M of $899.9M, six-tenths of one per cent**, so this is not a
  franchisor; it is an operator with a licensing sideline. It spent **$77.1M on advertising, 8.6%
  of segment revenue**, to make the phone ring, and it converts the drain-cleaning call into
  higher-ticket excavation and water-restoration work: those two lines are now **$428.6M, 48% of
  segment revenue**, and the filing says plainly that they *"are generally sold as a result of
  initial calls from customers regarding drain cleaning issues."* Gross margin excluding
  depreciation is **50.1%**, operating margin **17.9%**.

  **Corporate costs $69.5M a year, 2.7% of revenue**, for *"the stewardship, accounting and
  reporting, legal, tax and other costs of operating a publicly held corporation"* and, per the
  10-K, *"long term strategic planning, significant capital allocation decisions, investment
  activities."* Neither operating business needs the other, and the 10-K says so: *"There are
  few integrated business functions between the operating units and Chemed (such as sales,
  marketing or purchasing)."*

  **It employs almost no capital.** Net property and equipment is $205.7M against $2,530.0M of
  revenue, capital expenditure is 2.5% of revenue, and there is **no long-term debt at the
  2025 year end**. **Income from operations of $338.2M against roughly $225.1M of net tangible
  operating capital is a 150% pre-tax return**, and 34.7% pre-tax including the $667.0M of
  goodwill from past acquisitions.

- **The scarce input each business controls, and they are different inputs.**
  **VITAS controls a licensed, certified, capacity-constrained clinical workforce inside
  Certificates of Need.** The rate is not scarce (everyone gets the same rate); the ability to
  staff a nurse to a dying person's house at 3 a.m. in Miami is. The 2021 and 2022 collapse in
  VITAS's earnings was a **staffing** collapse, not a demand collapse, and the company's own
  remedy was the *"Difference Maker Program"* which *"paid over $39.2 million in bonuses."*
  **Roto-Rooter controls a name that arrives in a homeowner's head at the moment a drain
  blocks**, and a van fleet dense enough to be there fast. That is a genuine asset and it has to
  be re-bought every year with advertising.

- **Will the fundamentals look broadly the same in ten years?** **The mechanisms yes; one of the
  two parameters no.** Americans will keep dying and drains will keep blocking. But VITAS's price
  is a legislative and regulatory output that has been reset every October for decades, and the
  10-K says the resets have run behind cost: *"Payment rates … are adjusted annually for inflation
  … **however, the adjustments have historically been less than actual inflation**,"* on top of a
  **2% sequestration cut running through federal fiscal 2027**. And the way customers find a
  plumber is changing, which the company itself names in Item 1A. Both facts are recorded here and
  both are the substance of Q2.

- **[E4-46] check.** *"If we can't make a decision in five minutes, we can't make it in five
  months."* This is not a close call. Two segments, one page of unit economics each, published
  prices, physical volume series disclosed in the MD&A, no derivatives, no float, no insurance
  reserves, no foreign operations, no equity-method stakes, and a balance sheet with fifteen
  line items. **Q1 is a clean IN, and the run does not import from it any claim that the earnings
  are forecastable**, which is [E5-34]'s separate test and lives at Q5.
