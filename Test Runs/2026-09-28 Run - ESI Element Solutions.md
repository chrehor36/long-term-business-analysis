## Q1 — CAN I UNDERSTAND HOW THIS MAKES MONEY? **[E3-31]**
- Unit economics in my own words, no management language: **Element Solutions blends chemicals and metal alloys into formulations that other manufacturers use up in their own production lines, and sends technicians to keep those lines running.** A circuit-board shop buys its plating baths, surface finishes and etching chemistry; an electronics assembler buys solder paste, solder bar, flux and thermal materials; a chip packager buys copper plating chemistry and sintered-silver die attach; an automotive-parts plater buys chrome and nickel plating and pre-treatment chemistry; an offshore oil producer buys hydraulic control fluid. Each is consumed and reordered as the customer's line runs (the filer: *"Both segments provide products that, in substantially all cases, are consumed by customers as part of their production process"*; *"Due to the relatively short cycle times in the majority of our businesses, our order backlog levels are minimal."*). **Revenue FY2025 $2,551.2M** (Note 22): **Electronics $1,786.2M (70%)**, split Assembly $907.1M (solder and pastes; the filer: *"Of our total net sales for Assembly Solutions, approximately 50% is metal content whose price fluctuations are generally passed through to our customers"*), Circuitry $527.8M, Semiconductor $351.3M; **Specialties $765.0M (30%)**, split Industrial $651.4M, Energy (offshore fluids) $89.4M, Graphics $24.2M for the two months before its sale. About 5,200 employees, *"approximately 40% are research and development chemists and experienced technical service and technical sales personnel"*; research and development is small (*"$67.6 million"*, 2.6% of sales); no customer above 10% of sales. **The money is a margin on formulation and service over raw materials**: gross margin 42.0% in FY2025 on a revenue line that includes about $450M of tin, silver and other metal passed through at cost-plus (stripped of the filer's approximate half of Assembly sales, about $454M, gross profit is about 51% of the remaining $2,097M); selling, technical and administrative cost 25.9% of sales, because the service force is the product; capex $62.2M (2.4% of sales). **The metal is a large and growing distortion**: in the quarter to 2026-06-30 *"Net sales from pass-through metals were $317 million and $113 million for the three months ended June 30, 2026 and 2025"*, 41% of Electronics' sales, and the consolidated gross margin fell to 34.2% on the arithmetic of the metal alone.
- **The brief's Q1 prior checked against the segment note: right, and incomplete in three places.** (1) Graphics (flexographic printing plates) was sold in February 2025 and is not in today's company; (2) two businesses joined in 2026 that no filed year contains: Micromax (thick-film electronic inks and pastes, $193.4M of sales in the five months to June) and EFC (high-purity specialty gases, $34.7M in six months); (3) the metal pass-through is not a side note: in the first half of 2026 it was $573M of $1,817.9M of sales (32%), and the filer hedges it with derivatives whose losses sit below operating profit (*"$47.2 million of net losses associated with metals derivative contracts"* in FY2025). The recurring-revenue claim is the filer's and is consistent with the consumable model and the absence of backlog; it is not quantified in any filing read.
- The scarce input this business controls: **qualified formulations written into customers' and OEMs' process specifications, and the technical-service relationship that keeps them there.** The filer: *"We leverage these close relationships to win qualifications and specifications into their supply chains"*; *"operational risks and switching costs make it difficult for our customers to change suppliers"*; *"While they typically represent only a small portion of our customers' costs, our products, along with our high-value added technical support, are seen as integral to customer product performance."* Brands with long histories (Alpha solder, Enthone and MacDermid plating chemistry, Kester, Fernox) and about 2,600 patents, of which *"no specific group or groups of intellectual property rights are material to our business overall"*. Whether a qualified chemistry is one customers think has no close substitute is Q2's question.
- Will the fundamentals look broadly the same in ten years? **The way the money is made, yes; the product list, partly not, and the filer says so.** Plating, soldering and surface finishing are old industrial processes, and the FY2016 10-K describes the same Assembly, Electronics (circuit-board) and Industrial lines under the Performance Solutions segment that the FY2025 10-K describes under Electronics and Specialties. What turns over is the chemistry for each generation of board, package and part: *"The specialty chemicals industry is subject to constant and rapid technological change, product obsolescence, price erosion, evolving standards, finite product lifecycles, raw material price fluctuations"*, and *"Customers also have found, and may continue to find, alternative materials or processes, which no longer require our products."* That bears on the moat (Q2, [E4-04]); the unit economics (a margin on consumables sold into running production lines, rising and falling with electronics and automotive output) will be the same.
