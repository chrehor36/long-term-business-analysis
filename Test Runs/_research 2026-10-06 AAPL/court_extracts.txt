2. Epic Games, Inc. v. Apple Inc., 9th Cir. No. 25-2935, opinion filed 2025-12-11.
   Source: https://cdn.ca9.uscourts.gov/datastore/opinions/2025/12/11/25-2935.pdf
   p.16 "Finally, it referred Apple and one of its officers for criminal investigation."
   p.21 "The district court did not err in deciding that it could consider evidence of Apple's bad faith, nor did it
         clearly err in finding that Apple acted in bad faith."
   p.23 "Apple has ignored the district court's finding that Apple "attempted to mislead" in its Notice of Compliance
         and May 2024 hearing with "pretextual" justifications."
   p.23 Apple argued it was "penalized Apple for choosing what [it] understood to be the most advantageous option for
         its business and shareholders" ... "But the district court did not find Apple in contempt because it picked the
         most advantageous of several purportedly compliant options. Instead, it found that "at every step Apple
         considered whether its actions would comply, and at every step Apple chose to maintain its anticompetitive
         revenue stream over compliance.""
   Staff summary (not part of the opinion): "affirmed in part and reversed in part the district court's order imposing
   civil contempt sanctions"; "Affirming the district court's contempt findings".
