## Q1 — CAN I UNDERSTAND HOW THIS MAKES MONEY? **[E3-31]**
- **Unit economics in my own words, no management language.** Merck invents, tests, gets approved and sells prescription medicines and vaccines, and (about a tenth of sales) medicines, vaccines and tracking devices for farm and pet animals. The money is made in the years a product is protected by patent and regulatory exclusivity: while no one else may sell the same molecule, the price is set far above the cost of making it (consolidated gross margin *"74.8% in 2025 compared with 76.3% in 2024"*; the Pharmaceutical segment's profit before research, which the filer does not allocate to it, was **$45,754M on $58,142M of sales, 78.7%**). Research and development, the purchase of other companies' candidates, and the trials that prove them are the cost of having something to sell when the current products' protection ends; the 10-K's own words are *"In order to remain competitive, the Company, like other major pharmaceutical companies, must continue to launch new products."* The payer is mostly not the patient: governments, Medicare and Medicaid, insurers and pharmacy-benefit managers, and in vaccines public purchasers such as the CDC and national tenders. When a patent ends, *"lower priced generic or biosimilar versions become available"* and sales fall *"significant[ly] and rapid[ly]"* (Item 1A). One product, the cancer antibody Keytruda, is 49% of sales.
- **The scarce input this business controls.** **The legal exclusivity on each approved product, for a filed and dated term** (the patent table in Item 1: Keytruda 2028 in the US, 2031 in the EU, 2032-2033 in Japan, 2028 in China), and behind it **the clinical and regulatory machine that produces the next exclusive product**: the trial evidence, the approvals across Keytruda's many indications (the 10-K lists the 2025 approvals by date), the manufacturing of biologics and vaccines at scale, and the relationships with oncologists and payers. The molecules are scarce while protected and are not afterwards. Whether that is a franchise in [E3-03]'s sense is Q2's question.
- **Will the fundamentals look broadly the same in ten years?** **The mechanism, yes**: in 2036 Merck will still be selling protected medicines at high margins to government and insurer payers and replacing them as they expire. **The products, no, and the filer dates it**: Keytruda's US compound patent ends in 2028 with *"biosimilar competition between 2028 and 2029"*; Januvia and Janumet lost US exclusivity in May 2026 and Bridion in July 2026; Lynparza and Bravecto in 2027; Gardasil 9 in 2028 (US). The sales Merck earns in 2036 will come mostly from products that are candidates or recent launches today (Winrevair, Welireg, Capvaxive, Ohtuvayre, sac-TMT, enlicitide, MK-1406, MK-4208). That is [E4-04]'s question, carried to Q2 under the 2026-09-20 ruling that applies it as a competence limit there.
- **Relatively simple and stable in character [E3-31]? Understood, with the science limit named.** The economic mechanism (protected molecule, high price to a third-party payer, a dated end, a replacement bought or discovered) is simple and has been stable for the life of the industry, and I can state it in five minutes **[E4-46]**. What is not simple is judging which candidate will work and which company's pipeline will replace its current sales, and the corpus names exactly that limit for this industry and this company: at the 2008 meeting, *"when we invest in something like pharma, we don’t know the answer on the pipeline. It will be a different pipeline anyway five years from now. So we don’t know whether Pfizer or Merck, you know, or you name it — Johnson & Johnson — we don’t know which of those will come up with a blockbuster commercial drug three or four years from now, and we don’t try to assess it."* (`Annual Meetings/2008 Annual Meeting.txt`, line 1231; **on the shelf, not yet a ledger row**, so it is recorded as evidence and no judgment rests on it alone). The framework places the durability limit at Q2, not Q1 (*"So [E4-04] is applied as a competence limit, never as a fourth franchise criterion"*), and the unit of the test is the company **[E4-08]**. **IN at Q1 is not a finding that the durability of the product stream can be judged.**
- **VERDICT: [x] IN**: the money is made visibly, as protected-product sales to government and insurer payers, measured by product in the 10-K, with the end of each product's protection dated by the filer. **IN is not a finding on the franchise; the pipeline-durability question is Q2's.**
