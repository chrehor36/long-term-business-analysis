### What the Xeris row says about Korlym's position
- Recorlev went from $29.5M (FY2023) to $139.3M (FY2025) to a $213M annualized run-rate (Q2 2026 x4), with volume
  the driver every period and pricing turning favorable in 2026. Over the same window Korlym + AG revenue at
  Corcept was $761.4M in FY2025 (from the run file); Recorlev is now roughly one-fifth the size of the Korlym
  franchise and growing at 80-117% a year against Korlym + AG's +12.8% in FY2025 and +6.2% in H1 2026
  (Hypercortisolism Products, CORT 10-Q Q2 2026; see Section 5).
- Xeris's cost structure is not Korlym's: COGS 13-18% of revenue vs Corcept's 1.7%; SG&A is 63% of revenue; the
  company carries ~$190M of 11.4%-effective secured debt and an accumulated deficit of $671M. It reached GAAP
  operating profitability only in FY2025.
- Xeris names Korlym, the Teva generic, and relacorilant as competitors, by name, in every 10-K in the window.
  That is the relative claim Q2 requires: the competitor row exists, it is growing faster than the incumbent, and
  it is not yet self-funding on a net-income basis.

### The label event that put Isturisa on Korlym's ground
FY2025 press release 2026-02-17, verbatim: *"On April 15, 2025, the U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA)
approved the supplemental new drug application (sNDA) for Isturisa® (osilodrostat) for the treatment of endogenous
hypercortisolemia in adults with Cushing's syndrome for whom surgery is not an option or has not been curative. This
was an expansion of the previous indication for the treatment of patients with Cushing's disease, which is a
sub-type of Cushing's syndrome."*

Before April 2025 Isturisa's US label was Cushing's *disease* (pituitary) only; Korlym's is hyperglycemia
secondary to hypercortisolism in Cushing's *syndrome*. Since April 2025 the two labels overlap in the broad
syndrome population, and Recordati's US patient count went from "over 1,000" (H1 2025) to "approximately 1,400"
(end 2025), with H1 2026 Isturisa revenue up 58%.

### What the Recordati row says about Korlym's position
- Isturisa is now the second-largest branded Cushing's product in the world: €262.8M in FY2025 (about $285-300M at
  2025 average EUR/USD rates; the conversion is indicative only and not carried into any table), growing 29% in
  2025 and 58% in H1 2026, against Korlym + AG's $761M growing 12.8% in FY2025 and 6.2% in H1 2026.
- Isturisa and Recorlev together (FY2025: ~€263M + $139M) are roughly half of Korlym's revenue and both are
  growing at 30-117% a year. Korlym's share of the branded Cushing's market is falling even as its dollars hold.
- Recordati is a €2.6B-revenue diversified pharma with a 37.8% EBITDA margin (FY2025 release) funding Isturisa's
  US expansion from group cash flow; unlike Xeris it does not need Cushing's to succeed to survive. It is the
  better-capitalized competitor of the two.
- Limit: Recordati does not disclose Isturisa by geography, so the US figure (the only one that competes with
  Korlym directly) is not separable; the patient counts above are the only US-specific datum.

### The one product-level disclosure: the launch table
Both 10-Ks carry a table of the year's US generic launches. The FY2024 10-K ("In 2024, we launched the generic
version ... of the following branded products in the United States"), transcribed from the stripped text:
`Mifepristone Tablets | Korlym ® | January | $ 2` under the column header *"Total Annual U.S. Branded Sales at Time
of Launch (U.S. $ in millions (IQVIA)) *"*. The FY2025 10-K repeats the identical row (Mifepristone Tablets |
Korlym | January | $2).

Read this carefully. IQVIA puts Korlym's annual US branded sales at the time of Teva's launch at **$2 million**.
Corcept's own FY2023 10-K reports Korlym net product revenue of roughly $480M (the run file carries the exact
figure). The 240x gap is not an error in either document: IQVIA measures retail and wholesale channel volume,
and Korlym is dispensed almost entirely through a single specialty pharmacy (Optime Care) under an exclusive
arrangement, which IQVIA's panel does not see. That invisible channel is precisely what Teva's antitrust suit
attacks (below). For the framework this is a datum about the moat's mechanism, not about Korlym's size.

added a second specialty pharmacy (Curant Health) to the exclusive-dealing claim (2026-01-14); discovery is
ongoing. Teva is the plaintiff, so it discloses no reserve; the exposure sits on Corcept's side and belongs in the
CORT run file's Q3 (honesty) and Q6 (what would prove me wrong) sections, not here.

### What the Teva row says about Korlym's position
- The only same-molecule competitor discloses **no revenue** for its product, in a year when its US generics
  segment did $3.66B. If the mifepristone generic were material to Teva it would be named; it is not.
- Teva's own filed view is that the channel, not the molecule, is the barrier: the suit's core allegation is
  exclusive dealing through the specialty pharmacy. That is the same mechanism that makes IQVIA see $2M of a
  ~$480M product. The framework should treat the Korlym moat as a **distribution-and-prescriber-habit** moat
  whose legality is now being litigated, not as a patent moat (Teva launched at risk in January 2024 and the
  patent route is the part of the suit that survived dismissal only in part).

## THE ROW'S LIMIT

**What the row can show.**
1. Every named same-indication competitor to Korlym is on it, with its own revenue line, filing-sourced, for the
   same window: Recorlev (SEC), Isturisa and Signifor (Recordati IR site), and the Teva generic (SEC, disclosed as
   "no revenue disclosed", which is itself the datum). Nothing in the row rests on an aggregator.
2. The relative claim Q2 needs is answerable: the incumbent is growing more slowly than every branded competitor
   in its indication, in both dollars-growth-rate and patient terms, and its competitors say so in their own
   filings by naming Korlym.
3. The mechanism of the moat is visible from the competitors' side: Teva's IQVIA-measured $2M of Korlym sales at
   launch vs Corcept's ~$480M of reported revenue locates the moat in the dispensing channel; Xeris's and
   Recordati's US patient counts show that channel is not exclusive to Corcept.
4. The successor market is bounded: the one biomarker-gated branded PROC drug does $690M a year and is still
   growing 20-30%; Corcept's own 10-Q lists four approved branded rivals there, three of them biomarker-gated, and
   the non-gated standard of care (bevacizumab + chemotherapy) by name.

**What the row cannot show.**
1. **Korlym's US market share by patient.** None of the three competitors discloses Cushing's revenue by
   geography (Recordati reports Isturisa worldwide; Xeris is US-only but does not report patients; Teva reports
   nothing). Recordati's "approximately 1,400 net active patients" and "over 1,000" are the only US patient
   figures on the row. Corcept's own patient count belongs to the run file, not here.
2. **The Teva generic's size.** Teva does not disclose it, and the IQVIA figure Teva prints measures the wrong
   channel. The only route to the generic's volume is Corcept's own disclosure of AG vs branded mix, which is the
   run file's job, or the discovery record in the N.D. Cal. case, which is not public. **UNKNOWABLE from the
   competitor side**; the document that would resolve it (Teva's internal sales by product) does not exist in
   public form.
3. **Price.** Xeris reports Recorlev's net-price direction (down 7.2% FY2025, up 12.3% H1 2026); Recordati and
   Teva report no price for these products. Korlym's realized net price after the AG is not visible from the
   competitors' filings.
4. **Recordati's product-level profitability.** Recordati reports EBITDA at group and business-unit level only;
   there is no Isturisa margin, SBC or capex. The Recordati column is a revenue-only column by construction.
5. **A peer for Lifyorli.** Elahere is not a peer (biomarker-restricted, inside a $60B company); it is a ceiling
   reference for a PROC drug's revenue. The row cannot say what share of the non-FRα-high population Lifyorli
   will take, and it does not try.
6. **Currency.** Recordati's figures are euro; the row carries them in euro and does not convert them into the
   tables. Any dollar comparison to Korlym in the prose is indicative and labelled.
7. **The FY2024 Recordati group revenue** is left as a computed figure and flagged; it is not used. The Recordati
   nine-months-2025 release (the "peak sales > €1.2 billion" claim) was cited from its indexed title only, not
   read.
8. **Timing.** Xeris and AbbVie are current to 2026-06-30; Recordati to 2026-06-30; Teva to 2025-12-31 (its
   FY2025 10-K; the Q2 2026 10-Q was not swept for the litigation update). The litigation status recorded is as of
