### THE STRONGEST EVIDENCE AGAINST THIS VERDICT, stated as its holders would state it [E4-51, E4-26, E3-47]
The verdict below is OUT, so the case for IN is set out first, at full strength.
1. *"The cost savings stayed home. Product gross margin went from 14.7% to 37.8% in eight years and to 41.2% in the first half of 2026. [E3-62]'s second step asks how much of a saving flows through to the customer; in a commodity business all of it does. Here none of it did: XPEL's film got cheaper and its installers kept paying the same. That is pricing power shown by conduct, even if no MD&A ever used the word."* **Answer:** it is the best argument in the file and part of it is accepted: XPEL kept the gains its suppliers gave it. But the filer itself names where most of the margin came from, every year, and it is not the installer's willingness to pay more: FY2018, *"Approximately 51.9% of the product gross margin percentage increase related to improvements in material costs related to increased volumes leading to lower per unit cost"*, with 15.2% from warranty costs as supplier film quality improved; FY2019-FY2021, *"primarily due to a lower percentage of sales to lower margin distributors (primarily our China Distributor)"* with product costs and operating leverage; FY2022, *"improved product costs, lower percentage of sales to lower margin distributors (primarily our China Distributor), and favorable changes in product mix"*; FY2023-FY2025, *"decreases in product costs, favorable changes in product mix and improved operating leverage"*; H1 2026 adds *"the on-going sell through of higher cost inventory acquired in our China distributor acquisition"*. Distributors fell from about 42% of revenue to 16% and the China distributor from 29.2% to 5.7%; that shift alone moves the product margin up without any customer paying more for the same thing. And in 2025 the margin movement ran the other way when the supplier, not the customer, set a price: the CFO in the Q3 2025 release (8-K EX-99.1, accession `0001767258-25-000064`), *"We did see some gross margin pressure during the third quarter due to unfavorable, non-tariff related price increases that were not in line with the market."* The cost was absorbed (gross margin 41.8% against 42.5%), not passed on. What stayed home was the distributor's margin being withdrawn and the supplier's cost falling; neither is [E3-43]'s *"regularly price its product or service aggressively"*.
2. *"The returns are extraordinary. Operating income on net tangible operating assets ran 88-116% FY2018-FY2021 and 47-62% FY2022-FY2025. [E3-46] says the best businesses earn very high returns on capital employed over time, and XPEL does."* **Answer:** accepted as the return. But the return is made by the structure: XPEL owns no film plant (net property $15.8M at 2025-12-31 against $476.2M of sales), so the capital that makes the film is its suppliers', and a brand-and-distribution business earns a very high return on very little tangible capital whether or not it has pricing power (the GWW run's finding for a distributor: the measure *"does not separate a franchise from a business here"*). The trend is down, from above 100% to 47%, as inventory rose from $22.4M to $122.8M, and on the capital XPEL has actually paid for its purchases the return was 25.8% in FY2025. [E3-43] ties the return to the pricing; the pricing half is the half not shown.
3. *"The attacker's test [E2-45] was run in real life and XPEL won it. Eastman, a chemical company that makes its own film, names XPEL as a competitor of its LLumar and SunTek performance films in every 10-K read (FY2019, FY2021, FY2023, FY2025), announced its own installer software in 2019 (Eastman CORE, 'next generation analytics-based software platform for automotive window and paint protection film products, enabling more efficient application and overall business management for dealers'), and has 'one of the largest distribution and dealer networks'. Since then XPEL's paint protection film revenue went from $97.3M to $249.4M, and Eastman's performance films reporting unit was in FY2025 the one unit of all Eastman's whose fair value was not found to 'significantly exceed' its carrying value."* **Answer:** accepted as the direction of the contest to date, and it is why this verdict is not written lightly. But it shows XPEL out-selling one attacker in a category whose adoption was growing fast (*"awareness and adoption of paint protection film continued to increase, driving significant industry growth over the last several years"*, the filer's own account), which is [E3-51]'s wave as much as a moat; and Eastman's FY2025 10-K gives the reason for its own weakness as the market, *"Lower sales volume in the performance films product line was driven by weak consumer discretionary spending in the automotive end-market"*, not XPEL. The attacker's test asks whether an attacker with capital could compete; the filings name one that does, with the same bundle (brand, film, software, dealer network), plus *"numerous suppliers in Korea and China"*. An attacker that has not yet won is not an attacker that cannot compete.
4. *"The installer is locked in. The pattern library ('over 90,000 vehicle applications'), the monthly software fee, the cutbank credits that come with each roll, the certified-installer requirement and the dealer locator that sends end customers only to Authorized Dealers make leaving XPEL expensive for an installer, and the car owner asks for XPEL by name."* **Answer:** the filer lists these as its differentiation, and the software fee and credits are real ($8.7M and $16.5M of FY2025 revenue). But the Authorized Dealer standing is bought with *"purchase minimums"*, which is a volume incentive, not a price the installer pays for access; the rival's software exists (Eastman CORE, above); and no filing read says that car owners ask for XPEL by name. At the new-car dealer, which buys most of the film that is fitted to new cars, the filer's own words are that XPEL *"competes for “shelf space”"* and that *"Dealerships have several options to sell additional products and/or services to automobile purchasers in order to increase their gross profit per vehicle"*: the dealer chooses on its own gross profit, which is a price comparison.
5. *"The flat year passed. Paint protection film revenue fell 1.4% in FY2024 and product gross margin still rose, 36.4% to 37.3%: [E2-44]'s price under flat demand."* **Answer:** the FY2024 fall was one customer's destocking (*"Sales to China reached a record high in the fourth quarter of 2023. Consequently, first quarter 2024 China sales were negatively impacted as our distributor sold through the inventory purchased at the end of last year"*, China −78.2% in Q1 2024), and the margin rose because that low-margin channel shrank (*"lower margin distributors (primarily our China Distributor)"*). [E2-44] asks for *"an ability to increase prices rather easily (even when product demand is flat and capacity is not fully utilized)"*; no price increase is recorded in FY2024, so the test is not reached, let alone passed.

None of the five shows a price raised. The first and third are the strongest facts against this verdict and are carried into the reversal condition below.

### THE READING
**[E3-43]'s own test is single-company: *"The existence of all three conditions will be demonstrated by a company’s ability to regularly price its product or service aggressively and thereby to earn high rates of return on capital."*** XPEL shows the returns; it does not show the pricing.
- **The pricing half is not shown in eight years of filings. A search recorded here, not an absence claim beyond it:** every MD&A of the Form 10/A (FY2018), the 10-Ks FY2019-FY2025 and the 10-Qs of 2026 was searched for *price increase, pricing, price action, higher price, average selling price, price reduction, pricing pressure* (`sents.py`, output in this file's working notes); **no instance of a price increase taken was found in any MD&A or release read**. The only price sentences are risk factors, and they point the other way. From the FY2024 10-K on: *"Sustained price increases may lead to declines in volume as competitors may not adjust their prices or customers may decide not to pay the higher prices, which could lead to sales declines and loss of market share."* and *"There is no assurance that we will be able to fully offset any such cost increases through cost reduction programs or price increases of our products, especially given the competitive environment."* In every 10-K and the Form 10: *"Such competition may result in pressure on our profit margins and limit our ability to maintain or increase the market share of our products"*, and competitors *"may adopt more aggressive pricing policies"*. **That is [E4-37]'s question (*"if we raised the price 10 cents a pound, would sales fall off a cliff?"*) answered by the filer in writing: it tells its owners that its customers would move to rivals who did not follow.**
- **The margin gains are the supplier's and the channel's, in the filer's own words every year** (item 1 above), and in 2025 the supplier took some back (*"price increases that were not in line with the market"*). **The product is made by others and is available to others.** The filer: *"Historically, the Company has pursued an “asset-light” manufacturing model whereby third-party suppliers and manufacturers have been used to supply the Company with the majority of its products"*; one class of products is *"Products that we source from suppliers on a non-exclusive basis – the Company does not own the underlying IP but sources products on commercial terms from a third party"*. The principal film maker until 2022 held the technology: *"the primary supplier has retained all of the rights to its technology and products relating to protective films subject to the Company’s exclusive right to commercialize, market, distribute and sell products manufactured by the primary supplier to the automotive aftermarket"*, supplying *"approximately 87%"* of purchases in FY2018 and *"approximately 75%"* in FY2021, until *"On January 20, 2022, we gave notice to entrotech that the Company would not extend the term of the entrotech Agreement in its current form."* XPEL's answer to its margin now is to become a manufacturer: the CEO in the same Q3 2025 release, *"Given our scale today, we believe this is the right time to further invest in manufacturing and our supply chain with the intention of driving significant margin improvement"*, with the stated goal *"of increasing gross margin to a range of 52% to 54% and operating margin to the mid to high 20% range by the end of 2028"*. A franchise raises its return by its price; this plan raises it by owning the plant.
- **Direction [E4-32]: narrowing at the operating line.** Operating margin 16.9% (FY2023) → 14.1% → 13.2% → 13.9% (H1 2026) while gross margin rose, because selling and marketing went from 8.0% to 10.7% of revenue (*"increased personnel, and additional marketing projects including sponsorships and increased marketing efforts to dealerships and end customers"*) and overhead from 16.1% to 18.3%. Holding growth costs more each year. Units are not filed [E4-55].

**[E3-03] criterion by criterion.**
- **(1) needed or desired: passes**, as a desire rather than a need: an option bought with a new car.
- **(2) thought by its customers to have no close substitute: fails, in the filer's own filings.** XPEL's principal competitors, in its words: *"Eastman Chemical Company (under the LLumar and Suntek brands), numerous suppliers in Korea and China and several other smaller companies"*; the filer warns that a sustained price rise would lose volume to competitors who *"may not adjust their prices"*; the new-car dealer chooses among film suppliers for *"gross profit per vehicle"*; the rival sells the same bundle of film, pattern software and dealer network; and the film itself is made by third parties, some of it non-exclusively. A customer who would switch on price has a close substitute.
- **(3) not subject to price regulation: passes.**
- **[E2-58] is not argued**: XPEL has no filed cost advantage (it buys its film and plans to build a cost position by 2028), so the low-cost door is not its door. The CAT, GWW, ABT and ABBV operator questions do not bear on this file: XPEL has one segment (*"these segments have been aggregated into a single reportable segment"*), no government price, and no cost-advantage claim.

- **The row's limit, stated [E3-61]:** Eastman's films are a fifth of a segment of a chemical company; 3M, Saint-Gobain and the Korean and Chinese makers (the filer's *"numerous suppliers"*, Eastman's list: *"XPEL, Inc. 3M Company Saint-Gobain S.A."*) are unsegmented, foreign or private. **The class is not held PROVISIONAL** (the GWW and MSA precedent): the verdict does not rest on the row but on [E3-43]'s single-company test and on the filer's own words about its customers and competitors, which the framework's convention table names as the corpus's own test (*"the corpus's own moat test is single-company — pricing conduct plus returns on capital [E3-43]; requiring the same-metric peer row on top is OURS"*). Peers named: **one** filed, of an industry the filer describes as Eastman, 3M, *"numerous suppliers in Korea and China and several other smaller companies"*.

- **[E2-44] (2), growth with minor capital: met on property, not on working capital.** Net property stayed under $18M to FY2025, but inventory went from $10.5M to $122.8M (FY2017-FY2025) and $112.8M was paid for purchases FY2017-FY2025 (acquisition cash, of which $108.6M in FY2021-FY2025); in 2026 the $110M manufacturing programme begins (Step 0).
- **The attacker's test [E2-45]:** run above (item 3): the attacker exists, is larger, makes its own film and sells the same bundle.
- **[E3-33], untapped pricing power: not claimed.** Scoped by [E5-28] (*"a monopoly or a near monopoly"*); the row and the filer's own competitor list do not support near-monopoly.
- **[E4-04] and key persons [E4-23].** The advantage the filer names (brand, patterns, installer network, service) is **defended by spending, not rebuilt from zero** ([E5-23]); the pattern library must be kept current (*"When new vehicle models are introduced to the market, we strive to create the pattern as soon as practicable"*), which is defence of the same asset. **Not the [E4-04] perimeter close**: the question is answered in the filings, and the answer is about substitutes, not durability. Ryan Pape has been chief executive through the whole filed record; the filings do not show the business depending on him, and the ground here is not [E4-23].
- **[E4-36], which cause of success:** the record is the fast adoption of paint protection film carried by a brand that got to installers first, which is wave-riding (*"if he gets off the wave, he becomes mired in shallows"* [E3-51]) more than an ownable position; the wave's sign in the filings is the category growth the filer credits (*"awareness and adoption of paint protection film continued to increase"*).

**THE REVERSAL CONDITION, in words (a Q2 OUT arms no price band; the QLYS ruling of 2026-09-07).** Q2 would be re-opened only on filed evidence, never on a lower price: (1) **a filed price increase on XPEL film** (a statement in an MD&A or release that XPEL raised its prices) **held through a year of flat or falling film revenue outside a general inflation, with product gross margin not falling that year**, which is [E2-44]'s first characteristic shown; **and** (2) **the product margin holding once the manufacturing programme is complete** (from FY2028), so that the margin is XPEL's own and not its suppliers' concession; **and** (3) **operating margin above Eastman's Advanced Materials segment by a clear gap for three consecutive years**, or a filed performance-films margin from Eastman that XPEL exceeds. (1) is required; (2) and (3) are the corroboration.
