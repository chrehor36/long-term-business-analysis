**The hypotheses I formed while reading, both stated so they can be attacked [E4-26].** *(a) Kenvue owns some of the most trusted names in the drugstore; customers pay a large premium for Tylenol and Zyrtec over identical store-brand molecules and only a fifth to a quarter of them switch; gross margin is 58% and the return on tangible capital is 66-89%; the recent volume losses are retailer destocking, weak cold seasons and a skin-care execution problem that a franchise tolerates [E3-43]; so it is IN, NARROW, like PG, CL and CHD.* *(b) The same names have not grown in dollars for sixteen years; in the three years the standalone company has filed, it raised price 10.7% and sold 6.1% fewer units, lost share in skin care, cut prices in 2025 and still lost units, and earned less operating income in nominal dollars at the end than at the start; that is [E4-55]'s shape and the Clorox run's shape, and the static margins are what every branded staple in the row earns.* The file does not get to pick. Each is tested on the filings below.

**[E3-03], criterion by criterion.** *"An economic franchise arises from a product or service that: (1) is needed or desired; (2) is thought by its customers to have no close substitute and; (3) is not subject to price regulation."*
- **(1) Needed or desired [x]: passes.** Pain relievers, allergy tablets, mouthwash, bandages and sunscreen are bought by most households every year.
- **(3) Not subject to price regulation [x]: passes, with one qualification.** Prices are set by the company, competitors and retailers; the FDA regulates what a label may claim and how products are made, not what they cost. The filings name price-affecting regulation only abroad (the ORSL trade name impaired *"following regulatory changes in India"*) and in generic-approval policy (*"Legislative proposals emerge from time to time in various jurisdictions that would further encourage the early and rapid approval of generic non-branded products"*, IPO prospectus).
- **(2) Thought by its customers to have no close substitute: SPLIT BY SEGMENT, and on the company's record it is not shown.**
  - *Self Care (42% of 2025 sales, 56% of segment profit): the best evidence in the file for a franchise, in the filer's own figures.* The IPO prospectus: *"in the allergy care category, where Zyrtec had the number one market share on a global basis in 2022, private-label brands collectively accounted for approximately 27% of the global sales in that category. In addition, in 33 the pain care category, where Tylenol had the number one market share on a global basis in 2022, private-label brands collectively accounted for approximately 19% of the global sales in that category."* (the "33" is a page number inside the extracted sentence, left as extracted). The store brand is the same molecule at a lower price, and roughly four in five pain-care purchases worldwide still go to a brand. The segment earned **35.6%, 33.3% and 33.1%** of its sales (2023-2025, segment adjusted operating income, before depreciation and amortization of intangibles). **Recorded as the strongest evidence for hypothesis (a).** The share figure has not been repeated in any 10-K since (the 2023, 2024 and 2025 10-Ks return no instance of *"private-label brands collectively"*), so its direction after 2022 cannot be read from the filings.
  - *Skin Health and Beauty (27% of sales, 13% of segment profit): customers are finding substitutes, in the filer's words.* 2024: *"declines in Skin Health and Beauty due to volume-related decreases in the United States attributable to the carryover effects from prior fiscal year execution challenges and current fiscal year competitive pressures"*; 2025: *"Organic sales decreased 2.7% driven by both volume-related decreases of 1.8% and unfavorable value realization of 0.9% attributable to strategic price investments"* and *"distribution losses, underperformance in e-commerce, and current fiscal year competitive pressures resulting in market share losses"*. The segment's margin fell **15.5% → 14.3% → 11.6%** and its acquired Japanese brand was written down: *"a non-cash charge of $488 million ($337 million after-tax) to adjust the carrying value of intangible assets and property, plant, and equipment related to the Dr.Ci:Labo ® skin health business"* (2024; a J&J purchase of 2019).
  - *Essential Health (31% of sales, 31% of segment profit): priced hard in 2023 and lost units.* 2023: value realization +9.6, volume −6.0 (*"overall volume declines primarily due to category contractions"* and the Russia suspension); 2025 Oral Care: *"market deceleration and competitive pressures in Oral Care in North America"*; H1 2026 *"decreases in Oral Care largely attributable to competitive pressures"*.
  - The company is the unit of the test **[E4-08]**, so criterion (2) is decided by [E3-03]'s own demonstration below, with Self Care's evidence carried as the strongest against the verdict.

- **[E4-55], units. FAILS for the company.** *"Dollar revenue flattered by pricing is how a shrinking franchise hides; the physical series is the honest one"* (framework, on [E4-55]). Volume fell in **every** year the standalone company has filed, **−6.1% compounded in three years while value realization rose 10.7%**: organic dollars up about 4%, units down about 6%. That is Precision Steel's shape (*"holding dollar volume roughly level despite a precipitous drop in physical volume"*) on a shorter span, and it is worse per year than the Clorox record this project closed OUT (units about 94 in FY2026 on FY2019 = 100, after about 28 points of price, over seven years). The first half of 2026 (+0.2) is the first positive half-year on file; it is recorded, and one half-year does not reverse three years.
- **[E2-44]'s first characteristic, *"an ability to increase prices rather easily (even when product demand is flat and capacity is not fully utilized) without fear of significant loss of either market share or unit volume"*. Passes for Self Care in 2023-2024; fails for the company.** Self Care took +7.1 and grew units +1.3 in 2023, then +2.5 / −0.6 in 2024: the franchise behaviour. Skin Health and Beauty lost 4.8 points of units for 6.6 of price, and Essential Health 6.0 for 9.6, in 2023: a worse ratio than Procter & Gamble (FY2023 +9 / −3) or Colgate (+10 / −0.5) in the same year. In 2025 the company **could not price** (+0.1, with *"strategic price investments"*, that is, price cuts, in skin care and elsewhere) **and still lost units** (−2.3).
- **[E2-44]'s second characteristic, capital-light growth: passes on tangible capital, with no growth to carry.** Net tangible operating assets **$2.6-3.2bn** against **$15.1-15.5bn** of sales (`peers/kvrow_out.txt`). But there has been little dollar growth for the capital to accommodate (below).
- **[E4-47], nominal earning power through an inflation: FAILS.** Operating income as filed, impairments added back: **$2,920M (2021), $2,675M (2022), $2,512M (2023), $2,419M (2024), $2,437M (2025)**; the first two are J&J carve-out years with allocated costs, and 2023-2025 carry separation costs ($468M, $296M, $88M) and restructuring ($32M, $221M, $335M) that the J&J years did not. On the filer's own cleaner measure, before those items, **segment adjusted operating income fell from $3,989M (2023) to $3,942M (2024) to $3,762M (2025)**, down 5.7% in nominal dollars in two years while the company raised price.
- **The long series, from the predecessor's own filings.** J&J's consumer segment sales: *"Consumer segment sales in 2009 were $15.8 billion"*; 2010 $14.6bn (*"an operational decline"* of 8.9%, the McNeil recall year: *"McNeil’s recalls of products manufactured at both Las Piedras and Fort Washington facilities impacted the total year sales by approximately $900 million"*); 2013 $14.7bn; 2016 $13.3bn; 2019 $13.9bn; 2021 $14.6bn; Kenvue 2025 **$15.1bn**. **Sixteen years without nominal growth in dollars (−0.3% a year, 2009-2025).** The limits are stated: the perimeters are not identical (Kenvue's carve-out 2021 is $15,054M against J&J's consumer segment $14,635M, because Kenvue *"also included certain other product lines previously reported in another segment of J&J"*), brands were sold (SPLENDA, NIZORAL) and bought (Ci:z Holdings, the Dr.Ci:Labo owner: J&J's 2019 growth was *"primarily driven by incremental sales from the acquisition of Ci:z Holding"*), and the dollar rose against most currencies. In J&J's local-currency terms the segment reported "operational growth" of roughly 1.5-3.0% a year in the years read (2013 +2.8%, 2016 +1.5%, 2019 +3.0% of which +1.6 from acquisitions). Either way, the business has not grown in real terms across the span, and J&J's filings carry no unit series to separate price from volume.

**The remaining Q2 tests.**
- **Untapped pricing power [E3-33] / [E5-28]: no.** The class requires *"a monopoly or a near monopoly"*; the filer's 2025 action was the opposite (*"strategic price investments"*), and its risk factors say *"we may not be able to sustain these price increases, or sustained price increases may eventually lead to a decline in sales volume"*.
- **[E4-37], the agony metric.** Price realization went +7.7, +2.7, +0.1 while units fell each year, and in 2025 prices were cut in skin care to hold distribution: agony, in the filings' own sequence.
- **[E2-53], dominance.** Claimed by rank for Tylenol and Zyrtec in 2022 (*"number one market share on a global basis"*); not for the company, and the rank claims are not repeated in the 10-Ks.
- **[E2-45], the attacker's test.** The attacker is on the same shelf at a lower price in every Self Care category (the retailer's own label, made by Perrigo and others), and in skin care the filing concedes *"market share losses"* to competitors it does not name. The attacker's economics (Perrigo) are poor, which protects the brand margin; it has not protected the units.
- **[E4-32], direction: DOWN.** Segment adjusted operating income $3,989M → $3,762M (2023-2025); Skin Health and Beauty margin 15.5% → 11.6%; units down three years; the one segment holding its margin (Self Care, 35.6% → 33.1%) lost 3.4 points of units in 2025.
- **[E4-36], which cause of success?** The one in the filings is **old brand equity in categories where the molecule is generic**, the maximisation of one variable (trust) built over a century under J&J; the record since 2009 shows it held in dollars, not widened.
- **[E4-04] and [E4-23], the rebuild and key-person tests.** The core names are defended, not replaced: this is not the rapid-change class, and the perimeter close (UNKNOWABLE) is **refused in writing**: durability can be read from the filings, and the filings show direction down. No key-person dependence found.
- **[E4-40], exposure, not experience.** The acetaminophen allegations (the Texas petition of October 2025, the FDA's label-change process, two citizen petitions) are exposure on the largest Self Care brand: *"The foregoing actions may depress sales of products containing acetaminophen"* (2025 10-K). Recorded; not used to decide criterion (2), because no filed figure yet measures an effect (Pain Care 11-12% of sales in the second quarter and first half of both 2025 and 2026, the 10-Q's category table).

**THE STRONGEST EVIDENCE AGAINST THE VERDICT BELOW, hunted hardest [E4-26, E3-41, E4-51].** The verdict below is OUT, so the case for IN is set out first, as its holders would state it:
1. **Customers pay for the name over the identical molecule, and the filer measured it**: private label only about 19% of global pain care and 27% of allergy care in 2022, with Tylenol and Zyrtec first.
2. **The margin says so too**: gross margin 58% (the row's highest), Self Care earning a third of its sales before depreciation, pre-tax return on tangible capital 66-89%; the substitute's maker earns 3%.
3. **The volume losses have named temporary causes**: US retailer destocking, weak cold, flu and allergy seasons, China shipment timing, a 2022 skin-care execution failure and a new management since July 2025; the first half of 2026 has units up 0.2% and Skin Health and Beauty volume up 2.6% with segment profit up 46.9%.
4. **A franchise tolerates mismanagement [E3-43]**: *"Inept managers may diminish a franchise’s profitability, but they cannot inflict mortal damage"*; the skin-care problem is described by the filer as execution, which is management.
5. **The names survived worse**: the 2010 McNeil recalls took 8.9% off J&J's consumer sales in a year, and the brands came back.
6. **Kimberly-Clark agreed a price that *"values Kenvue at an enterprise value of approximately $48.7 billion"*** (joint release, EX-99.1 to the 8-K of 2025-11-03, on KMB's 2025-10-31 close), *"approximately 14.3x Kenvue’s LTM adjusted EBITDA"*: a purchaser's judgment on the brands.

**Answered.** (1) and (2) are recorded as the strongest evidence and they are why Self Care is carried as a probable franchise inside a company that does not show one; but [E3-43]'s demonstration asks for pricing *regularly* and returns that follow, and the company's pricing ran +7.7, +2.7, +0.1 with units lost in every year, while every branded staple in the row earns high returns on tangible capital (the Clorox run's finding, which closed OUT on the same pattern). The 2022 share figures have not been refiled, so their direction is not on the record. (3) Each cause is named by the filer and each is plausible; together they are three consecutive years, across all three segments, and 2025's price cuts are not a season. The first half of 2026 is recorded, and a half-year does not answer [E4-55]'s seven-year Precision Steel test. (4) The corpus's sentence cuts both ways: if Kenvue is a franchise diminished by inept managers, the diminution is what the filings show, and [E3-43]'s contrast is that "a business" *"can be killed by poor management"*; the filer's own attribution of the skin-care decline to *"execution challenges"* is evidence that that segment depends on daily execution, the mark of "a business". (5) The recall recovery is J&J-era and carries no unit series; dollars in 2025 are below dollars in 2009. (6) Kimberly-Clark's price is its judgment, and the KMB run of 2026-09-01 did not score it; a purchaser's premium is not [E3-03]'s evidence. **[E3-47]** (a wrongly closed file inside the circle of competence is the costliest error) was weighed against OUT in writing: it is why Self Care's evidence is set out in full and why the reversal conditions below are written to reopen the file on filed units.

**The analyst's own incentive, stated [operator rule 9, E4-27, E3-41].** Two pulls, opposite. The last three household runs (PG, CL, CHD) were admitted NARROW, which pulls toward IN by analogy; the Clorox run closed OUT on a similar unit record, which pulls toward OUT by analogy; and a live merger that will end the security makes any verdict low-stakes, which pulls toward a quick one. None was used as evidence: the verdict rests on Kenvue's own component tables, its segment profits and J&J's consumer sales series, and the case for IN was written at full strength first.
