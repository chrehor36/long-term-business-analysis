## Q1 — CAN I UNDERSTAND HOW THIS MAKES MONEY? **[E3-31]**
- **Unit economics in my own words, no management language:** Bio-Techne makes and sells the biological ingredients laboratories use in experiments and tests: purified signalling proteins (cytokines and growth factors), antibodies, small-molecule chemicals, cell-culture media and ready-made test kits that measure a protein in a sample, from a catalog the company says holds more than 500,000 products (the merger release: *"Its more than 500,000 products are used globally by academic researchers, biopharmaceutical and biotechnology companies, and clinical diagnostic laboratories"*). A scientist orders a vial for tens or hundreds of dollars and uses it up; most orders ship the day they arrive (*"The majority of our products are shipped within one day of receipt of the customers’ orders"*). **FY2026 revenue $1,215.0M: consumables $985.1M (81%), instruments $110.6M (9%), services $94.3M (8%), royalties $25.0M (2%).** The buyers are university and government laboratories, drug companies and contract research firms, cell-therapy developers and diagnostic manufacturers; *"no single end-user customer accounted for more than 10% of the Protein Sciences segment’s net sales"*, and none of the other segment's; Thermo Fisher Scientific is also a distributor (*"through a distribution agreement with Thermo Fisher Scientific"*). **Protein Sciences ($874.6M) is the reagent catalog plus protein-analysis instruments** (ProteinSimple's automated western blot and immunoassay machines, which then consume cartridges); **Diagnostics and Spatial Biology ($336.4M)** sells tissue-staining assays and an automated staining instrument (ACD's RNAscope, Lunaphore's COMET) to researchers, and quality-control materials and test kits to diagnostic-instrument makers, mostly under supply agreements (*"Most of these products are sold on an Original Equipment Manufacturer (OEM) basis to instrument manufacturers"*). **Costs:** gross margin 65.8% (75.0% in Protein Sciences, 55.2% in the other segment, before the unallocated amortization), research and development $94.8M (7.8% of revenue), selling and administration $452.4M. **Segment operating income FY2026: Protein Sciences $359.4M (41.1% of its revenue), Diagnostics and Spatial Biology $37.7M (11.2%)**, before $61.2M of acquired-intangible amortization, $41.4M of stock compensation and other unallocated costs; GAAP operating income $251.9M (20.7%).
- **The scarce input this business controls:** the **accumulated catalog and its reputation in the published literature**: fifty years of proteins and antibodies made and characterised in its own plants, so that a protocol written around an R&D Systems cytokine is repeated with the same vial (*"We believe we are the world leader in providing high quality proteins, both for research use and under current Good Manufacturing Practices, or cGMP"*); the **manufacturing know-how, held as trade secrets** (*"we also preserve much of our innovation as trade secrets, particularly in the Reagent Solutions division"*); **cGMP protein and small-molecule capacity** for cell-therapy makers; and in the smaller segment, patents (*"approximately 487 granted patents"*, the Analytical Solutions and Spatial Biology products *"protected primarily through pending patent applications and issued patents"*) and diagnostic-maker qualifications. Whether any of this lets it set prices is Q2's question.
- **Will the fundamentals look broadly the same in ten years?** The reagent business: yes, on the record: it has sold the same class of product to the same class of customer since 1976, and the FY1999 10-K describes the same business (*"The Biotechnology Division develops and manufactures purified cytokines (proteins), antibodies and assay kits which are sold to biomedical researchers and clinical research laboratories"*). The demand is tied to research budgets (the FY2026 organic decline is recorded at Q2) and is cyclical, not structural. The instruments and spatial-biology assays are in a faster-changing field, and the filer says so: *"We believe that our future success depends, to a large extent, on our ability to keep pace with changing technologies and market needs."* That part is 20-30% of revenue; it is [E4-04]'s question and is asked at Q2, where the framework places it.
