### THE STRONGEST EVIDENCE AGAINST THIS VERDICT, stated as its holders would state it [E4-51, E4-26, E3-47]
The verdict below is IN, NARROW. The case against it is set out first, at full strength.
1. *"In the one real depression this business has been through as a filer it lost money two years running and cut prices: the test and measurement segment went from $4.5bn of revenue to $2.6bn, lost 27 cents on the dollar in 2002 and 12 in 2003, and the 2003 10-K itself lists 'erosion in average selling prices', 'pricing pressures from both customers and competitors' and 'the competitive pressures of the used equipment market'. Gross margin in 2003 was 43%. A franchise does not lose money for two years."* **Answer:** accepted as the record of FY2002-FY2003, and it is the strongest fact in the file. Three limits on how far it reaches: the perimeter then included optical and wireline network test lines Agilent later exited (the FY2003 10-K: *"decisions made to discontinue or exit several weak product families in the wireline market"*), the collapse was the telecommunications capital-spending bust, and the same 10-K says of the rest: *"we compete primarily on the basis of performance or differentiated capabilities and our equipment is not subject to severe discounting."* It is carried into the reversal condition as the test that has not been repeated.
2. *"Tektronix, the closest filed rival, did better through the same bust and earned higher gross margins for five years after it: 49.4% and 51.3% in its FY2002-FY2003 against Agilent's 43%, and 56.8-59.8% in FY2004-FY2007 against 51-57%, with operating margins about level. On that record Agilent's measurement business was not the stronger franchise; it was the bigger one."* **Answer:** accepted; the row below carries it. The relative position has since reversed: Keysight's gross margin rose above 60% from FY2020, and Tektronix's successor segment inside Ralliant earned 61.1%, 59.1% and 55.4% in 2023-2025 with operating margins of 20.3%, 13.1% (6.4% without a property gain) and −3.0% before a $1,441.7M goodwill impairment, on revenue down 14.5% in 2025 while Keysight's rose 8.0%.
3. *"2009 again: revenue down 30%, gross margin down four points, operating margin from 13.3% to zero. Operating leverage that brutal means customers stop buying, and a product customers can defer is not a product without substitutes."* **Answer:** accepted as the fact of FY2009, with the filer's reason: *"Gross margins for products and services declined 4 percentage points year-over-year in 2009 compared to 2008 due primarily to the significant decline in revenue"*, and *"Volume-adjusted gross margins compared to 2008 improved"*. [E3-55] scopes it: volatility with a certain endgame is not a defect, and the margin was 15.7% the next year and 22.9% the year after. Deferral is a cycle, not a substitute; the substitute question is price, and FY2009 names none.
4. *"The filer says its own products go obsolete: 'Without the timely introduction of new solutions, services and enhancements, our solutions and services will become technologically obsolete over time', in 'a highly competitive and rapidly changing global marketplace', and it spends 17-19% of revenue on R&D to stay level. That is [E4-04]'s 'industries prone to rapid and continuous change' in the filer's words, and [E4-57]: 'we have no insights into which participants in the tech field possess a truly durable competitive advantage'."* **Answer:** this is the question the verdict turns on and it is answered at [E4-04] below: the filings show a lead maintained through every generation since 2001, against the same named competitors, not rebuilt from zero.
5. *"The margins are good, not exceptional. National Instruments earned 67.7-77.1% gross margins in every year it filed, Teradyne's operating margins beat Keysight's in 2017-2023, and EDA software earns more. Keysight is the largest, not the best."* **Answer:** accepted for gross margin (NI's modular, software-heavy product carries higher gross and lower operating margins: its GAAP operating margin was below Keysight's in every year FY2013-FY2022 except the two Ixia years, FY2017-FY2018). Teradyne sells mainly semiconductor production testers, a market next to Keysight's, though the filer names it among its competitors. The row supports NARROW, not WIDE.
6. *"The moat bought in 2017 failed within eighteen months: $1,622M for Ixia and a $709M goodwill impairment in FY2018. What was bought as a position in network test turned out to be worth less than paid."* **Answer:** accepted as a capital-allocation fact (Q3); at Q2 it shows that the network-test position Keysight bought was not the franchise the price assumed, and it is why the network-test part (now also Spirent) is read as the weaker part of the business.

### THE READING
- **The filer never names price as a basis of competition, and names price increases, not price cuts, as a margin cause since 2022.** Every Keysight 10-K that names a basis of competition names performance: FY2014 *"these products compete primarily on the basis of product quality and functionality"*; FY2016 *"product quality, differentiated capability, leading edge technology and long term value to our customers"*; FY2019 and FY2022 *"differentiated capability, first-to-market leading-edge technology, product quality and long-term value to our customers"*; the revenue-recognition note (FY2014-FY2018) that *"the solution offered differs substantially from that of competitors"*. The gross-margin explanations name **price increases** in FY2022 (*"primarily driven by lower amortization of acquisition-related balances, price increases, higher revenue volume and lower variable people-related costs, partially offset by higher material costs"*), **FY2023** (*"Gross margin increased 1 percentage point in 2023 compared to 2022, primarily driven by price increases and favorable mix"*) and FY2025 (*"partially offset by favorable pricing"*; for EISG *"the impact of tariffs and unfavorable mix, was offset by favorable pricing"*). **Price pressure** is named in FY2008 (Agilent: *"changes in product mix and pricing pressure"*), FY2003 (above) and FY2014 (*"higher inventory charges and and pricing pressure in the wireless manufacturing market"*, one market). No filing read since FY2003 attributes a margin fall to price across the business.
- **[E2-44] (1), tested in the years it bites.** FY2023 is the flat-demand year: revenue +0.8% ($5,420M to $5,464M) while *"Orders of $5,190 million for 2023 decreased 13 percent compared to 2022"*, and the margin rose on *"price increases and favorable mix"*; the test is met, in an inflation year, which flatters it. FY2013 (revenue −13%, orders −13%), in the measurement segment's words (FY2014 10-K): *"Gross margin in 2013 remained flat when compared to 2012 primarily due to the lower sales volume, offset by the favorable impact of a lower proportion of wireless manufacturing business"* — the margin held, price not named either way. FY2024 (revenue −9%, about −12% before acquisitions): gross margin −1.7 points, *"primarily driven by lower revenue volume, higher amortization of acquisition-related balances and higher restructuring costs"*, and for CSG *"flat compared to 2023, as lower revenue volume was offset by lower material and variable people-related costs"* — held, price not named. FY2020 (revenue −1.9%, COVID): gross margin +1.1 points, *"primarily driven by favorable mix and lower variable compensation"*. Since the spin the consolidated gross margin has fallen more than two points in one year only, FY2017 (55.7% to 53.2%, *"primarily driven by the unfavorable impacts from amortization of acquisition-related assets, fire-related costs at our corporate headquarters"*, Ixia's first year), and in no year on price except the one wireless-manufacturing market in FY2014.
- **[E2-44] (2), growth with minor capital: met.** Revenue rose from $2,933M (FY2014) to $5,464M (FY2023) with capex of $70-197M a year and net tangible capital of $648M-$2,181M; the added capital was purchases, and their goodwill is shown both ways above.
- **Returns on capital [E3-46]: high, on the business's own capital.** 46-77% pre-tax on net tangible capital in every year since FY2019; 13-34% with the purchases' goodwill left in, falling to 13.4% in FY2025 on the Spirent and OSG goodwill.
- **No administered price [E2-59].** Government is a customer (aerospace, defense and government $1,238M, 23% of revenue) but no price is set by regulation. **[E3-62], the second step:** the instruments' own gains (bandwidth, noise floor) are sold to the customer as the product; the margin record since 2010 shows gains staying home, not flowing through.

**[E4-04], applied as the framework rules it: a lead maintained, not rebuilt from zero; not the perimeter close.** The filer's words make this a have-to-stay-smart business ([E3-74]): products turn over with each communications standard, and the R&D bill (FY2025 $1,007M) is the cost of staying level. The test is whether the advantage's **basis** must be replaced or merely defended [E5-23]. The filings answer it across twenty-five years: the same business, selling the same classes of instrument to the same kinds of engineer (the FY2003 Agilent 10-K: *"Tektronix, Inc.. In communications test, our primary competitors are Acterna, Aeroflex Incorporated, Anritsu, IXIA, Marconi Communications Ltd., Network Associates, Inc., Rohde & Schwartz, Spirent, Tektronix"*; the FY2019 Keysight 10-K: *"our primary competitors are Rohde & Schwarz GmbH & Co. KG, Anritsu Corporation, Fortive Corporation, Teledyne Technologies, National Instruments Corporation, Teradyne, Inc. and VIAVI Solutions Inc."*), through 2G to 5G, 10G to 1.6T Ethernet and the move to software, with its margin at the top of the filed row in the last ten years and two of the 2003 competitors (Ixia, Spirent) now inside it. The basis (measurement science, the proprietary components made in its own fab, the installed base and its calibration, the customer and standards-body relationships) is carried from one generation to the next; what is replaced is the product built on it. That is the KLAC case (*"a lapse narrows the lead; it does not vaporise the structure"*), not the INTC or TSM case, where the process itself is rebuilt at tens of billions a generation. **Durability is judged from filings here**, so the [E4-04] perimeter close does not apply; the competence limit is recorded as a live width and is the reason the class is NARROW. **Key persons [E4-23]:** no dependence on one person is shown at the business level.
