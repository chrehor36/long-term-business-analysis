## Q1 — CAN I UNDERSTAND HOW THIS MAKES MONEY? **[E3-31]**
- **Unit economics in my own words, no management language.** Interparfums rents the right to put a famous fashion or jewellery name on a bottle of perfume, and owns a few such names outright. For each name it decides the scents, the bottle and the box, hires outside perfume houses to compose the juice, buys glass, caps, pumps and boxes from component makers, pays contract fillers to fill and pack, and sells the finished goods to department stores, duty-free operators and exclusive distributors in over 120 countries (*"we do not own manufacturing facilities. We act as a general contractor and source our needed components from our suppliers"*). It pays the brand owner a royalty on sales (*"Royalty expense ... aggregated $ 121.7 million ... 8.2 % ... of net sales"* in FY2025) and must spend minimum amounts advertising the brand (*"Promotion and advertising ... aggregated $ 294.7 million ... 19.8 %"*). FY2025: net sales **$1,488.5M**, gross margin **63.6%**, operating income **$270.3M (18.2%)**, net income $208.1M of which $39.8M belongs to the 28% outside holders of the Paris subsidiary and **$168.4M to IPAR's owners**. The money is made in the gap between a gross margin of about two thirds and the royalty plus the advertising the licence requires plus selling and overhead. **The capital is in stock and receivables, not plant**: inventory $351.4M (*"inventory days on hand decreased to 244 days in 2025"*) and receivables $320.6M (*"days sales outstanding was 73 days"*) against property of $184.9M, most of it the Paris headquarters bought in 2021. 662 employees.
- **The scarce input this business controls.** A portfolio of **exclusive, time-limited licences** to other companies' trademarks for fragrance (Jimmy Choo to 2031, Coach to 2031, Montblanc to 2030, GUESS to 2048, Lacoste to 2038, Donna Karan/DKNY to 2032, Ferragamo to 2031, and seventeen others in the FY2025 licence table, two of them (David Beckham, Nautica) not yet in force; Puma was signed in September 2026), a handful of **owned** fragrance trademarks (Lanvin in Class 3, subject to the seller's repurchase right in 2027; Rochas; Off-White in Class 3; Goutal; Solférino), and the know-how and distribution network that brand owners hire it for (*"Certain fashion designers and other licensors choose us as a partner because our Company's size enables us to work more closely with them in the product development process as well as our successful track record"*). Whether any of that is scarce against the other licensees, and to whom the consumer's preference belongs, is Q2's question.
- **Will the fundamentals look broadly the same in ten years?** The trade will: prestige fragrance is made and sold the same way in the FY2004 10-K and the FY2025 10-K (outsourced composition and filling, selective distribution, launches and flankers). **The portfolio will not**: every licence in the top seven expires between 2030 and 2048 unless renewed, and the filed record shows portfolios turning over (Burberry, 62% of sales in FY2004, gone after FY2012). That is a Q2 and Q4 matter, not a failure of understanding.
- **Relatively simple and stable in character [E3-31]? A decision in five minutes [E4-46]?** Yes on the mechanism: buy components, pay fillers, pay a royalty and the advertising, sell to retailers. Two features are carried forward, not held against understanding: the dependence on public taste (*"pronounced and rapidly changing public tastes"*), and the two-currency structure (about half of the European sales in dollars against euro costs).
