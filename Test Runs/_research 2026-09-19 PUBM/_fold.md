### THE PRIOR THE BRIEF ASKED TO BE ARGUED AGAINST, AND HOW IT FARED
The brief expected Q2 on price competition, as at PATH, and asked for the case against: PubMatic is profitable where PATH was not, owns its hardware, and cut its
cost per impression 19%, 8%, 18% and 20% in four years. **All of that is true in the filings, and it is why the closing record is the unit series rather than a
headline**: revenue per impression fell faster than cost per impression, so the advantage did not stay home [E3-62].

### ONE THING TO KEEP
**Divide revenue by the filer's own volume series, and check that the cost side reproduces the filer's own number first.** PubMatic never files its take rate, but
it files impressions and states each year how far its cost per impression fell. The cost column rebuilt from filed figures matched those statements exactly, which
licensed the same construction on the revenue side, and there the unit price fell faster than the unit cost. For a business whose moat claim is a cost advantage,
that is [E3-62]'s *"how much is going to stay home"* answered from the filings.
