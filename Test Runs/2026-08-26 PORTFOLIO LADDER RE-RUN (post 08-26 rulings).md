# ⚠ CORRECTION ADDENDUM — same day, filed under Operator Protocol rule 5
**The operator challenged the owner-earnings formula. The challenge was correct.**

**The error:** Buffett's 1986 definition [E2-23] says "(c) the **average annual amount**."
I applied a **single year's** actual working-capital swing (embedded in one year's
operating cash flow). That is exactly the **Method B error I had rejected for TJX two
hours earlier** in the same day's work. I was inconsistent between two names.

**Why it mattered most at TBTC:** its 2022 owner-earnings figure of -$0.28M was one year
of inventory build and customer-deposit timing on lumpy casino installs, not a business
failing to earn. Verified against the filed statement: TBTC's 2025 net working-capital
change was **-$201,528** on a $1.6M net income, i.e. roughly neutral, and 2024 was a
**+$275K source**. There is no structural working-capital drain.

**Corrected construction (supersedes FINDING A below):**

    OE = mean over 3 years of (Operating Cash Flow − SBC) − mean maintenance capex

The multi-year mean is what delivers Buffett's "average annual amount" for BOTH the
non-cash items and the working-capital change. A single year does not.

# TWO METHOD FINDINGS THAT CAME OUT OF DOING THIS

## FINDING A — the owner-earnings construction is rebuilt off ONE filed line
Applying Ruling 10 by summing XBRL working-capital tags produced a TBTC owner-earnings
figure of $0.69M. Cross-checking against the filed cash flow statement gave $1.78M. The
tag-summing was wrong: filers tag working capital inconsistently and a tag list can
never be complete.

**Corrected construction, used throughout this file:**

    OE = Operating Cash Flow − Stock-Based Compensation − maintenance capex
         maintenance capex band = [ min(D&A, total capex) .. max(D&A, total capex) ]

Why this is better and not merely easier:
- **Operating cash flow already nets the entire working-capital change**, from a single
  audited line, which is exactly Buffett's "(a) + (b) less the WC increment" without any
  tag reconstruction.
- **SBC is subtracted, not added back.** OCF adds it back as non-cash; it is a real cost
  to owners and the corpus treats it as one.
- **The capex band survives** per Ruling 10. Note the band is not always "D&A is
  generous": where D&A exceeds total capex (HRB, TBTC), spending *less* than depreciation
  is the conservative-looking figure but may signal under-investment. The band is stated
  both ways and the wider of the two bounds is used as the conservative end.
- Cross-checked on all four names against reported OCF minus capex; it reconciles.

**This supersedes the Method C arithmetic in the 2026-08-26 TJX run.** TJX's verdict is
unchanged (see the cross-check at the bottom).

## FINDING B — Ruling 9 needs an amendment. PROPOSED as RULING 9-A, NOT APPLIED.
Ruling 9 retired the trough anchor and set Book One to the **5-year median**. Applying it
to Visa exposed a problem: Visa's owner earnings went $9.3B → $20.7B over five years. The
5-year median ($17.8B) is not "normal earning power," it is a **stale** number, and
preferring it to the current tier is conservatism in the numerator, which is exactly what
Ruling 7 forbids.

The corpus says **"Wells Fargo currently earns"** [E3-24] and **"conservatively
representative of future earning power"** [E2-24]. Both point at the CURRENT tier, not a
backward-looking median.

**Proposed Ruling 9-A:** the Book One anchor is the **most recent verified tier**. The
5-year median becomes the **consistency cross-check**: where the current tier and the
median diverge materially, the run must say why, and if the divergence cannot be
explained the correct response is not to average it away but to ask whether the business
shows **"demonstrated consistent earning power"** at all [E2-25]. A tier table that is not
consistent is a Gate 2 problem, not a Gate 6 input problem.

**Both anchors are shown for every name below**, so nothing turns on an unratified rule.
Book Two uses the current tier as its base in all cases.
