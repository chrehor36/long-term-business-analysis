## STEP 0 — RATE REFRESH
Japan 30-yr JGB **3.76%**, floored at **4.00%**. Mkt cap $6.71B.

## GATE 1 — CIRCLE OF COMPETENCE
Builds and manages rental housing (apartments/small multi-unit buildings)
for individual landowners in Japan under a build-to-suit/subleasing model —
Daito builds the property, then master-leases it back from the landowner
and manages tenanting, a distinctive Japanese business model (guaranteed-
rent subleasing) not common in Western markets. **VERDICT: PASS**, with the
subleasing-guarantee structure specifically flagged as needing more
verification than a standard homebuilder — Daito bears occupancy risk on
guaranteed-rent contracts, a genuine, non-obvious liability structure.

## GATE 2 — MOAT
Franchise test: needed/desired — yes; no close substitute — **mixed**: the
guaranteed-rent subleasing model creates real switching costs for existing
landowner clients (once locked into a master lease, changing operators is
disruptive), a more specific moat candidate than plain homebuilding;
unregulated — largely yes. **Class: NARROW** (contractual lock-in with
existing landowner clients, not pricing power on new business).
**VERDICT: PASS with NARROW classification.**

## GATE 3 — MANAGEMENT / FORTRESS
Not independently verified this pass.
**VERDICT: PASS (provisional)**, unverified — **and Daito's guaranteed-rent
liability structure makes independent balance-sheet verification more
important here than for a typical homebuilder**, a gap worth naming
explicitly rather than treating as routine.

Fortress — **partial follow-up completed 2026-07-15/16 PM (web research,
not a primary-source filing, since none exists for this unsponsored ADR)**:
Daito's own disclosure shows **future guaranteed-rent lease payments
totaling approximately ¥2.4945 trillion as of March 2024** (roughly $16-17B
at recent JPY/USD rates) — a real, quantified, and very large committed
payment stream relative to Daito's $6.71B market cap. **This is a gross
future-payment commitment, not a net loss exposure**: it is expected to be
substantially funded by tenant rent Daito collects on the same properties;
the actual risk is the GAP between what Daito guarantees to landowners and
what it actually collects from tenants if vacancy rises (the scenario named
at Gate 5), not the full ¥2.4945T figure being "at risk." **This run still
could not obtain Daito's own balance-sheet equity/asset figures** to compute
what fraction of capital this guarantee stream represents — the follow-up
is now partially complete (the scale of the obligation is now quantified and
sourced) but the capital-adequacy question against that obligation remains
open.
**VERDICT: PASS (mechanically)**, with the guarantee-obligation scale now
quantified but the capital-coverage question still unresolved — downgraded
from "single most important open item, entirely unquantified" to "open item,
now partially scoped."
