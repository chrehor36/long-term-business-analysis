## Q1 — CAN I UNDERSTAND HOW THIS MAKES MONEY? **[E3-31]**
- Unit economics in my own words, no management language: **Core Lab is paid to measure things and to sell one consumable, in two segments.** **Reservoir Description (FY2025 revenue $347.7M, 66%; operating income $43.9M, 12.6%)** runs a network of laboratories (*"over 70 offices in more than 50 countries"*, five large technology centres in Aberdeen, Abu Dhabi, Houston, Kuala Lumpur and Rotterdam) to which oil companies send rock cores and fluid samples from their wells; Core Lab measures porosity, permeability and how the oil, gas and water behave at reservoir pressure and temperature, and bills per test or per study. About two-thirds of that segment is fluids work, which includes testing crude oil and refined products for quality and quantity when cargoes move (*"Fluid analyses account for approximately two-thirds of Reservoir Description revenue."*). It also sells multi-client studies whose data it owns and resells, and a small line of laboratory instruments ($13.0M of product sales in FY2025). Costs are the scientists, the buildings and the equipment, which run whether or not samples arrive, so margin swings with volume. **Production Enhancement ($178.8M, 34%; operating income $12.1M, 6.7%)** is mainly a manufacturer: it makes perforating charges and guns (the explosives lowered into a cased well to open it to the rock) in Texas, Alberta and Wales, and sells them per well ($114.1M of product sales), plus a diagnostic service ($64.8M) that puts chemical tracers into fracturing fluid and reads which stages of a horizontal well are flowing. Services were 76% of consolidated revenue and products 24% in FY2025 (*"Services accounted for 76%, 74% and 73% of our revenue for the years ended December 31, 2025, 2024 and 2023, respectively."*). Total FY2025 revenue $526.5M; segment operating income $56.0M; net income attributable to the company $29.7M after $10.6M of interest.
- **The brief's Q1 prior checked against the segment note: right in outline, incomplete in three places.** It omits (1) the crude-oil and refined-products assay and inspection work inside Reservoir Description's fluids line (the FY2025 MD&A names *"laboratory assay services and laboratory instrument sales, which yield lower margins"*), (2) that Production Enhancement is mostly a product business (64% products) whose product is perforating hardware, and (3) the multi-client studies Core Lab owns and resells (*"Core Lab retains rights of ownership to complete joint industry project studies, which can be resold at a later date."*).
- The scarce input this business controls: **the global laboratory network and its staff, the accumulated measurement databases and multi-client studies (*"Core Lab’s proprietary World Wide Rock Catalog™ provides a database and analog reference set for predicting properties when physical measurements are unavailable."*), patented perforating-charge designs and tracer chemistry, and relationships with national and international oil companies across fifty countries.** Whether any of this is scarce in [E3-03]'s sense is Q2's question, not Q1's.
- Will the fundamentals look broadly the same in ten years? **The way the money is made, yes; the volume, no one can say from the filings.** The company was established in 1936, and the FY2007 10-K describes the same measurement and completion businesses in three segments. What changes is demand: Reservoir Description follows international and offshore field development and Production Enhancement follows North American well completions, and the filer names a technology risk in its own words (*"As genAI technologies continue to improve, we may not be able to replicate or compete with potential applications relevant to our business, including, for example, enhanced computer modeling of reservoir rock and fluid properties, which could displace some of our current products or services."*). That bears on the moat (Q2) and on survival (Q4); it does not change the unit economics, which are a fee per measurement on a fixed-cost laboratory network and a margin per perforating system sold.
- **Simple and stable in character [E3-31]; a decision that can be made on the filings [E4-46].** Testing samples for a fee and making an explosive consumable require no technology forecast to understand. The science inside a test is specialist, but the business question (how many samples arrive and what each one pays) is not.
