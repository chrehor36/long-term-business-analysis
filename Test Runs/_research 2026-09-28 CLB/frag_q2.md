### THE STRONGEST EVIDENCE AGAINST THIS VERDICT, stated as its holders would state it [E4-51, E4-26, E3-47]
The verdict below is OUT, so the case for IN is set out first, at full strength.
1. *"For six years this business earned more than its whole capital every year, pre-tax: 123% to 162% on net tangible capital from 2009 to 2014, operating margins of 27% to 32% at a time when Schlumberger earned 21% and Halliburton 11% to 19%. That is [E3-43]'s 'high rates of return on capital' at a level almost nothing in the oilfield reaches, and it was earned on a laboratory network, proprietary databases and patented charges, which is a scarce-input business, not a rented rig."* **Answer:** accepted as the fact of 2009-2014, and it is the best fact in the file. It is also the record that has not been repeated. The same network, databases and patents earned 17.5% to 23.5% in FY2021-FY2025, a fifth to a sixth of the earlier level, on a capital base that did not grow (net tangible capital $140-269M in every year). Operating income fell from $346.5M (FY2014) to $56.5M (FY2025) on revenue down 51%. [E3-43] asks for the ability to price *"regularly"*; the record shows it in one cycle's up-leg and not in the eleven years since, which include the 2022-2023 years of high oil prices. And in no filing read, boom or bust, does the filer attribute a margin gain to price: its explanations are demand for proprietary diagnostics (2014), activity levels, fixed-cost absorption, cost restoration and mix.
2. *"2009 was a real test and Core Lab passed it: revenue fell 11% and the operating margin rose from 26.5% to 26.9%; Reservoir Description's margin rose from 23.4% to 25.6% on revenue down 5%. A business without pricing power loses margin when volume falls."* **Answer:** accepted as the 2009 record. Two cautions: the downturn was short (revenue was above 2008 by 2010), and Production Enhancement's margin fell that year with the filer naming price in its own words (*"Additionally, reduced demand in North America has decreased margins due to pressure on pricing"*, FY2009 10-K). The longer downturn that followed is the better test, and there Reservoir Description's margin fell in every year from 27.7% (FY2014) to 13.1% (FY2019), the filer giving *"lower activity levels"* as the cause, and it has not been back above 15.5% since. A margin that falls with volume for five straight years and never recovers is the fixed-cost laboratory meeting a customer who will not pay more per test to cover the idle capacity.
3. *"The returns today are still respectable: 20% to 23% pre-tax on tangible capital. A commodity business in [E2-58]'s sense earns 'poor profitability'; this one does not."* **Answer:** accepted that 20% is not ruin, and it is carried into the reversal condition. But [E2-58]'s test is relative and over the cycle: a laboratory business that earned 130% and now earns 21% has moved from the exception to the middle of its peers, and the row below shows where the middle is. Core Lab's FY2021-FY2025 operating margin (mean 10.1%) is below Schlumberger's (17.4%, three years filed), Halliburton's (13.9%) and Weatherford's (12.2%), and above only the smaller completion-tool filers.
4. *"The laboratory side is not the oilfield cycle. It is 80% international, it follows long-cycle offshore development, and the 2024 MD&A credits 'continued improvement in utilization of our global laboratory network'. The recovery is under way: Reservoir Description's margin went 7.4% → 12.3% → 14.9% from 2022 to 2024."* **Answer:** accepted as the direction of 2022-2024. It reversed at once: 12.6% in FY2025, which the filer explains by mix, not price (*"The changes in operating income and operating margins were primarily attributable to a different mix of revenue with a lower level of revenue from reservoir rock and reservoir fluid analysis in 2025, which yields a higher margin, and higher level of laboratory assay services and laboratory instrument sales, which yield lower margins."*), and 4.7% in the June 2026 quarter, with the Middle East conflict named. A 14.9% peak is about half the margin of 2008-2014.
5. *"The measurements are hard to replicate: a World Wide Rock Catalog, decades of proprietary studies, reservoir-condition flow systems Core Lab builds itself. A customer does not easily switch the lab that holds its field's history."* **Answer:** accepted that switching is slow. The filer's own description of who takes the work is the rebuttal: *"we face competition in these lines, primarily from independent regional companies and internal divisions of major integrated oil and gas companies. We compete in different service and product lines to various degrees on the basis of price, technical performance, availability, quality and technical support."* The largest customers can do the work themselves, and price is the first basis named. The filer adds a technology risk to exactly this asset (*"enhanced computer modeling of reservoir rock and fluid properties, which could displace some of our current products or services"*, FY2025 10-K).

None of the five shows Core Lab holding a price through a period of spare capacity since 2014. The first and third are the strongest facts against this verdict and are carried into the reversal condition.

### THE READING
- **The filer has said the same thing about its prices in all nineteen 10-Ks read, FY2007 to FY2025:** *"The oil and gas industry has historically experienced periodic downturns, which have been characterized by diminished demand for our oilfield services and products and downward pressure on the prices we charge."* And in the competition section of every one of them, price is named first among the bases of competition; from FY2009 the competitors are *"independent regional companies and internal divisions of major integrated oil and gas companies"* (FY2007-FY2008: *"primarily from independent regional companies"*). The filer does not describe its services as without substitute; it describes them as priced against regional laboratories and the majors' own.
- **[E2-44] (1), tested in the year it bites: failed in 2022.** The first characteristic is the ability to raise prices *"even when product demand is flat and capacity is not fully utilized"*. In FY2022 Reservoir Description revenue was flat (−1.9%), costs rose, and the margin fell from 9.2% to 7.4%, the filer's reason being cost, not price: *"the decrease in operating margins reflect the increase in costs during 2022, associated with the Company restoring employee compensation costs and benefits."* Production Enhancement the same year: *"operating costs in 2022 increased due to the restoration of employee compensation and benefit costs and the inflationary impact on our raw materials and shipping costs."* Costs rose in an inflation year and the prices did not follow them up; that is the reverse of [E2-44]'s test, in the year it was set.

- **[E3-62], the second step.** The laboratory technology the filer describes (computed tomography, nuclear magnetic resonance, digital rock typing) is capital that customers value; the record since 2014 shows the gains going to the customer, not staying home.
