## Q2 — IS IT A FRANCHISE? **[E3-03]**
*The analyst's incentives, stated before the reading (operator rule 9, [E4-27], [E4-26], [E3-41]): the last three years of this filer look like a franchise (gross margin about 30%, operating margin about 20%, customers paying in advance, a record backlog), and the screen row's own flags already call the window a boom. Two easy priors pull in opposite directions: "the numbers say franchise" and "the row says cycle". Neither is evidence. **So the case for IN is written first and at full strength** [E4-51], from Powell's own filings, and the reading must answer each item on the filings.*

### THE STRONGEST EVIDENCE FOR IN (the case against this file's verdict), stated as its holders would state it [E4-51, E4-26]
1. **The margins are a franchise's margins now, and they have held for three years.** Gross margin **27.0% (FY2024), 29.4% (FY2025), 29.6% (nine months of FY2026) and 30.6% (the June 2026 quarter)**; operating margin **17.7% (FY2024), 19.7% (FY2025), 19.1% (nine months of FY2026: 164,428 on 859,539)**. Pre-tax return on tangible operating capital (below) **54% and 63%** in FY2024-FY2025, before counting the customers' advances, which make the capital employed smaller still.
2. **Customers pay ahead of the work.** Contract liabilities of **$466.4M at 2026-06-30** against revenue of about $1.15bn a year: *"Contract liabilities typically represent advance payments from contractual billing milestones and billings in excess of revenue recognized"*. A supplier whose customers fund its working capital has some bargaining power; [E3-52] calls customer-prepaid liabilities *"a different animal"* from bank debt.
3. **Demand is broad and the order book is at a record.** *"Backlog (3) as of June 30, 2026 totaled $2.4 billion, an increase of 69%"*; *"New orders (2) totaled $934 million, an increase of 158%"*, *"a book-to-bill ratio of 3.0x"*, including *"the previously announced data center order with a value exceeding $400 million"* (EX-99.1, 2026-08-03). The CFO's outlook: *"We expect that gross margins will maintain levels consistent to the trailing twelve months"*. The end markets named (LNG, utilities, grid, data centres) are not one customer's wave.
4. **The filer claims a lasting advantage and has survived every cycle since 1947.** *"We believe that our products and services, integration capabilities, technical and project management acumen, application engineering expertise and specialty contracting experience, together with our financial strength and responsiveness to the needs of our customers, give us a sustainable competitive advantage in our markets"* (FY2021-FY2025; similar words since FY2015); *"a significant portion of our business is from repeat customers and many times involves third-party EPC firms hired by the end user and with whom we often have long, established relationships"*. It never borrowed to survive the FY2016-FY2022 trough (long-term debt ran off to zero by FY2022).
5. **The asset class is valued by the leader.** Eaton paid *"$ 1.43 billion, net of cash acquired"* in April 2025 for Fibrebond, *"a U.S. based designer and builder of pre-integrated modular power enclosures for data center, industrial, utility and communications customers"* with *"sales of approximately $ 378 million"* (Eaton 10-K 2025, `0001551182-26-000007`): about 3.8 times sales for the business Powell is in.

### THE CASE AGAINST (the reading)
1. **Criterion (2) fails in the registrant's own words, every year the sentence has been filed.** *"The majority of our business is in support of capital investment projects that are highly complex and competitively bid"*; *"Our products and services are typically awarded in competitive bid situations"*; *"By their nature, these projects are typically non-recurring"*; *"The competitive factors used during bid evaluation by our customers vary from project to project and may include technical support and application expertise, engineering and manufacturing capabilities, equipment rating, delivered value, scheduling and price"* (the same sentence in every 10-K FY2009-FY2025, `grep` over `cache/k2009`-`k2025`); *"Ultimately, our competitive position is dependent upon our ability to provide quality custom-engineered products, services and systems on a timely basis at a competitive price"*. The named rivals are the four largest electrical-equipment makers in the world: *"Our principal competitors include ABB, Eaton Corporation, GE, Schneider Electric and Siemens"* (FY2009-FY2011; GE named through FY2017 and dropped from FY2018; *"ABB, Eaton, Schneider, and Siemens Industries, Inc."* FY2024-FY2025), *"Some of our competitors are significantly larger and have substantially greater global resources"*, and *"Certain of our competitors may have lower cost structures and may, therefore, be able to provide their products or services at lower prices than we are able to provide"* (FY2013-FY2018; the clause *"may, therefore, be able to provide their products or services at lower prices"* is in every 10-K FY2007-FY2025, checked by `q2check.py`; FY2025 adds *"or a more favorable geographic footprint"*). A product the customer puts out to bid, one project at a time, against four larger makers who may bid lower, is a product with close substitutes on the filer's own statement.
2. **The price follows the cycle; no filed year credits a price increase to margin.** In the trough: *"The reduction in available projects, across the markets we serve, will increase market price pressures during this downward cycle"* (FY2015); *"has increased market price pressures during this downward market cycle"* (FY2016); *"a decline in our project backlog due to depressed market conditions and competitive pricing pressures, primarily in our core oil, gas and petrochemical markets"* (FY2017, FY2018). In inflation, the customer did not pay: *"increased costs impacting older projects in our backlog that were subject to inflationary pressures on raw materials and components, with little or no recourse from the customer to reprice the project"* (FY2022). In the upturn, the filer credits volume and execution, and describes price as following cost: *"higher volume levels across the manufacturing facilities driving key factory efficiencies, strong project execution as well as, effectively managing product pricing that corresponds to current cost levels"* (FY2023; FY2024 the same with *"favorable volume leverage"*); *"favorable volume leverage and strong project execution in a stable pricing environment throughout Fiscal 2025"* (FY2025); *"a stable pricing environment"* (10-Q, June 2026); *"higher volume levels and a continued strong and stable pricing environment"* (EX-99.1). **[E2-44]'s first characteristic** (*"an ability to increase prices rather easily (even when product demand is flat and capacity is not fully utilized) without fear of significant loss of either market share or unit volume"*) **is contradicted in the trough years, when capacity was not fully utilised and the price fell, and is not shown in any year.**
3. **Twenty-five years of filings show a cyclical business, and the last two years are its peak.** Gross margin (Item 6 tables and the statements of operations): **20.9% (FY2001), 22.0%, 19.3%, 17.5%, 16.8% (FY2005), 18.4%, 16.9%, 19.8%, 21.8% (FY2009), 25.8% (FY2010), 17.8%, 19.2%, 21.6%, 19.4%, 16.4% (FY2015), 18.8%, 12.8% (FY2017), 14.6%, 16.8%, 18.2% (FY2020), 16.0%, 16.0% (FY2022), 21.1% (FY2023), 27.0%, 29.4% (FY2025)**. Operating margin FY2008-FY2025: **6.6%, 9.3%, 8.3%, 0.5%, 6.8%, 7.1% (FY2013), 4.5%, 3.1%, 2.8%, −4.9% (FY2017), −2.0%, 2.2%, 3.7%, 0.2% (FY2021), 1.4%, 8.9% (FY2023), 17.7%, 19.7%** (`peers_out.txt`, the FY2011, FY2016 and FY2021 five-year tables); pre-tax margin FY2005-FY2007 1.1%, 3.5%, 2.7%; net margin FY2001-FY2004 5.0%, 5.8%, 2.8%, 0.8%. **Twenty-three of the twenty-five filed years sit between −4.9% and 9.3% operating (or the pre-tax and net equivalents; FY2006 is an eleven-month transition period, 10-KT); the two above that range are the last two, FY2024 and FY2025, and nine months of FY2026 (19.1%) continue them.** Revenue and backlog move together with the customers' capital spending: backlog $507.1M (FY2014), $441.4M, $291.4M, **$250.1M (FY2017)**, $260.9M, $419.0M, $476.8M, $414.9M, $592.2M (FY2022), **$1.3bn (FY2023, FY2024), $1.4bn (FY2025), $2.4bn (June 2026)**. The filer's first risk factor says what this is: *"The end markets that we serve have historically been cyclical and will continue to be vulnerable to general downturns"*.
4. **[E3-43] names the class exactly.** *"In contrast, “a business” earns exceptional profits only if it is the low-cost operator or if supply of its product or service is tight. Tightness in supply usually does not last long."* Powell is not the low-cost operator on its own statement (item 1). Supply is tight: the filer is adding capacity as fast as it can (*"a $12.4 million investment to expand production capacity at our Jacintoport manufacturing facility"*, *"a 62% increase from the current yard capacity"*; the release: *"evaluating greenfield capacity expansions incremental to our added leased capacity in Houston and Ohio"*), and so are the rivals (Eaton's Fibrebond purchase). **[E2-58]** describes what follows: *"the rebound to prosperity frequently produces a pervasive enthusiasm for expansion that, within a few years, again creates over-capacity and a new profitless environment. In other words, nothing fails like success. What finally determines levels of long-term profitability in such industries is the ratio of supply-tight to supply-ample years."* Twenty-five filed years give that ratio for this company, so far: two full years and a third in progress above the range, twenty-three inside it.
5. **The competitor row puts Powell below the rival it names first, at every point of the cycle.** Eaton's electrical systems segment earned **13.6%, 14.9%, 16.3%** of sales in 2017-2019 (Electrical Systems and Services: *"770"* on *"5,666"*, *"896"* on *"6,024"*, *"1,027"* on *"6,287"*) when Powell earned **−4.9%, −2.0%, 2.2%**; Eaton's Electrical Americas earned **26.5%, 30.2%, 29.9%** in 2023-2025 (*"2,675"* on *"10,098"*, *"3,455"* on *"11,436"*, *"3,972"* on *"13,276"*) when Powell earned **8.9%, 17.7%, 19.7%**. Powell's margin moves with the industry's and sits below the leader's by 10-20 points in the trough and 10-20 points at the peak. A moat is a claim about relative position [E3-28]; this row shows a follower.
6. **The attacker's test [E2-45] is being run by the leader, in cash.** Eaton bought a builder of pre-integrated modular power enclosures in 2025 and reports it inside Electrical Americas. ABB, Siemens and Schneider are named every year. A rival with *"substantially greater global resources"* entering the E-house directly is the test answered in the rival's favour, not Powell's.
7. **The customer advances are the industry's terms, not a toll.** Milestone billing ahead of the work runs through the whole record (*"billings in excess of costs"* $31.0-48.7M in FY2010-FY2015 at the tags, contract liabilities $42.4-79.9M in FY2018-FY2022, before the boom), and every contract can be ended: *"Typically, our contracts may have an early termination for convenience clause at the discretion of our customers; however, most of these contracts typically provide for the reimbursement of our costs incurred"* and, across a page break in the filing, a reasonable margin. The advances rose from $79.9M (FY2022) to $466.4M with the backlog; they measure the order book's size, not a price the customer cannot refuse.
