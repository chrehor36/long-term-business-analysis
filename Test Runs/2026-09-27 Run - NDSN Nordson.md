## Q1 — CAN I UNDERSTAND HOW THIS MAKES MONEY? **[E3-31]**
- **Unit economics in my own words, no management language.** Nordson designs and builds the equipment that puts a precise amount of glue, sealant, coating, paint, powder or plastic melt onto somebody else's product on somebody else's production line (diaper lines, carton and case sealing, can coating, appliance and car assembly, circuit boards), and the tips, nozzles, syringes, cartridges, filters and spare parts those machines use up. Since about 2010 it has bought its way into two further trades: single-use plastic medical components and devices (catheter shafts, balloons, tubing, needle-free valves, fluid connectors; Atrion in 2024) and inspection and test machines for electronics and semiconductor packaging (x-ray, acoustic, optical, bond testers; CyberOptics, completed 2022-11-03, in fiscal 2023), plus agricultural spray nozzles and controls (ARAG, 2023). It sells mostly direct (*"primarily through a direct sales force and qualified distributors and sales representatives"*), from *"direct operations in more than 35 countries"*, to a broad base: *"In 2025, no single customer accounted for ten percent or more of our sales."* **The money is a gross margin of about 55% on machined, molded and assembled parts** (*"Our production operations include machining, molding and assembly"*; raw materials *"sheet metal, fabricated metal parts, bar stock, castings, forgings, tubing, plastic parts and pellets"*, *"available through multiple sources"*), less selling and administration of about 29% of sales, leaving an operating margin of **25.5%** in FY2025 (sales $2,791.7M, gross margin $1,539.8M or 55.2%, operating profit $711.7M, net income $484.5M; 10-K FY2025, MD&A). The equipment is bought out of customers' capital budgets (*"the highest volume of sales occurs in the second half of the fiscal year due in large part to the timing of customers’ capital spending programs"*); the parts and consumables follow the installed machines.
- **The scarce input this business controls.** The candidates, stated before Q2 tests them: (a) **application know-how and the installed base**: machines engineered into customers' lines, whose tips, nozzles and parts are Nordson's; (b) **the direct global sales-and-service network** the filer names as *"a competitive advantage"*; (c) **patents and trade secrets** (*"more than 2,100 granted and pending patents"*), which the filer itself discounts (*"we believe that our competitive advantage is also largely attributable to the technical, marketing, and sales competence and capabilities of our employees, rather than on any individual patent or trademark"*); (d) **qualified positions in medical devices**, where a component designed into a customer's regulated device is costly to requalify. Whether any of these lets Nordson set its price rather than accept it is Q2's question.
- **Will the fundamentals look broadly the same in ten years?** The core trade has not changed across the twenty-four 10-Ks read (FY2002-FY2025): the FY2004 10-K describes *"one of the world’s leading producers of precision dispensing equipment that apply adhesives, sealants and coatings to a broad range of consumer and industrial products during manufacturing operations"*, *"approximately two-thirds of the Company’s revenues are generated outside the United States"*, and the same *"pricing, product quality and service"* grounds of competition as FY2025. **[E3-03], criterion by criterion.** *"An economic franchise arises from a product or service that: (1) is needed or desired; (2) is thought by its customers to have no close substitute and; (3) is not subject to price regulation."*
- **(1) Needed or desired [x]: passes.** A production line that applies adhesive, coating or melt cannot run without the dispenser, and the tips, nozzles, filters and spares are consumed by use.
- **(3) Not subject to price regulation [x]: passes.** Medical components are regulated for safety (FDA device rules, named in Item 1A); price is not.
- **(2) Thought by its customers to have no close substitute: MET NARROWLY, at the level of the installed line, not at the level of the market.** Against: the filer's own words that pricing is the first of its competitive factors and that cost pass-through *"is restricted by the need for competitive pricing"*; alternative techniques exist for most applications. For: a gross margin of 52-61% held for twenty-six years with no customer above 10% of sales (none above 5% in FY2006-FY2008), which a buyer who treated the maker as substitutable at a price would not permit; price held through a 23.3% volume collapse (FY2009), when a price-taker cuts; and a segment return of 46-73% on all its assets for six years in the original business. **The substitute exists for the next line a customer builds; it does not, on this record, exist for the line already running.** That is a narrow franchise, and the class below is written that way.

**[E3-43]'s demonstration, *"a company’s ability to regularly price its product or service aggressively and thereby to earn high rates of return on capital"*, and its other door: *"In contrast, “a business” earns exceptional profits only if it is the low-cost operator or if supply of its product or service is tight."***
- **The return half: met, strongly, on the operation's own capital.** 65-171% pre-tax on tangible operating capital in every normal year since FY2004 ([E2-43], *"the best guide to the economic attractiveness of the operation"*); the original segment 46-73% goodwill included; [E3-46]'s *"very high returns on capital employed over time"*. On the owners' total capital including acquisition premia, 13-16% since FY2023: the purchases, not the operation.
- **The pricing half: met by LEVEL, not by INCREASE.** Nordson sells at about 2.2 times the cost of goods (gross margin 55.2% in FY2025) and has done so for twenty-six years; that is aggressive pricing in [E3-43]'s sense, sustained. It has **not** regularly raised prices: pricing was *"neutral"* or *"not material"* in every year FY2002-FY2019, and the filer says pass-through is restricted by competition. The margin was held by cost and mix, not by taking price.
- **The other door, tested.** Nordson is not the low-cost operator: it sells through *"a direct sales force"* and application engineers at selling and administrative cost of 29-41% of sales, the opposite of a low-cost model; and supply has not been tight for twenty-six years. **By the passage's own three-way division, twenty-six years of exceptional returns that come from neither low cost nor tight supply come from the product's position.** That is the strongest single argument in the file for IN, and it is the corpus's argument, not the analyst's.
