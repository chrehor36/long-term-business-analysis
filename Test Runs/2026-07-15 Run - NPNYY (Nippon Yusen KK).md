## GATE 1 — CIRCLE OF COMPETENCE
NYK Line is one of Japan's major global shipping companies — container
shipping, dry bulk, tankers (energy/LNG transport), car carriers, and
logistics. Shipping is a notoriously **cyclical, capital-intensive, largely
commodity business** with earnings that swing dramatically with freight
rates (container shipping rates in particular have been extraordinarily
volatile since 2020 — the pandemic-era spike and subsequent collapse are
well-documented industry-wide, not NYK-specific research). **VERDICT:
PASS-able but with a real honesty flag**: shipping economics are
understandable at a surface level (revenue = freight rate × volume, capital-
heavy fleet), but genuine conviction requires real judgment about global
trade cycles and fleet-supply dynamics that a general-knowledge pass does
not confer — closer to Buffett's "quantitative/statistical" mode [E1-15]
than a franchise read, the same caveat this project applied to Unit Corp's
E&P economics.

## GATE 2 — MOAT
Franchise test [E3-03]: needed/desired — yes generically (global trade
needs shipping); no close substitute — **fails cleanly**, shipping capacity
is a fungible commodity across carriers on most routes; unregulated —
largely yes. **Class: NONE-to-NARROW** — global shipping alliances (NYK
participates in "ONE," a joint container-shipping venture with two other
Japanese lines, K Line and MOL, formed specifically because none of the
three could compete alone at global scale) suggest the honest read is that
**scale/alliance membership, not company-specific moat, is what matters
here** — a real, disclosed structural fact (the ONE alliance itself),
not invented color.
**VERDICT: PASS with NONE/NARROW classification** — the harshest moat
classification of any Japan company run so far, appropriately mirroring
Unit Corp's commodity-business treatment in this same project.
