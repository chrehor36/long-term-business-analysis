## Q1 — CAN I UNDERSTAND IT? Where its economics will be in ten years, inside my perimeter. STOP.
**The business, from the filings.** A holding company (reorganised 2023; NW Corp became a subsidiary) over two regulated utilities: NW Corp (Montana electric and natural gas, and Yellowstone National Park) and NWE Public Service (South Dakota electric and gas, Nebraska gas). About 850,300 customers; Montana electric 440,700 customers across 73 percent of the state's land area; residential, commercial, industrial and other sales 46%, 46%, 5% and 3% of Montana retail electric revenue (10-K FY2025, Item 1). Two reportable segments, electric and natural gas, both regulated; "certain limited unregulated activity" sits in Other, net (10-K FY2025, Note 22). Rate base at year-end 2025 was estimated at $5,570M, of which Montana electric and Colstrip Unit 4 $3,682M, Montana gas $914M, South Dakota $919M, Nebraska $55M (10-K FY2025, Item 1, rate base table). Owned generation supplied about 67 percent of retail load in 2025 and is expected to supply about 95 percent in 2026 after the Colstrip interests acquired for $0 on 2026-01-01 (10-K FY2025, Electric Supply).

**The test as the draft states it.** Understanding is "a reasonable fix on about what the earning power and competitive position will look like in five or 10 years" **[M2012-065]**; "a reasonable probability of being able to asses where the business will be in 10 years" **[M2000-037]**. The key variables, "evaluating how predictable they were first" **[M1998-044]**: (1) the rate base and its growth, published in the filings each year ($3,412M authorized at end-2022, $5,142M authorized at end-2025; capex plan $683M, $643M and $667M for 2026 to 2028, 10-K FY2025 MD&A); (2) the return the regulators allow on it (Montana 9.65% electric and 9.60% gas on a 47.84% equity layer, South Dakota and Nebraska overall returns 6.81% to 7.09%, 10-K FY2025); (3) the gap between allowed and earned, which is the regulatory lag and the regulator's disposition; (4) the cost of the debt and equity that fund the growth. Variables (1), (2) and (4) are printed in the filings; variable (3) is observable over a decade (Q3) and is the one the speakers flag: "when you get an allowed return in the electric utility you’re almost certain of earning it, I mean, if you behave yourself." **[M2010-037]**, against "the regulatory climate in a few states has raised the specter of zero profitability or even bankruptcy" **[L2023-011]**. Montana's record in the filings is lag and disallowance, not zero profitability: the 2018 case asked $34.9M and settled at $6.5M (10-K FY2019); the 2022 case asked a 10.60% ROE and settled at 9.65% (10-Ks FY2022 and FY2023); the 2024 case asked 10.80% and a $156.5M electric base increase and received 9.65% and $105.5M with a $30.9M disallowance of Yellowstone County plant costs (10-Ks FY2024 and FY2025). That is a knowable, printed variable, so the forecast is of a utility earning somewhat below its allowance on a growing rate base, not of an unknowable thing: "If something’s important but unknowable, forget it." **[M2006-076]** does not apply, because this is important and knowable.

**Routing.** No fast change: an electric and gas distribution monopoly's ten-year economics do not turn on technology. The holding-company paragraph reads the whole by its parts; here both parts are regulated utilities with the same economics, and the electric part decides (Q2, parts table). **The merger changes the object of understanding, not the kind:** Black Hills is the same kind of business (regulated gas and electric utilities in eight states, its FY2025 10-K `0001193125-26-046028`), so understanding the combined company is the same test applied to a larger rate base; a doubt would arise only if Black Hills held a part that mattered and could not be read, and its filings show none (gas utilities in six states and electric utilities in three, authorized ROEs 9.30% to 9.85% in its own table). "if you have doubts about something being into your circle of competence, it isn’t." **[M2002-092]**: I have no doubt about what this business does or roughly what it will earn; the doubt is about whether what it earns is enough, which is Q3 and Q7.

**VERDICT: IN.** The economics ten years out are foreseeable from the filings: a regulated rate base growing with a published capital plan, an allowed return printed in each order, and an earned return that has sat below the allowance by a measurable margin for a decade **[M2012-065]**, **[M2010-037]**, **[M1998-044]**. ## Q2 — WHY IS THE CASTLE STILL STANDING, and what keeps it standing ten to twenty years? STOP.
**What keeps it standing.** The castle is a franchise granted by law: exclusive service territories in Montana, South Dakota and Nebraska, with rates set by three state commissions and FERC and "an obligation to provide service to our customers with an opportunity to earn a regulated rate of return" (10-K FY2025, MPSC Regulation). Each test, with its fact:
- *The attacker with money* **[M2011-015]**: no one may build a competing distribution system in the territory; the money test is passed by statute, not by economics. Munger's "why are there no new entrants into the field?" **[M2000-077]** is answered by the franchise.
- *Pricing power and the agony before a rise* **[M2005-020]**: the company has no pricing power of its own; every increase is a rate case before the commission, with interim rates subject to refund and settlements below the ask (2018: $6.5M of $34.9M; 2022: 9.65% of 10.60%; 2024: 9.65% of 10.80% and a disallowance). The prayer session is the business. This is the regulated case the rows carve out, not the weak-moat case: the moat's "ability to keep its width and its impossibility of being crossed as the primary criterion of a great business" **[M2000-018]** does not fit a franchise whose width is fixed by law, and the rows send the regulated return to Q3 as a second best **[M2009-104]**, **[L2009-012]**.
- *Unit volume and share of mind*: customers grew from about 734,800 in 2019 to about 850,300 in 2025 including the Energy West acquisition (10-Ks FY2019 and FY2025, Item 1); retail volumes rose in 2025 on weather and customer growth (10-K FY2025, MD&A). Share of mind is not the relevant idea; the customer has no choice.
- *The low-cost position* **[L2007-004]**: not applicable between utilities; the filing says typical residential bills are "significantly lower than the national average" (10-K FY2025, Item 1), which bears on the regulator's temper, not on competition.
- *Would the customer still choose it over the low bid*: no bid exists.
- *Ask the competitors*: none in the territory; the peers are rivals for capital, read below.
- *Widening or narrowing* **[M1999-108]**: the moat is the franchise and does not widen; what moves is the return the regulator lets through it, and that has narrowed in practice (Q3: earned ROE 10.0% in 2016 and 10.5% in 2018 to 6.3% in 2025, against an allowed 9.65% throughout).
- *What could destroy, modify or reduce it, five to fifteen years out* **[M2000-014]**: (a) the regulatory compact, the risk the 2023 letter names **[L2023-011]**: Montana's commission suspended the PCCAM 90/10 supply-cost sharing, cut the PCCAM base from $208.3M to $119.0M and disallowed $30.9M of plant costs in December 2025 (10-K FY2025); (b) wildfire liability, which Montana's House Bill 490 (April 2025) and South Dakota's Senate Bill 36 (March 2026) now limit by abolishing strict liability and creating a presumption of reasonableness for a utility following an approved mitigation plan, with the MPSC approving NWE's plan in November 2025 (10-K FY2025; 10-Q Q2 2026); (c) Colstrip, a coal plant of which NWE now owns 55 percent of Units 3 and 4 after taking Avista's and Puget's shares for $0, with the sellers keeping pre-closing environmental and decommissioning liabilities, and EPA GHG and MATS rules that "would require expensive upgrades" unless the 2025 proposals to rescind them are adopted (10-K FY2025, Note 20); (d) the merger's conditions, since the Montana commission's order is the last approval outstanding (10-Q Q2 2026, Note 2).

## Q3 — HOW MUCH CAPITAL MUST GO IN to get the earnings out, and what does the added capital earn? WEIGHING.
**What it has earned on the capital it needs, over time.** Net income on average shareholders' equity: 10.0% (2016), 9.4%, 10.5%, 10.2%, 7.5% (2020), 8.5%, 7.3%, 7.1%, 7.9%, 6.3% (2025); the 2021 to 2025 mean is 7.4% against an allowed 9.65% in Montana electric and 9.55% to 9.60% in Montana gas (XBRL facts of both registrants; rate base tables of the 10-Ks FY2022 to FY2025). The proxy's own measure says the same: three-year return on average equity 8.5% (2021), 7.4%, 7.7%, 7.5%, 7.4% (2025) (DEF 14A 2026, Pay Versus Performance). The segment note's test is the CODM's, "to evaluate if our operating segments are earning their authorized rate of return" (10-K FY2025, Note 22); by that test they have not, in any year since 2019. The rows read a utility's return as the regulator's gift, "within a range, anyway — a guaranteed return" **[M2009-104]**, and here the range has sat two to three points below the allowance for six years. Nothing flatters the figure: there is no leverage above the allowed 52 percent debt layer (debt to total capital 55.4% at 2026-06-30 against a 50 to 55 percent target, 10-Q Q2 2026 and 10-K FY2025), no cyclical peak and no buyback **[L1994-009]**.

**How much must be reinvested to stand still, and to grow.** The filer gives the maintenance guess the rows ask for, "my best guess" **[M2000-144]**. Its capital maintenance additions were $314.1M (2021), $295.4M, $321.9M, $324.0M and $372.7M (2025) against depreciation and depletion of $187.5M, $195.0M, $210.5M, $227.6M and $249.5M (10-Ks FY2022 to FY2025, Investing Activities and the cash-flow statements). The filer's own maintenance is 1.5 times its depreciation charge in 2025, the BNSF condition: "the depreciation charge we record in our railroad business falls far short of the capital outlays needed to merely keep the railroad running" **[L2015-004]**. Growth spending ("capacity related capital expenditures") was $120.2M, $219.7M, $245.0M, $225.3M and $151.8M, plus the $35.9M Energy West purchase in 2025. Total capital spending over 2021 to 2025 was $2,590M against operating cash flow of $1,818M and depreciation of $1,070M; the plan is $683M, $643M and $667M for 2026 to 2028 with equity issuance "expected beginning in 2027" (10-K FY2025, MD&A). "To earn more, their owners must invest more" is the 2011 letter's description of utilities, and the row reads: "Ideally, these assets should have the ability in inflationary times to deliver output that will retain its purchasing-power value while requiring a minimum of new capital investment." **[L2011-019]**; this business is the opposite case, and the reinvestment is compulsory: a utility must serve load whether or not the return is attractive, "you have to spend money like crazy if it’s attractive to spend money, and you have to spend it the same way if it’s unattractive" **[M1998-128]**.

**What the added capital has earned.** The rows measure it "by the operating earnings after depreciation" **[M2014-086]**. Operating income was $236.2M in 2020 and $325.8M in 2025, up $89.6M, while net property, plant and equipment grew from $4,953M to $6,739M, up $1,786M, and cumulative capital spending was $2,590M: the added capital earned about 5.0 percent pre-tax after depreciation on the net addition, and 3.5 percent on the gross spending (XBRL facts; 10-K FY2025 balance sheet). The retained-earnings test fails outright: diluted earnings per share were $3.06 in 2020 and $2.94 in 2025 on equity per share that rose from about $41 to about $47, so "normalized earning power" per share fell while capital per share rose, which the rows call failure: "if it doesn’t, you know, one way or another, we’re failing you because we’re retaining those earnings" **[M2018-045]**. The 2023 meeting's warning fits exactly: "we’re not earning a higher rate of return on capital than we were when we started. We just put way more capital into the business" **[M2023-081]**, except that here the rate of return fell.

**The growth arithmetic and its caps.** Sales grew 4.1 percent a year 2021 to 2025 ($1,372M to $1,611M); rate base grew faster (authorized $3,412M at end-2022 to $5,142M at end-2025); owner cash did not grow at all (Q7). The three savings accounts: this is the account that "both pays an inadequate interest rate and requires you to keep adding money at those disappointing returns" **[L2007-010]**, softened only by the regulator's floor under the rate. The second-best reading the rows give utilities, "they’re good enough" **[M2018-056]** and "as the second-best choice, still a good choice" **[M2018-055]**, assumed the allowed return is earned; "there’s no way we get poor, either" **[M2009-104]** still holds, since the regulator's floor is a floor on earnings, not on cash to the owner.

**WEIGHS AGAINST.** A capital-heavy second best whose added capital has earned about 5 percent pre-tax after depreciation and whose earned return on equity has fallen from 10 percent to 6.3 percent while the allowance stayed at 9.65 percent **[M2014-086]**, **[M2023-081]**, **[L2007-010]**; the "little or no debt" criterion is applied at Q9 (recorded under AFTER THE STOP).

## WHAT IN THE FRAMEWORK WAS WRONG OR UNCLEAR
Five things. **(1) Whose tax, for a regulated utility.** The convention charges tax at the statutory rate where a carryforward runs out inside ten years and adds the shelter's present value. A utility's low book tax is mostly regulatory flow-through that already belongs to its customers through rates, so the statutory rate charges the owner for a cost the customer bears; the NOL is the only owner-level shelter. This run kept the as-filed cash central and stated the statutory and the filer's-own-rate variants beside it, and confesses the departure; the convention should say which rate a utility's post-shelter cash is computed at (the filer's guided effective rate is the honest candidate). **(2) The merger STOP is written for the acquirer only.** **[L2009-019]** closes the file on a buyer that pays in undervalued stock; it says nothing of the seller that takes stock at the market price, which is this case. The run read the row's mirror and found no harm because Q7 found the market price above value, but the text should say how a seller in a signed all-stock deal is read at Q6. **(3) Specific (b) and the utility paragraph collide.** Where the owner-cash series changes sign, specific (b) makes the range the no-growth case alone, and then the growth-spending and debt-credit machinery written on 2026-10-23 for exactly this kind of business never enters the range. The run showed it as an illustration beside; the text should say whether, for a utility, the growth case is built on the sales or rate-base growth when the owner-cash growth is meaningless, or whether the no-growth case alone is the intended result. **(4) What "maintenance" means for a utility.** The filer's "capital maintenance additions" ($372.7M) exceed depreciation ($249.5M) by half; much of that spending replaces old plant at today's cost, enters rate base and earns the allowed return, so it is partly the growth of the regulated asset base and partly standing still. The framework charges the filer's guess in full to the equity, which may be right ("the maintenance guess is charged in full to the equity") but produces a central owner cash of $32M for a company with $2.9B of book equity earning 6 to 8 percent; the depreciation variant ($144M) is nearer to what the regulator treats as the owner's earnings. The run followed the text and showed both; the text should say whether a regulated filer's maintenance figure is the guess **[M2000-144]** means when the regulator pays a return on it. **(5) The reporting figures for a company under a signed all-stock merger.** The fair price is defined on the standalone central figure; after closing, no one will ever be offered that figure, only Black Hills shares. The run reported the standalone figures as required and the merger beside; the brief could say that for a signed deal the alert is set on the acquirer's stock.
