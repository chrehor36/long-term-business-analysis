## Q1 — CAN I UNDERSTAND IT? Where its economics will be in ten years, inside my perimeter. STOP.
- **The test.** Understanding is "a reasonable fix on about what the earning power and competitive position will look like in five or 10 years" **[M2012-065]**; "the first question is, can I understand it?" **[M1995-051]**. Each of these is reported every year and moves slowly; the contracts run 12 to 20 years. They are "important and knowable" **[M2006-076]**, and the insiders do write the numbers down (pipeline, signings, rooms growth), which is the opposite of the case in which they "would not want to put down on paper their predictions" **[M2000-105]**.
- **By parts** (Q1's holding-company paragraph, extended to segments: a part matters when it carries more than half of five-year operating profit, or else is the largest). The fee business decides.

That bears on the castle, not on whether the economics can be foreseen. And doubt means outside **[M2002-092]**: the doubt here is about Hyatt's position, which Q2 answers, not about how the business earns.

**The castle tests, on the deciding part.**
- *What keeps it standing, and how permanent* **[M1995-038]**. Owners sign for the brand, the reservation and loyalty system and the revenue it brings; guests come for the brand and the points: 63 million members, member stays about 49% of system room nights in 2025 and 47% in 2024 on the same method (10-K Item 1). The contracts make the stream durable: 16 years remaining for managed full-service hotels, 13 for select-service, 12 for all-inclusive, about 14 for franchises (Item 1). It does not hang on one manager: the fee system runs on brands, and "The really great business is one that doesn’t require good management" **[M1996-037]**.
- *The attacker with money* **[M2011-015]**. The attackers already exist and have more money: Marriott and Hilton are four to five times Hyatt's size in rooms and fees and bid for the same owners. Hyatt pays about twice their key money per fee dollar (table), and its contra revenue, the amortization of what it paid owners, rose from $22M in 2019 (3.6% of gross fees) to $86M in 2025 (7.2%). That is the evidence that a rival with money can contest Hyatt's contracts, and "one competitor is frequently enough to ruin a business" **[M2012-108]**. Against it: over the same span Hyatt's rooms and fees grew faster than both leaders', and its 2025 organic rooms growth (6.7%) matched Hilton's and beat Marriott's (4.3%, including an acquisition).
- *Pricing power* **[M2005-020]**. The royalty rates are stable ("typically ranging from 2.75% to 5%"); the agony in this field shows instead in what is paid to the owner to sign, and that has risen.
- *The brand against the intermediaries* **[M2001-090]**, **[M2019-041]**. The 10-K's own risk is that bookings move to intermediaries "rather than to our brands" (Item 1A). The loyalty share of room nights rising from 47% to 49% is evidence that the brand is holding the guest against them, not losing him.
- *Widening or narrowing* **[M1999-108]**, **[L2005-010]**. Widening in reach: rooms up 8.9% a year 2019-2025, pipeline up 7% in 2025 to about 148,000 rooms, two-fifths of the system. Narrowing in price: key money and contra revenue up, and owner cash converted from fees at 7% against the leaders' 44% and 51% in 2025 (part of that gap is Hyatt's owned hotels, distribution arm, deal costs and taxes on sales, weighed at Q3 and Q4, but the gap was there in 2019 too).
- *What could destroy, modify or reduce it* **[M2000-014]**. AI platforms and intermediaries taking the guest relationship, and short-term rentals: Airbnb's revenue grew 16.9% a year 2019-2025. Through that whole span the brands' fees still grew 6% to 12% a year, which is the evidence that the substitute has not yet filled the moat.
- *The field's returns over the cycle, in words.* Excellent, not ordinary: the two leaders turned $5.4B and $3.5B of fees into $2.4B and $1.8B of owner cash in 2025 on capex of $0.6B and $0.2B, and did the same on a smaller base in 2019, through the 2020 trough. The rule against the standing castle that protects only ordinary returns does not reach the field. Hyatt's own conversion is far lower; that is weighed at Q3 and Q7.
- *Rebuilt or maintained.* "A moat that must be continuously rebuilt will eventually be no moat at all" **[L2007-005]**; rising key money is the sign of rebuilding, and "There’s been more moats that have been - become susceptible to invasion" **[M2018-042]**. On the evidence the rebuilding costs more each year but is not failing: the system is growing, not shrinking.
- **VERDICT: IN**, narrowly. The deciding part's castle stands: brands, a loyalty system and long contracts in a field whose durable players earn excellent returns **[L2007-004]**, **[M1995-038]**. Carried against it to Q3 and Q7: Hyatt is the smallest of the three systems and pays the most per fee dollar to hold its place **[M2012-108]**, **[L2007-005]**; the distribution part is open and the owned part ordinary, both carried at no growth.
