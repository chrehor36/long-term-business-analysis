## STEP 0 — RATE REFRESH
US 30-yr **5.10%**. Mkt cap $20.17B — no size premium.

## GATE 1 — CIRCLE OF COMPETENCE
One of the largest US homebuilders by deliveries/revenue ($32B homebuilding
revenue, ~94% of FY2025 consolidated revenue), plus mortgage origination,
title insurance, and multifamily/single-family rental fund sponsorship.
Homebuilding economics are simple (buy/develop land, build, sell) though the
financial-services/fund-sponsorship tail is a second thing to understand.
**VERDICT: PASS**, with the homebuilding core treated as the dominant
business.

## GATE 2 — MOAT
Franchise test [E3-03], from Lennar's own 10-K Competition section:
**"The residential homebuilding industry is highly competitive. In each of
the market regions where we operate, we compete for homebuyers with
numerous national, regional and local homebuilders, as well as with resales
of existing homes and with the rental housing market. We compete for
homebuyers on the basis of a number of interrelated factors including
location, price, reputation for customer satisfaction, amenities, design,
quality and financing."**
- **Needed/desired**: yes, generically (housing is needed), not Lennar's
  product specifically.
- **No close substitute**: **FAILS**. Lennar's own disclosure names
  competition from other builders AND resale homes AND rentals — three
  substitute categories for the same underlying need, not a narrow
  competitive set.
- **Unregulated**: no — land-use, zoning, and environmental regulation are
  material, ordinary-course constraints (not quoted here, but standard for
  the industry and not disputed by anything in this filing).
- **Proven by pricing power + returns on capital**: **FAILS**. Unlike
  D.R. Horton's filing (run separately), Lennar's own Competition section
  does **not** claim a scale-based cost advantage — it lists the same
  homebuyer-facing factors (location, price, amenities, design, quality,
  financing) that every builder competes on. Net income fell from $4,614M
  (FY2022) to $2,078M (FY2025), a 55% decline over three years, tracking the
  broader housing-affordability downturn — commodity-cyclical behavior, not
  a franchise holding up its margins.

**Class: NONE.** **VERDICT: FAIL — STOP.**
