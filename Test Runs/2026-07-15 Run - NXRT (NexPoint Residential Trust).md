# Company Run — NexPoint Residential Trust (NXRT) — 2026-07-15
Framework v3.0. From the REIT mechanical screen. OE from real FY2025 10-K
data (Archives/edgar/data/1620393). Smallest, most leveraged REIT run so far.

## STEP 0 — RATE REFRESH
US 30-yr **5.10%**. Mkt cap $691.571M — **+1% small/mid-cap premium
applies** → hurdle **6.10%**.

## GATE 1 — CIRCLE OF COMPETENCE
NXRT is explicitly a **"value-add" strategy REIT** — it acquires
multifamily properties below replacement cost specifically because prior
owners under-invested, then spends capital deliberately to raise rents
("value-add program"), and recycles capital through asset sales. This is a
**genuinely different business model** from every other REIT in this batch
(which are largely buy-and-hold-and-maintain operators): capex here is
partly a **growth lever by design**, not just upkeep. **VERDICT: PASS**,
with this structural difference flagged as directly relevant to Gate 4
below, not a footnote.

## GATE 2 — MOAT
Franchise test [E3-03]: needed/desired — yes; no close substitute — **weak**
(same commodity dynamic, Southeastern/Southwestern Sun Belt overlap with
MAA/CPT's supply concerns); unregulated — largely yes. **Class: NARROW-to-
weak**, and the value-add model itself is a source of execution risk
(each acquisition must actually deliver the underwritten rent lift) rather
than a moat in the traditional sense.
**VERDICT: PASS with NARROW-to-weak classification**, execution risk of the
value-add model flagged as distinct from a moat question.

## GATE 3 — MANAGEMENT
CEO not independently confirmed this pass. NexPoint Residential Trust is
externally advised by an affiliate of NexPoint Advisors (part of the
Highland Capital/NexPoint investment-management complex) — **an external-
management structure, unlike every other REIT run so far in this batch**,
which are all internally managed. External management is a real, disclosed
structural difference worth naming (potential for fee-driven incentive
misalignment is a classic REIT governance concern, not confirmed as an
actual problem here, but not independently checked either).
**VERDICT: PASS (provisional)**, external-management structure flagged as
the single biggest Gate 3 difference from the rest of this batch.

## GATE 4 — FINANCIAL QUALITY (OE formula — capex/maintenance split
genuinely ambiguous for this business model)
Net income (loss) attributable to common stockholders: **$(32.027)M (2025)**,
$1.110M (2024) — 2025 was an outright loss year, driven substantially by
lower gains on real estate sales versus 2024. Depreciation and amortization:
$95.752M (2025), $97.762M (2024).

**A clean maintenance-vs-value-add capex split was not found in this pass**
(unlike AVB/EQR/ESS/AMH's clear disclosures) — and given NXRT's own stated
strategy, that split may not even be conceptually clean here, since
"capital expenditures" are explicitly part of the return-generation thesis,
not purely upkeep. **This run does not force a false-precision number.**
Upper bound (no capex subtracted, i.e., treating ALL of D&A as if it were
non-cash and unnecessary — the most generous possible reading):
OE(2025) = -32.027 + 95.752 = **$63.725M** (upper bound only).
Any real maintenance capex subtraction — even a conservative one — pushes
this toward the $35–45M range, **materially closer to the hurdle or below
it.**
**LOWEST VERIFIED TIER: uncertain, bounded above by $63.725M (FY2025), true
figure likely lower.**
Fortress (financial/leverage-ceiling track): Total assets $1,886.419M ÷
Total equity $295.496M = **6.384:1** — **by far the highest leverage of any
REIT in this batch** (next-highest was UDR at 3.23:1), and equity nearly
halved year-over-year ($410.4M → $295.5M) due to accumulated losses. Still
technically under the 10:1 ceiling, but this is a genuine, material
deterioration, not a stable structural position like the other REITs.
**VERDICT: PASS (mechanically, on the ceiling test)**, but flagged as the
weakest Fortress position of any REIT in this batch by a wide margin —
closer to the ceiling than comfortable, with a visibly eroding equity base.

## GATE 5 — INVERSION
1. **Moat/execution** — value-add thesis depends on continued execution;
   the FY2025 results (loss year, reduced gain-on-sale) are early evidence
   the recycling/value-add engine is not currently firing as designed.
   Scored **[DS]** — this is not hypothetical, it is the current result.
2. **Management/key-person** — external management structure, not
   independently assessed for fee/incentive alignment this pass. Scored
   **[U]**.
3. **Balance-sheet stress** — 6.38:1 assets/equity and a rapidly eroding
   equity base is the most concerning balance-sheet picture in this batch,
   even though it stays under the mechanical 10:1 ceiling. Scored **[P]**,
   not [D] — this is real, live deterioration, not a comfortable structural
   position.
4. **Capex/data-quality risk** — the maintenance-vs-growth split could not
   be cleanly established (see Gate 4). Scored **[U]**.

**Two [U]s present** (management/external-advisor alignment, and capex data
quality) **plus one [P]** (balance sheet). **Written justification to
proceed**: the combination of an eroding equity base, an unclear OE figure,
and a loss-year anchor already points toward a clear WAIT/caution verdict
regardless of how the [U]s resolve — but unlike IRT/CSR's situational
justification, **this company's risk picture is genuinely worse across
multiple independent dimensions simultaneously**, not just one weak metric.
**VERDICT: PASS (nominally), but this is the weakest, least-resolved Gate
1–5 sequence of any REIT in this batch** — a real candidate for exclusion
on qualitative grounds even before reaching Gate 6, similar in spirit to
the GAMCO override earlier in this project (accumulated red flags, not one
disqualifying fact).

## GATE 6 — VALUATION
**6-B Statute**: even the generous upper-bound OE ($63.725M, no capex
subtracted) yields only 63.725/691.571 = **9.21%** against the naive
NI+D&A read — but the true OE, after any real maintenance capex, likely
falls to a yield **in the 5–6.5% range against a 6.10% hurdle — genuinely
too close to call with the data available this pass.**
**Book Two DCF**: not built — would not resolve the underlying data-quality
problem anyway.
**VERDICT: WAIT** — not on a clean valuation miss like MAA/ESS/UDR/IRT/CSR,
but because **the qualitative picture (external management, eroding
leverage, unclear capex) does not support committing capital even if the
arithmetic were to land on the passing side.** This is the GAMCO pattern:
accumulated caution outweighs a single-number verdict.
