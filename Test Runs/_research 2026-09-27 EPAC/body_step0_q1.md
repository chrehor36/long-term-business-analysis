## Q1 — CAN I UNDERSTAND HOW THIS MAKES MONEY? **[E3-31]**
- **Unit economics in my own words, no management language.** Enerpac sells the tools that push, lift, pull and tighten very large things: hydraulic cylinders and jacks, the pumps that drive them at *"approximately 5,000 to 12,000 pounds per square inch"*, hydraulic torque wrenches and bolt tensioners for the flanges of pipelines, refineries, wind towers and power plants, and heavy-lifting gantries and skidding systems. It mostly assembles rather than makes: *"our manufacturing consists primarily of light assembly of components we source from a network of global suppliers"*, built to its specifications *"in best-cost locations including various countries in Asia."* It sells *"primarily through our world-class, global network of distributors, as well as direct sales to OEMs and select end users."* Beside the tools sits a service business (the Hydratight name): technicians who do bolting, machining and joint-integrity work on customers' plant and who rent out tools, **$116.8M of FY2025's $595.8M of IT&S sales (19.6%)**, the rest ($479.0M) product. FY2025 consolidated: sales **$616.9M**; gross margin **50.5%**; selling and administrative cost **27%**; operating profit **$133.5M (21.6%)**; capital spending **$19.3M, 3.1% of sales** (including a new head office). So the money is a wide margin on a bought-in and assembled tool that a maintenance crew uses rarely but cannot do the job without, sold through distributors at a price the filer raises every year it needs to (Q2 tests that), with a lower-margin labour business attached.
- **The scarce input this business controls.** The candidates, stated before Q2 tests them: (a) **the brand and its trademarks in a safety-critical tool** (*"the termination of certain of our trademarks, including ENERPAC®, HYDRATIGHT®, LARZEP®, SIMPLEX® and DTA the Smart Move ®, could have a material adverse effect on our business"*, while *"no individual patent is believed to be of such importance"*); (b) **the distributor network and the installed base** of pumps, cylinders and fittings that work together at one pressure standard; (c) **the engineering and application knowledge** for heavy lifting and bolting. The filer names what it does not control: *"The markets for our products are highly competitive"*, *"many of which are highly fragmented"*; raw materials, tariffs, and end markets in oil and gas, power, infrastructure and mining. Whether (a)-(c) let Enerpac set its price is Q2's question.
- **Will the fundamentals look broadly the same in ten years?** The hydraulic-tools business has been reported as its own segment since FY2005 and its description has not changed: in FY2013 *"Industrial segment ... innovative integrated solutions, the commercialization of new products"*; in FY2025 *"branded tools, cylinders, pumps, hydraulic torque wrenches, highly engineered heavy lifting technology solutions."* What changes is the perimeter around it: every other business Actuant owned has been sold, and the SFE Group purchase (above) adds welding, pipe-fabrication and portable-machining equipment of about $170M of sales. That is a Q2, Q3 and Q4 matter, not a change in how the core makes money.
- **Relatively simple and stable in character [E3-31]? A decision in five minutes [E4-46]?** Yes. A branded maker and distributor-seller of high-pressure hydraulic and bolting tools with an attached service business; the arithmetic is a gross margin on assembled tools less selling and engineering cost. The perimeter history is long but documented segment by segment in the filings.
