## Q2 — IS IT A FRANCHISE? **[E3-03]**

*Every Enerpac figure below is read from the segment note and MD&A of its filed 10-Ks FY2005-FY2025, the three FY2026 10-Qs, or SEC companyfacts checked against those statements (accessions in Step 0). Scripts and outputs, all in `Test Runs/_research 2026-09-27 EPAC/`: `seg2.py` and `seg4_out.txt` (the segment tables of every 10-K), `q2_series.py` (`q2_series.txt`), `pricegrep.py` (`price_sentences.txt`, `price_sentences_2021_2026.txt`: every MD&A sentence on price and pricing), `peers/row.py` (`peers/row_all.txt`).*

**The question this file has to answer: does the hydraulic-tools business set its prices, and is that visible in its returns, or has it been a competent industrial supplier whose margins are made by cost programmes?** The two hypotheses, stated so they can be attacked [E4-26]. *(a) A narrow franchise of brand and distribution in a safety-critical tool: a maintenance crew lifting a bridge or tightening a refinery flange pays for the name it trusts, the tool is a small part of the job's cost, and the Industrial segment's returns show it.* *(b) A good industrial-products maker in fragmented, "highly competitive" markets, whose recent margins are the work of a new chief executive's cost and pricing programme (ASCEND, 80/20), and whose consolidated record for FY2017-FY2022 earned single-digit margins.* Each is tested below.

**How the filer describes its competition.** *"The markets for our products are highly competitive. We provide a diverse and broad range of industrial products and services to numerous global end markets, many of which are highly fragmented. Although we face larger competitors in several served markets, some of our competition is comprised of smaller companies which may lack the footprint or financial resources to serve global customers. We compete for business principally on the basis of customer service, product quality and availability, and engineering and research and development expertise"* (10-K FY2025, Item 1). Until the FY2018 10-K the same sentence ended *"... engineering, research and development expertise and price"*; the word "price" was dropped from the FY2019 10-K on. Item 1A since FY2017: *"some of our competitors may be willing to reduce prices and accept lower margins to compete with us."* The earnings releases of 2026 list *"operating margin risk due to competitive pricing and operating efficiencies"* among the risks. The filer names no competitor.

**The price record, in the filer's words (`price_sentences.txt`).** FY2006: *"sales grew 15%, primarily the result of price increases due to rising raw material costs"*; FY2007 and FY2008: *"price increases"* and *"modest price increases"* in strong demand; FY2009 10-K (for FY2008): *"customer price increases"* among the reasons operating profit grew; every 10-K FY2004-FY2025: *"We strive to ... pass along such commodity price increases to customers to avoid profit margin erosion."* FY2022: *"product pricing actions that the Company took beginning in late fiscal 2021 and during fiscal 2022 in response to significant inflationary pressures ... were the primary drivers of the increase in net sales"*, with *"improved gross profit margins on product primarily due to the aforementioned pricing actions outweighing the impact of inflation."* FY2023: *"The product sales growth was primarily due to pricing actions, with some volume contribution."* FY2024: *"The increase in product organic sales was driven by pricing actions and mix within the IT&S product offerings"* on product organic growth of 1%, and gross margin up two points *"primarily attributed to operational improvements from the ASCEND transformation program, as well as pricing actions and the disposition of Cortland Industrial."* **Consolidated gross margin (current perimeter): 42.2% FY2017, 44.2%, 44.7%, 44.0%, 46.0%, 46.5%, 49.3%, 51.1%, 50.5% FY2025.** Price has been raised into inflation and kept after it, with gross margin nine points higher at the end than the start.

**The current perimeter's return record, which is the weak part.** The IT&S segment (Industrial plus the Hydratight bolting and machining services, 20% of sales) earned **14.4-17.3% on sales FY2017-FY2022** and **24.5%, 26.8%, 27.5% FY2023-FY2025**; on its segment assets (which carry $260-290M of goodwill) 11.4-17.7% FY2018-FY2022 and 21.7-25.5% since. The consolidated company, after corporate cost, earned 4.9-9.7% on sales FY2018-FY2022 (5.4% in FY2022) and 14.0%, 20.6%, 21.6% FY2023-FY2025; operating income on average tangible non-cash assets 5.2-15.1% FY2018-FY2022 and 26.2%, 39.9%, 41.4% FY2023-FY2025 (`peers/row_all.txt`). On unleveraged net tangible capital [E2-43] (equity plus debt less cash, goodwill and intangibles: $113.7M at FY2024, $135.1M at FY2025) FY2025 operating profit of $133.5M is about **107% pre-tax**; on the same capital **with** goodwill ($445.6M average) about **30%**. The step-up since FY2022 has three filed causes: pricing, the ASCEND programme (*"approximately $54 million of annual operating profit"* realized by FY2023, at a cost of *"$75 million"*), and 80/20 exits from low-margin service work (*"a more selective process for quoting projects in the MENAC region"*).

- **Peers taken: eleven SEC filers**, chosen as the makers of industrial tools and fluid-power products in Enerpac's trade (Stanley, Snap-on, Kennametal), hydraulics (Parker, Helios), engineered pumps and dispensing (IDEX, Graco, Nordson, ITW), and welding and fabrication equipment, which is what SFE Group sells (Lincoln, ESAB). **Not taken, and the reason:** the like-for-like hydraulic-tool rivals (Holmatro, Hi-Force, Hytorc, SPX's former Power Team line) are private or reported inside larger segments; the filer names none. The class below **rests on the filer's own segment record and pricing sentences**, which the row supplements; it is not held PROVISIONAL (the NDSN precedent).
- **What the row shows.** **Today Enerpac sits in the top third on gross margin** (behind Nordson and Graco) and in the upper middle on operating margin and on tangible assets; **across FY2018-FY2022 its consolidated operating margin (4.9-9.7%) was the lowest in the row but for Kennametal.** Its old Industrial segment (22-31% margins FY2007-FY2017) sat with Graco, Snap-on and ITW. The row therefore says two things: the hydraulic-tools core belongs with the high-return industrial franchises, and the company that owned it did not earn like one for six years. **Its limit [E3-61]:** position, not conduct.

**The remaining Q2 tests.**
- **[E3-43], the demonstration** (*"a company's ability to regularly price its product or service aggressively and thereby to earn high rates of return on capital"*): **met on the core, by level and by passing on cost.** Eleven years of 26-53% pre-tax on segment assets that include purchased goodwill; price raised with raw materials in FY2006-FY2008 and FY2022-FY2024 with gross margin rising, not falling; the recession year held 23.5% on sales with no reported price concession. What is not shown is price raised aggressively beyond cost in a flat year before FY2024.
- **[E2-44], the two characteristics.** (1) *"an ability to increase prices rather easily (even when product demand is flat and capacity is not fully utilized) without fear of significant loss of either market share or unit volume"*: **met in part.** FY2024 is the clean case on file: product organic growth of 1% *"driven by pricing actions and mix"*, gross margin up two points; no unit series is published, so the volume side cannot be read directly [E4-55], and share is not reported. (2) *"an ability to accommodate large dollar volume increases in business ... with only minor additional investment of capital"*: **met on capital.** Capital spending ran 1.5-3.1% of sales FY2017-FY2025 (the FY2025 figure includes the new head office); tangible net capital of about $114-135M carries $600M of sales. Growth has come mainly by purchase (HTL, Larzep, Hayes, Simplex, DTA, and now SFE Group).
- **[E4-32], direction: widening in the numbers since FY2022, stationary on the long record.** Operating margin 5.4% (FY2022) to 21.6% (FY2025) and IT&S 14.9% to 27.5%; but the Industrial segment earned 27-29% in FY2012-FY2014, so the recent level is a return to the core's long-run economics after the conglomerate's overhead and low-margin services were cut, more than a new widening. **[E4-55], units:** none published; the FY2024 growth was price and mix, the nine months to 2026-05-31 show product organic growth of 5% with service down 18%.
- **[E3-33] untapped pricing power / [E5-28]: not claimed** as a near monopoly. The FY2022-FY2024 record is the corpus's own shape in miniature (margin raised by pricing under a new manager), but the filer calls its markets fragmented and competitive, and no share figure is filed.
- **[E2-53], dominance: not shown.** No market-share statement; the filer says it faces *"larger competitors in several served markets."*
- **[E2-45], the attacker's test.** An attacker with capital (the filer says it faces *"larger competitors in several served markets"*) can build a pump and a cylinder; what it cannot quickly build is the distributor shelf, the brand a crew trusts at 10,000 psi, and the installed base of fittings that work together. That is a narrow moat, not a wide one.
- **[E4-04], in the ruling's form.** Not the ground: hydraulic tools are a slow-changing product, the brand dates from 1910, and the lead is maintained, not rebuilt from zero.
- **[E4-23], key-person dependence: a prompt, recorded here as the rule requires.** The margin step-up since FY2022 follows the programme of the chief executive appointed in October 2021 (ASCEND, 80/20). The core earned 25-31% under earlier management (FY2005-FY2014), so the economics do not require this manager; the recent level may. Recorded, not the ground.
- **[E3-74] and [E3-38], the class.** The product half is a have-to-be-smart-once business (a brand and a distributor network); the service half (Hydratight, 20% of sales, organic service sales down 18% in the nine months to 2026-05-31) is labour sold by the job and carries no franchise. Weighed at Q3's weight case.
- **[E4-36], which cause of success.** Extreme on one variable: a tool whose failure is dangerous and whose price is small beside the job it enables. That cause is ownable, and it is the same one Graco and Snap-on show in the row.
- **The pending purchase is a new leg, not the franchise.** SFE Group (welding, pipe fabrication, portable machining, about $170M of sales) is bought at *"10.6x trailing-twelve-month adjusted EBITDA"*; its competitors are Lincoln and ESAB, whose margins in the row are 14.5-17.1%. Whether it earns like the core is a Q3 (capital allocation) and Q4 (camouflage) question, not a Q2 finding.

### THE STRONGEST EVIDENCE AGAINST THIS VERDICT, stated as its holders would state it [E4-51, E4-26, E3-47]
The verdict below is IN, so the case for OUT is set out first, at full strength:
1. **The filer says its markets are "highly competitive" and "highly fragmented", warns that competitors "may be willing to reduce prices and accept lower margins to compete with us", and listed price among the bases of competition until FY2018.** A franchise's filer does not write that.
2. **The company on offer earned 4.9-9.7% on sales for five years (FY2018-FY2022)**, the second-worst record in an eleven-filer row, and its high margins date only from FY2023. Three good years under a turnaround programme are [E4-18]'s narrowed window, not a demonstration.
3. **The recent margin is a manager's, not a castle's.** ASCEND cost $75M and produced about $54M a year of profit; 80/20 exited service work; the filer credits "operational improvements" alongside pricing. [E4-23]: if the economics rest on the knight, the business is not great.
4. **No unit series, no share, no named competitor.** Dollar sales flattered by price are how a shrinking franchise hides [E4-55]; FY2024 product growth was price and mix on 1% organic growth.
5. **A fifth of the business is labour**, cyclical with oil and gas maintenance, and now falling 18%; and the company is about to spend $472M on a welding and machining business outside the core.

**Answered.** (1) is weighed against the filer's own segment record: the same filings that call the market competitive show the hydraulic-tools segment earning 22-31% on sales and 26-53% on its assets (with goodwill) for eleven years, holding 23.5% through a 23% sales fall with no price concession reported; the risk-factor sentence is boilerplate in the sense that it has stood unchanged while those returns were earned. (2) is the strongest point and is answered by the perimeter: FY2018-FY2022 consolidated figures carry general corporate cost of $20.2-48.8M a year (FY2019 $42.1M, FY2022 $48.8M, against $32.8-36.5M in FY2024-FY2025), the Other segment's losses (minus $11.8M in FY2019, minus $10.4M in FY2021) and the low-margin services; the IT&S segment on its own earned 14-17% in those years and the product core, last reported alone in FY2017, 22%. The verdict does not rest on FY2023-FY2025. (3) is taken seriously and recorded as [E4-23]'s prompt; it is answered by the FY2005-FY2014 record, when a different management earned 25-31% on the same product line: the castle's economics predate this knight, who restored rather than created them. (4) is conceded as a gap and carried to Q6 as the monitoring metric; it does not overturn a return record read over eleven years. (5) is why the class is NARROW and not wide, and why the SFE Group purchase is carried to Q3 and Q4.

**The analyst's own incentive, stated [operator rule 9, E4-27, E3-41].** OUT would end the run early, a pull toward OUT; the NDSN file of the same day cleared Q2 NARROW on a similar record, a pull toward IN by analogy.
