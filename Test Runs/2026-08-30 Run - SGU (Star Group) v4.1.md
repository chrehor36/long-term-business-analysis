## Q1 — CAN I UNDERSTAND HOW THIS MAKES MONEY? **[E3-31]**

- **Unit economics in my own words:** Star delivers heating oil (and, increasingly by
  acquisition, propane) to about 406,400 full-service homes and businesses in the
  Northeast and Mid-Atlantic — 59% in NY/NJ/CT — plus 63,200 delivery-only accounts.
  It buys the commodity wholesale, trucks it to the customer's tank at a per-gallon
  margin over cost, and wraps the delivery in a service relationship (24/7 burner
  repair, installations, service contracts — 19% of sales) that makes leaving
  inconvenient. 93% of customers are on automatic delivery; ~25% pay level monthly
  budgets. Price-protected offerings are hedged with derivatives; warm winters are
  partially hedged with degree-day contracts (FY2026: receive up to $15.0M, pay at most
  $5.0M). Volume is weather × customer count; the customer count melts every year and
  is partially rebuilt by buying small dealers (FY2019-25: $276M spent, +71.7M
  gallons/yr acquired). FY2025: sales $1,784M, net income $73.5M, OCF $71.0M, term loan
  $189M vs partners' capital $297M.
- **The scarce input the business controls:** route density in Northeast metros plus
  the installed service relationship (the burner contract, the tank, the trust that
  gets a truck to your house at 2 a.m. in January) — and a standing pipeline of
  retiring one-town dealers who sell their customer lists to the biggest buyer.
- **Ten years:** the mechanism will look identical and the base will be smaller — the
  10-K says so itself: the industry is *"mature, with total market demand expected to
  decline in the foreseeable future due to conversions to natural gas and electricity"*,
  and maintaining the business *"is dependent on the acquisition of other retail
  distributors."* That is a legibility PASS and a franchise question.
- **VERDICT: [x] IN** — among the most legible businesses run through this framework;
  the company files its own decay rate quarterly. The secular direction is Q2's
  question, the weather distribution is Q4's.
