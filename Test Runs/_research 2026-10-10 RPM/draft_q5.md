
## Q5 — WHO RUNS IT: able, honest, in love with the business, the same after being paid. STOP on integrity.
**The people** (proxy `0001193125-26-365259`; 10-K FY2026 executive officers; 8-K `0001193125-26-311276`): Frank C. Sullivan,
Chair and CEO (CEO since 2008, with the company since 1987, son of the former chairman); David C. Dennsteadt, President and COO
from 2026-07-17; Russell L. Gordon, CFO; Tracy D. Crandall, General Counsel and Chief Compliance Officer since 2024. Board of
13, 12 independent, a Lead Director, declassified from 2026. The CEO's brother is Vice President, Corporate Development
($640,000 salary and bonus, disclosed as a related-person transaction).

### The first yardstick: how well they run it **[M1994-008]**
Sales from $3.4B (FY2010) to $7.9B (FY2026); returns on tangible capital high throughout (Q3); the construction margin raised
by about four points since FY2020 by two restructuring programmes (Q2). Capital allocation is read at Q6. **Ability WEIGHS
FOR**, with the reservation that the margin gains are recent and that "records" are counted in adjusted figures.

### The habits carried from Q4, read with the integrity record
"things that they do in public in relation to their investors and the promises they make" **[M2004-067]**: quarterly and
annual outlooks; a featured adjusted figure, now adjusted EBITDA; "the 16th time we have generated record adjusted EBIT out of
the past 18 quarters" (Q4). Against the habit, and to the company's credit: the three-year PSUs for FY2024 to FY2026 paid
**0%** because the targets were missed, and the annual PERS paid 44.4% of target (proxy, CD&A). The pay plan let the
executives miss; that is not the conduct of a company that makes its numbers whatever happens.

### The integrity record, from the primary documents
1. **The DOJ matter.** From 2011 to 2013 a subsidiary's (Tremco Roofing's) government contracts were under DOJ and GSA
   investigation; the company expected to pay "approximately $65.1 million" to resolve it (annual report FY2013, Exhibit 13 to
   `0001193125-13-300693`). The SEC's account: "RPM sent DOJ estimates showing RPM's subsidiary overcharged the government on
   the contracts under investigation by a material amount", and "prior to submitting the settlement offer to DOJ, RPM's
   overcharge estimates increased substantially to at least $28 million" (SEC Litigation Release No. 23639, 2016-09-09,
   sec.gov, a primary source).
2. **What the general counsel did not tell.** The SEC alleged that the General Counsel and Chief Compliance Officer "knew but
   failed to inform" the CEO, the CFO, the audit committee and the auditors of those facts (same release). The company restated
   three quarters of FY2013 for the timing of the accrual; its audit committee "concluded that there was no intentional
   misconduct on the part of any of our officers" (10-K FY2016, `0001564590-16-021706`). The SEC's Wells notice had also
   proposed a clawback of the CEO's and CFO's incentive pay for the restated periods (10-K FY2016); no charge was brought
   against either.
3. **How the company answered.** For four years its 10-Ks called the allegations "without merit" and said they "mischaracterize"
   the company's and the General Counsel's actions (10-Ks FY2017 to FY2020). On 2020-12-22 it consented to a final judgment:
   a $2.0M penalty and permanent injunctions under the reporting, books-and-records and internal-controls provisions; the
   General Counsel paid $22,500 and was enjoined under the rule against falsifying books and records; neither admitted nor
   denied (10-K FY2021, `0001564590-21-037949`; SEC Litigation Release No. 24994, 2020-12-23).
4. **What the board did with the man.** The General Counsel stayed General Counsel and Chief Compliance Officer, and a named
   executive officer, through fiscal 2024 (proxy 2026, pay-versus-performance notes listing him among the NEOs for FY2022 to
   FY2024; 10-K FY2026 names his successor "elected ... in 2024"): about twelve years after the conduct and four after the
   injunction. The rows: "the main problem was they didn’t act when they learned about it" **[M2017-005]**; "a pound of cure,
   promptly applied, is worth a ton of cure that’s delayed" **[M2017-007]**; the instruction to managers is "tell us the bad
   news immediately" **[M1998-039]**, and the conduct alleged was the opposite, kept from the very people whose job is to hear it.
5. **A jury's finding of fraud at a subsidiary (2024).** A Consumer subsidiary was found by a jury to have breached contracts
   with a former supplier, misappropriated trade secrets and committed fraud; verdict $190.0M with punitive damages; judgment
   $110.8M plus interest; company appealing, accrual $0.5M (10-K FY2026 Note P). Disputed and on appeal; recorded, given less
   weight than the settled matter.
6. **What weighs the other way.** The restatement moved accruals between quarters and changed no year; the CEO and CFO were not
   charged; the general counsel was replaced in 2024; a clawback policy has stood since 2012; incentive pay has been allowed to
   fail (above); the board is largely new (seven of eleven independent directors joined since 2021, proxy).

### The judgment
The test is not proof but doubt: "If you’ve got doubts, forget it." **[M2013-088]**; the speakers accept the cost, "we rule out
90 percent of the times. And we may be wrong about a fair number that we’re ruling out" **[M2007-092]**. The question is whether
I can count on this management to tell owners the bad news when it is theirs to tell. The record is a government-overcharging
investigation in the company's largest segment, a general counsel who on the SEC's account kept the rising estimates from the
CEO, CFO, audit committee and auditors, a company that called the case meritless for four years and then took a permanent
injunction, and a board that left the same man in charge of compliance for years after; and today, a management that has just
moved its featured measure of profit to one that leaves out depreciation **[R1996-023]**, **[M2002-026]**. "everybody mouths the
integrity, even when it’s lacking" **[M2010-069]**; "There are also readings in corporate culture that have to be made"
**[M1998-121]**. I do not find dishonesty proved against the present CEO; I do have doubt, of the kind the rows say closes the
file, about the culture of candour with owners and auditors at the top of this company **[M2015-047]**.

**VERDICT on integrity: OUT (on doubt, M2013-088).** Ability WEIGHS FOR. The file closes here; Q6 to Q12 are NOT REACHED as
judgments. Facts found for later questions are written under AFTER THE STOP, and the Q7 arithmetic the brief asks for is
reported there under COMPUTATION, NOT A CLEARANCE.
