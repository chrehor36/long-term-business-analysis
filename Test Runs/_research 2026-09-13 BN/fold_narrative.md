### HOW A HOLDING COMPANY'S Q2 WAS ASKED - a method finding for the next multi-leg name
The BRK run found a moat at the level of the whole (a funding structure no leg carried); the L run asked the question where the
earnings are. **BN needed both, in that order:**
1. **The whole-level claim first**, because BN makes one in writing (the *"Brookfield Ecosystem"*, *"five distinct advantages for our
   franchise"*). Tested on the one number BRK's whole-level moat rested on - **the cost of the permanent capital** - and on the
   attacker's test. **It fails on both:** BN's own risk factor says competitors *"may have … a lower cost of capital"*, BWS has the
   highest life and annuity cost of funds of four filers, and Apollo-Athene and KKR-Global Atlantic run the same system at larger scale.
2. **Then the legs, weighted three ways** (segment book equity, cash reaching the Corporation, quoted market), **because each single
   weight flatters a different leg**: book puts real estate at 43%, market puts BAM at 44%, cash puts the fee engine at 37%. **The three
   legs carrying 84-87% on every basis were rowed; the three carrying 12-16% were read from the parent's filing and not rowed**, with the
   directional reason stated (the parent's own text puts them in regulated, contracted or highly competitive classes) and each row named
   as a reversal work order.
**Why the BAM row transferred as a floor, not a ceiling:** at BN the fee engine's "conferred" durable leg is partly **BN paying itself**
(BWS's $243M of fees to Brookfield; BEP and BIP's $466M of IDRs to BAM). A fee paid between two controlled legs cannot be a customer's
judgment about substitutes, because at the parent there is no customer.
