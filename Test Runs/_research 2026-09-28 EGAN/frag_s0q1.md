## Q1 — CAN I UNDERSTAND HOW THIS MAKES MONEY? **[E3-31]**
- **Unit economics in my own words, no management language:** eGain rents software to the customer-service departments of large companies and government agencies. The core product is a searchable, maintained library of answers (policies, procedures, product facts) that call-centre staff, customers on a website and now AI chatbots draw on while handling a question, plus tools for handling chat, email and messaging conversations. A customer signs a contract of about three years (*"We generally offer these services through a 36-month contract, with pricing based on the number of agents or self-service sessions"*), is invoiced a year ahead, and eGain runs the software on rented cloud capacity. **Revenue FY2026 $91.1M: subscriptions $85.3M (94%), setup and consulting $5.9M (6%)**; 89% from enterprises over $1bn of revenue or governments; North America 79%. **Who pays is concentrated:** the ten largest customers were about 56% of revenue (*"our ten largest customers accounted for approximately 56% of our total revenue, compared with approximately 58%"*), one customer that is also a partner 15% (20% in FY2023, 16% in FY2025), and **Cisco pays a per-agent royalty for eGain software embedded in Cisco's contact-centre product** ($10.9M, 12%: *"Cisco original equipment manufacturer (OEM) | 10,930 | 10,583"*; *"the customer is to provide a combined fixed fee, per agent, for each software license sold containing the embedded software"*). The costs are engineers (197 of 422 staff; research and development $29.4M, 32% of revenue), cloud hosting and support (cost of subscription $17.7M), a sales force ($19.5M) and administration ($10.0M). Gross margin 73%, operating margin 8.7% in FY2026. **The disclosed customer split:** *"AI customers | 55,129 | 45,920"* (+20%), Cisco OEM (+3%), *"Other | 25,077 | 31,928"* (−21%), the last described as *"the expected decline from customers that do not actively utilize our AI offerings"*. No net retention rate is disclosed; remaining performance obligations are $87.0M, $62.1M of it within a year. The company holds $73.3M of cash and no debt.
- **The scarce input this business controls:** in the customer's building, the **installed knowledge base and its integration**: the answers written, tagged and approved in eGain's format and wired into the call-centre desktop and the CRM, which is work the customer would have to redo to switch. Outside it, a **qualification with the platform owners** (the Cisco OEM agreement, connectors to Amazon Connect, Genesys, Salesforce, Five9, Zoom, Microsoft), and the **security certifications it holds** (*"SOC2, PCI, HIPAA, FedRAMP"*; *"The Internal Revenue Service uses our solutions served from the eGain Cloud."*). The filer itself names only two barriers, and that is Q2's question: *"Other than product innovation and existing customer relationships, there are no substantial barriers to entry in this market"*.
- **Will the fundamentals look broadly the same in ten years?** The need will: large companies will still answer customers and will still need approved, current answers to give them. **How it is met is changing now, and the filer says so:** *"The market for our products changes rapidly and is characterized by evolving industry standards, swift changes in customer requirements, and frequent product introductions."*; *"customers are currently assessing and evolving their AI utilization strategy, so it is difficult to estimate with any reasonable degree of precision the impact of generative AI product offerings on our future revenue"*; the competitors now include *"providers of general-purpose generative AI platforms"* and *"enterprises that develop knowledge management, AI and related capabilities using their own internal resources."* Whether eGain's position is maintained or must be rebuilt through this change is [E4-04]'s question and is asked at Q2, as the framework places it.
- **Why this is inside the circle:** how the company makes money is plain: a subscription priced per seat or per session on a three-year term, a royalty per seat from Cisco, an engineering cost base, and all of it filed in two revenue lines and one reportable segment.
