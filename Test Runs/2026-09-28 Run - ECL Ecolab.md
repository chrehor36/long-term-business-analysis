## Q1 — CAN I UNDERSTAND HOW THIS MAKES MONEY? **[E3-31]**
- Unit economics in my own words, no management language: Ecolab makes consumable chemicals (detergents, sanitizers, water-treatment additives, process aids) and sells them together with the dispensing and dosing equipment and a salesman-technician who calls on the site, sets the dose and checks the result. The customer is a kitchen, a hotel laundry, a hospital, a food or dairy plant, a paper mill, a refinery, a power station or a factory cooling tower. The chemical is used up and reordered, so most revenue recurs as long as the site runs. **Four segments (FY2025, at the filer's fixed exchange rates):** Global Water (industrial water treatment, food-and-beverage plant cleaning, paper-mill chemistry, refinery additives; $7,679.9M of sales, $1,263.9M operating income, 16.5%); Global Institutional & Specialty (warewashing, laundry, housekeeping and infection-prevention chemistry for restaurants, hotels, hospitals and food retailers; $5,962.0M, $1,357.8M, 22.8%; warewashing alone *"approximately 13% of consolidated net sales in 2025"*); Global Pest Elimination (a service business, technicians on a route; $1,219.2M, $237.1M, 19.4%); Global Life Sciences (cleaning and contamination control for drug and cosmetic makers, and Purolite's purification resins; $706.1M, $120.7M, 17.1%). Corporate carries the amortization of the Nalco, Purolite and Ovivo intangibles and the special charges (−$353.2M). Reported: sales $16,081.2M, operating income $2,737.6M (17.0%), gross margin 44.5%. **The cost structure:** the chemicals are made in Ecolab's own plants from *"more than 10,000 raw materials"* (*"the largest single raw material representing approximately five percent of raw material purchases"*), and the largest cost after materials is the field force: *"The largest component of our workforce is comprised of more than 25,000 sales and service employees"* of 48,000. The dispensing and monitoring equipment placed at customer sites is Ecolab's own capital (*"systems used by customers to dispense our products as well as to monitor water systems"*), which is why capital expenditure runs above depreciation in a chemical seller.
- **The brief's Q1 prior, checked against the segment note: right as far as it goes, and incomplete in three places.** (1) It is not only programs through a field force: *"The majority of our Light & Heavy revenue is from product sales"*, farm products go *"through dealers and independent, third-party distributors"*, QSR customers *"are primarily supplied through third party distributors"*, and 78% of revenue is product and sold equipment ($12,618.5M of $16,081.2M). (2) It omits the refinery and petrochemical process chemistry kept after the ChampionX separation (*"specialty programs to the petroleum and fuels industry – refining process applications, fuels and feedstocks additives"*) and Purolite's resins. (3) **It omits the two 2025-2026 purchases that make the company a hardware seller to semiconductor fabs and AI data centers**: Ovivo Electronics (*"ultrapure water technologies for semiconductor manufacturing"*) and CoolIT (*"designs and manufactures high-performance liquid cooling systems, including coolant distribution units (CDUs), cold plates and direct-to-chip cooling technologies"*), about $550M of expected sales, some 3% of the total, bought at 29 times the filer's own forward EBITDA estimate.
- The scarce input this business controls: **the installed position at the customer's site, held by a field force of 25,000 and equipment Ecolab owns and places: the dispenser in the kitchen and laundry, the controller on the cooling tower (the 3D TRASAR monitoring the filer names as a material trademark with Ecolab and Nalco), and the relationships with multi-unit chains (*"Corporate account sales personnel establish relationships and negotiate contracts with larger multi-unit or “chain” customers"*); behind it, formulations registered with regulators (*"We maintain several hundred product registrations with the U.S. Environmental Protection Agency"*) and scale in manufacture and purchasing.** Whether any of this is scarce in [E3-03]'s sense is Q2's question.
- Will the fundamentals look broadly the same in ten years? **Yes for the core, which has not changed in its essentials across the filings read.** The FY2007 10-K (`0001104659-08-012647`) describes the same model: *"The Institutional Division manufactures and markets various chemical dispensing device systems, which are made available to customers, to dispense our cleaners and sanitizers"*, *"The Institutional Division sells its products and programs primarily through company-employed field sales personnel."* Restaurants, hotels, hospitals, food plants and cooling towers will still need cleaning and treatment chemistry in ten years. **What may not look the same is the mix:** the two newest purchases put hardware, sold on projects to a handful of hyperscale and semiconductor customers, into Global Water; the announcement says CoolIT *"will double Ecolab’s Global High-Tech market opportunity from $5 billion to $10 billion"*. That is a direction for Q2 (competence) and Q3 (capital allocation), not a change in how the core makes money.

Four segments and seven operating units are more to read than one, but each is described in terms that need no months of study. **The one part outside the circle is CoolIT, liquid cooling for AI chips**, a technology product whose economics depend on the next generations of data-center hardware; at about 3% of sales it does not decide how Ecolab makes money, and it is carried to Q2 as a perimeter note, not a Q1 close.

### THE STRONGEST EVIDENCE AGAINST THIS VERDICT, stated as its holders would state it [E4-51, E4-26, E3-47]
The verdict below is IN, so the case for OUT is set out first, at full strength.
1. *"The price is cost pass-through, with a lag, which is the commodity signature. When raw materials rose in 2021-2022 Ecolab's gross margin fell six points (43.9% in 2019 to 37.8% in 2022) and took until 2024 to recover. The filer says so itself: 2021, 'the 1.8 percentage point positive impact from accelerating pricing was more than offset by the 3.4 percentage point negative impact of significantly higher delivered product costs'; 2022, 'the 9.0 percentage point positive impacts of accelerating pricing was more than offset by the 12.7 percentage point negative impacts of higher delivered product costs'. Nalco, the water half of today's Ecolab, told its own shareholders in its last 10-K that its growth was measured 'excluding price increases designed to only offset higher raw material costs'. A franchise sets the price; this business chases its costs."* **Answer:** accepted as the fact of 2021-2022, and it is the best fact against the verdict. The question [E2-44] asks is the other half: what happens to price when costs and demand fall. In 2009 volume fell 3% and price rose 3%, delivered product costs declined *"on a year over year basis during the second half of 2009"*, and the U.S. cleaning business's margin rose from 16.2% to 18.6% on *"Increased pricing, favorable raw material costs and cost savings actions"* (FY2009 annual report); the price was kept when the cost went away. In 2015-2016, with oil and its derivatives falling (the FY2014 annual report: *"lower oil prices will provide benefits across our segments in the form of lower raw material costs"*), every non-energy segment still took +1% to +2% and the adjusted gross margin rose (*"our 2016 adjusted gross margin was 48.0% compared against a 2015 adjusted gross margin of 47.3%. The increase was driven primarily by lower delivered product costs"*, FY2016 10-K). In 2020 the Institutional segment lost 21% of its volume and took +2% of price. A pass-through business gives the price back when the cost falls; this one did not in any filed year, outside the segment it has since split off.
