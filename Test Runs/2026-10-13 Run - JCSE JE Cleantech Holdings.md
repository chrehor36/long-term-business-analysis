- **The economics, from the filings.** Two businesses (20-F FY2025 Item 4 and segment note, acc 0001493152-26-021923). (a) **Cleaning systems** (S$12.5M of S$20.3M revenue in 2025): aqueous and plating cleaning machines designed and built to order at one plant in Woodlands, Singapore, sold mainly to hard-disk-drive and semiconductor makers in Malaysia and Singapore; "approximately 100%" of sales from repeat customers; "generally no long-term commitment from customers [...] to purchase an agreed amount" (Item 3.D). (b) **Centralized dishwashing and general cleaning** (S$7.8M): a labour service for food courts, hawker centres and an inflight caterer in Singapore, moved in November 2025 into the same plant after the dishwashing site was sold. The key variables are plain: the order book of a few multinational customers' production-line spending, the gross margin earned on each job, labour costs and foreign-worker rules for both segments, and the overhead of a Nasdaq listing on a business this size.
- **Do the past statements tell the future ones?** They tell the shape: seven years of revenue between S$14.8M and S$21.4M (2019 S$18.2M, 2020 S$21.4M, 2021 S$14.8M, 2022 S$18.6M, 2023 S$18.0M, 2024 S$19.3M, 2025 S$20.3M; 20-Fs FY2021, FY2022, FY2025), cleaning-segment gross margins between 23.3% and 40.8%, and operating income between a loss of S$30k and a profit of S$3.5M. What a job shop serving a few large buyers earns, and why it swings, can be read off the figures **[M2008-069]**. What cannot be foreseen is the size of any one customer's orders in a given year; that is a question about the castle (who holds the power in the price), and it is carried to Q2, not a failure to understand the economics.
- **Routing.** Not a business of fast-moving technology in its own right: the machines wash components to a cleanliness standard; the technology risk is the customers' (disk drives against solid-state storage), and it enters through their orders, which Q2 reads. Not a bank. Not a holding company of unrelated parts; the two segments are read as parts at Q2. Doubt test **[M2002-092]**: my doubt is about durability and bargaining power, not about what the business is or how it makes money.
- **VERDICT: IN.** The economics are simple and visible in the filed figures (**[M2012-065]**, **[M2011-014]**, **[M2008-033]**); the doubt about the customers' orders and the price they pay is a castle question and goes to Q2.

## WHAT IN THE FRAMEWORK WAS WRONG OR UNCLEAR
(1) **Section B's threshold** names "five-year operating profit", but this filer reports segment operating results only for 2023 to 2025 (the year segment-expense disclosure began); I read the five-year share from segment gross profit and checked it against the three filed years, a CONVENTION of this run; both measures gave the same deciding part, so nothing turned on it here, but a filer with a closer split would need a rule. (2) **Section C's ordinary-returns rule** reads the field's returns "on the evidence of the filer's and the rivals' own filings"; when the 20-F names no rival and none files with the SEC, the text does not say whether the filer's own full-cycle record suffices for OUT. I used the filer's record alone, together with the filer's own words on how it wins work, and said so; a stricter reading would send the name to TOO HARD (WORK) for a rival search that the brief itself forbids beyond named rivals. (3) **Specific (d)** shows the range "at the business's working-capital intensity before the event" where a draw is tied to one event; it does not say what to do when the event's draw never reverses and becomes the new intensity, as here (inventory 17% of revenue before, 45% six years later, matched by deposits). I kept the draw in the central figure and showed the working-capital-neutral case beside; the floor failed on both. (4) **The equity-only figure** is defined as owner cash after interest against market value; for a company holding net cash this leaves the cash out entirely and gives a figure (US$0.13) far below the all-equity figure (US$0.76), the reverse of the leverage case the convention was written for. I followed the text and flag the asymmetry. (5) **The sovereign table** in the protocol has no SGD row; I took the longest SGS benchmark the MAS publishes (50-year, 2.73%), as the brief instructed, with the 30-year (2.52%) noted; a standing choice between the 30-year and the 50-year would make runs comparable. (6) **A range that collapses to one case** (specific (b), where the growth is meaningless) makes the fair price's "average of the two ends" a single case; the text allows it by saying the range is then the no-growth case alone, but the fair-price paragraph of Part VII does not mention it.
