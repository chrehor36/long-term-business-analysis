### ETSY'S TAKE RATE ROSE. RECORD THE COUNTEREXAMPLE.

Etsy publishes the take rate directly. From the Key Operating and Financial Metrics
table, FY2025 10-K, as filed:

> "GMS $ 11,916,900 $ 12,586,952 (5.3)% · Revenue $ 2,883,501 $ 2,808,332 2.7% ·
> **Revenue take rate 24.2% 22.3% 190 bps**"
> Footnote (3) as filed: "Revenue take rate is revenue divided by GMS."

| FY | GMS ($K) | revenue ($K) | **take rate (filed)** |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2024 | 12,586,952 | 2,808,332 | **22.3%** |
| 2025 | 11,916,900 | 2,883,501 | **24.2%** |

**This cuts against the commoditization thesis and must be recorded as such.** But
read the mechanism before crediting it: Etsy's GMS **fell 5.3%** while revenue rose
2.7%. The take rate rose because the denominator shrank and Etsy raised seller fees,
not because pricing power expanded volume. Etsy is also a **marketplace** (it owns
the demand) rather than a **platform** (Shopify does not own the demand). A
marketplace can raise the rake against a captive seller base in a way a
merchant-owned-storefront platform cannot. The counterexample is real, and it is a
different business model. Both halves belong in the row.

Also of note: Etsy agreed on 2026-02-15 to sell Depop to eBay for $1.2 billion cash
(disclosed in this 10-K as a subsequent event); Reverb was sold 2025-06-02. Etsy is
shrinking to the core marketplace.

**LIMIT, STATED PLAINLY:** Block stopped publishing the numerator in FY2025. The
FY2025 10-K reports Commerce enablement revenue of $11,514,162K for the whole
company and $7,425,962K for the Square segment, which bundles processing, hardware
and subscription and is not a take-rate numerator. A FY2025 Square take rate cannot
be computed from the filing and is **not estimated here**. Total GPV kept growing
(+7.8%) while Square segment revenue grew +10.0%, so the direction in FY2025 is not
determinable from filed data.

Definition-change note: Block widened the Square GPV definition between FY2023 and
FY2024, from "all card payments processed by sellers using Square, net of refunds,
and ACH transfers" to "all card and bank payments processed by sellers using Square,
net of refunds", and the FY2025 total-GPV definition adds "GPV does not include
transactions related to our BNPL products." A widening denominator biases the take
rate DOWN. The bias is small relative to the move but it is real and is recorded
here rather than buried.

## A.3 WHAT THE TWO SERIES SAY TOGETHER

Two incumbents, two independent volume bases, two different products, same
direction. PayPal down 46 bp in five years; Block down 19 bp in four. Neither
company reversed in any year of the series. Both explain it in the filings as
merchant mix and larger-seller mix, which is the structural version of the argument:
as merchants get bigger they negotiate the rate down, and the platform cannot refuse
because processing is substitutable.

**The comparator fact for the Shopify run:** Shopify's merchant solutions revenue is
approximately 76% of the total and rests on the same mechanic. Two of its largest
comparators have been losing take rate every reported year, and one of them stopped
publishing the metric. The row does not establish that Shopify's take rate is
falling. It establishes that the two firms in the same business that do disclose the
rate have not held it.

**On SBC/OCF, Shopify sits in the middle and on the good side of the middle.** At

  Wix and Block hand roughly twice as much of their operating cash to employees.

capex is low in part because it rides on other people's rails.

against $139.5B of operating cash. BigCommerce's is negative because $23.6M of stock

One is a choice; the other is a condition.

# THE FOUR FACTS THIS ROW ESTABLISHES

**1. THE TAKE RATE IS FALLING AT THE INCUMBENTS, ON FILED DATA, IN EVERY SERIES THAT
EXISTS.** PayPal -21.8% over five years with no up year. Block -6.4% over four years
with no up year. Adyen -31.1% over four years, then one up year driven by a shift
into physical POS against a 5% fall in online volume. This is the most important
comparator fact in the run and it is not a single-company artifact.

**2. THE MARGIN CONVERGENCE IS ALREADY VISIBLE.** Shopify gross margin 48.1%. Block's
Square segment gross margin 46.6%. PayPal's derived transaction margin 46.6%. Three
companies of $11.6B, $8.5B (segment) and $33.2B of revenue in the same 1.5-point
band. The pure-software comparators that do NOT run payments sit 20 to 40 points
higher: BigCommerce 78.7%, Squarespace 79.5% (FY2023), Adobe 89%, Etsy 71.6%,
Wix 68.1%. **Wix's own payments leg (Business Solutions) runs at 31.6% gross margin
against its subscription leg's 83.2%.** The 48.1% is what payments does to a software
gross margin, and the filings show it happening inside a single company.

**3. ONE FILER CLASSIFIES SHOPIFY AS A MERCHANT ACQUIRER, NOT AS SOFTWARE.** Global
Payments' FY2025 10-K lists "Shopify Inc." between "Stripe, Inc." and "Block Inc." in
its United States merchant-acquiring competitor set, alongside Fiserv, Chase
Paymentech, Elavon and two bank merchant-services arms. That is a filed statement by
a competitor about what category Shopify is in, and the category has 8% capex to
revenue, flat revenue and $21.6B of debt.

**4. THE LARGEST COMPETITOR CANNOT BE MEASURED.** Stripe files nothing. Amazon's
third-party seller services line alone is $172.2B, roughly 15x Shopify's entire
revenue, growing 10.3%, and Amazon does not name Shopify or anyone else. The two
largest competitive threats in this row are respectively **unmeasurable** and
**unnamed by their own disclosure.** Any moat conclusion has to survive that.

# METHOD NOTE

  used and stated.
- Customer float is excluded from every net-cash figure and the exclusion is named
  (PayPal funds receivable/payable, Block customer funds/customers payable, Etsy and

- No aggregator was used for any figure in this file. No live share price was needed

- Where a filer publishes no gross profit line, none is invented; a derived figure is
