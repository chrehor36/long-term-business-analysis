**[E3-03] criterion by criterion.**
- **(1) needed or desired: passes.** Every organisation with a network buys switching, routing and security; revenue $63,325M from *"businesses of all sizes, public institutions, governments, and service providers"*.
- **(2) no close substitute: fails, in the filer's own words and in the market's own record.** Twenty-five consecutive 10-Ks say barriers to entry *"are relatively low"* and that new ventures to compete *"are regularly formed"*; price is a principal ground of competition in 1995, 1998 and 2026; price-focused Chinese competition is filed for twenty-two years, white-box hardware for thirteen, and customers able to design their own for the first time in FY2026. **The substitute exists and has been bought at scale**: Arista, which files that the market *"historically"* belonged to Cisco, grew from $193M of revenue (2012) to $9,006M (2025), 25.9% a year over 2016-2025, at the same gross margin as Cisco (63.5% against 63.4%) and a higher operating margin (31.6% against 25.3%), while Cisco's revenue grew 1.6% a year with $46.0bn of acquisition cash inside the window (below).
- **(3) not subject to price regulation: passes.** No filing read describes a rate regime; government customers buy through procurement, not tariff.

### THE STRONGEST EVIDENCE AGAINST THIS VERDICT, stated as its holders would state it [E4-51, E4-26, E3-47]
1. *"A 60-70% gross margin held for thirty-three years through three complete turnovers of the competitor list is the demonstration [E3-03] asks for. Commodity businesses do not do that. Lucent, Nortel, 3Com, Cabletron, Fore, Foundry, Redback, Sycamore and Juniper are gone or absorbed and Cisco is still here at the same margin; that is a maintained lead, which the ruling says does not fail [E4-04]."* **Answer:** this is the best point for IN and it is true as stated: Cisco is the survivor of every generation, and the ruling's maintained-lead distinction is the right frame. But the demonstration clause asks for a margin held by *pricing*, and the filer's own bridge says the margin was held by cost: price took 1.4-3.1 points a year out of product margin in every quantified year and productivity put it back. A margin level that the whole product class earns (Arista 63.5%, Juniper 58.8%, Extreme 56.0%) is the economics of designing networking hardware and software, not evidence that Cisco's customers see no close substitute. Survival across generations is a finding about the company's competence and scale, which is Munger's surfer: *"when a surfer gets up and catches the wave and just stays there, he can go a long, long time"* **[E3-51]**; the framework's own gloss: *"A surfing run is not a moat; the advantage lives in the wave, not the surfer."*
2. *"The rivals themselves say Cisco dominates. Arista, Extreme and Juniper all put it in writing. Dominance is [E2-53]'s strongest class of franchise: good or bad, it will prosper."* **Answer:** [E2-53]'s dominance is the one-newspaper town, where the position sets the price regardless of the product. Here the dominant firm cuts price every year, calls barriers to entry low in its own 10-K, and has watched one attacker build a $9bn business inside its core at the same gross margin. The rivals' word is *"historically"*.
3. *"The moat is the installed base and the switching cost of an enterprise network trained on Cisco's software, and the services annuity proves it: $15bn a year at a 68.8% gross margin, $46.7bn of remaining performance obligations."* **Answer:** the services stream is real and is the part of the business closest to a franchise. The filer gives no retention, renewal or attach rate for it, and services revenue was flat at $14,550M, $15,046M and $15,030M in FY2024-FY2026 while product revenue rose 23%; a switching cost that the filer does not measure and that did not stop Arista's entry into Cisco's customer base cannot carry criterion (2) against the filer's own *"Barriers to entry are relatively low"*. If the services stream were separately disclosed with a retention series, it would be the document that could re-open this gate (reversal condition below).
4. *"The corpus applies [E4-04] to technology as a competence limit, so the honest close is UNKNOWABLE, not OUT; an OUT is a finding about the business the analyst cannot make in a fast-changing industry [E4-57, E4-58]."* **Answer:** the ruling of 2026-09-20 closes UNKNOWABLE a name *"that passes [E3-03] and whose durability cannot be judged from filings"*. Cisco does not pass [E3-03]: the failure is in criterion (2) and the demonstration clause, read in the filer's own annual reports and margin bridges, not in a forecast of technology. The ruling says the nineteen Q2 OUTs that cite [E4-04] *alongside* an independent criterion-2 failure do not move; this is that class. **No verdict here rests on [E4-04].**
5. *"FY2026 changed the picture: revenue +12%, product +16%, networking +22%, operating margin 24.3%, hyperscaler AI orders from less than 2% to about 6% of revenue, and inventory up $2.5bn on Silicon One demand. Direction outranks level [E4-32]."* **Answer:** one year, concentrated in the customers the filer says can *"design their own networking equipment, semiconductors and software, or to purchase directly from contract manufacturers"*, with the price following a memory-cost rise; [E3-43]'s supply-tight clause and its *"usually does not last long"* apply, as they did to FY2022-FY2023. It is recorded as the thing the reversal condition watches, not as a demonstration.

None of the five turns the filer's own twenty-five annual reports or its own margin bridges. Reading (b), OUT on the business, survives its own hardest test. **The competence reading (UNKNOWABLE on [E4-04]) was the second-best answer and is recorded as such**: had criterion (2) not failed in the filer's own words, the durability question would have closed the file without prejudice.
