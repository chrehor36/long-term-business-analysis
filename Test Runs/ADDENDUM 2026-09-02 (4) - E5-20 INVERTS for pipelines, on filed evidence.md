# ADDENDUM — 2026-09-02 (fourth)
## [E5-20] inverts for pipelines, and the evidence is filed rather than argued

**This is a Q5 finding about (c) that applies to any midstream name, so it is written where
a run will find it rather than buried in the Loews file.** It does not change a ledger row,
and it must not be read as changing one — **PRIME RULE 2: the text wins; audit the framework,
do not defend it.** What follows audits how the framework's *class boundary* has been applied.

---

## WHAT THE FRAMEWORK SAYS, AND WHY IT SAYS IT

**[E5-20]** governs the capital-intensive class: for it, **the D&A end of (c) is INVALID, not
merely optimistic.** The reasoning is that a business whose plant genuinely wears out and must
be replaced at today's prices consumes more capital than yesterday's depreciation charge
records.

The VZ run of 2026-09-01 is the case that made this operational, and it is exact: **Verizon's
$157.0bn of wireless licences are indefinite-lived and are not amortised**, so its
depreciation charge contains **nothing** for the asset it spent $58,395M acquiring over five
years. The screen's 9.30% became 6.67% once spectrum was charged at its run-rate. **The D&A
end was not conservative there. It was empty.**

Since then the class has been applied by analogy — railroads, airlines, telecoms — and the
analogy has been doing work that the filings should be doing.

---

## WHAT THE LOEWS RUN FOUND, FROM THE FILER'S OWN DISCLOSURE

**Boardwalk Pipelines discloses maintenance capital directly** — it does not have to be
inferred:

| | |
|---|---:|
| **maintenance capital, disclosed** | **$194M** |
| depreciation and amortisation | $443M |
| **ratio** | **0.44×** |

**The competitor row confirms it across every midstream filer that shows its work: 0.15× to
0.48×.** Williams and ONEOK **disclose no maintenance figure at all**, which is itself an
[E4-55]-shaped finding about the sector's disclosure.

**So for pipelines, D&A OVERSTATES maintenance capital by more than two times — the exact
opposite of the direction [E5-20] warns about.** A run that applied [E5-20] to Boardwalk by
analogy with railroads would have charged $443M where the filer says $194M is required, and
would have been wrong by $249M a year in the *pessimistic* direction.

---

## WHY THE ANALOGY FAILED, STATED AS A RULE FOR THE NEXT RUN

**A pipeline is not a locomotive.** The steel in the ground is inspected, coated and
cathodically protected rather than replaced; the depreciable life the accountant assigns is
shorter than the physical life of the asset, so the charge runs ahead of the spending.
Verizon's spectrum is the inverse case — an asset that is **not depreciated at all** while
being genuinely consumed and repurchased.

**The rule that survives both cases is the one the framework already has, and it is not an
analogy: [E3-44]'s direction question must be settled FROM THE FILING, per name.** Ask which
end of the (c) band is the conservative one, and answer it with the filer's own numbers.
Where a filer discloses maintenance capital, **that disclosure outranks any class rule** —
including this project's own.

**What [E5-20] retains, undisturbed:** where the capex line is the only construction
available and the asset is genuinely consumed, the D&A end is invalid. Verizon is still
Verizon. **What it does not license is applying that verdict to a sector because the sector
sounds capital-intensive.**

---

## THE OTHER DIRECTION THIS CUTS, AND IT IS THE UNCOMFORTABLE ONE

**This project has already ruled on (c) for eight names by settling the [E3-44] direction from
filings, and this addendum does not disturb any of them** — DG measured the new-store split
because DG publishes it; Kroger's estate shrank so its D&A end was invalid; ULTA's fleet is
leased and renews through cash rent; QCOM is fabless and its real maintenance is R&D above the
OCF line; Nike owns no factories. **In every one of those the filing decided, not the class.**

**The risk this addendum names is the one running the other way: that a class rule becomes a
shortcut precisely when a run is tired or a name is unfamiliar.** [E5-20] is the most
quotable rule in the (c) machinery and therefore the most likely to be reached for. **A run
that cites it without a filed number has not applied the framework — it has cited it.**

---

## WORK ORDER

Any midstream, pipeline or storage name reaching Q5 in this queue must:
1. **Search the filing for a disclosed maintenance-capital figure first.** Boardwalk, Plains,
   DT Midstream and USA Compression are in the priced universe; several disclose it.
2. Where none is disclosed, **say so as a finding** — Williams and ONEOK do not — and build
   (c) from the physical asset record rather than from the class.
3. **Report the ratio of disclosed maintenance capital to D&A**, so the next run inherits a
   number rather than an analogy.
