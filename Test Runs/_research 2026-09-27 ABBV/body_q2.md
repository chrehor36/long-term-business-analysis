**The precedent, stated so it can be tested rather than copied.** The MRK run of 2026-09-26 closed Merck at Q2, OUT on the business, on [E3-03] criterion (3) in the filer's words and on [E4-04]'s scope test (the spending buys replacements), and recorded UNKNOWABLE as the nearest alternative reading. AbbVie differs from Merck in three ways the filings show, and each is tested below rather than assumed away: (i) **AbbVie has already been through the expiry of the product that carried it**, and its sales held (Humira, 61-65% of revenue in 2014-2017, US exclusivity lost 2023-01-31; total net revenues $58,054M in 2022 and $61,160M in 2025), which is the analogue of the first reversal condition the MRK file wrote for Merck; (ii) **the replacement came in the same diseases through the same commercial position**, which bears on whether the lead was maintained or rebuilt; (iii) **about 8% of sales are cash-pay aesthetics**, outside government price setting.

**The hypotheses I formed while reading, both stated so they can be attacked [E4-26].** *(a) AbbVie is the one research-based maker in the row that has shown, on filed figures, that its immunology position outlives the molecule: Humira was extended six years past its compound patent by later patents and settlements and priced up in the US throughout (2015 and 2022, "favorable pricing"); when it finally fell, Skyrizi and Rinvoq, sold to the same prescribers for the same diseases, grew from $7,687M (2022) to $25,866M (2025) and total sales passed the pre-expiry peak within three years; the machine is the franchise, and it is maintained, not rebuilt.* *(b) Every one of those profits rests on an exclusivity the law grants for a fixed term; the filer dates the next one (the Skyrizi and Rinvoq compound patents 2033); the government now sets the Medicare price of four named AbbVie products carrying 18% of 2025 sales, with a measured fall on the first; the replacements were bought (Skyrizi's molecule from Boehringer Ingelheim in 2016, Imbruvica with Pharmacyclics, Botox and Vraylar with Allergan); and the 2023-2025 record is Humira's collapse from $21,237M to $4,540M, the thing a franchise does not do.* The file does not get to pick. Each is tested on the filings below.

**THE STRONGEST EVIDENCE AGAINST THE VERDICT BELOW, hunted hardest [E4-26, E3-41, E4-51].** The verdict below is OUT, so the case for IN is set out first, as its holders would state it:
1. **AbbVie has done what a franchise-less drug maker is not supposed to be able to do**: it lost the best-selling medicine in the world (Humira, $21.2bn in 2022) and within three years sold more in total ($61.2bn against $58.1bn) and more in the same diseases ($30.4bn against $28.9bn), with no purchase in the intervening years supplying the difference (the Allergan revenue was already in the 2022 base; ImmunoGen's Elahere added $690M in 2025). That is exactly the first reversal condition the MRK file wrote for Merck, met by AbbVie on its own expiry.
2. **The lead was defended, not merely replaced**: Humira's US market was held six years past its compound patent (December 2016 to January 2023) by later patents and settlements, and Rinvoq's generics are held to April 2037 by settlement, about four years past its 2033 compound patent. [E5-23]'s *"defending your own moat all of the time"* describes this; the scope paragraph says [E4-04] does not exclude defended moats.
3. **The price regulation touches the tail, not the head**: the four set prices carry 18% of 2025 sales; the two products carrying 42% are not selected, and aesthetics (8%) is cash-pay.
4. **The returns on the capital the business needs are extreme** (NTOA returns in the hundreds of percent), and the protected products priced up in the US for a decade (Humira).
5. **The corpus's own words on the industry** (the 2002 and 2008 meeting passages the MRK run quoted, *"as an industry, a very, very good business over time"*; not ledger rows) cut against a company-level OUT.

**Answered.** (1) is the strongest and is accepted as fact: AbbVie's position in immunology survived its expiry, and this file records it as the best filed evidence in the row for a maintained lead. It answers [E4-04]'s rebuild limb, not criterion (3), and it was bought partly with a molecule purchased in 2016 whose royalty still costs $2.9bn a year in operating cash; the new position has a filed term (2033, 2037 by settlement), and its price, by the statute's own clock as the filer describes it, becomes eligible for government setting in 2028 (Rinvoq) and 2032 (Skyrizi). (2) Accepted as evidence that AbbVie defends each term hard; the defence moved Humira's end by six years and did not remove it (US Humira −84% from 2022 to 2025). (3) is the argument the verdict weighs most, and it is answered on the filer's own words: the regulation is not confined to the four named products, because the inflation rebate and the Part D manufacturer discount apply to every Medicare Part D product and the Medicaid, 340B, Japanese and European regimes apply to the leaders too; the filer itself says further selections *"could, among other things, accelerate revenue erosion prior to expiration of intellectual property protections"*; and in January 2026 the company granted *"pricing concessions"* across its US business in exchange for three years' exemption from *"future pricing mandates"*, which is a price set with the government for the whole portfolio. [E3-03]'s third criterion is a condition on the product's price, and on AbbVie's filings the price of the medicines that are 92% of sales is administered in part by the buyer. (4) Recorded; [E3-43] classes exceptional profits on supply made tight for a term as *"a business"*. (5) Weighed, not waved away: the same passages prescribe a basket (OPEN QUESTION 4), not a single-company franchise finding, and are the corpus's own description of a competence limit; the MRK file recorded them for a ruling and this file adds AbbVie to that request. **[E3-47]** (a wrongly closed file inside the circle of competence is the costliest error) was weighed against OUT in writing, and it is heavier here than for Merck because of (1); it is why the reversal conditions below name the filed documents that would reopen the file.
