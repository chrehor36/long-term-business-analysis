# Company Run — Essex Property Trust (ESS) — 2026-07-15
Framework v3.0. From the REIT mechanical screen. OE from real FY2025 10-K
data (Archives/edgar/data/920522).

## STEP 0 — RATE REFRESH
US 30-yr **5.10%**. Mkt cap $19.45B — no size premium.

## GATE 1 — CIRCLE OF COMPETENCE
Owns and operates apartment communities concentrated almost entirely on the
West Coast tech-hub markets — Southern California, Northern California, and
Seattle Metro. Same rent × occupancy − opex − capex economics as the other
apartment REITs run so far, but with the narrowest, most concentrated
geographic footprint of the batch (essentially a single-region thesis).
**VERDICT: PASS**, with the geographic concentration noted as a real
difference from AVB/EQR's broader coastal spread.

## GATE 2 — MOAT
Franchise test [E3-03]: needed/desired — yes; no close substitute — **weak**
(same commodity dynamic); unregulated — **fails more than any peer so far**:
California carries the heaviest rent-control/tenant-protection regime of any
state in this batch, and ESS's concentration there means this exposure is
not diversified away the way it is for AVB/EQR. **Class: NARROW**, location
scarcity (California/Seattle housing supply constraints are real and severe)
offset by the heaviest regulatory-risk concentration in the batch.
**VERDICT: PASS with NARROW classification**, regulatory concentration
flagged as a material, not cosmetic, risk carried into Gate 5.

## GATE 3 — MANAGEMENT

**VERDICT: PASS**, succession-verification limitation flagged consistently

## GATE 4 — FINANCIAL QUALITY (full OE formula, real maintenance capex)
ESS discloses the cleanest capex split of any REIT run so far — "Capital
expenditures on rental properties" is reported as its own line, separate
from acquisitions, redevelopment, AND development spend:

| FY | Net income (common) | Depreciation & amort. | Capex on rental properties | **OE = NI+D&A−capex** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2023 | $405.825M | $548.438M | $140.371M | **$813.892M** |
| 2024 | $741.522M | $580.220M | $136.395M | **$1,185.347M** |
| 2025 | $669.666M | $607.542M | $140.348M | **$1,136.860M** |

FY2023's low NI is largely explained by a much smaller one-time "gains not
included in FFO" figure that year ($59.2M vs. $386.1M in 2024 and $305.0M
in 2025) — i.e., a lighter year for property-sale gains, not necessarily an
operating trough. **Per the range-anchoring convention (P47), the lowest
VERIFIED tier is used regardless of the reason for the dip.**
**LOWEST VERIFIED TIER: $813.892M (FY2023).**
Fortress (financial/leverage-ceiling track): Total assets $13,159.009M ÷
Total equity $5,708.632M = **2.305:1** — far under the 10:1 ceiling.
**VERDICT: PASS.**

## GATE 5 — INVERSION
1. **Moat destruction** — California/Seattle-specific regulatory escalation
   (further rent-control tightening) is the single biggest, most concentrated
   risk in this batch — not diversified across other geographies at all.
   Scored **[P]**, the most exposed reading of any REIT run so far.
2. **Management/key-person** — orderly internal promotion, longest company
   history in the batch. Scored **[D]**.
3. **Balance-sheet stress** — 2.31:1 assets/equity, comfortably under
   ceiling. Scored **[D]**.
4. **Regional concentration** (tech-sector layoffs/relocation trends
   affecting Bay Area/Seattle specifically) — a real, California/Seattle-
   specific demand risk not present for the more geographically diversified
   peers. Scored **[P]**.

No [U], but **two [P] scores tied specifically to geographic concentration**
— a distinct risk profile from the broader-footprint REITs (AVB/EQR).
**Thesis-breaking metric:** adverse California/Washington rent-control
legislation materially tightening statewide caps, or a sustained Bay
Area/Seattle tech-employment contraction.
**VERDICT: PASS**, geographic concentration flagged as the central risk to
watch, more so than for any other REIT in this batch.
