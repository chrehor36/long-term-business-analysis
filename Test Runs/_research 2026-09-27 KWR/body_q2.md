## Q2 — IS IT A FRANCHISE? **[E3-03]**

*Every Quaker figure below is read from the filed 10-Ks for 2014-2025 and the 10-Q to 2026-06-30 (accessions in Step 0), or from SEC companyfacts checked against those statements. The price, volume, currency and acquisition parts of each year's sales change are the filer's own MD&A sentences, parsed from all twelve 10-Ks by `pv.py` and read in context (quoted below). Peer figures are each peer's own SEC companyfacts run through the MDT run's `row.py` unchanged (`peers/<TICKER>_row.txt`); Fuchs, the one direct rival that publishes in English outside EDGAR, is taken from its own release (evidence ladder rung 3, company IR, flagged).*

**The precedent, stated so it can be tested rather than copied.** The FUL run of 2026-09-26 closed H.B. Fuller, another formulator of petrochemical inputs sold into customers' processes, at Q2, OUT on the business: criterion (2) failed in the filer's words, and over twenty-one years its price followed raw materials both ways while volume fell. Quaker differs in ways that could matter, and each is tested below rather than assumed away: (i) **it claims *"the leading global position in the market for industrial process fluids"***, where Fuller claimed a pure-play title; (ii) **its staff work on the customer's floor** under Fluidcare contracts, which could make switching costly; (iii) **Legacy Quaker earned 23-28% pre-tax on its tangible operating assets** in 2014-2018, a higher figure than Fuller's.

**The hypotheses I formed while reading, both stated so they can be attacked [E4-26].** *(a) Quaker is the largest maker of fluids that are a small, critical input to steel, aluminum and parts plants; its people sit inside the customer's process, its formulas are qualified on the customer's lines, it wins new business every year in every segment, its customers are spread thin (largest 3%), and its gross margin came back from 31.5% to 36-37% within two years of the worst raw-material inflation in decades: a quiet franchise.* *(b) Quaker sells a formulated commodity whose price follows its raw materials both ways by contract and by competition; the filer says that competitors' pricing can force it to cut, that failing to cut in time loses share, and that its own price rises of 2022-2023 cost it volume; its organic volume fell about 15% in 2022-2025; and on the $1.66bn it paid for Houghton it has earned a single-digit pre-tax return, writing off the whole EMEA goodwill in two impairments.* The file does not get to pick. Each is tested on the filings below.

**[E3-03], criterion by criterion.** *"An economic franchise arises from a product or service that: (1) is needed or desired; (2) is thought by its customers to have no close substitute and; (3) is not subject to price regulation."*
- **(1) Needed or desired [x]: passes.** Metal cannot be rolled, cut, ground, formed or quenched at production speed without process fluids; net sales $1,888.6M in 2025 and $1,013.0M in H1 2026.
- **(3) Not subject to price regulation [x]: passes.** No government sets or bids the price. The *"index-based customer contracts"* that move the price are private contracts, not regulation, and are read under criterion (2) and the demonstration.
- **(2) Thought by its customers to have no close substitute: NOT SHOWN, and contradicted by the filer's own words in every 10-K read.** Item 1, *Competition*: *"Competition in the industry is based primarily on the ability to supply products and provide technical services that meet the needs of the customer at an appropriate price and value to both the Company and the customer"*; *"All competitors provide different levels of technical services to individual customers"* (so the on-site service is offered by rivals too). Item 1A (10-K 2025): *"Some competitors may be able to offer more favorable or flexible pricing and service terms ... potentially resulting in reduced profitability and/or a loss of market share for us"*; *"The pricing decisions of our competitors could affect demand for our offerings, and could lead us to decrease our prices, which could negatively affect our margins and profitability"*; *"Our competitors may develop new products or enhancements to their products that offer better performance, features that render our products less competitive or obsolete, or lower prices, any of which may cause us to lose business and/or significant market share"*; *"it is possible that competitors or other unauthorized third parties may obtain, copy, use, disclose or replicate our formulae, products, and processes"*; *"If we are unsuccessful with differentiating ourselves ... we could lose market share to our competitors."* And the sentence that describes a commodity seller exactly, in the 10-Ks for 2014 and 2018: *"Inability to obtain sufficient price increases or contract concessions to offset increases in the costs of raw material could result in a loss of sales and/or market share"* and **"Conversely, an inability to implement timely price decreases to compensate for changes in raw material costs could result in a loss of sales and/or market share"**: a supplier that must cut its price when its costs fall, or lose the customer, is one whose customers have a close substitute. **A limit, stated**: risk-factor language is partly industry boilerplate (the FUL run found near-identical sentences in Fuller's 10-K), so its weight rests where the price and volume record confirms it, which is below.

- **What the column shows.** **The price follows the raw materials, both ways, and the margin stays in its band.** In 2014-2016, with oil falling, price fell 1% a year; in 2021-2023 it rose about 40% compounded (+8, +22, +7) *"to offset"* raw-material costs; in 2024-2025 it fell 4% and 2% as the *"index-based customer contracts"* passed falling costs back to customers. Gross margin sat between 34.6% and 37.6% in every year but two, and the two years outside the band (33.8% in 2021 and 31.5% in 2022) are the years costs rose faster than price, which is the lag the filer names (*"may be unable to do so (or may be delayed in doing so)"*; the 2022 goodwill impairment cites *"the ongoing lag in margin recovery"*). **The price rises restored the margin; they did not widen it**: 36.2% in 2020, 36.0% in 2025. That is pass-through, the reverse of [E3-43]'s *"regularly price ... aggressively"*.
- **[E2-44]'s first characteristic** (*"an ability to increase prices rather easily (even when product demand is flat and capacity is not fully utilized) without fear of significant loss of either market share or unit volume"*) **is contradicted in the filer's own words**: the 2023 volume decline of 7% is attributed in part to *"the Company’s value-based pricing initiatives"*, a phrase that recurs in the segment paragraphs for EMEA, Asia/Pacific and the Americas. The price rises cost units.
- **[E4-55], units.** Organic volume −7% (2022), −7% (2023), −2% (2024), 0% (2025): **about 15% fewer units in 2025 than in 2021** (0.93 x 0.93 x 0.98 x 1.00 = 0.85), while price and mix rose about 32% compounded over 2021-2025 and dollar sales moved from $1,761M to $1,889M with $0.2bn of purchases inside them. This is Precision Steel's pattern exactly: dollar revenue held up by price while the physical series fell. The filer attributes the volume loss mostly to *"soft end market conditions"*, which is real (auto strikes, Ukraine, European industry), and partly to its own pricing and to the *"wind-down of the tolling agreement for products previously divested related to the Combination"*. The H1 2026 recovery (+5%, Q2 +7%, *"primarily a result of new business wins across all segments"*) is recorded as the strongest recent fact against.
- **[E4-37], the agony metric: on file.** Three years of price rises that cost volume, then two years of contractual price give-backs, then in 2026 new *"pricing actions taken to offset higher raw material costs"* as oil moved again with *"the escalation of the military conflict involving Iran"* (10-Q). The price is set by the cost of oil, with a lag, and argued customer by customer.
- **Returns (`peers/KWR_row.txt`; operating profit as filed).** **Legacy Quaker** (2014-2018): operating margin 7.7-11.4%; pre-tax return on average net tangible operating assets (NTOA) 19.4-27.7%; **including goodwill and intangibles 13.1-18.7%**. **Quaker Houghton** (2020-2025): operating margin 4.2%, 8.5%, 2.7%, 11.0%, 10.6%, 2.8% as filed (the 2022 and 2025 figures after the $93.0M and $88.8M EMEA goodwill impairments; 7.5% in 2022 and 7.5% in 2025 before them); return on NTOA 9.3%, 22.2%, 6.8% (18.8% before impairment), 27.6%, 26.9%, 6.7% (17.9% before impairment); **including goodwill and intangibles 2.5%, 6.4%, 2.2%, 9.7%, 9.3%, 2.5% as filed, and no better than 9.7% in any year even before the impairments** (2022 about 6.2%, 2025 about 6.6% before them). Goodwill and intangibles are 61-73% of operating capital since the Combination; tangible equity at 2026-06-30 is about $44M. The filer's own measure of the Houghton purchase: *"a pre-tax, non-cash impairment charge of $88.8 million ($86.7 million after-tax) to write down the remaining carrying value amount of the EMEA reporting unit Goodwill"* (2025), after $93.0M in 2022. On [E2-43]'s guide (*"What a business can be expected to earn on unleveraged net tangible assets"*) the operation earns a good formulator's return; on the capital including the $1.66bn paid for Houghton it earns single digits.
- **The non-GAAP margin the company reports** (*"Non-GAAP operating margin"* 11.7%, 11.6%, 10.6% in 2023-2025; adjusted EBITDA margin 16.4%, 16.9%, 15.8%) **is falling**, before any question of what it excludes (Q3's matter).

**THE STRONGEST EVIDENCE AGAINST THE VERDICT BELOW, hunted hardest [E4-26, E3-41, E4-51].** The verdict below is OUT, so the case for IN is set out first, as its holders would state it:
1. **It is the world leader in its niche**, by its own estimate, *"including leading global positions in the markets for process fluids in portions of the automotive and industrial markets, and a leading position in the market for process fluids to produce sheet steel and aluminum."*
2. **It wins business every year in every segment** (the phrase *"new business wins"* stands in every year's volume paragraph, 2022-2026), and in H1 2026 organic volume rose 5% (Q2: 7%, *"in all segments"*).
3. **Its staff work inside the customer's plant** and its formulas are qualified on the customer's lines; switching a rolling lubricant in a mill is a production risk the customer will not take lightly, and the fluid is a small part of what the mill spends.
4. **The margin came back**: from 31.5% (2022) to 36.1-37.3% (2023-2025), the price rises of 2021-2023 held long enough to rebuild it; a commodity seller in over-capacity would not have recovered.
5. **Legacy Quaker earned 13-19% pre-tax on all its capital and 19-28% on tangible assets** in 2014-2018, a record that says the business is good and the Houghton price was the problem.

**Answered.** (1) Accepted as the filer's estimate; it is a claim of share, not of price, and [E2-53]'s test is whether position sets the economics, which the price column answers no. (2) Accepted as fact, and weighed most: new wins are real; they came in the same years that organic volume fell 15% overall and the filer attributed part of the fall to its own pricing, so they measure a salesforce winning plants, not customers who think there is no substitute; H1 2026 is two quarters and is carried to the reversal conditions. (3) Accepted as the strongest structural argument, and it is the one the filer's own words refute: *"All competitors provide different levels of technical services"*, and *"an inability to implement timely price decreases to compensate for changes in raw material costs could result in a loss of sales and/or market share"*: a supplier whose embedded position forced no price concession would not write that sentence. **The "small part of the customer's cost" argument is not in the filings**; it is recorded as the holders' argument, unsourced here, and not relied on. (4) Accepted, and it is what pass-through looks like: the margin returned to its band, it did not rise above it, and the price then fell back through the index contracts; the FUL file recorded the same two-year trough and recovery. (5) Accepted as fact and the heaviest point for [E3-47] (*"our most egregious mistakes fall in the omission"*): Legacy Quaker's returns were good. But the same Legacy Quaker record shows the same price pattern (−1% a year in 2014-2016 as oil fell, +2% in 2017-2018 as it rose) and the same risk-factor sentence about timely price decreases, so its returns were earned by operating well inside a pass-through business, which [E3-43] classes as *"a business"* that *"can be killed by poor management"*; and the business on offer at this price is the combined one, whose return on the capital including what was paid has not exceeded 9.7% in six years.
