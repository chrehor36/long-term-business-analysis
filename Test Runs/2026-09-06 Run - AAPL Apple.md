## Q1 — CAN I UNDERSTAND HOW THIS MAKES MONEY? **[E3-31]**

**Unit economics in my own words, no management language.**
Apple sells a pocket computer at a price four to six times its bill of materials, and it
sells it to the same person again about every three to four years. It does not make the
computer: outsourced partners assemble it, and Apple's own risk factors describe capital
assets it owns *sitting in other companies' factories*. What Apple owns is the design, the
silicon, the operating system, the identity the customer signed into, and the retail and
payment relationship. Roughly 74% of revenue is that hardware sale and its accessories,
carried at a **36.8% gross margin** (FY2025, filed).

The second business is different in kind and the run has to judge it separately **[E5-37]**.
Once the customer is inside the identity — photos, messages, purchased apps, payment
credentials, a watch and earbuds that only pair properly with the phone — Apple rents them
services: the App Store's commission on other people's software, iCloud storage,
subscriptions, advertising, AppleCare, payment fees, and a fee from a search engine for
being the default. That business did **$109,158M in FY2025 at a 75.4% gross margin**. It
consumes almost no incremental capital: the marginal App Store transaction costs Apple
essentially nothing.

The arithmetic that follows from those two margins is the whole company. FY2025 gross
profit was **$195,201M**, of which **Products contributed $112,887M and Services
$82,314M**. So Services is **26.2% of revenue and 42.2% of gross profit** — and the share
has gone 35.7% → 39.3% → 42.2% in three years. Apple is a hardware company by revenue and
is becoming a toll-collector by profit. **This is the HAS/EFX profit-mix question and it
is answered in the filings, in a table Apple prints itself.**

**The scarce input this business controls.** Not manufacturing — that is rented, and the
lease note says so ($16.1bn of *variable* lease payments in FY2025, "primarily based on
purchases of output of the underlying leased assets"). Not components — those are bought.
The scarce input is **the installed base of signed-in, payment-credentialed users and the
software distribution monopoly over them**. Every dollar of the 75%-margin business is a
toll on access to people Apple already has. That is the asset, and it is the asset the
regulators have arrived for.

**Will the fundamentals look broadly the same in ten years?** The *mechanism* will:
people will still carry a personal computing device, still store their identity in it,
and someone will still collect a toll on the software that runs on it. Two things are
genuinely uncertain and they are named here rather than smoothed: **who sets the toll rate**
(Q2 criterion 3, and it is live litigation, not speculation), and whether the search-default
fee — a single counterparty paying near-pure margin — survives the *US v. Google* remedies.
Those are Q2 and Q4 questions, not Q1 questions. The business model itself is legible in an
afternoon from one 10-K, which is the [E3-31] test: *"relatively simple and stable in
character."*

**The [E4-46] check** — *"if we can't make a decision in five minutes, we can't make it in
five months."* Apple is not a business that requires months of study to understand. It
requires reading one revenue table and one gross-margin table. It passes on legibility.

**THE ANSWER TO THE PROFIT-MIX TEST: Services is 26.2% of revenue and 42.2% of gross
profit, and its share has risen 23.7% → 42.2% in seven years — about 2.6 points a year.**
Allocating operating expense either by revenue or by gross profit puts Services at
**roughly half of Apple's operating income on a quarter of its sales**. The tail is now
half the dog, and it is the half every regulator in the file is aiming at.

**And the second reading of the same table, which matters more:** Products gross margin has
been **flat in the mid-30s for eight years** (34.4% → 36.8%). **Every point of Apple's
consolidated gross-margin expansion — 38.3% in FY2018 to 46.9% in FY2025 to 49.1% in 9M
FY2026 — is mix shift into Services.** There is no hardware margin story. That is the
[E4-44] fact the Q5 growth case has to live with.

### The three criteria [E3-03], run separately

**PRODUCTS — (1) YES · (2) contested · (3) YES, not price-regulated.**
Criterion 2 is the weak one and Apple's own Item 1 concedes it: the FY2025 10-K describes
markets that are *"highly competitive"* and names a long list of competitors, including
firms that ship far more phones than Apple does. At the device level Android is a close
substitute. What is *not* substitutable is the **accumulated switching cost** — the photo
library, the message history, the purchased-app ledger, the paired watch and earbuds, the
payment credential. That is real and it is why Products gross margin held in the mid-30s
through a decade in which the smartphone became a commodity. **NARROW, not WIDE.**

**SERVICES — (1) YES · (2) YES, and emphatically · (3) NO — AND THIS IS THE FINDING.**
Criterion 2 is *stronger* here than anywhere in Apple: there is no substitute at all for
the App Store as a way of reaching an iOS user. But that is precisely the problem, and the
corpus predicted it: **[E5-28]** — *"If you name some business that has incredible pricing
power, you're talking about a business that's **a monopoly or a near monopoly**."*
**The strength of the Services moat is the cause of its regulation.**

**Criterion 3 fails on Apple's own filed words, not on my inference.** The FY2025 10-K
states that the Company *"is currently subject to a court order preventing it from imposing
any commission or fee on certain purchases."* That is price regulation **in force and
admitted**, on the highest-margin dollar the company earns. Alongside it:
- the **EU Digital Markets Act** — a €500m fine levied April 2025, a second investigation
  open, and a **statutory maximum of 10% of worldwide net sales** (≈**$41.6bn** on FY2025
  revenue);
- ***Epic v. Apple*** — the April 2025 contempt ruling barred any commission on out-of-app
  purchases; the Ninth Circuit **partially reversed 2025-12-11** (Apple "can charge some
  commission" on link-outs) and the **Supreme Court granted certiorari 2026-06-30**, but
  only on the civil-contempt standard, not the antitrust merits;
- ***DOJ v. Apple*** (filed March 2024), unchanged since filing.
- **No accrual is disclosed for any of these matters.**
