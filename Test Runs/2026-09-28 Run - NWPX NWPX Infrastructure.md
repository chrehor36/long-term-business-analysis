## Q1 — CAN I UNDERSTAND HOW THIS MAKES MONEY? **[E3-31]**
- **Unit economics in my own words, no management language:** Two businesses. **(a) Steel water pipe (66.7% of FY2025 net sales, $350.9M):** when a city or water district designs a large transmission main (24 to 156 inches across, carrying water from a source to a treatment plant or from the plant into the network), NWPX prices the pipe from the drawings, bids it to the installation contractors, and the contractor with the lowest total bid wins the job: *"We typically bid to installation contractors who include our bid in their proposals to public water agencies. A public water agency generally awards the entire project to the contractor with the lowest responsive bid."* It then buys steel coil for that contract (*"Steel is normally purchased after the steel pressure pipe orders are confirmed with an executed contract"*), spiral-welds, lines and coats each engineered piece in one of seven plants, fabricates the fittings, and trucks or barges it to the site. The price is fixed at the bid (*"WTS contracts are generally quoted on a fixed-price basis"*); steel is *"approximately 29%"* of the segment's cost of sales in FY2025 and *"approximately 32%"* in the first half of 2026; revenue is recognised as the pipe is made and billed later, so the company carries the unbilled work (the contract assets above). Backlog *"$305 million"* at 2026-06-30. **(b) Precast concrete (33.3%, $175.1M):** reinforced concrete pipe, manholes, box culverts, vaults, catch basins, and pre-assembled lift stations and separators, poured in six plants in Texas and Utah (a seventh, Pueblo, from February 2026) and sold to contractors for local sewer, storm-water and site work: *"Demand for these products is generally influenced by general economic conditions such as housing starts, commercial development, population growth, and interest rates."* FY2025: net sales **$526.0M**; gross profit **$103.6M (19.7%)** (steel pipe 19.1% of its sales, precast 20.8%); operating income **$50.9M (9.7%)**; net income $35.4M; 1,318 employees.
- **The scarce input this business controls:** **large-diameter spiral-weld and fabrication capacity sited near the water projects**, which matters because the product is heavy: *"high freight costs which may limit the ability of manufacturers located in other market areas to compete with us"*; and the engineering relationships through which it tries to get its product written into the specification (*"promotion of specifications consistent with our capabilities and products, and close contact with the project designers and owners throughout the design phase"*, *"often years in advance of a project bid date"*). In precast, local plants near the jobsite for the same freight reason. Proprietary joints (*"Permalok®"*, *"InfraShield®"*) exist; the filer says the loss of any of them *"would not have a material adverse effect"*. Whether any of this lets it set the price is Q2's question.
