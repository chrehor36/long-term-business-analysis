# DG COMPETITOR ROW — built 2026-09-02, all figures from primary SEC filings
Required by [E3-28]: *"I can't be an intelligent owner of a business unless I know what all the
other businesses in that industry are doing."* A moat is a claim about **relative** position.

**Peers taken: 5 of the 6 the industry actually has.** DG's own 10-K names its *"primary direct
competitors"* as **Walmart, Family Dollar and Dollar Tree**. This row takes all three (Family
Dollar through its former owner's filings — see the disposition below), adds **Five Below** as
the small-box specialty contrast and **BJ's** and **Costco** as the membership contrast the
operator asked for, and records **Aldi** as an evidence-ladder obstacle.

**Aldi — NOT OBTAINABLE, named with the obstacle.** Aldi Süd and Aldi Nord are privately held
German entities (Stiftungen). **No SEC registrant, no 20-F, no exchange filing at any rung of
the evidence ladder.** EDGAR full-text search for "Aldi" across the four peer 10-Ks pulled for
this row returns **zero hits** — no US registrant quantifies Aldi in a filing either. Under the
four-verdict test this specific datum is **UNKNOWABLE, not UNRESEARCHED**: no document exists
that this project's shelf can reach. Its absence is recorded and the moat class does **not**
rest on it — the row is decided by the three competitors DG names itself, all of which file.

## THE THREE LIMITS, STATED BEFORE THE ROW IS USED [E3-61]

1. **Sales per square foot is NOT strictly comparable across these six.** DG and Dollar Tree
   both disclose **selling** square feet; **Walmart discloses total retail square feet**, which
   includes backroom and non-selling space. Correcting for that would make Walmart's $691 look
   **better still**, not worse, so the direction of the finding is safe; the level is not, and
   the run does not rank on the level. Five Below and BJ's do not publish a square-foot
   productivity metric at all; per-store sales are used instead.
2. **Comparable-sales definitions differ.** DG uses stores open ≥13 full fiscal months
   restated to comparable 52 weeks; Dollar Tree includes expanded, relocated and remodeled
   stores and excludes new stores; Walmart includes eCommerce in comparable sales, which DG
   effectively does too (third-party delivery is *"becoming a meaningful contributor to our
   comparable store sales performance"*). **The DG-vs-Walmart comp comparison is the one that
   most needs this caveat and it is stated where it is used.**
3. **[E3-61]'s deeper limit governs everything above:** *"The row shows position; it cannot
   show conduct."* Five Below's +12.8% and Dollar Tree's +5.3% are conduct as much as position,
   and the corpus says even Munger had no model for predicting it.

## WHAT THE ROW SAYS — the five findings

### 1. THE ATTACKER'S TEST [E2-45] HAS BEEN RUN, IN PUBLIC, AND THE ATTACKER LOST $6.3 BILLION

Family Dollar is the only company that ever attacked Dollar General's format head-on at scale.
Dollar Tree bought it in 2015 and has now sold it. From the DLTR fiscal 2025 10-K, verbatim
and filed:

- *"**sale of the Family Dollar business to 1959 Holdings, LLC**"*, completed **2025-07-05**.
- *"the operating results and cash flows of the Family Dollar business are reported as
  **discontinued operations for all periods presented**"*; *"**with the Dollar Tree banner
  comprising substantially all of our retail operations**"*.
- Cash-flow statement: **proceeds from sale of discontinued operations $680.0M**, less **cash
  divested from sale of discontinued operations $(246.0)M** — **net cash of roughly $434
  million**.
- **Income (loss) from discontinued operations, net of tax: $57.2M (fiscal 2025), $(4,072.6)M
  (fiscal 2024), $(2,264.2)M (fiscal 2023). Dollar Tree wrote off $6,336.8 million after tax on
  Family Dollar in two years and then sold what was left for net cash of about $434 million.**

**This cuts BOTH ways and the run uses both halves.**
- **FOR the moat:** the best-resourced, most-motivated direct attacker in the format's history
  put 8,000+ stores against DG's in the same towns and destroyed $6.3 billion of capital doing
  it. Under [E2-45] — *"how I would like, assuming I had ample capital and skilled personnel,
  to compete with it"* — the attack has been made and it failed. **That is a genuine and rare
  piece of evidence FOR a location moat, and it is the strongest thing in this file on DG's
  side.**
- **AGAINST the moat:** Family Dollar's collapse means the trade areas were **overstored** and
  the incumbent's own returns fell anyway. DG's after-tax return on capital including leases
  went 18.5% → 8.6% across exactly the years Family Dollar was dying. **A moat that lets your
  rival lose $6bn while your own return on incremental capital goes negative is not paying
  you.** And the buyer of Family Dollar is a private-equity vehicle with no public disclosure
  obligation — **the surviving competitor is now invisible**, which is a monitoring problem
  recorded at Q6.

### 2. WALMART'S U.S. OPERATING MARGIN IS 5.2%. DOLLAR GENERAL'S IS 5.16%. THEY ARE THE SAME.

This is the row's decisive line and it is filed on both sides.

| | Walmart U.S. FY2026 | Walmart U.S. FY2025 | Walmart U.S. FY2024 | **DG fiscal 2025** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| net sales | $482,975M | $462,415M | $441,817M | $42,724.4M |
| operating income | $25,158M | $23,882M | $22,154M | $2,203.7M |
| **operating margin** | **5.2%** | **5.2%** | **5.0%** | **5.16%** |
| comparable sales | **+4.3%** | +4.8% | +5.5% | **+3.0%** |
| retail units | **4,611** | 4,605 | 4,615 | 20,893 |
| retail square feet | **699M — FLAT for three years** | 698M | 699M | ~158M (+1.5%) |
| sales per square foot | **$691** | $663 | $632 | **$270** |

**Read it plainly.**
- **DG's small-box convenience format earns exactly the same operating margin as the
  1.5-acre supercenter it is supposed to be a premium-priced alternative to** — 5.16% against
  5.2%. The entire theory of the format is that proximity commands a price and a margin the
  big box cannot match in a small town. **It does not show up in the margin.**
- **Walmart U.S. has not opened a net store in three years** (4,615 → 4,605 → 4,611, square
  footage flat at 699M) **and its comparable sales beat DG's in each of those three years**
  (+5.5 / +4.8 / +4.3 against +0.2 / +1.4 / +3.0). Walmart is taking share out of the same
  trade areas without building anything, through delivery and price. DG added 907 net stores
  over the same three years to grow comps more slowly.
- Walmart's small-format count — *"Neighborhood Markets and other small formats: **694**"*,
  average 42,000 sq ft — is not a dollar-store attack and this run does not claim it is. **The
  attack is coming through the delivery van, not a new box**, and DG's own filing concedes the
  vector: competition intensifying *"including through the expansion of availability of
  delivery services"* and *"increase[d] availability of mobile, web-based and other digital
  technology."*

### 3. DOLLAR TREE — THE OTHER DOLLAR STORE EARNS 65% MORE MARGIN ON 44% OF THE STORES

| | **DG fiscal 2025** | **Dollar Tree fiscal 2025** |
|---|---|---|
| stores | 20,893 | 9,282 |
| net sales | $42,724.4M | $19,395.7M |
| **operating margin** | **5.16%** | **8.5%** (8.3% FY24, 10.6% FY23) |
| **comparable sales** | **+3.0%** | **+5.3%** (1.8% FY24, 5.8% FY23) |
| **sales per selling sq ft** | **$270** | $241 (232, 234) |
| comp driver | traffic +1.6%, ticket +1.4% | **ticket +4.3%**, traffic +1.0% |
| locations | *"rural, suburban and urban"* | *"principally in **suburban** locations"* |
| store size | ~8,500 selling sq ft (new format) | 8,000–10,000 selling sq ft |
| mix | **82.0% consumables** | broad discretionary/seasonal; ~8,400 items |

**DG has 2.25x the stores and 2.2x the sales, and Dollar Tree makes 65% more operating margin
on each dollar and grew comps 77% faster.** The difference is mix and it is structural: DG is
82% consumables — the lowest-margin category in retail, chosen because it drives trips — while
Dollar Tree is a discretionary/seasonal assortment at a multi-price point. Two companies with
nearly identical boxes in overlapping trade areas, and the one selling *less* of what people
need earns *more.* **Dollar Tree also does not name Dollar General in its 10-K at all.**

### 4. THE MEMBERSHIP CONTRAST — the row's answer to "what a moat in retail actually looks like"

| | **DG** | BJ's | **Costco** |
|---|---|---|---|
| **prepaid annuity revenue** | **$0** | $499.8M (+9.5%) | **$5,323M (+10%)** |
| share of pretax income from fees | **0%** | ~65% | **~49%** |
| renewal rate | **no such thing** | 90% | **92.3% US/Can** |
| sales per box | $2.06M | $79.7M | **$272M** |
| operating margin | 5.16% | 3.90% | 3.85% |
| after-tax ROIC incl. leases | **8.6%** | — | ROE ~31% on net cash |

**Costco and BJ's earn a *lower* operating margin than DG and are far better businesses**,
because half of Costco's profit pool and roughly two-thirds of BJ's is a **fee paid in advance
for the right to shop**, renewed by 81 million and 8 million households respectively. That is
the [E3-03] criterion-2 evidence in its purest form: customers who prepay have told you there
is no close substitute. **Dollar General has no such instrument and no such evidence. Its
customer's commitment is zero and can be withdrawn on any trip.** The contrast is exactly what
the operator asked for and it is the clearest single statement of what DG's moat is *not*.

### 5. FIVE BELOW — the small-box format that IS working, in the same size of box

Five Below runs 1,921 stores of comparable size *"across a variety of urban, suburban and
semi-rural markets"*, opened 150 net new in fiscal 2025 and plans 150 more, and delivered
**+12.8% comparable sales** (after −2.7% in fiscal 2024) at a **9.6% operating margin** with
**36.0% gross margin** and **average net sales per store of $2.5 million** against DG's $2.06
million. It is one-eleventh DG's store count and it out-earns DG per box and per dollar.

**The point is not that Five Below is a better company — it is a different, discretionary,
teen-focused assortment.** The point is the one Q2 has to answer: **a small box in a small
market is not by itself a moat.** Two small-box operators in the same country in the same
year produced +12.8% and +5.3% comps at 9.6% and 8.5% margins; DG produced +3.0% at 5.16%.
**The format is not the advantage. The assortment and the mix are, and DG's mix is the worst
of the five.**

## THE ROW'S SUMMARY VERDICT ON RELATIVE POSITION

**DG is last or next-to-last of the five filed competitors on operating margin (5.16%, ahead
only of the two membership clubs whose profits come from fees instead), last on comparable
sales growth (+3.0% against +4.3%, +5.3%, +12.8%), and its sales per square foot is 39% of
Walmart's.** Its one clear win — 20,893 stores and *"approximately 75% of the U.S. population
… within five miles"* — is a scale of **presence**, and the row shows presence is not
converting into either margin or growth relative to anyone.

**And not one of the five names Dollar General in its filings.** The largest discount retailer
in the United States by number of stores does not appear in the competition disclosure of the
company it says is its primary direct competitor, nor of the other dollar-store chain, nor of
either club. [E2-53]'s dominance class is about a position that rivals must reckon with. **Five
competitors filed 10-Ks and none of them reckoned with this one.** (Recorded with its limit:
10-K competition disclosures are generic by convention and this is evidence, not proof.)
