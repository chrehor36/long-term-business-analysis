**THE FINDING. The as-reported range is 7.91x (351 → 2,780). Ex-working-capital it is 2.41x
(941 → 2,271).**

- **Peak → trough (fiscal 2020 → 2022) was a −$2,428.3M swing in owner earnings. The
  working-capital line alone swung −$2,478.5M over the same two years — MORE than the whole
  decline.** Fiscal 2022's $351M trough is not an earnings collapse; ex-WC it was the **best
  year in the series** ($2,271.4M).
- **The real earnings collapse is fiscal 2023-24, and it is invisible in the headline series.**
  Ex-WC owner earnings fell from $2,271.4M (2022) to $941.0M (2023) and $1,002.6M (2024) —
  **−58%**. That is the shrink-and-margin event.
- **Fiscal 2025's $2,301.8M is flattered by $613.8M of working-capital release** (inventory
  drawdown plus an accrual DG attributes "primarily to an increase in accrued incentive
  compensation") **plus $199.2M of income-tax timing**. Ex-WC: $1,688.0M.
- **Net over eight years working capital ABSORBED $1,043.1M ≈ $130M/yr.** That is the genuine
  [E2-23] working-capital increment of a chain adding ~600-1,000 stores a year. It is not zero
  and it is NOT removed from owner earnings — the ex-WC column is a **diagnostic**, never the
  reported number.
- **The window spread is the same artifact.** 2021-25 is net WC-absorbing by $1,471.4M
  (−$294M/yr, making the 5y mean conservative); 2023-25 is net WC-releasing by $937.4M
  (+$312M/yr, making the 3y mean generous). **The +14.7% conservative-end window spread is
  almost entirely which side of one inventory roundtrip the window lands on.**

## 3. SHRINK — quantified in DOLLARS from the segment note

The FY2025 10-K's Note 12 segment reconciliation discloses **shrink included in cost of goods

**Excess over the fiscal-2025 rate: $336M in fiscal 2023 (13.7% of that year's operating

fiscal 2025 is 60% of the $489.6M operating-profit improvement.

Dollar disclosure does not exist before fiscal 2023 (the standard did not require it). The
qualitative trail across the window, verbatim:
- FY2017/FY2018 10-Ks: *"an improved rate of inventory shrinkage"* (a gross-margin tailwind).
- FY2019/FY2020 10-Ks: *"a higher rate of inventory shrinkage"* (a headwind).
- FY2023 10-K: *"During 2023, our inventory shrink and damages results remained significantly
  elevated and materially impacted our results."* … *"We experienced higher damages in the
  first half of 2023, as well as significantly higher shrink throughout 2023. We believe that
  our shrink results in 2023 were due to a number of factors, including materially higher
  inventory levels in the first half of the year, **store standards and other operational
  challenges such as self-checkout and store manager turnover**, as well as the macroeconomic
  environment."*
- FY2024 10-K: *"During 2024, our inventory shrink and damages levels remained significantly
  elevated and materially impacted our results."*
- FY2025 10-K: *"Inventory shrink has significantly improved from prior elevated levels, and
  although damages remain elevated, we made progress reducing damages in 2025."*
- Q2 FY2026 10-Q: *"Inventory shrink has significantly improved from elevated levels in recent
  years, and although damages remain elevated, we made progress reducing damages in the second
  quarter of 2026."*

**Standing risk factor, every year:** *"We experience significant inventory shrinkage and
damages. … In addition, sustained high rates of inventory shrink at certain stores have
historically contributed, and inventory shrink and/or damages may contribute, to the closure
of certain stores and the impairment of long-term assets in the future."*

**Ruling: the 2023-24 spike was a self-inflicted operational event (self-checkout + store
manager turnover + a $1.7bn inventory over-build), largely reversed. But a 1.48% base rate is
a permanent structural cost of a 20,900-store, ~9-employees-per-store format — DG's own risk
factor calls it "an unavoidable cost of doing business" and links it to store closures.**

for the Company's leases was **9.1 years**, and the weighted average discount rate was **4.6%**.
For 2025, 2024 and 2023, **operating lease cost of $1.99 billion, $1.89 billion and $1.75

for amounts included in the measurement of operating lease liabilities of $2.01 billion, $1.91

| fiscal | P&L operating lease cost | cash paid, inside OCF | **net effect on OCF vs the expense** |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2023 | 1.75bn | 1.76bn | **−$10M (a drag)** |
| 2024 | 1.89bn | 1.91bn | **−$20M (a drag)** |
| 2025 | 1.99bn | 2.01bn | **−$20M (a drag)** |

**ANSWER TO THE OPERATOR'S QUESTION: OCF IS NOT DISTORTED BY ASC 842.** The full $2.01bn of
fixed rent plus $0.41bn of variable lease cost is genuinely deducted inside the $3,634.5M of
operating cash flow. The ROU-amortisation add-back and the lease-payment deduction net to a
$20M **drag**, 0.55% of OCF. Same conclusion as the SHOE run's $2.6M drag, on a base 770x
larger. There is no ROU-asset inflation of operating cash flow here.

**But the balance-sheet fact is enormous and is a separate finding.** Lease maturity ladder,
$000: 2026 **2,006,108** · 2027 1,897,407 · 2028 1,724,577 · 2029 1,517,313 · 2030 1,295,667 ·

**present value 11,138,374**, plus ~$222.2M of signed-but-not-commenced leases. **That is 2.4x
the $4,580.3M of funded debt** and it is the real near-term cash requirement under [E5-11].
