## Q1 — CAN I UNDERSTAND HOW THIS MAKES MONEY? **[E3-31]**
- **Unit economics in my own words, no management language.** Lovesac designs two furniture products and has other people make them. The first is a sectional couch built from two standard boxes, a seat and a side, that bolt together in any arrangement and wear removable, washable covers (*"Sactionals"*, **91.1%** of fiscal 2026 net sales); the second is a large shredded-foam beanbag (*"Sacs"*, 6.1%). It owns no factories (*"We do not currently own or operate any manufacturing facilities"*); contract makers in Vietnam, Malaysia, China, Indonesia, Taiwan, India and the United States build to its design. It sells only to consumers, through **278 leased small showrooms in malls and lifestyle centres** (67.1% of sales), its **website** (27.6%) and pop-ups, Costco events and, until 2026, Best Buy shop-in-shops (5.3%), and it ships the boxes by parcel courier. Fiscal 2026: net sales **$697.1M**; gross margin **56.4%** after inbound freight, tariffs and last-mile delivery; showroom rent, payroll and overhead **40.7%** of sales; advertising **12.7%**; depreciation 2.2%; **operating income $5.4M (0.8%)**. So the money is a wide product margin almost entirely spent finding and serving each buyer: of every $100 of sales about $56 is left after the goods are made and delivered, and about $55 of that goes to showrooms, sales staff and advertising. The second leg of the model is the installed base: the parts are standard and *"we guarantee their compatibility over time"*, so an owner can buy more seats, sides, covers and the StealthTech speaker sides later; *"Repeat customers accounted for approximately 50.4% of all transactions in fiscal 2026 compared to 46.8% in fiscal 2025."* The business is seasonal (35.7% of fiscal 2026 sales in the fourth quarter, Black Friday and the holidays).
- **The scarce input this business controls.** The candidates, stated before Q2 tests them: (a) **the patented modular design** (*"55 issued U.S. utility patents and 85 issued foreign utility patents, that expire between 2035 and 2043"*, covering *"our proprietary geometric modular system and segmented bi-coupling technology"*); (b) **the compatibility promise and the installed base** that buys add-ons; (c) **the brand** and its trademarks; (d) **the parcel-shippable form**, which the filer believes is unique in its category (*"We believe our Sactionals are the only product in its category that enjoys this logistical advantage"*). The inputs it does not control are named by the filer: contract manufacturing abroad, tariffs, freight, mall rents, and the customer's attention, which it buys with advertising. Whether any of (a)-(d) lets Lovesac set its price is Q2's question.
- **Will the fundamentals look broadly the same in ten years?** On the record available (ten audited years, fiscal 2017 to fiscal 2026), the method has not changed: the same two products, the same showrooms-plus-web channel, the same outsourced making. The Sac *"was developed in 1995"*. What could change is named in the filing: consumer spending on furniture and housing turnover, tariffs on the countries of manufacture, and competition from *"furniture stores, big box retailers, department stores, specialty retailers and online furniture retailers and marketplaces"*. None of these changes how the money is made; each is a Q2 or Q4 matter.
- **Relatively simple and stable in character [E3-31]? A decision in five minutes [E4-46]?** Yes. A designer and direct retailer of two furniture products, with outsourced manufacturing; the arithmetic is gross margin on a boxed couch less rent, staff and advertising. The one accounting complexity (barter of returned stock for media credits, $32.6M of unused credits at 2026-02-01) is disclosed in a paragraph and is read at Q4.
