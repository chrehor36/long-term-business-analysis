PRINCIPAL RISKS AND UNCERTAINTIES 
The Group continued to promote work in relation to its risk management. The principal risks faced by the Group
during the year were adequately identified and assessed according to the requirements of the Risk Management
System of China Everbright Environment Group Limited and the Risk Management Operation Manual of China
Everbright Environment Group Limited. The 2025 principal risks of the Group included accounts receivable
risk, environmental compliance and safety management risk, market competition risk, policy changing risk,
procurement compliance risk, operational stability risk and staff placement risk. The Company has formulated
and implemented targeted control measures based on the respective specific performance of each of the
principal risks to ensure that the overall risk of the Company is under control.
I. Accounts receivable risk
Description:
Affected by changes in national subsidy policies and the macro-
economy, the ability of governments at all levels to pay has been
impaired, increasing the likelihood of deferring settlement of national
subsidies, waste and waste water treatment fees, resulting in an
increase in financing pressure and capital costs for the Company.

Responsive measures:
1. System first: The Company issued core systems and relevant

transformed individual experience into universal models.

special bonds, sorted out channels for appealing government debt
arrears, and followed up on the confirmation of national subsidies.
5. Organisational support: The Company established a dedicated
debt recovery department implementing the "6+1" appraisal system
to strengthen process control and performance assessment.
6. Targeted breakthroughs: The Company convened progress
meetings to collectively resolve debt recovery issues in relation to
large, long-outstanding and high-difficulty projects.

II. Environmental compliance and safety management risk
Description:
The regulations on production safety and emission of pollutants were
increasingly stringent, thus imposing higher requirements on the
refinement of operation of the enterprises. With the increasing number
of operating projects, the basis numbers and difficulties involved with
safety and environmental management kept on rising, and the Company
was under high pressure associated with environmental compliance and
safety management.
Responsive measures:
1. Ideological foundation building: The Company collected safety
accident cases of the Company and the industry, compiled warning
manuals, and organised company-wide trainings to reinforce safety
awareness among all personnel (particularly key project responsible
persons).
2. Capacity improvement: The Company conducted hierarchical and
classified safety trainings to consolidate the basic literacy across
all personnel and provided specialised trainings and qualification
examinations for newly appointed key project responsible persons.
3. Supervision closed-loop: The Company intensified unannounced
safety inspections, reinforced the safety responsibilities of the
"top-leader" of each project, and promoted the implementation of
responsibilities through strict supervision.
4. System upgrades: The Company aligned with the three-year
campaign for fundamental solutions and tackling key problems,
advanced refined operation, safety and environmental management
in 2025, compiled and promoted guidance manuals to improve the
level of safety management.

III. Market competition risk
Description:
As the environmental protection industry enters a period of in-depth
adjustment, the incremental market for traditional businesses has shrunk
sharply, and market competition has become increasingly fierce. In
the process of strategic optimisations, the Company could not shape
competitive advantages for new businesses in the short term, and
therefore might be exposed to greater market competition pressure.

III. Market competition risk (continued)
Responsive measures:
1. Strategic anchoring: The Company took technological innovation
as the core, deepened the implementation of the three major
development strategies, promoted the internationalisation of
advantageous businesses, adopted a balanced approach to market
expansion, and focused on the implementation of light-asset and
ToB collaborative businesses.
2. Overseas layout: The Company promoted the international market
in a hierarchical manner, further developed green and low-carbon
services in Hong Kong, expanded heavy-asset waste-to-energy
projects in Southeast Asia, and focused on light-asset businesses
such as technology and equipment export in regions including Africa
and the Middle East.
3. Industry benchmarking: The Company strengthened linkage with
peers and partners, conducted regular benchmarking analysis to
assess industry trends and cutting-edge technologies, focused on
emerging sectors, policy orientations and government requirements
to maintain market sensitivity.
