### THE QUESTION THE BRIEF ASKED: DOES THE MOTORCYCLE RECORD SHOW WHAT THE CAR RECORD DOES NOT?
- **Yes, on every filed measure.** Motorcycle segment margin 14.3 -> 18.2% FY3/22-26 and never below 9.9% in eleven filed years;
  automobile margin never above 4.9% in the same eleven. Motorcycles earn 27-31% pre-tax on segment assets; automobiles 0.5-5.3% before
  the write-off. Motorcycle group units +30% in four years; automobile group units -17% (and -35% from FY3/18). Against peers,
  motorcycles co-lead with Bajaj (standalone 18.4% pooled, including three-wheelers) above Hero (11.6%), Harley (9.4%), Suzuki
  (9.1%), Yamaha (7.9%) and Kawasaki's powersports segment (7.8%); automobiles sit second from the bottom of eleven (pooled (0.62)%, 1.70% before the EV losses), above only Nissan.
- **And it does not rescue the security.** Motorcycles are 71.8% of five-year segment profit on 17% of industrial assets; automobiles
  take 79% of the assets, 85% of the capex, 88% of the R&D and the ¥6.2tn FY3/27-29 plan. **The HAS test ("the rest neither grows nor
  eats material capital") fails on Honda's own filing**, which says the motorcycle and finance businesses' *"strong profitability and cash
  generation capabilities"* will fund the automobile transition. The motorcycle class is NARROW, not WIDE: Hero files 28.7% of India's
  domestic ICE two-wheelers, Bajaj 15.6% of its motorcycles, and the battery transition is named by Honda itself as the replacement risk.

### Q4, RECORDED - AND THE (c) ARGUMENT RUN THE OTHER WAY FROM TM
Toyota's D&A end was invalid because it spent 1.53x D&A to hold flat units. **Honda's is invalid because it spent 0.95x D&A over ten

ways, because leaving them out books the loss and never the cash. Industrial owner earnings ¥486bn (8-yr) to ¥671bn (3-yr).
