## GATE 1 — CIRCLE OF COMPETENCE
Mitsubishi Corp is a **sogo shosha** (general trading company) organized
into 7 business groups (Mineral Resources, Energy & Power Solutions, Urban
Development & Infrastructure, Mobility, Food Industry, Materials Solution,
Smart-Life Creation) spanning commodities trading, industrial equity stakes,
logistics, and consumer businesses. This is a genuinely complex, diversified
conglomerate — the honest circle-of-competence read is **not** "I fully
understand every one of these businesses," and Buffett's own framing is the
most useful primary-source anchor here: **"[Berkshire holds] its passive and
long-term interest in five very large Japanese companies, each of which
operates in a highly-diversified manner somewhat similar to the way
Berkshire itself is run"** [2023 Letter]. The honest circle-of-competence
claim for this framework is therefore structural, not line-item: this is
understandable as **a diversified holding company with disciplined capital
allocation**, the same lens applied to Berkshire itself — not as a business
whose every mineral-resources or mobility-segment economics are independently
verified here.
**VERDICT: PASS**, on the Berkshire-analogy structural argument explicitly,
not on claimed segment-by-segment mastery.

## GATE 2 — MOAT
Franchise test [E3-03] does not map cleanly onto a trading-house structure
— the real "moat," to the extent one exists, is **scale, deal-flow access,
and embedded equity stakes across strategic global supply chains** (LNG,
mineral resources, food value chains) built over decades, not pricing power
on any single product. **Class: NARROW** (scale/relationship-based, not
price-setting), applied by analogy to how this framework treats other
scale-advantaged but commodity-adjacent businesses (e.g., Unit Corp's
cost-advantage classification, though at a vastly larger and more
diversified scale here).
**VERDICT: PASS with NARROW classification** — genuinely different in kind
from a consumer-franchise moat, flagged as such rather than forced into a
familiar category it doesn't fit.

## GATE 3 — MANAGEMENT
This is the strongest-evidenced gate of the five sogo shosha runs, resting
directly on Buffett's own diligence, not just this run's research:
**"We increased our holdings in all five last year after Greg Abel and I
made a trip to Tokyo to talk with their managements"** [2023 Letter] — a
direct, disclosed management site visit by Berkshire's own CEO and eventual
successor.
On capital discipline and alignment, verbatim: **"In certain important ways,
all five companies — Itochu, Marubeni, Mitsubishi, Mitsui and Sumitomo —
follow shareholder-friendly policies that are much superior to those
customarily practiced in the U.S. Since we began our Japanese purchases,
each of the five has reduced the number of its outstanding shares at
attractive prices... the managements of all five companies have been far
less aggressive about their own compensation than is typical in the United
States. Note as well that each of the five is applying only about 1/3 of
its earnings to dividends. The large sums the five retain are used both to
build their many businesses and, to a lesser degree, to repurchase shares.
Like Berkshire, the five companies are reluctant to issue shares"**
[2023 Letter]. This directly satisfies this framework's own retention-test
and buyback-discipline principles (OM-9, [E5-01]) — sourced from Buffett's
own words about this specific holding, not inferred by analogy.
**VERDICT: PASS**, the best-sourced Gate 3 of any company run in this
project to date.

## GATE 4 — FINANCIAL QUALITY (OE convention documented explicitly)
**FRAMEWORK CONVENTION — not attributable to Buffett/Munger**: for a sogo
shosha, the standard OE formula (NI + D&A − maintenance capex) does not map
cleanly onto the business. A large share of reported earnings flows from
equity-method income in associated companies and trading-related working
capital, not depreciable owned property/plant/equipment the way it does for
a REIT or an industrial operator. A clean, disclosed maintenance-capex
figure was not obtainable this pass (no SEC filings exist for this
unsponsored ADR; the company's own investor-relations site was checked but
did not yield a clean figure in the time budgeted). **This run uses Net
Income directly as the OE proxy for Mitsubishi Corp and the other four sogo
shosha, as a documented convention, not a silent shortcut.**

2. **Management/key-person** — broad, diversified management team across
   7 business groups; Buffett's own site-visit diligence is real evidence,
   but this run did not independently verify current CEO/board composition.
   Scored **[P]**.
3. **Balance-sheet stress** — resting on general investment-grade knowledge,
   not a fresh pull. Scored **[P]**, not [D] — genuinely less-verified than
   the REIT batch's SEC-sourced balance sheets.
4. **Currency/yen risk** (relevant to a USD-based investor even though
   Berkshire hedges via yen debt) — a real, structural consideration for
   any non-Berkshire investor in this ADR. Scored **[P]**.

No [U], but **four [P] scores and zero [D] scores** — every inversion here
carries real, unresolved uncertainty, a genuinely different risk profile
than the REIT batch (which had mostly [D]s with occasional [P]s).
**Thesis-breaking metric:** a sustained, multi-year decline in the retained-
earnings-and-buyback discipline Buffett specifically cited, or a credit-
rating downgrade below investment grade.
**VERDICT: PASS**, but with the least independently-verified Gate 5 of any
company run in this project — resting more heavily on Buffett's own
disclosed diligence than on this run's primary-source work.
