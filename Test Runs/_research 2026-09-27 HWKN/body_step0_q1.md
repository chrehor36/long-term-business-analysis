## Q1 — CAN I UNDERSTAND HOW THIS MAKES MONEY? **[E3-31]**
- **Unit economics in my own words, no management language.** Hawkins buys chemicals by the railcar and truckload from the large US producers (caustic soda, sulfuric, hydrochloric and phosphoric acid, chlorine and hypochlorite, coagulants, aqua ammonia), stores them in its own terminals and plants around Minneapolis, the Midwest and the South, and resells them in smaller lots, some unchanged (*"bulk commodity products ... that we do not modify in any way"*), most blended, diluted, repackaged or reacted into a specific product. It earns the difference between what it pays and what it charges, about **22.6 cents of gross profit per dollar of sales in fiscal 2026** (sales $1,083.7M, gross profit $245.1M), less selling and administration of about 11.4 cents, leaving an **operating margin of 11.2%** (operating income $121.3M; net income $81.5M). Three trades: (a) **Water Treatment**, half of sales: chemicals, filtration media and equipment delivered on the company's own trucks by employees who *"typically serve as route driver, salesperson and trained technician"*, from 53 warehouses in 28 states, to municipal drinking-water and wastewater plants, pools and industrial water users; gross margin 27%; (b) **Food and Health Sciences**, 30%: food, pharmaceutical, nutrition and agricultural ingredients, partly made (lactates, phosphates, salts), partly distributed (vitamins, amino acids, botanicals through the Stauber business); gross margin 21%; (c) **Industrial Solutions**, 20%: acids and alkalis received, stored, blended and repackaged for manufacturers; gross margin 15%, of which 21% of sales is untouched bulk. Customers are many and small: *"none of our customers accounted for 10% or more of our total sales."* **Price follows the raw material**: the filer uses LIFO because it is *"consistent with our business practices of pricing to current chemical raw material prices"*, and *"sales dollars tend to fluctuate as raw material prices rise and fall"*, which is why it says it manages *"total profitability dollars ... as opposed to profitability as a percentage of sales."*
- **The scarce input this business controls.** The candidates, stated before Q2 tests them: (a) **the local delivery-and-service network in water treatment**: permitted chemical storage, hazmat trucks (*"a fleet of approximately 400 commercial vehicles"*) and technicians on routes to small municipal and pool customers, where the chemical is a small cost and a missed delivery is a public-health failure; (b) **terminals and plants with rail and bulk storage** near Midwest customers, and the purchasing volume the filer says gives it *"buying power"* and *"competitive cost positions"*; (c) **supplier relationships** (*"we are often able to leverage those relationships to obtain products when supplies are limited"*); (d) formulations and food and pharmaceutical quality registrations. The filer credits none of them with pricing power; it says it competes *"at competitive prices"* against rivals *"offering products equivalent to substantially all of the products we offer."* Whether any of these lets Hawkins set its price rather than accept it is Q2's question.
- **Will the fundamentals look broadly the same in ten years?** Yes. The fiscal 2006 10-K describes *"the formulation, blending and distribution of bulk and specialty chemicals"* in *"Water Treatment, Industrial and Pharmaceutical"* segments; fiscal 2016's *"distributes, blends and manufactures chemicals and specialty ingredients"*; fiscal 2026's the same trades, larger by purchase (Stauber in fiscal 2016; a run of water-treatment distributors since fiscal 2021). Municipal water treatment, food ingredients and industrial acids are not rapid-change trades. The mix has moved toward water treatment, and that is the direction the filer says it will keep taking (*"We expect to continue to invest in existing and new branches to expand the group’s geographic coverage"*).
- **Relatively simple and stable in character [E3-31]? A decision in five minutes [E4-46]?** Yes: a regional chemical distributor and blender with a route-delivery water business; the unit economics are a spread over purchased cost, visible in every filing. Nothing requires months of study; the questions are whether the spread is a position or a practice, and what the price is, which are Q2's and Q5's.
