## Q2 — IS IT A FRANCHISE? **[E3-03]**

*Every Hawkins figure below is read from its filed 10-Ks for fiscal 2004 to fiscal 2026 (accessions in Step 0; income statements, balance sheets and the segment note of each), or from SEC companyfacts checked against the fiscal 2026 statements. **The question this file has to answer: does Hawkins set its prices, or does it pass its costs through and earn a service margin that competitors discipline?** Hawkins has earned a consolidated operating margin of 5-12% in every year for twenty-three years outside two tight-supply years and one impairment, and its Water Treatment route business earned 46-93% pre-tax on its segment assets from fiscal 2003 to fiscal 2014. **The two hypotheses, stated so they can be attacked [E4-26].** *(a) A local franchise of delivery and trust in water treatment: permitted storage, hazmat trucks and technicians on dense routes to small municipalities and pools, where the chemical is a small cost and a failed delivery is a public-health event, so the customer does not re-shop the supplier at a price; the Water Treatment segment's returns show it.* *(b) A well-run regional chemical distributor and blender that prices at current raw-material cost plus a margin held per unit, disciplined by competitors selling equivalent products, whose best years came from inventory gains in shortages and whose recent step-up came with the 2021-2023 inflation and with purchases.* Each is tested on the filings below.

**How the filer describes competition, customers and price, in its own words.**
- **Competition, every 10-K read:** *"We operate in a competitive industry and compete with many producers, distributors and sales agents offering products equivalent to substantially all of the products we offer. ... We compete by offering quality products with outstanding customer service at competitive prices coupled with value-added services or product formulation where needed"* (10-K FY2026, Item 1; the FY2016 10-K carries the same sentence). The risk factor, FY2008 to FY2026: *"We operate in a highly competitive environment and face significant competition and price pressure"*, and in FY2026: *"Additionally, competitors’ pricing decisions could compel us to decrease our prices, which could adversely affect our margins and profitability. Our ability to maintain or increase our profitability would be dependent upon our ability to offset competitive decreases in the prices and margins of our products by improving production efficiency, investing in infrastructure to reduce freight costs, identifying and selling higher margin products, providing higher levels of technical expertise and customer service"*. The FY2006 10-K: *"We face competition from other chemical companies, which places downward pressure on the prices and margins of our products."* **No competitor is named in any 10-K read.**
- **How price is set, in the filer's words:** *"We seek to maintain relatively constant gross profit dollars per unit sold on each of our products as the cost of our raw materials increase or decrease, subject to competitive pricing pressures that may negatively impact our gross profit dollars per unit sold"* (10-Ks FY2014-FY2017; FY2010 without the last clause); *"The LIFO inventory valuation method and the resulting cost of sales are consistent with our business practices of pricing to current chemical raw material prices"* (every 10-K FY2010-FY2026); *"While we attempt to maintain competitive pricing and stable margin dollars, the potential variance in our cost of inventory from the current market pricing can cause significant volatility in our margins realized"* (FY2026, Item 1A). To the SEC staff in 2023: *"we sell similar products to different customers at substantially different prices and margins in multiple segments, depending on market conditions"* (CORRESP of 2023-03-30).
- **Price given away to competitors, by year and segment (`price_sentences.txt`):** FY2004 (*"A highly competitive market environment resulted in an increase of approximately $600,000 in product costs that the Company was unable to pass on to its customers"*); FY2005-FY2006 (*"rising product costs, which to some extent, the Company was unable to pass on to customers due to competitive pressures"*); FY2011 (*"Both reporting segments saw a decline in their gross profit dollars due to competitive pricing pressures"*); **FY2012, in Water Treatment itself** (*"lower selling prices due to competitive pricing pressures"*); FY2013-FY2016 (*"heightened competitive pricing pressure, resulting in overall lower per-unit margins"*; *"lower raw material prices and competitive pricing pressures in certain product lines resulted in lower per-unit selling prices"*); FY2018 (*"lower margins on certain commodity products with rising material costs, driven by competitive pricing pressures"*); FY2025-FY2026 in Food and Health Sciences (*"Sales of our Food products decreased $11.1 million due to decreased selling prices driven by competitive pricing pressures"*; *"Gross profit decreased as a result of lower selling prices, primarily as a result of competitive pricing pressures"*).
- **The good years, in the filer's words:** FY2009, when operating income tripled: *"we benefited from selling at the higher market prices the lower cost inventory that we had on hand"* and *"our ability to continue to obtain supplies of key raw material ingredients at favorable prices while many of our competitors were facing raw material shortages ... allowed us to significantly expand our market share"*; the next year it expected profit *"to return to levels more in line with our historic results prior to fiscal 2009."* **That is [E3-43]'s tight-supply door, named by the filer.**

**[E3-03], criterion by criterion.** *"An economic franchise arises from a product or service that: (1) is needed or desired; (2) is thought by its customers to have no close substitute and; (3) is not subject to price regulation."*
- **(1) Needed or desired [x]: passes.** Drinking-water plants, pools, food makers and manufacturers cannot run without the chemicals.
- **(3) Not subject to price regulation [x]: passes.** The regulation named in Item 1 (DOT, FDA, EPA) is of safety and handling, not price.
- **(2) Thought by its customers to have no close substitute: FAILS, on the filer's own words.** It says its rivals offer *"products equivalent to substantially all of the products we offer"*, that it competes *"at competitive prices"*, that *"competitors’ pricing decisions could compel us to decrease our prices"*, and the MD&A of fourteen of the twenty-three 10-Ks read reports price, margin or sales lost to competitors (FY2004-FY2008, FY2011-FY2016, FY2018-FY2019, FY2026; several repeat the prior year's comparison), in every segment, **Water Treatment included (FY2012)**. - **The other doors, tested, and both are open here.** *Tight supply*: the filer names it for FY2009-FY2010, the two best years before the inflation (*"higher margins caused by supply constraints for certain products"*), and it ended as the corpus says (*"Tightness in supply usually does not last long"*): FY2011 operating margin 10.8%. *Low-cost operator*: the filer names it as its answer to competition (*"buying power among two or more groups and competitive cost positions"*; offsetting *"competitive decreases in the prices and margins"* by *"improving production efficiency, investing in infrastructure to reduce freight costs"*). ### THE STRONGEST EVIDENCE AGAINST THIS VERDICT, stated as its holders would state it [E4-51, E4-26, E3-47]
The verdict below is OUT, so the case for IN is set out first, at full strength:
1. **The Water Treatment route business earned 46-93% pre-tax on all its assets for twelve years (FY2003-FY2014)**, a return no price-taker earns for long, and it is now half the company. The customers are small municipalities and pools for whom the chemical is a trivial share of cost and reliable delivery of chlorine by a trained technician is the product; the filer calls itself *"the trusted water treatment expert for many of the municipalities and other customers that we serve"*. That is a local franchise of trust and density, [E3-43]'s *"no close substitute"* at the level of the route.
2. **In FY2024 Water Treatment raised prices while its costs fell and its volumes rose**, lifting its gross margin five points, which is [E2-44]'s first characteristic observed once.
3. **Consolidated returns have stepped up and held**: net income on equity 16-20% for six years (FY2021-FY2026), and 36-38% pre-tax on tangible operating capital for the last three, above Univar's and level with Ecolab's on total capital.
4. **The company has never lost money outside one impairment year**, through two recessions and two commodity cycles, and has no customer above 10% of sales.

**Answered.** (1) is accepted as the best evidence in the file and weighed against the same segment's filed record: its gross margin fell from 30-33% to 25-29% after FY2010 and to 22.0% when costs outran price in FY2023, the filer reports *"lower selling prices due to competitive pricing pressures"* in Water Treatment in FY2012, and the returns came from asset turnover on a light base, which [E3-43] assigns to the low-cost operator, *"a business"*. A franchise of trust does not concede price in a weak-weather year. (2) is accepted as observed and read in its series: the margin restored in FY2024 is the FY2011-FY2021 band, recovered after the inflation lag, not raised above it; once in twenty-three years, after two years of price lagging cost, is the lag, not the characteristic. (3) is accepted as a fact about the years since the inflation and weighed with the filer's own explanation (the raw-material cycle and $410.5M of purchases since FY2021); the same company earned 9-13% on equity FY2013-FY2020 (FY2018's impairment year excepted), and the screen's own `level_shift` 2.78 *"STEP UP - normalize down [E4-41]"* marks the same break. (4) is accepted and is a Q4 fact about survival, not a Q2 fact about price. **[E3-47]** (a wrongly closed file inside the circle is the costliest error) was weighed: the file is inside the circle, and it closes on the business's own sentences about its prices, which are the evidence [E3-03]'s second criterion asks for.
