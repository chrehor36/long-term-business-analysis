## GATE 1 — CIRCLE OF COMPETENCE
Owns a small portfolio of NYC-area (primarily Brooklyn) properties: Tribeca
House, Flatbush Gardens (a 59-building, 2,494-unit residential complex),
141 Livingston Street and 250 Livingston Street (**office buildings**,
not apartments), Aspen and Clover House (residential), and others. **This
is a genuinely mixed-use portfolio, not a pure apartment REIT** — 250
Livingston Street specifically is described as "office and residential,"
and commercial rental income was $34.3M of $153.2M total revenue (2025) —
roughly 22% of revenue from office/commercial leasing, not apartments.
This is a materially different business mix than every other REIT in this
batch and than the user's framing of the list as "apartment REITs."
**VERDICT: PASS**, with the commercial-office component flagged explicitly
as a real difference from the rest of the batch, not a footnote.

## GATE 4 — FINANCIAL QUALITY — **FAIL, STOP**
Real, disclosed figures from the FY2025 10-K:

| FY | Net loss | Real estate depreciation | Capex ("Additions to land, buildings and improvements") |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2024 | $(6.582)M | $29.786M | $68.781M |
| 2025 | $(52.335)M | $31.226M | $31.302M |

FY2025's net loss ballooned nearly 8x versus 2024 — driven substantially by
the disclosed NYC office-tenant vacancy at 250 Livingston, plus lease-up
drag from the newly-delivered Dean Street property. **FFO itself turned
negative in 2025**: $(21.008)M, down from a positive $23.310M in 2024 — a
genuine operating deterioration, not just a GAAP accounting artifact.

**Fortress check (financial/leverage-ceiling track): CLPR has NEGATIVE
total stockholders' equity.**
- TOTAL LIABILITIES: $1,315.073M
- TOTAL LIABILITIES AND EQUITY (DEFICIT) (= Total assets): $1,234.319M
- **TOTAL EQUITY (DEFICIT): $(80.754)M** — liabilities exceed assets on a
  book basis.

This is **not an edge case the mechanical ceiling test can wave through**:
the entire purpose of Ruling 2's 10:1 assets/equity ceiling is to catch
dangerous over-leverage, and negative equity is the most extreme form that
condition can take — worse than any finite ratio, not an undefined
exception to be treated favorably. (GAAP book equity going negative on a
real estate company can partly reflect historical-cost accumulated
depreciation rather than true economic insolvency if properties are worth
more than book value — but this run did not independently verify a
fair-value cushion, and the framework's mechanical ceiling test is not
designed to be overridden by an unverified fair-value assumption.)

**VERDICT: FAIL — STOP.** Per protocol, a Gate 4 FAIL stops the run here;
no valuation-lock, Gate 5, or Gate 6 work follows. **Recorded in the
Eliminated register below.**
