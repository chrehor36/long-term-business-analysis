# Company Run — D.R. Horton, Inc. (DHI) — 2026-07-15
Framework v3.0. From the mechanical screen. Source: FY2025 10-K (fiscal
year ended Sep 30, 2025, CIK 882184, filed 2025).

## STEP 0 — RATE REFRESH
US 30-yr **5.10%**. Mkt cap $42.98B — no size premium.

## GATE 1 — CIRCLE OF COMPETENCE
The largest US homebuilder by homes closed (126 markets, 36 states),
plus rental, a majority-owned lot-development company, and financial
services (mortgage/title). Homebuilding is the core, understandable
business (buy/develop land, build, sell); the ancillary businesses are
smaller, related tails, not independent complexity. **VERDICT: PASS.**

## GATE 2 — MOAT
Franchise test [E3-03]. DHI's own 10-K, unlike Lennar's (run separately,
eliminated at this gate), makes an explicit structural claim under its own
**"Economies of Scale"** heading: **"We are the largest homebuilding
company in the United States as measured by number of homes closed in
fiscal 2025, and we are also one of the largest builders in most of the
markets in which we operate. We believe that our national, regional and
local scale provides us with benefits that may not be available to the
same degree to some other smaller homebuilders, such as: Greater access to
and lower cost of capital due to our balance sheet strength..."**
- **Needed/desired**: yes, generically, not DHI's product specifically.
- **No close substitute**: **still weak** on a pure product basis (a house
  is a house), but the scale-based cost advantage is a real, disclosed,
  structural characteristic distinct from Lennar's filing, which made no
  equivalent claim.
- **Unregulated**: no — land-use/zoning/environmental regulation is
  material, ordinary-course.
- **Proven by pricing power + returns on capital**: **partial**. Scale
  lowers DHI's cost of capital and (plausibly) input costs, a genuine
  advantage over smaller competitors, though this is a cost advantage, not
  a pricing-power/brand-loyalty franchise — homebuyers still shop
  primarily on location/price/design, not builder brand loyalty in the way
  a consumer-staples brand commands loyalty.
**Class: NARROW** (scale-based cost advantage, not pricing power),
**Direction: NARROWING under current conditions** — a housing-
affordability downturn compresses volume for even the largest, lowest-
cost builder, and net income fell from $5,857M (FY2022) to $3,585M
(FY2025), a 39% decline, tracking the same industry cycle that eliminated
Lennar's moat case but here alongside a real, disclosed structural
advantage rather than none at all.
**VERDICT: PASS with NARROW/NARROWING classification** — the one
homebuilder in this batch with an actual disclosed structural edge, treated
as real but modest, not inflated into a consumer-franchise-style moat.

## GATE 4 — FINANCIAL QUALITY (full OE formula)
Homebuilders deploy the bulk of their capital into land/inventory, not
depreciable PP&E — DHI's small disclosed PP&E capex figures are corporate/
operational infrastructure, not the core capital-allocation decision (same
treatment logic as excluding REIT development capex from "maintenance
capex" — land/inventory investment is excluded from this formula entirely,
consistent with how this framework has handled homebuilder/REIT capital
structures elsewhere). Full disclosed PP&E capex used as-is (already small
relative to NI, so no further P44 band reduction applied):

| FY | Net income | D&A | PP&E capex | **OE = NI+D&A−capex** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2022 | $5,857.5M | $81.4M | $148.2M | **$5,790.7M** |
| 2023 | $4,745.7M | $91.6M | $148.6M | **$4,688.7M** |
| 2024 | $4,756.4M | $87.1M | $165.3M | **$4,678.2M** |
| 2025 | $3,585.2M | $101.3M | $137.4M | **$3,549.1M** |

**LOWEST VERIFIED TIER: $3,549.1M (FY2025)** — the most recent year is
also the lowest, consistent with the ongoing housing downturn rather than
a one-time item.
Fortress (non-financial survival track): Total assets $35,471M, equity
$24,190M (FY2025) — equity funds ~68% of the balance sheet, a
conservatively-levered structure by homebuilder-industry standards
(implied liabilities ~$11.3B against $3.5B/yr OE even at the current
depressed level). Credit rating not independently pulled this pass —
flagged — but the equity ratio itself argues for real resilience through a
further downturn.
**VERDICT: PASS.**

2. **Management/key-person** — not independently verified. Scored **[U]**.
3. **Balance-sheet stress** — conservative equity ratio, comfortably
   survives further OE decline from an already-depressed base. Scored
   **[D]**.
4. **Land-price/input-cost inflation** eroding the scale-cost-advantage
   thesis specifically. Scored **[P]**.

**One [U], two [P]s, one [D]**. **Thesis-breaking metric:** a further OE
decline below the FY2025 anchor combined with equity-ratio deterioration
(the two together, not either alone, would signal the scale advantage
itself is failing to cushion the cycle).
**VERDICT: PASS, with a documented [U]** on management verification.
