## GATE 2 — MOAT
Franchise test [E3-03]. NVR's own competition language is generic
("numerous homebuilders... some of which have greater financial
resources... compete on price, location, design, quality, service and
reputation"), similar to Lennar/PulteGroup's filings (both eliminated at
this gate). **What differs, and is independently verified in NVR's own
10-K**: NVR's **lot-acquisition strategy**. Verbatim: **"The deposits
required under the LPAs [lot purchase agreements] are in the form of cash
or letters of credit in varying amounts and typically range up to 10% of
the aggregate purchase price of the finished lots. We believe that our lot
acquisition strategy avoids the financial requirements and risks
associated with direct land ownership and land development. We may, at our
option, choose for any reason and at any time not to perform under these
LPAs... Our sole legal obligation and economic loss for failure to perform
under these LPAs is limited to the amount of the deposit..."**
- This is a genuine, disclosed, structural difference from Lennar/
  PulteGroup/D.R. Horton's direct-land-ownership models: NVR options
  finished lots from developers rather than buying and developing raw land
  itself, capping its downside to the option deposit rather than the full
  value of an owned land position. This is the mechanism that let NVR avoid
  the balance-sheet damage many peers suffered in the 2008-09 housing bust
  — a real, verifiable capital-efficiency advantage, not a marketing claim.
- **No close substitute**: still weak on the finished-home product itself
  (a house is a house), but the CAPITAL STRUCTURE advantage is real and
  distinguishes NVR from a pure commodity-cyclical peer.
**Class: NARROW** (capital-efficiency/balance-sheet-risk advantage, not
pricing power), **Direction: STABLE** — this is a durable structural
choice, not a cyclical condition.
**VERDICT: PASS with NARROW classification**, on a different and more
substantiated basis than D.R. Horton's scale argument — each homebuilder
in today's batch (LEN, PHM, DHI, NVR) was read and judged individually.

   verified. Scored **[P]**.
3. **Balance-sheet stress** — strongest equity ratio of the homebuilders

4. **Housing-affordability cycle** (same industry-wide risk as the other
   builders, dampened but not eliminated by the business-model advantage).

**Three [P]s, one [D], no [U].** **Thesis-breaking metric:** a shift away
from the lot-option model (e.g., forced direct land purchases due to
developer-market changes), or NI decline exceeding peer builders' despite
the structural advantage (would suggest the advantage itself is
weakening).
**VERDICT: PASS.**
