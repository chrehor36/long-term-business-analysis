## Q1 — CAN I UNDERSTAND HOW THIS MAKES MONEY? **[E3-31]**

**Unit economics in my own words, no management language.** Matador drills horizontal wells
into the Wolfcamp and Bone Spring formations of the Delaware Basin, on 212,500 net acres in
Eddy and Lea Counties, New Mexico and Loving, Ward and Winkler Counties, Texas. In FY2025 it
lifted **75.6 million barrels of oil equivalent** — 43.7 MMBbl of oil and 191.3 Bcf of gas
(58% oil by volume, and gas is reported "two-stream," so NGL value sits inside the gas price).
It sold that stream for **$42.85 per BOE** and spent **$30.41 per BOE** of total operating
expense, of which **$15.82 was depletion** — so the cash field-and-corporate margin was about
$27 per barrel before capital. Off that came $208.5M of interest on $3.4bn of debt and $7.1M
of cash income tax, and into it went **$1,844.2M of capital expenditure**: $1,542.3M drilling
and completing, $297.7M on pipes and plants, plus another $339.1M buying acreage.

There is a second business bolted on. **San Mateo Midstream** gathers, treats, processes and
disposes — for Matador's own wells and for third parties — and it is **51% Matador, 49% Five
Point Infrastructure**. In FY2025 the midstream segment earned **$278.3M of operating income**
on $549.9M of gross revenue, of which **$101.5M belonged to Five Point, not to a Matador
shareholder**. The midstream exists mostly to guarantee that Matador's own gas has somewhere
to go: the 10-K calls it *"in support of, and to provide flow assurance for"* the E&P.

So: **buy rock, drill it, sell the molecules at whatever the screen says, and own enough pipe
that you can keep selling when the basin's pipes are full.** That is the whole model, and it
is legible in a paragraph.

**The scarce input this business controls: none, and the filing says so twice over.**
- The *product* is a commodity: *"The prices we receive for oil, natural gas and NGLs heavily
  influence our revenues, profitability, cash flow… Oil, natural gas and NGL prices are subject
  to wide fluctuations in response to relatively minor changes in supply and demand."*
- The *input* — undrilled Delaware acreage — is bought in open auction. In **May 2026 Matador
  paid approximately $1.16 billion for 5,154 net undeveloped acres** at a Bureau of Land
  Management lease sale. That is **$225,000 per net acre**, in an auction, against every other
  Permian operator.
- What Matador *does* control is a **transportation position**: firm capacity of 500,000
  MMBtu/day on Energy Transfer's Hugh Brinson pipeline, secured *"at no capital expense,"*
  plus San Mateo's own gathering and processing. That is a genuine, filed, differentiating
  asset in a basin where the Waha-Henry Hub basis differential **averaged $(2.96)/MMBtu in
  FY2025 and was approximately $(5.11)/MMBtu at 2026-02-24**. Whether it is a *moat* is Q2's
  question. It is not a scarce input; it is a contract, and contracts are bought.

**Will the fundamentals look broadly the same in ten years?** The *mechanism*, yes — pump,
sell at the posted price, spend to replace. The *asset*, no, and the company writes it down
itself in the MD&A:

> *"Like other oil and natural gas producing companies, our properties are subject to natural
> production declines. **By their nature, our oil and natural gas wells will experience rapid
> initial production declines. We attempt to overcome these production declines by drilling to
> develop and identify additional reserves, by exploring for new sources of reserves and, at
> times, by acquisitions.**"* — FY2025 10-K, General Outlook and Trends

Total proved reserve life at the current rate is **8.8 years**; **proved developed life is
5.40 years** (408.0 MMBoe ÷ 75.6). Ten years from now, absent replacement, there is nothing.
That is not a forecast; it is the definition of the asset.

- **VERDICT: [x] IN.** The economics are legible in one paragraph, the segment disclosure
  separates the two businesses, and the reserve and cost tables let an outsider compute the
  replacement arithmetic without management's help. Whether legible is *franchise* is Q2's
  question; whether a 5.4-year-developed-life asset survives is Q4's.

### Must the moat be continuously rebuilt? **[E4-04]** — worked from the filed reserve tables

The corpus scopes [E4-04] precisely: what "enduring" excludes is *"the moat whose basis must
be **periodically replaced** — rapid-change industries, **depleting assets**."* The test is
*"does a lapse in spending destroy the structure, or merely narrow it — and does the spending
defend the same advantage, or **buy its replacement**?"*

**Matador is squarely in the excluded class, and — this is the operator's decisive method
point — it is in that class even though its accounting is honest about the cost.**

nothing until drilled — and only 23.0 MMBoe were wells actually turned to sales. Holding
production requires continuous PUD conversion: Matador converted **77.6 MMBoe of PUDs to
proved developed in FY2025**, and its filed future development cost on the remaining 259.1
MMBoe PUD book is **$2,683.4M, or $10.36 per PUD barrel still to be spent**.

**The company's own capital budget settles it.** In **February 2026** Matador guided full-year
2026 capital expenditure of **$1.35–1.44bn D/C/E plus $100–110M midstream = $1.45–1.55bn**, and
guided that this would deliver **+3% oil production**. So the filer's own near-flat-volume
capital requirement is roughly **$1.45–1.55bn against $1.20bn of depreciation, depletion and
amortization.** **[E5-20]'s exception therefore does bite at Matador — but through the
production-replacement channel rather than the reserve-replacement channel, and by about
$250–350M a year rather than by half.** That is the honest, filed answer, and it is materially
kinder to Matador than the SandRidge answer was to SandRidge.

### The commodity doctrine **[E2-58]**, applied

*"Persistent over-capacity without administered prices (or costs) equals poor profitability"*,
with long-term profitability set by *"the ratio of supply-tight to supply-ample years."*

own number: realised gas per Mcf of **$6.06 → $7.98 → $3.25 → $2.38 → $2.08 → $(0.79)**

gas arrives from oil-directed drilling whether or not anyone wants it, and every operator's
individually rational decision to drill for oil collectively destroys the gas price. The
Waha-Henry Hub basis differential **averaged $(2.96)/MMBtu in FY2025** and stood at
**$(5.11)/MMBtu at 2026-02-24**. Natural gas is **44% of proved reserves and 42% of production
volume.**

- **Untapped pricing power [E3-33] / [E5-28]: none, structurally.** A 221,000 BOE/d producer
