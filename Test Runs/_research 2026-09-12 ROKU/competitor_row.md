# ROKU: Q2 MANDATORY COMPETITOR ROW
*Built 2026-09-12. Filing-sourced. **No moat conclusion is drawn here.** That is the run's
job. This file is the row, the sources, and the named limits.*

**Window rule:** fiscal 2025 for every filer whose year permits it. Where the filer's year
differs (Apple FY ends September; Walmart FY ends January; Vizio ceased to exist in
December 2024) the window is stated on the row and the mismatch is named, not smoothed.

**The asymmetry to be aware of before reading:** Roku is a $22,996M market cap that files a
TV-operating-system P&L. Four of the six operating-system substitutes are either (a) segments
inside companies 20x to 200x Roku's size that disclose **no** OS revenue, **no** OS gross
profit and **no** OS installed base, or (b) foreign private issuers that file nothing with
the SEC at all. **The comparable that filed the same metrics is dead**. Vizio was acquired
by Walmart on 2024-12-03 and deregistered ten days later. So the competitor row cannot be
built symmetrically from filings. That fact is itself a finding and it is documented below
rather than papered over.

### THE LIMIT, STATED
The only filer that published a TV-OS installed base, a TV-OS ARPU, a platform gross margin
and a negative device gross profit **in the same statements** stopped filing on 2024-12-03.
From FY2025 forward, Roku's platform economics have **no like-for-like filed comparator
anywhere in EDGAR**. Any FY2025 relative claim about Roku's platform must therefore rest on
(a) Vizio data that is 15 months stale at Roku's FY2025 balance-sheet date, or (b) peers who
disclose nothing at this level. The run must say which.

**EXPLICIT STATEMENT OF THE LIMIT:**
1. **There is no 10-K and no 20-F for Samsung Electronics Co., Ltd., in any year.** Samsung's
 ~35 CIK-indexed filings are Schedule 13D/13G and tender-offer filings it made as a
 *holder or bidder in other people's securities*, plus ARS and SUPPL packets that stopped
 in 2010. Samsung Electronics is not a foreign private issuer with a US-listed registered
 security; its shares trade on the KRX and its ADRs are unsponsored/OTC, which requires no
 Exchange Act periodic reporting. **Tizen revenue, Samsung TV Plus revenue, Samsung Ads
 revenue and Samsung smart-TV installed base are therefore NOT OBTAINABLE from any SEC
 filing.**
2. **The same is true of LG Electronics Inc.** Its last SEC filing of any kind was in 2008
 (a SUPPL packet); it has never filed a 20-F. **webOS revenue, LG Channels revenue and LG
 smart-TV installed base are NOT OBTAINABLE from any SEC filing.**
3. **LG Display Co., Ltd. (NYSE: LPL) does file 20-Fs**, the most recent on 2026-04-28, but **it is the wrong entity**: LG Display is the LCD/OLED *panel* manufacturer, a
 separate listed company. It does not own webOS, does not sell TV advertising, and its
 20-F is not a substitute. Naming it here is a statement of what is obtainable, not a
 substitution.
4. **What IS obtainable about Tizen and webOS from EDGAR:** only what *other* filers say
 about them. EDGAR full-text search (`sources.fts_count`) returns **85 documents
 containing "Tizen"**, **390 containing "webOS"**, **159 containing "Samsung TV Plus"** and
 **87 containing "SmartCast"**, every one of them a third party's characterisation, not
 the OS owner's own reporting. A hit count is a prompt to read, never a test; none of these
 was opened for this row because none of them would be the operator of the platform
 speaking about its own economics.

### THE MOAT-CLASS CONSEQUENCE, STATED (not concluded)
Samsung and LG are, by unit share, the **two largest smart-TV operating systems in the
world outside North America**, and Roku's own filing names TV brands with their own OS as
competitors (see the SUBJECT'S OWN COMPETITION LANGUAGE below). For these two, the framework
has **no filed installed base, no filed OS revenue, no filed OS gross profit and no filed
competitor statement**. Under the framework's four verdicts this is the distinction the run
must make explicitly:
- **UNRESEARCHED** would require naming the document that resolves it. For a US-filing-based

 disclosures, K-IFRS annual reports) are **not** on the SEC citation path, are not in the
 currency-of-earnings sovereign chain used here, and were not read.
- **The honest label for the Samsung and LG rows of this competitor row is therefore
 UNKNOWABLE-from-SEC.** The run must then decide whether a moat claim about a global TV

## THE SUBJECT'S OWN COMPETITION LANGUAGE: WHO ROKU SAYS IT COMPETES WITH
*(From the Roku FY2025 10-K, acc. 0001628280-26-008114, included because Q2's competitor row
is a relative claim and the subject's own naming is the cheapest available cross-check on
whether this row covers the right peers.)*

Item 1, Competition, verbatim: *"The TV streaming industry is highly competitive and, as it
continues to evolve, we will continue to face strong competition in every aspect of our
business. **We compete with much larger companies which have resources and brand recognition
that pose significant competitive challenges.**"*

The risk factor is where the names are, verbatim, **and it names five of this row's peers**:
> "**Large companies such as Amazon, Apple, and Google offer TV streaming devices** that
> compete with Roku streaming devices and those of our licensed Roku TV partners and the Roku
> TV OS. **Google licenses its Android operating system software** for integration into smart
> TVs, including those of certain of our existing TV partners, and service provider set-top
> boxes, and **Amazon licenses its operating system software** for integration into smart TVs
> and sells Amazon-branded smart TVs. **We also face increased competition from Walmart**
> (which makes and sells Onn. branded streaming products, including co-branded Roku TV
> models), **in light of its acquisition of Vizio** (which makes and sells smart TVs with a
> proprietary operating system) **and the integration of Vizio's operating system into Walmart
> products instead of other third-party proprietary operating systems.** These companies have
> greater financial resources than we do and **can subsidize the cost of their streaming
> devices or licensing arrangements to promote their other products and services**, which
> could make it harder for us to acquire new users, retain existing users, increase Streaming
> Hours, and monetize our streaming platform."

And on the distribution leg, verbatim:
> "...we compete for Streaming Hours with many TV brands, **including certain of our existing
> TV partners**, that offer their own TV streaming solutions within their TVs... Similarly,

> "We operate in a highly competitive advertising industry and compete for revenue from

> adversely affect our ability to compete for advertisers and may result in **lower revenue
> and gross profit from advertising**."

**Two things in Roku's own filing that the run should not miss:**
1. **Roku names its TV licensing partners as competitors**, verbatim: *"our existing licensed
 Roku TV partners may... at times our existing licensed Roku TV partners have chosen to work

 operating system developers."* The licensees are also the rivals.
2. **Retailer concentration**, verbatim: *"Amazon, Best Buy, Target, and Walmart in total
 accounted for **81% of our Devices revenue** for each of the years ended December 31, 2025
 and 2024."* **Two of the four retailers that sell 81% of Roku's devices. Amazon and
 Walmart, are named by Roku as operating-system competitors in the same document.**

**Roku's own stock-performance peer group**, verbatim, for comparison with the substitutes
chosen in this row: *"Our Peer Group consists of Alphabet, Inc., Fox Corp, fuboTV, Inc.,
Interpublic Group of Companies, Inc. ("IPG"), LiveRamp Holdings, Inc., Magnite, Inc., Meta
Platforms, Inc., Netflix, Inc., Paramount Global, Pinterest, Inc., Pubmatic, Inc., Snap, Inc.,
Tradedesk Financial Corp, Vizio Holding Corp, Walt Disney Co., and Warner Bros. Discovery,
Inc. Vizio Holding Corp was acquired in fiscal year 2024 and IPG was acquired in fiscal year
2025, and the data for these companies has been included in the graph below through the last
trade date."* **Roku's own chosen peer group contains Alphabet and Netflix but NOT Amazon,
Apple, Samsung, LG, Comcast or Charter**, i.e. it is an advertising peer group, not an
operating-system one, and it still carries a company that stopped existing in 2024.

### THE NAMED LIMITS OF THIS ROW, IN ONE PLACE
