## GATE 1 — CIRCLE OF COMPETENCE
Japan's largest security services company — home and commercial security
monitoring, guard services, and an expanding presence in medical/nursing
and insurance-adjacent services. Security monitoring is a genuinely
recurring-revenue, subscription-like business model (customers pay ongoing
monitoring fees, not one-time hardware sales) — a business type this
framework treats favorably elsewhere (recurring/subscription economics),
though this specific comparison is general reasoning, not a corpus
citation. **VERDICT: PASS** — one of the more structurally attractive
recurring-revenue business models in this Japan batch.

## GATE 2 — MOAT
Franchise test: needed/desired — yes, security is a genuine, non-
discretionary-feeling need once adopted; no close substitute — **real**:
once a home or business installs a Secom monitoring system, switching
providers involves real disruption/cost (equipment removal/reinstallation,
re-establishing monitoring-center relationships) — a genuine, plausible
switching-cost moat, similar in kind to ADT-style security businesses in
Western markets (a general industry analogy, not independently verified
for Secom's own customer-retention data this pass); unregulated — largely
yes. **Class: NARROW-to-WIDE** — recurring monitoring revenue plus real
switching costs is one of the stronger moat cases in this batch, alongside
Nihon Kohden's medical-device switching costs and Unicharm's demographic-
tailwind consumer staples case.
**VERDICT: PASS with NARROW-to-WIDE classification** — genuinely one of
the more attractive qualitative cases in the Japan batch.
