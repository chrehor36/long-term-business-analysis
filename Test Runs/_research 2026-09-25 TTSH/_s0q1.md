## Q1 — CAN I UNDERSTAND HOW THIS MAKES MONEY? **[E3-31]**
- **Unit economics in my own words.** It buys tile, stone and vinyl from about 200 overseas and domestic makers (top ten
  43.3% of purchases, largest 12.7%; 44% of 2025 purchases from North America, 31% Europe, 17% Asia, 7% South America),
  lands them at three distribution centres (Michigan, Oklahoma, Virginia), mixes its own grout and thinset there, and sells
  through **140 leased showrooms of about 20,000 square feet in 31 states** to homeowners and the tradespeople they hire.
  Gross margin is high (**63.8% in 2025, 65.7% in 2024, 68-73% before 2021**) because it imports direct and sells own-label
  (Rush River, Fired Earth, Superior). Almost all of that gross margin is then spent on a largely fixed store base: **SG&A
  $220.8M in 2025, 65.6% of sales** (wages for 1,196 employees, rent on $134.8M of operating lease liabilities, delivery,
  marketing, depreciation). Profit is the thin spread between a high gross margin and a high fixed cost, so it swings with
  store traffic: **operating income $22.6M on $394.7M of sales in 2022, minus $5.8M on $336.8M in 2025.** Demand follows
  remodelling, which the company tracks through *"existing home sales trends as a leading indicator"*.
- **The scarce input this business controls.** None that the filing shows. The stores are leased (*"All of our stores are
  leased"*), the suppliers are replaceable (*"We believe that alternative and competitive suppliers are available for many
  of our products"*), and the brands are own-label names on imported product. The candidate is the direct-sourcing
  relationships and the showroom format; whether that is a moat is Q2's question, not Q1's.
- **Will the fundamentals look broadly the same in ten years?** Yes. Tile is sold through showrooms, home centres and
  independents now and was twenty years ago; a showroom importer's economics (landed cost, rent, labour, traffic) do not
  depend on a technology that could change underneath it.
- **The going-dark fact is not a Q1 fact.** It changes what can be known about the business from here on, which bears on
  Q3 (candor), Q4 (the evidence for survival) and Q6 (whether a thesis-breaking metric can ever be read). It does not make
  a tile showroom harder to understand.
