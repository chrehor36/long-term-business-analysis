## THE FOUNDATIONS (not a gate)
A share is a business. The test is the question "Would I be happy buying this stock if the market closed for five years?" **[M1997-109]**, and the question below is what the asset "itself, produces", not what the next buyer pays **[M1997-052]**. Two further foundations bear hardest on this name.

**No macro forecast enters.** The company's own case leans on defence budgets, the UK's Defence Investment Plan and a "civil nuclear renaissance" (results statement, 22 June 2026). Those are forecasts of what governments will do, and "macro conclusions are — just never enter into the discussion." **[M2000-094]**. What counts is "the average profitability of the business over time and how strong its competitive mode is" **[M2015-016]**.

**Who is paid to tell you.** The annual report and the results statement lead with "underlying" figures, and in FY26 with figures "excluding Type 31 charge". Those are management's own numbers, and the executives' pay is measured on them (Q6). The rows ask who gains by the framing: "you do not get impartial advice from Wall Street" when "there’s (an) enormous amount of fees possible from one action" **[M2020-037]**.

**Contrary evidence, written down as found** **[M1997-127]**:
1. The FY21 report found "material errors which impact the prior period", and the FY22 report restated FY21 a second time (AR2021 p.174; AR2022 p.159 and note 3).
2. The Financial Reporting Council sanctioned the former auditor over the FY17 and FY18 audits (Final Settlement Decision Notice of 8 March 2023, AR2023, Audit Committee report). A press report found by web search (July 2026; Bloomberg Tax, City A.M. and others; not a filing, flagged) says it fined the same firm again over the FY19 and FY20 audits.
3. The FY22 report names "the past practice of period-end working capital management (principally creditor deferrals and debt factoring)" (AR2022 p.31). The cash flows of the years before FY22 were therefore dressed at the year-end.
4. In FY26 a second loss charge of £140.0m was taken on the Type 31 frigate contract, after an earlier charge in FY24.
5. The submarine-support contract (FMSP) in the deciding part ended on 31 March 2026. It is on a six-month bridge while its replacement, "Gateway", is negotiated with the only customer. The AGM update of 16 September 2026 still describes Gateway as being "finalised".
6. A subsidiary was fined €51m by the Italian Competition Authority in February 2019 over a helicopter association's "tariff list" (AR2019 note 32). The fine was later annulled and recalculated, and about £15m was paid in FY22 (AR2022 p.31).

## THE STANDING RULE
The rule binds the buyer, not the target. A part-interest bought for cash, unlevered, and sized so that a fall of half or more could be sat through, does not "risk what we have and need for what we don’t have and don’t need" **[M2012-081]**; "Never risk permanent loss of capital." **[L2023-005]**; "borrowed money has no place in the investor's tool kit" **[L2014-005]**. Nothing in Babcock's own debt (Q9) reaches the buyer of its shares. No breach.
