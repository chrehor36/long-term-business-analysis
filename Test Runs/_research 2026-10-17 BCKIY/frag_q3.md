## Q3 — HOW MUCH CAPITAL MUST GO IN to get the earnings out, and what does the added capital earn? WEIGHING.
"does it look like it has good economics? Has it earned high returns on capital?" **[M1995-051]**, on "the capital actually needed in the business" **[M2010-090]**, with goodwill set aside for the business and counted for the allocation of capital **[M2011-060]**.

- **Capital actually needed, FY26** (AR2026 p.198, GBP m). The tangible operating assets are property, plant and equipment 601.3, right-of-use assets 248.5, inventories 206.9, trade receivables 552.1 and contract assets 352.8: 1,961.6 in all. Against them stand trade payables 1,041.5, contract liabilities 786.2 and provisions 201.5: 2,029.2. **Net tangible operating capital is about −£68m.** The customer and the suppliers fund the working capital and more. On the fixed assets alone (PPE plus right-of-use, £849.8m), underlying operating profit plus joint ventures of £300.7m is a 35% pre-tax return, and 52% before the Type 31 charge. The return on the capital the business needs is high. "a monopolistic position" is the first reading the rows say to rule out or own **[L1994-009]**; here it is owned. The return is a near-monopoly's return on a buyer-funded base, set in negotiation with that buyer (Q2).
- **To stand still and to grow** **[L2000-035]**, **[M2000-144]**. Net capital spending ran ahead of depreciation and amortisation (excluding right-of-use and acquired intangibles) in every year of the window, in GBP m: FY22 135.2 against 74.4; FY23 86.2 against 84.9; FY24 111.8 against 67.3; FY25 122.2 against 78.3; FY26 107.2 against 85.2. The company says "Net capex [...] remains ahead of depreciation as we continue to invest across the portfolio" (results statement p.13). New leases added £73.3m in FY26 and £87.2m in FY25 on top. My maintenance guess is the depreciation charge, about £80m a year. The excess, about £35m a year over the window, is growth spending (Rosyth manufacturing, aircraft for new training and helicopter contracts).
- **What the added capital earned.** Underlying operating profit rose from £237.7m (FY22) to £433.3m before the Type 31 charge, or £293.3m after it (FY26). Over the same years the company's invested capital fell from £1,478.7m to £992.3m (AR2023 and AR2026 ROIC tables), because of disposals, customer advances and the end of pension catch-up. The incremental return is therefore not computable as a positive ratio. Profit grew while measured capital shrank.
- **The capital the past demanded.** The pension schemes took £515m of cash above the income-statement charge over FY22 to FY26 (151.7, 141.9, 107.6, 90.3 and 23.4). That was capital the owners had to put back for promises already made, and the company expects about £20m a year from now on (results statement p.17).
- **The capital that was bought and lost.** Goodwill was £2,608.8m in FY17 and £779.8m in FY26. Most of the gap is the FY20 and FY21 impairments, £1.3bn of them in Aviation and Land (AR2021, Audit Committee report), on businesses bought in the decade before. Counted, as the rows say to count it, when judging the allocation of capital **[M2011-060]**, the decade's record is a large destruction of the owners' capital (Q6).
- **Unprecedented returns.** The KPI return rose from 17.4% to 37.0% in three years. The rows "would not want to buy things on the basis that these returns would be sustained" **[M1998-016]**. Part of the rise is the smaller denominator after the write-off, and part is the final, best-margin year of a contract (FMSP) now being renegotiated.

**WEIGHS FOR, as a second best.** The business needs almost no capital of its own to run, because its customer funds it, and so it "gives you more and more money" without much "putting up" **[M1998-081]**. But the return is a negotiated one, as the second best of **[M2018-055]**, and the decade's record on bought capital weighs against the people who allocate it (Q6). The "little or no debt" half of the criterion is applied at Q9.
