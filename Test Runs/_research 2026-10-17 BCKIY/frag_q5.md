## Q5 — WHO RUNS IT: able, honest, in love with the business, the same after being paid. STOP on integrity.
**The people.**
- David Lockwood was chief executive from September 2020 to 31 July 2026 and stays until January 2027.
- Harry Holt has been chief executive since 31 July 2026. He was chief executive of the Nuclear sector for three years before that and deputy CEO from 1 February 2026 (AGM update; AR2026 p.167).
- David Mellors has been finance director since November 2020.
- Dame Ruth Cairnie has chaired the board since April 2019, so she chaired it through the FY20 accounts that the regulator later found deficient in their audit, and through the review that corrected them (AR2026 Board biographies).

No current executive director served before September 2020.

**The two yardsticks** **[M1994-008]**. On *how well they run it, against the hand dealt*: they were dealt a company with £1.7bn of overstated assets, dressed working capital, deficient controls and pension deficits. Over FY22 to FY26 they sold non-core businesses (proceeds £447m in FY22 alone), repaid £0.9bn of borrowings, closed the defined-benefit schemes to accrual, and raised the underlying margin before the Type 31 charge from 5.8% to 8.2%. They also carried two loss charges on a fixed-price frigate contract signed before their time and still in their hands. On *how they treat the owners*, the remuneration report is read under Q6 Part B.

**The integrity record, read on doubt** **[M2013-088]**.
- *The past, not theirs.* The material errors in the FY17 to FY20 statements, the period-end window-dressing of working capital, the subsidiary's Italian antitrust fine (2019), and the regulator's sanctions against the former auditor for the FY17 to FY20 audits all belong to the management before September 2020. The present team found the errors and published them: the review of "around 100" contracts and 147 adjustments (AR2021 Audit Committee report), the second restatement (AR2022), and the end of creditor deferrals and factoring (AR2022 p.31). The FCA, which asked about the review in January 2021, "had concluded its review and did not intend to take any further action" in March 2022 (AR2022, Audit Committee report). Reputation is not honesty, in either direction **[M1995-066]**; what is read is conduct.
- *How they talk about mistakes* **[L2024-003]**, **[M1998-039]**. The Type 31 charge was announced in a trading update on 13 May 2026, before the results, with its causes: "higher than expected levels of rework as a result of changes to the design and the long-term impacts of out-of-sequence build activity" (results statement p.9). The Audit Committee reported "financial reporting errors below Group external audit materiality" in March 2023 and the root-cause work on them (AR2024). The bad news is told, and told early: "a pound of cure, promptly applied" **[M2017-007]**.
- *The habits carried from Q4* **[M2004-067]**. These are the featured figure "excluding Type 31 charge", medium-term guidance repeated each period, and a margin target reported as exceeded before the charge. Read with the rest of the record, they are a company's investor-relations habit, set beside full statutory figures printed first and a plain explanation of the charge. They are not a pattern of reports that "dance" round the figure that matters. One fact cuts against the habit being self-serving: the board charged the Type 31 loss against the executives' FY26 bonus, giving "a nil payout under the operating profit element, which is worth 40% of the bonus opportunity" (AR2026 p.154).
- *What the proxy-equivalent shows* **[M1994-009]**. In FY26 the chief executive's single figure was £8.88m, of which £6.85m was the FY24 performance share award at 95.3% vesting. The finance director's was £5.29m (AR2026 p.167). To let that award vest, the committee adjusted the margin test to exclude Type 31, noting that "The reported margin including the Type 31 impact would have been below threshold" (AR2026 p.169). That is how they "treat themselves versus how they treat the shareholders". It weighs against at Q6 Part B. It is disclosed, reasoned and consulted on, and it is not, by itself, dishonesty.
- *Searches for other integrity facts.* A web search for fines, prosecutions or investigations of Babcock in 2025 and 2026 at Devonport, Rosyth or on the Vanguard submarine found none against the company. It found the regulator's July 2026 fine on the former auditor over the FY19 and FY20 audits (press reports, flagged) and a fuel theft from a Babcock-run tanker at Devonport, undated in the report found. Principal risk 12 states that the group holds MOD and NDA indemnities for nuclear liabilities and names no current investigation (AR2025).

**VERDICT on integrity: IN.** I find no doubt about the honesty of the people now running the company. The doubts in the file attach to the management before September 2020, which this team exposed and replaced, and to pay, which is weighed at Q6. "we’re looking for the obvious cases of people you can trust" **[M2007-092]**: this is not an obvious case of the kind the speakers like. It is a case where the record of conduct in public since 2020 runs the honest way. **Ability WEIGHS FOR**: the turnaround's facts are on the balance sheet (Q4). Against it weigh the fixed-price contract they could not bring to estimate twice, and a chief executive change in July 2026 that leaves the new chief executive's record short. "if the CEO's talents or motives are suspect, today's value must be discounted." **[L2010-002]**: neither is suspect on the record read.
