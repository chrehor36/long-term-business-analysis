## Q6 — WHAT WILL THEY DO WITH THE MONEY AND THE OWNERS? WEIGHING.
"the biggest judgment you have to make is how well capital will be deployed in the future" **[M2001-111]**.

### Part A: the money
- **The decade's allocation, counted with the goodwill** **[M2011-060]**. The company spent the 2010s buying (goodwill £2.6bn in FY17), paid dividends of £132.5m to £152.1m a year in FY17 to FY20 out of cash flows later shown to be dressed (Q4), and then wrote off £1.7bn. Dividends were suspended from FY21 to FY23. That record belongs to the management before September 2020. It is counted here because it is what retained capital became: "Some companies will turn these retained dollars into fifty-cent pieces" **[L2010-002]**.
- **The retention test, FY21 to FY26** **[R1995-009]**, **[M1998-110]**. Profit attributable to owners over FY22 to FY26 was £753.2m (164.2, −35.0, 165.7, 247.1, 211.2). Dividends were £69.9m, so about £683m was retained, before buybacks of £186.6m. The market value went from about £1.16bn (228.6p at 31 March 2021, Yahoo close, aggregator, flagged, × 505.6m shares) to about £5.73bn (1,158p at 31 March 2026 × 494.6m). The market leg passes many times over. The rows warn that the market leg alone misleads; the rise in price came mostly from a re-rating of UK defence names, which no row credits to management. The intrinsic leg is read at Q7: at the central range's top of 998p, the business is worth about £4.9bn, still far above the £1.16bn of 2021. The retention of FY22 to FY26 has passed, on the present team's own record.
- **Uses now** (AR2026 p.8, "Our capital allocation framework"): organic investment first, then balance-sheet strength and an investment-grade rating, the ordinary dividend, and then "selective M&A where value-accretive, and shareholder returns". The dividend rose 15% to 7.5p. Pension catch-up has ended.
- **Buybacks.** £200m was announced in June 2025 and completed on 28 April 2026 at 16,954,061 shares. The FY26 part was 12,543,295 shares at a cost of £152.6m, an average of about **1,217p**; the April 2026 part was about 4.41m shares for about £47.4m, about **1,075p** (AR2026 p.180 and note 23). A further £200m was announced on 13 May 2026 "to be executed over the course of the year ended 31 March 2027" (AR2026 note 32). **No price is stated** above which purchases stop, in any document read **[L2016-002]**. The company does not state its estimate of value to the selling holders either **[L1999-023]**. *Read against Q7:* the prices paid, about 1,217p and 1,075p, sit far above the bottom of Q7's central range, **632p**, and above its top, 998p. Read by what they did, the purchases did not buy below "intrinsic value, conservatively-calculated" **[L1999-023]**: "what is smart at one price is dumb at another" **[L2011-003]**. **Weighs against.**
- **Issuance and deals.** There has been no all-stock deal and no share issue in the window: share capital has been £303.4m (505,596,597 shares) in every balance sheet from FY17 to FY26 (figures.csv). The narrower STOP does not arise.

**Part A: WEIGHS AGAINST.** The present team's retention has passed, and its debt repayment and pension clean-up were sound. But it has twice spent £200m on buybacks with no price limit, at prices above any value this run can defend, and it lists acquisitions as a further use.

### Part B: the pay, the board and the owners
- **Pay against what the person controls** **[M2003-019]**. The bonus is 80% underlying operating profit and underlying operating cash flow, 20% non-financial. The FY24 performance share plan was three-year organic revenue growth, weighted underlying margin, cumulative underlying free cash flow and two non-financial measures (AR2026 pp.154 to 169).
- **The partner in both directions** **[L1994-020]**. For FY26 the committee let the Type 31 charge reduce the bonus, nil on the profit element. For the FY24 performance shares it did the opposite. It "should adjust the margin calculation to exclude the impact of Type 31", although "The reported margin including the Type 31 impact would have been below threshold". It said it anticipated "taking the same approach when assessing the outcomes of the FY25 PSP and the FY26 PSP" (AR2026 pp.154 and 169). The award vested at 95.3%, valued at £6.85m for the chief executive and £3.97m for the finance director. The report says £4.91m and £2.85m of those values are share-price appreciation over the period (p.167). A downside the executives' own contract produced was removed from the long-term award, which is the larger and longer one. Weighs against.
- **The ride on the market.** The performance shares carry no strike price. They were granted in September 2023, when the share price was about 380p to 413p (Yahoo closes, 25 to 29 September 2023, aggregator, flagged), against the 1,381.7p three-month average used to value them; the report attributes most of their value to the price rise. That is the "lush gains" of an award that does not reflect "the fact that retained earnings are building up the wealth of the company" **[L1994-021]**, taken further: no strike at all.
- **Other terms.** The FY26 bonus deferral was waived and the bonus paid all in cash because the directors exceed their holding requirements (p.154). The incoming chief executive received in FY24 a restricted stock award "without any performance" conditions, as the report puts it (p.169). The chief executive's accommodation benefit was £98k.
- **Ownership.** The chief executive holds 1,029,845 shares and the finance director 718,804, both above their requirements (p.177). The rise came largely from vested awards; whether any were bought with their own savings was not traced. The chair holds 120,000 shares. Most non-executives hold none or a few thousand.
- **The board.** The chair is separate from the chief executive. Directors' fees were not found to be large against the directors' circumstances; not tested further.

**Part B: WEIGHS AGAINST.** The pay committee adjusted a measure for the executives' benefit where the reported figure would have failed the test. Its discretion runs one way on the larger award.

**WEIGHS AGAINST, on both parts.**
