**What the figures say, what they do not say, and what they cannot say** **[M2025-032]**:
- *Equity against goodwill.* In FY17 equity of £2.7bn was nearly all goodwill and acquired intangibles (£3.2bn), so tangible equity was negative. The write-downs of FY20 and FY21 brought the books to what the acquisitions were worth. Equity today, £629m, is still less than goodwill plus intangibles (£914m). The equity was rebuilt from £229m to £629m by profits, a pension remeasurement gain in FY22 and disposals. It was not rebuilt by a run of retained earnings: retained earnings are −£1.3bn.
- *Receivables and inventory against sales.* Current receivables and contract assets were 19.5% of revenue in FY17, 22.8% in FY18 and 17.5% in FY26. The FY18 peak sits in the years the company later called "period-end working capital management". Inventories are steady at 3% to 4% of sales. Nothing builds "suspiciously high" today **[M1995-064]**.
- *Contract liabilities* doubled from £396.5m (FY21) to £786.2m (FY26). That is the customer paying ahead. It funds the business (Q3), but it also means cash flow runs ahead of profit while advances build, and behind it when they unwind. FY26's cash conversion of 119% included a £95.5m increase in contract liabilities that came from the Type 31 revenue reversal. The company itself shows conversion at 84% excluding it (results statement p.13).
- *Debt.* Borrowings fell from £1.7bn (FY22) to £0.8bn (FY23), paid from disposals. Net debt excluding leases was £22.7m at 31 March 2026.
- *What they cannot say.* The decade's statements before FY21 cannot be read at face value. The FY21 contract profitability and balance sheet review made 147 adjustments and found "material errors which impact the prior period" (AR2021 p.174 and the Audit Committee report). FY21 was restated again in AR2022, "from further scrutiny of prior periods and benefited from refreshed challenge from the new auditors" (AR2022 strategic report). The former auditor was sanctioned for the FY17 and FY18 audits, with the Vanguard refit, the MSDF naval-base contract, the RD57 Rosyth settlement, pass-through revenue on the army vehicle contract and goodwill testing among the matters (AR2023, Audit Committee report). The FY22 report confirms "the past practice of period-end working capital management (principally creditor deferrals and debt factoring)". The AR2024 Audit Committee report records "March 2023 financial reporting errors below Group external audit materiality". The audit moved from PwC to Deloitte (FY22) and to Forvis Mazars (FY25) after a tender.

**The real costs.**
- Depreciation is a real cost and is charged in full; capital spending exceeds it (Q3).
- Stock pay is expensed: £17.2m in FY26, £14.3m in FY25.
- Pension deficit repair is real and large, £515m over FY22 to FY26.
- The specific adjusting items between statutory and underlying profit are now small: FY26 statutory £305.1m against underlying £293.3m, the difference being the amortisation of acquired intangibles (£10.0m) offset by gains on derivatives and on a loan recovery.
- Restructuring was charged as exceptional in FY21 and FY22 (£33.8m in FY22), not since.
- EBITDA appears only as the covenant measure (net debt/EBITDA 0.2x), not as earnings. "The one figure we regard as utter nonsense is the so-called EBITDA" **[M1998-086]**; it is not featured here, and that weighs for.

**The tells, as the accounts show them today.**
- *Profits that may not exist, and estimates that move.* About £50m a year of revenue comes from re-estimating prior-period performance obligations (£50.1m in FY26 before Type 31, "broadly in line" with FY25). The company calls this "a normal part of our trading" (AR2026 note 3). Against it stand two Type 31 charges in three years, the latest £140.0m, £95.5m of it as a revenue reversal. Long contracts estimated to completion are the rows' named case: "anything where you’ve got construction in progress or progress payment-type things — there’s so many ways you can cheat in accounting" **[M2013-086]**. No sign of cheating was found in the current accounts. The estimates have been wrong in both directions under the present team, and they say so.
- *The big bath.* In FY21 the new management took £1.6bn of impairments and £274.7m of contract-review charges. The rows' suspicion of a bath is that costs are "dumped into a single quarter" to be released later **[L1998-031]**, and that a reserve raised at a change of control lays "the groundwork for future infusions of" earnings **[L1998-034]**. A release of the FY21 reserves into later earnings was looked for. No instance was found in the provisions table (provisions rose from £113.5m in FY22 to £201.5m in FY26) or in the segment reviews read. The prior-period re-estimates above are the only channel through which such a release could show, and the notes do not tie them to FY21.
- *An adjusted figure featured* **[L2016-006]**. The FY26 headline leads with "Underlying operating profit excluding Type 31 charge" of £433m, and the bullet list's first profit figure is that one. The statutory figures are printed first in the results table. A charge on the same contract in two of the last three years is a real cost, not an unusual one. Weighs against; carried to Q5.
- *Guidance and targets* **[L2019-006]**, **[L2002-041]**. Medium-term guidance is given and repeated: "average mid-single digit organic revenue growth, underlying operating margin of at least 9% and average underlying operating cash conversion of at least 80%". The FY26 statement reports the margin "exceeding our target of 8.0%" (before the Type 31 charge). Weighs against; carried to Q5.
- *Clear speech.* The financial review explains the Type 31 charge, the cash-conversion effect of the reversal, the pension schemes and the covenant adjustments in plain terms. That is the plus of **[M1994-018]**: a management that "does explain to you in clear terms what’s going on".

**The recast earnings for Q7.** Owner cash after every real cost is in Step 0 and `scripts/owner_cash.py`: FY22 −205.3, FY23 65.9, FY24 148.0, FY25 139.1 and FY26 244.6 (GBP m, equity-only), against the company's own "underlying free cash flow" of −191.3, 75.3, 160.4, 153.4 and 261.8. The difference is mainly the stock-pay deduction and exceptional cash flows. The FY22 figure was driven down by the unwinding of the dressed working capital of earlier years, a VAT deferral, factoring and an accelerated pension payment (AR2022 pp.30 to 31). That is the filer's own tie of one year's draw to named events, which Q7(a) and (d) take up.

**VERDICT on the accounts: WEIGHS AGAINST; not a STOP.** The confusion lies in the statements before FY21. The present statements correct those years, disclose how, and can be read: statutory and underlying profit are close, and cash reconciles to the balance sheet. Suspicion is not raised by the tells that live in today's accounts. Prepaid or deferred accounts are not building; there are no reserves moving with a share sale; no released bath reserves were found; and pensions and options are charged. What weighs against is the history, the estimate risk on fixed-price contracts, the featured figure before the Type 31 charge, and the guidance. Short of confusion or suspicion, Q4 weighs and does not exclude **[M1994-018]**. Carried to Q5: the featured adjusted figure, the guidance and the target reported as exceeded.
