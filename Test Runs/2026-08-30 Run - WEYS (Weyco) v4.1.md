## Q1 — CAN I UNDERSTAND HOW THIS MAKES MONEY? **[E3-31]**

- **Unit economics in my own words:** Weyco buys finished men's shoes from ~80 Asian
  factories (China and India dominant; its two largest suppliers, both in China, each
  exceed 10% of purchases), puts four brand names on them — Florsheim (dress, $92.0M
  2025 wholesale), Nunn Bush (opening-price casual, $45.4M), Stacy Adams (dress/fashion,
  $44.6M), BOGS (winter/outdoor boots, $32.0M) — and wholesales them through 10,000+
  footwear, department and specialty store doors in the US and Canada (78% of sales),
  sells direct on its websites (13%), and runs a small Australia/South Africa arm (9%).
  The spread is the brand premium over landed cost: 43.2% consolidated gross margin in
  2025, S&A ~33% of sales, ~10.6% operating margin. 569 employees worldwide. Capital
  needs are almost nil: capex $1.0-1.8M/yr against $2.6M depreciation; one owned
  distribution center in Glendale, WI; the inventory book ($49-128M across the cycle) is
  the working asset. No debt; $98.1M cash+securities at 2026-06-30.
- **The scarce input the business controls:** four legacy trademarks (carried at $32.9M)
  and 130 years of shelf relationships in the moderate-price men's channel. Nothing
  else: manufacturing is outsourced, the channel is the customer's, and the product
  category is undifferentiated at the mainstream price points.
- **Ten years:** shoes will still be bought from Asia, branded, and sold — the mechanics
  are stable; the open questions (category units, channel, tariffs) are Q2/Q4 matters,
  not intelligibility failures.
- **VERDICT: [x] IN** — among the simplest businesses this project has run [E3-31].
