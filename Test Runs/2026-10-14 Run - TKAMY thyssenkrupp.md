## THE FOUNDATIONS (not a gate)
A share is a business: the question is whether I would be happy owning this stock "if the market closed for five years" **[M1997-109]**. The market was loud in the year read: the chairman's letter reports that the share price "increased by around 240%" in fiscal 2024/25 (AR p.6). That is the market serving a price: "it doesn’t tell us anything. It just tells us prices." **[M2006-077]**, and "the fact that a given asset has appreciated in the recent past is never a reason to buy it" **[L2013-007]**. No macro view enters; the Iran conflict, tariffs and the EU steel measures appear in the filings and are read only as facts about the business **[M2000-094]**. The analyst's habits govern the reading: look for "what’s wrong in things" and "what you’re missing" **[M2025-013]**, and the worst anchor "is always your previous conclusion" **[M2016-054]** (see the contamination declaration in the self-audit: I saw another steel company's box in a commit subject before starting). **Contrary evidence, written down as found** **[M1997-127]**: (1) owner cash was negative over the five-year window, and the company's own free cash flow before M&A was negative in six of the nine years FY2017 to FY2025; (2) the group survived FY2019 only by selling its best business; (3) the company will not write a margin target for Steel Europe ("Target for adjusted EBIT margin subject to the business plan being prepared", AR p.26); (4) the target it does write for Materials Services is an adjusted EBIT margin of 2% to 3%, below the decade margin of every rival read; (5) the group is being split into stand-alone companies, so the past statements describe a group that will not exist in ten years; (6) for the steel route change, "Prices for hydrogen supplies may be higher than assumed because there is no functioning hydrogen market" (AR p.202).

## THE STANDING RULE
Nothing in owning a part-interest need put the buyer at risk of ruin if it is bought without borrowed money and sized so that a large fall can be borne; the rule is the buyer's conduct: "borrowed money has no place in the investor's tool kit" **[L2014-005]**, and "Never risk permanent loss of capital." **[L2023-005]**. Noted for the buyer: the target's own value is exposed to the loss of a whole segment (Steel Europe) and to pension obligations larger than its net cash, so any position would be sized on the view that it could fall to a fraction; "it’s our job to figure out what can really go wrong" **[M2012-081]**.

**Q1 on the deciding part, Materials Services.**
- *Where it will be in ten years; the key variables.* A distributor and processor of materials, "one of the world’s leading materials distributors and service providers", core markets North America and Europe (AR p.23); 7.6 million tons sold in FY2025 (p.51); sales EUR 11.4 billion. Dependence on a commodity price is never by itself a STOP at Q1 or Q2 (Q2, A commodity business); Q1 asks whether the business can be read off its figures: "if you can’t make a decision on something like PetroChina off the figures, forget about going further" **[M2008-069]**.
- *Do the past statements tell me the future ones?* **[M2008-033]** Yes, in the plain sense: ten years of the segment (FY2016 to FY2025) show a thin-spread business with an EBIT margin of 1.5% of sales over the decade, one price spike (FY2021 and FY2022, 4.8% and 5.9%) and a loss in FY2020. Its economics are a spread over metal, not a technology.
- *Would the insiders write it down?* **[M2000-105]** They do: the medium-term target is "Adjusted EBIT margin of 2% to 3%" (AR p.26), and the FY2025/26 forecast for the segment is written as a range, EUR 200 to 250 million (9M report p.23).
- *Is it important and knowable?* **[M2006-076]** The deciding question for this part, whether it holds a cost or service position that protects its returns, is knowable from its own and its rivals' filings, and it is Q2's.
- *Ownership:* the extraordinary general meeting of August 7, 2026 approved the spin-off of the segment (tk accelis) with thyssenkrupp keeping 51% and full consolidation (9M Note 17); the part's economics do not change by it.

**Contrary evidence for Q1, written down as found** **[M1997-127]**: the part that does not decide on EBIT, Steel Europe, cannot be foreseen ten years out on the company's own words. It gives no margin target (AR p.26); its route is being replaced by a hydrogen-capable direct reduction plant whose fuel has "no functioning hydrogen market" (p.202); the free allocation of CO2 certificates "is to be phased out gradually" (p.198); the EU Steel Safeguard was to expire in mid-2026 and "it is currently hard to gauge whether the EU will replace it" (p.198); the Jindal talks were paused on May 2, 2026 (H1 report p.7). **The castle tests on the deciding part.**
- *The castle questions; the money test.* What keeps it standing is a network of sites and supply-chain services (AR p.23). The filings show no barrier a funded entrant could not cross: the segment's own growth in North America is built by buying and opening sites (Cobotix in Canada, a new site in New Mexico; AR pp.7, 51), which is exactly what an attacker would do. "Normally, if you’ve got a profitable business, you know, a dozen people want to go into it." **[M2000-077]**; here three listed rivals and a European peer are already in it.
- *Pricing power.* None shown. The segment's margin follows metal prices: 4.8% and 5.9% in the FY2021-22 price spike, 1.5%, 0.07% and 0.7% in FY2023-25, when "In the case of key products such as stainless and finished steel, both prices and volumes were lower than in the prior year" (AR p.51). A business with pricing power holds its margin when its input price falls; the test is "the agony they go through in determining whether a price increase can be sustained" **[M2005-020]**, and over time "the businesses with strong competitive positions manage to pass through increases in raw material costs" **[M2005-017]**. This one passes through nothing it can keep.
- *The low-cost position, read from the operators' filings.* The deciding question for a commodity business is the cost position, "we would measure it, probably, more by cost of production" **[M2006-004]**, and "The figures are available." **[M2009-059]**. On the figures, tk Materials Services earned the lowest margin of the four over the decade, about a sixth of Reliance's and a third of Ryerson's, and its own management's written target ("Adjusted EBIT margin of 2% to 3%", AR p.26) sits below the decade margin of every rival read. It is the high-cost operator of its field: "commodity businesses have risk unless you’re the low-cost producer, because the low-cost producer can put you out of business" **[M1997-010]**; "the guy with the lower cost comes in and kills you" **[M2001-013]**.
- *The brand and the low bid.* The products are standard steel, stainless, aluminium, copper and plastics; the filing names no product the customer asks for by name. The customer who buys a standard metal "for the low bid" is the failing answer **[M2017-009]**; "most insureds don't care from whom they buy" **[L2004-003]** is the row's form of the same fact.
- *Widening or narrowing.* Narrowing on the segment's own figures: the margin fell from the FY2017-18 level (1.7% to 1.9%) to 0.07% and 0.7% in FY2024-25 outside the spike, and management describes "restructuring measures in Germany with the goal of improving the cost base" and "renegotiation of contracts with major customers" (AR p.51), work that repairs a position rather than widens one. The 9M FY2026 improvement (adjusted EBIT EUR 178 million against 82) is credited by management to cost cuts and "increased prices for key product groups" (9M report p.8), a price effect, not a moat; a change for the better is credited only on a full cycle.
- *Ask the competitors.* Not possible from the record; the filings of the rivals show who the low-cost operator is.

**The parts that do not decide, read for the record.**
- *Steel Europe*: a commodity producer of flat steel whose own management is cutting capacity "to increase the segment’s competitiveness and profitability" (AR p.27) and seeking "a competitive cost level" (letter, p.6); five-year EBIT EUR (1,448) million on average capital employed of about EUR 4.3 billion (EBIT/CE (6.7)%); EUR 4.1 billion of impairments; capex of EUR 4.35 billion against depreciation of EUR 0.97 billion over the five years. - *Marine Systems*: submarines and naval vessels, order backlog about EUR 20 billion (9M report p.20), adjusted EBIT margin 5.8% in 9M FY2026 against a target of more than 7%; a castle may stand here (few builders, sovereign customers), but it carried EUR 368 million of the five-year EBIT and is 49% owned by others since October 2025.
- *Decarbon Technologies*: project engineering and electrolyzers; adjusted EBIT negative in 9M FY2026; its electrolysis business lives on a market the company calls immature. Not read further.

**VERDICT on Q2: OUT.** The deciding part is the high-cost operator in a commodity field, on the evidence of its own and three rivals' filings over a decade, and its management writes a target below every rival's decade margin; a castle shown open on the evidence closes OUT, and "In an unregulated commodity business, a company must lower its costs to competitive levels or face extinction." **[L1994-035]**. The moat that would protect "excellent returns on invested capital" **[L2007-004]** is not there; the returns the part earned (5.6% to 7.8% pre-tax on capital over ten years) are ordinary at best, and "you can have only two competitors and they’re still terrible businesses" **[M2013-052]**. A low price does not reopen it: "What you can’t do is turn any investment into a good deal by paying little" **[M2019-015]**.
