## Q2 — IS IT A FRANCHISE? **[E3-03]**

*Every AYI figure below is read from the filed 10-Ks for FY2006 to FY2025 and the 10-Q to 2026-05-31 (Items 1 and 1A, the MD&A net-sales, gross-profit and segment discussions, the statements and the segment and disaggregation notes), or from SEC XBRL companyfacts checked against the filed FY2025 statements (Step 0) and, for FY2006-FY2009, from the filed FY2008 and FY2009 statements. Extractions in `Test Runs/_research 2026-09-26 AYI/`: `xbrl.py` (`xbrl_series.json`), `roc.py` (`roc_out.txt`), `peers/lsiseg.py`, and the peer filings in `peers/`.*

**The hypothesis I formed while reading, stated so it can be attacked [E4-26]:** *Acuity is the most profitable fixture maker in North America, but it sells a product its customers can and do substitute: its own 10-Ks for twenty years say the market is "highly competitive", name "hundreds of manufacturers", record prices cut when LED costs fell and when competitors attacked the basic end, and warn that the market will not let it keep price when costs fall or pass on more than cost when they rise. That is [E3-43]'s "business" that earns exceptional profits as the low-cost, best-run operator, not a franchise; the competitor row should show Acuity far ahead of its peers, and the pricing record should show that the lead is made in cost and execution, not in price.* The evidence against it, above all the competitor row, is hunted and answered before the verdict.

**[E3-03], criterion by criterion.** *"An economic franchise arises from a product or service that: (1) is needed or desired; (2) is thought by its customers to have no close substitute and; (3) is not subject to price regulation. The existence of all three conditions will be demonstrated by a company's ability to regularly price its product or service aggressively and thereby to earn high rates of return on capital."*

- **(1) Needed or desired [x]: passes.** Every building is lit; fixtures are bought in every construction, renovation and maintenance cycle (FY2025 10-K: new construction and renovation markets *"about the same"* in size).
- **(3) Not subject to price regulation [x]: passes.** No tariff, fee or cost rule sets the price; energy codes set efficiency, not price (Item 1, Regulations).
- **(2) Thought by its customers to have no close substitute [ ]: FAILS, in the registrant's own description of its market and its prices, year after year.**
  - *The market, in the company's words.* FY2015 10-K Item 1: *"The lighting market served by the Company is highly competitive, with some of the largest suppliers of lighting components also serving many of the same markets and competing for the same customers. ... The Company's largest competitors in the North American lighting market include the lighting divisions of Koninklijke Philips N.V., Eaton Corporation, Hubbell Incorporated, Schneider Electric, Cree, Inc., and General Electric Company. The Company estimates that the largest publicly traded manufacturers (including Acuity Brands), which participate in varying degrees in the North American lighting market, have over half of the total market share. In addition to these large competitors, the Company also competes with hundreds of manufacturers of varying size."* FY2025 10-K: *"We operate in highly competitive industries"*; competition *"based on numerous factors, including product vitality, service capabilities, price, brand name recognition ..."*; *"there are competitors, including importers outside of North America, small startup companies, and global electronics, technology, and software companies, offering competing solutions"*; and no single patent matters (*"no single patent or patent application is individually material to us"*).
  - *The price, in the company's words.* FY2025 Item 1A, carried in substance since FY2021: *"Aggressive pricing actions by competitors may affect our ability to manage the price/cost relationship to achieve desired revenue growth and profitability levels. ... Even if we were able to increase prices to cover our costs, competitive pricing pressures may not allow us to pass on any more than the cost increases. Alternatively, if costs were to decline, the marketplace may not allow us to hold prices at their current levels."* That sentence is the negation of [E2-44]'s first characteristic, written by the registrant about itself.

**The remaining Q2 tests.**
- **[E2-44], the two characteristics.** (1) *"an ability to increase prices rather easily (even when product demand is flat and capacity is not fully utilized) without fear of significant loss of either market share or unit volume"*: the registrant writes that it may not be able to pass on *"any more than the cost increases"*; when it raised prices (FY2019, FY2022) volume fell or the margin fell, and the retail and corporate-accounts channels halved between FY2019 and FY2025. **THE STRONGEST EVIDENCE AGAINST THIS VERDICT, hunted hardest [E4-26, E3-41, E4-51].**
1. **The competitor row.** Acuity earns 1.5-3 times every filed peer's margin for seventeen years; peers lost money, sold out at under one times sales, or earned mid-single digits. A moat is a claim about relative position [E3-28], and on relative position Acuity wins by a distance. The project's precedents lean on exactly this: ITW (2026-08-31, WIDE) on returns *"two to five times the peer set's"*; PNR and CSL (NARROW) on the same kind of row.
2. **The returns.** 44-82% pre-tax on tangible operating capital and 15-30% on total operating capital including every acquisition premium, every year FY2008-FY2025. [E3-03] says the conditions are *"demonstrated by"* high returns; [E3-46] asks the returns question about the business before the manager.
3. **FY2024 and after.** Gross margin rose 310 basis points *"due primarily to favorable material and import costs"*: costs fell and the price did not, the opposite of the FY2013-FY2018 behaviour and of the filer's own risk factor. ABL's margin reached 16.6% in the nine months to May 2026. A pricing power that has only recently shown itself would be [E3-33]'s class arriving.
4. **The consolidation.** Of the six largest competitors the FY2015 10-K named, Cree, Eaton and Hubbell have sold their lighting businesses and Philips's became Signify; the structure is concentrating, and Acuity is the one left standing with its margins.
5. **[E3-47]**: a wrongly closed file on an understood business is the costliest error, and this business is understood.

**Answered.** (1) The row is recorded and it is the strongest evidence in the file: Acuity is the best operator in its industry by a wide margin, and I say so plainly. [E3-03] needs criterion (2), and the row does not supply it: it compares companies selling interchangeable fixtures through the same distributors, and it shows who runs that business best, which [E3-43] says a "business" can do *"for a much longer time"* *"with superior management"* while *"unceasingly"* facing attack. (4) Consolidation among competitors is a change in the industry's structure, which [E3-61] says the row cannot turn into a prediction of conduct; the *"hundreds of manufacturers"* and the importers remain, and the retail and corporate-accounts channels, where substitution is easiest, are the ones that shrank.
