## Q1 — CAN I UNDERSTAND HOW THIS MAKES MONEY? **[E3-31]**
- **Unit economics in my own words, no management language.** Acuity designs and makes light fixtures and the electronics inside and around them (LED drivers, sensors, lighting controls) for offices, schools, hospitals, warehouses, shops, streets and homes, in North America mainly; since 2016 it has also sold building-management controls (Distech), and since January 2025 audio-video-and-control systems for rooms and venues (QSC). The lighting segment (ABL, $3,612M of FY2025's $4,346M) makes about three quarters of what it sells in its own plants (*"Manufactured ... Total 76 %"*, of which Mexico 55%) and buys the rest from contract makers (Asia 15%). A fixture reaches a building in one of two ways: on a construction or renovation project, an independent sales agency (paid commission) gets the fixtures written into the lighting designer's or engineer's specification, the contractor buys them through an electrical distributor, and they ship from Acuity's plants or distribution centres; or it is bought off the shelf through a home-improvement retailer, by a national chain renovating its stores (*"corporate accounts"*), or by another manufacturer (OEM). Demand follows non-residential and residential construction and renovation (*"Demand for our products is highly dependent on economic drivers"*; *"the size of the markets to be about the same"* for new construction and renovation). The money is a gross margin on each fixture (47.8% of consolidated sales in FY2025; ABL 45.8%), less selling, distribution and administration (34% of sales: agents' commissions, freight, the distribution network, engineering), leaving ABL an operating margin of 16.4% (FY2025) on a small tangible capital base: net property, plant and equipment $343M and working capital (receivables plus inventories less payables) of about $666M at FY2025 against $4.3bn of sales. The intelligent-spaces segment (AIS, $764M) sells controls, software and QSC's audio-video systems through system integrators, at a 55.5% gross margin and a 10.0% operating margin after the QSC acquisition accounting.
- **The scarce input this business controls.** **Not a resource; a position: the largest North American fixture catalogue sold through the densest agent-and-distributor network, with its brands on specifiers' drawings** (Lithonia Lighting, Holophane, Gotham, Juno and some twenty more). The filing names nothing scarcer: *"no single patent or patent application is individually material to us"* (about 1,700 patent assets), raw materials *"are generally available from multiple sources"*, and competition runs on *"product vitality, service capabilities, price, brand name recognition, product quality, product and system design, energy efficiency, and customer relationships"*. Whether that position is a franchise is Q2's question; Q1 only records that nothing the company owns is scarce in the way a licence, a site or a patent is.
- **Will the fundamentals look broadly the same in ten years?** **The way it earns, yes; the product inside it, it has already changed once.** The FY2006-FY2008 10-Ks describe the same business model (Lithonia, Holophane and the other brands; independent agents, electrical distributors, Home Depot, national accounts; plants in the US and Mexico). Between about 2010 and 2018 the product itself changed from lamp-and-ballast fixtures to LED fixtures with electronic drivers (FY2015 10-K: *"the adoption of solid-state lighting"*; the FY2013-FY2017 MD&As attribute price changes to *"the continued decline in the cost of purchased LED components"*), and the channel, the customers and the margin structure came through it. The next ten years: fixtures will still be specified, sold and installed through the same channel; the company is adding connected controls, building software and audio-video (AIS, 18% of FY2025 sales), where it says *"the market for artificial intelligence and software solutions is active with a wide range of competitors"*. The lighting business is the mechanism a run can understand from the filings; AIS is a newer, smaller leg whose durability is a question for Q2 under the [E4-04] ruling.
- **Relatively simple and stable in character [E3-31]?** The lighting business is simple: make fixtures cheaply in Mexico, sell them through a channel that specifies them, collect a gross margin. Its demand is cyclical (FY2009-FY2010 and FY2020 revenue fell with construction) but its character has been stable for twenty filed years, through a complete technology change in the product. Not a business that would take months to understand [E4-46].
