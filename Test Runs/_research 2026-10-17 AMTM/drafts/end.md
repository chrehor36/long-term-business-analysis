The framework gives no span or measure for "failing" (net contract losses, organic revenue, backlog). 2. **The business-of-parts threshold names "five-year operating profit"**, but this filer reports segment profit only as Adjusted EBITDA, and on the current segment basis for four periods only. I used the filer's segment measure to size the parts, never as earnings; the framework does not say whether a non-GAAP segment measure may size parts.
3. **A reverse acquisition inside the window.** The legal acquirer (the CMS company) issued the shares; the accounting acquirer (Legacy Amentum) is the predecessor whose statements the 10-K carries. The merger rule (a deal paid in shares, valued on the combined averages) fits, but the framework does not say how to fill a year one half never filed (CMS's FY2024); a CONVENTION of the run was needed.
4. **The serial-acquirer test does not say whose purchases count.** The $2,661M of purchases in the window were made by Legacy Amentum's private-equity owners before the company existed in its present form; the test, applied as written, sent the central figure to the organic pair.
