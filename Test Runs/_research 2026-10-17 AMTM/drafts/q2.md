**The castle tests, each with its filing fact (GES, and the company where the filer does not split).**
1. *The castle questions: the key factors and how permanent.* The filer names its own: "technical capabilities, successful performance history, qualified/security- cleared personnel, reputation with customers, price, geographic presence, and size" (10-K, Item 1, Competitors). For GES the relationships are long: "many decades in the U.K." and "nearly 30 years in Australia"; the site list is the large DOE and NDA cleanups (Sellafield, Savannah River, Hanford, Oak Ridge, Portsmouth, Idaho; Item 1). How permanent: the filer's own answer is that every position is awarded for a term and then competed again: contracts "typically involve an annual base period of performance followed by renewal option periods" (Item 7, Critical Accounting Policies), "substantially all of our contracts [...] are subject to cancellation, termination, or suspension at the discretion of the customer" (Item 1), and the customer may "attempt to “insource” the services to government employees" (Item 1A). None of the named factors belongs to this company alone: Item 1 names sixteen rivals in the U.S. government market (nine services firms and seven large defense contractors), five in the U.K. defense market and seven in the global environmental business.
2. *Would it stand without the lord?* Yes; nothing in the filings ties the work to one person. This test passes.
3. *The money test.* "if I had a hundred million dollars and I wanted to go in and take on See’s Candy, could I do it? [...] If the answer had been yes, we wouldn’t have done it." **[M2011-015]** The filings answer it twice, and not in the company's favour. The filer reports "takeaway wins against longtime incumbents on Missile Defense Agency (“MDA”), the United States Space Force (“USSF”), and NASA programs" (Item 1): in this field an incumbent's position is taken by a rival who bids for it. And the company itself was assembled with money in under five years: a private-equity partnership bought AECOM's Management Services business in January 2020 (Item 1), then PAE (the FY2022 revenue bridge credits "the acquisition of PAE contributing $1.5 billion"; information statement), then merged with CMS. Buying positions is not attacking them, so this second fact weighs less than the first.
4. *Pricing power, and the agony before a rise.* "you can almost measure the strength of a business over time by the agony they go through in determining whether a price increase can be sustained" **[M2005-020]**. Here the company does not set its price at all: on cost-plus work (56% to 69% of revenue in the years read) the customer reimburses audited cost "plus a fee (contract profit)"; on fixed-price and time-and-materials work the price is fixed at the bid for the period of performance (Item 1); and "Most government contracts are awarded through a rigorous competitive process, which may emphasize price over other qualitative factors" (Item 1A). The test fails: there is no price to raise.
5. *Unit volume.* The measure the filer gives is revenue and backlog. Legacy Amentum's FY2023 bridge: "a full year of performance on legacy PAE contracts contributing $0.9 billion as well as new contract awards and growth on existing programs of $0.5 billion, partially offset by the completion of certain contracts which totaled $1.2 billion, including a follow-on contract which transitioned from a consolidated joint venture to an equity method investment" (10-K FY2024, MD&A). The FY2022 bridge: PAE $1.5 billion, new awards and growth $0.7 billion, completions $0.4 billion (information statement). FY2024 revenue rose 6.6% on "new contract awards and growth on existing programs" (10-K FY2025, MD&A). The CMS half grew from $5,080M to $5,525M over FY2021 to FY2023. In the nine months of FY2026 GES revenue fell 7%, on JV transitions, a divestiture and "the expected ramp-down of historical programs", partly offset by new awards; total backlog rose from $44.6B to $48.2B in the year to 2026-07-03 (10-Q, MD&A). Mixed: one year of net losses (FY2023), years of net gains, and a growing backlog.
6. *The low-cost position.* Not readable. On cost-plus work a lower cost lowers the fee earned on it, so the company's margin, below every filing rival's in the competitor row below, cannot be read as a cost position either way; the filer's SG&A was 3.5% of revenue in the quarter to 2026-07-03 against 4.6% a year earlier, on merger savings (10-Q), which is the only cost fact filed.
7. *The brand in the customer's mind.* No instance in the filings: the customer chooses by technical evaluation, past performance and price (Item 1A), not by name.
8. *Would the customer still choose it over the low bid?* Partly. Price "may" be emphasized (Item 1A), and task orders under multiple-award vehicles bring "significant additional pricing pressure" (Item 1A); but past performance and cleared staff also count, so the government does not simply take the low bid either.
9. *Ask the competitors.* Not available from the filings.
10. *Widening or narrowing?* **[M1999-108]** Narrowing signs: Legacy Amentum impaired goodwill on a reporting unit in FY2022 ($108M) and FY2023 ($186M) (information statement; 10-K FY2024); the CMS half's Cyber & Intelligence unit carried "cumulative impairment losses of $304 million" before FY2021 (information statement, risk factors); the 10-Q names "NASA’s increased focus on insourcing certain activities". Widening signs: backlog up 8% in a year; the takeaway wins above. No row-quality test separates them: the speakers say the moat is judged, not measured **[M1999-108]**.
11. *What could destroy, modify or reduce it, five to fifteen years out?* **[M2000-014]** The filer names them: insourcing, termination for convenience, small-business set-asides that "restrict our ability to recompete on incumbent work", budget cuts or shifts (the GFY2026 request "reduced non-defense discretionary spending by approximately 21%", Item 7), and for GES the risk that the U.S. and U.K. governments "de-emphasize nuclear matters" (Item 1A). Each is a decision of the one customer.

**The field's returns over the span, judged in words** (framework Q2, CONVENTION: no figure). On the capital the work needs **[M2010-090]**, the field earns excellent returns and has for ten years in all three rivals' filings: the customer pays as the work is billed and the work needs almost no plant, so tangible capital is a few hundred million dollars against billions of revenue. On what the buyers of these businesses paid (goodwill counted), the returns are good to ordinary, 12.7% to 27.1% pre-tax at the rivals and 10% to 13% at Amentum and its halves. So this is not "the standing castle that protects only ordinary returns": the returns are not ordinary, and that OUT does not apply. Amentum's margin is the lowest of the row in every year read. Its growth apart from purchases (Legacy Amentum about +5% in FY2022 and about −9% in FY2023 on the bridges above, +6.6% in FY2024; the CMS half +4.3% a year over FY2021 to FY2023) cannot be set cleanly against the rivals' totals, which include their own purchases.

**What the evidence shows, and what it does not.** The excellent returns on tangible capital belong to the field, and they are made by the customer's way of paying (a fee on audited cost, or a bid price for a term), not by anything this company owns that a rival lacks. The company's hold on any one stream of them ends at each recompete: "A moat that must be continuously rebuilt will eventually be no moat at all." **[L2007-005]**. The filer's own words describe exactly that rebuilding, its "business development engine" (Item 1) and its exposure to protest, set-aside and insourcing. But the framework sends the rebuilt moat to OUT only "where the evidence shows the rebuilding failing" (Q2, What it rules OUT), and over FY2021 to FY2025 the evidence does not show it failing: one year of net losses (FY2023), years of net gains, backlog growing, margins steady in a band. Nor does the evidence show it holding: no filing read states the recompete win rate, the schedule on which GES's largest contracts come up for award, or how the predecessors' positions fared through a full cycle of awards before FY2021. "Leadership alone provides no certainties" **[L1996-031]**, and I cannot say from what I have read whether GES's positions are the kind that last or "Impostors".

**Why not OUT.** The castle is not shown open on the evidence: no test shows GES's positions being taken faster than they are won over the span, and the field's returns are not ordinary. The pricing-power test fails, and the money test leans against, but a business whose price is set by its customer for a fee on cost can still hold its positions for decades if the customer keeps renewing it; that is the question, and the filings read do not answer it.

**Why not IN.** No castle test passes except the lord test. The returns the field earns are protected by the customer's procurement rules, which the customer can change (insourcing, set-asides, the fee policy), and the company's share of them must be re-won every few years against named rivals with the same qualifications. "when we see a moat that’s tenuous in any way [...] it’s just too risky. We don’t know how to valuate that, and therefore we leave it alone." **[M2000-019]**

**Which cause.** The deciding question, whether GES keeps its large positions through recompetes at the fee the customer allows, is important and knowable **[M2006-076]**: the federal award record and the U.K. NDA's awards are public, the predecessors' segment histories are filed by Jacobs and AECOM, and the rivals' filings may state recompete experience. The insiders would write it down: a contractor's recompete calendar is a planning document of the trade. So the cause is the work not done, not the nature of the industry: **TOO HARD (WORK)** (framework section I, the two causes, CONVENTION).

**VERDICT: TOO HARD (WORK)**, deciding part GES. The castle's future cannot be judged from the filings read **[M2000-019]**, **[M2006-013]**: the pricing-power test fails **[M2005-020]**, the money test leans against **[M2011-015]**, the moat is one that must be continuously rebuilt **[L2007-005]**, and the evidence over FY2021 to FY2025 neither shows the rebuilding failing nor shows it holding.
