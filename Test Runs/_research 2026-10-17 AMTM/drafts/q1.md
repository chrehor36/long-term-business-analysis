## Q1 — CAN I UNDERSTAND IT? Where its economics will be in ten years, inside my perimeter. STOP.
**The lines of evidence, chosen from the 10-K's Items 1, 1A and 7 (the brief's stated departure: the analyst derives them).** I chose five, because these are the five things the filer itself says decide its revenue, its margin and its cash: (1) who pays and how: the U.S. federal government, 81% of FY2025 revenue, under cost-plus-fee (63%), fixed-price (24%) and time-and-materials (13%) contracts (Item 1); (2) how work is won and lost: competitive award, task-order competition, protests, small-business set-asides, termination for convenience and insourcing (Item 1A); (3) the two segments and their margins (Item 7, segment Adjusted EBITDA); (4) backlog and its funded part (Item 7); (5) the debt and the cash (Item 7, Liquidity; Item 1A, indebtedness; the MARPA receivables sales). Lines (1), (3) and (5) bear on Q1, Q3, Q4 and Q9; line (2) is the castle and is Q2's; line (4) is evidence for both.

**The test as the draft states it.** Understanding is "a reasonable fix on about what the earning power and competitive position will look like in five or 10 years", with "some notion of how the industry will develop and where the company will stand within the industry" **[M2012-065]**; the first step is "to identify the key variables in that particular business, and evaluating how predictable they were" **[M1998-044]**.

**The key variables, from the filings.**
1. *What the customer spends on outsourced services in these markets.* The filer names the departments (DOE, the Intelligence Community, the Department of War, NASA, DHS; the U.K. MOD and NDA; the Australian DOD). The level moves with appropriations, and the filer itself says "levels of U.S. federal government spending are difficult to predict and subject to significant risk" (Item 1A). This is a macro variable, which the foundations keep out as a forecast **[M2000-094]**; what is readable is its nature: a large, continuing, politically allocated budget that has bought these services for decades (Item 1 names predecessors back a century: EG&G, Morrison-Knudsen, URS, Lear Siegler, DynCorp, PAE and Jacobs's government business).
2. *The share the company keeps at recompete and wins new.* This is the castle question and is owned by Q2.
3. *The fee the customer allows.* Cost-plus contracts pay "reimbursement of our direct contract costs and allocable indirect costs, plus a fee (contract profit)"; fixed-price contracts carry the overrun risk; time-and-materials contracts pay fixed hourly rates (Item 1). The result is a margin in a narrow band: cost of revenues was 90.1%, 90.5% and 89.5% of revenue in FY2023, FY2024 and FY2025, and 89.9% in the nine months of FY2026 (10-K and 10-Q, MD&A). Segment Adjusted EBITDA margins were 7.2% to 8.5% in each segment and period read (FY2023 to FY2025 and nine months of FY2026) (GES $437M on $5,966M in FY2023 and $667M on $8,850M in FY2025; DS $437M on $5,543M in FY2025). The CMS half ran at operating margins of 7.0%, 5.5% and 5.9% in FY2021 to FY2023 (information statement: operating profit $355M, $287M, $328M on revenue $5,080M, $5,176M, $5,525M). This is the most predictable of the four, because the customer sets it by contract type.
4. *The capital the work needs.* Capital spending was $12M, $11M and $27M against revenue of $7,865M, $8,388M and $14,393M in FY2023 to FY2025 (10-K FY2025, cash flow statement): the work is done by people, paid as they bill.

**Do the past statements tell me the future ones?** **[M2008-033]** Mostly yes, with two stated limits. They are the statements of a services contractor whose revenue is labour and pass-through cost and whose profit is a fee on it; they are readable. The limits: the company is two years old, so the combined statements cover FY2025 and nine months of FY2026 only and the predecessors must be added by hand (Step 0); and the filer warns that classified work "may limit our ability to provide information about these classified programs" (Item 1A), which bears mostly on DS.

**Is it important and knowable?** **[M2006-076]** The deciding variables (the fee band, the capital need, how work is won) are knowable from the filer's and the rivals' filings. The budget level is important and not knowable; the rows keep it out of the decision as a forecast **[M2000-094]**, and it is carried as width at Q7.

**Would the insiders write it down?** **[M2000-105]** The field's insiders do: the filer reports $48.2B of backlog at 2026-07-03, of which $6.2B funded (10-Q, MD&A), and the field's economics (a fee on cost, little capital) are stated in every rival's filing read at Q2. Nothing here turns on a technology no insider would forecast: the technology words of Item 1 (hypersonics, AI, 5G) describe customers' programmes on which the company supplies labour and engineering, not a product the company must invent.

**The industry or the company?** **[M2012-067]** Seeing the industry is not seeing the company. Where this company will stand inside it is the Q2 question, and Q1 passes it there.

**Parts.** GES carries more than half of segment Adjusted EBITDA in every year read (FY2023 $437M of $596M, FY2024 $459M of $618M, FY2025 $667M of $1,104M, nine months of FY2026 $504M of $828M; 10-K FY2025 and 10-Q, MD&A), so GES decides under the business-of-parts convention (framework Q1 and Q2, CONVENTION). GES (environmental remediation for DOE and the U.K. NDA, nuclear services, platform engineering, sustainment and supply chain) is labour and engineering services on government sites and can be understood by the tests above. DS (intelligence analytics, cyber, IT, space systems) is the part the classified-programme warning touches most; it does not decide.

**Doubt.** "if you have doubts about something being into your circle of competence, it isn’t" **[M2002-092]**. My doubt is not about how the business makes money, which is plain, but about whether this company's share of it lasts; that is the castle, which Q2 asks. I record it there rather than pass it here on the plainness of the business alone, since what would bother me is "if I think I understand a business and I don’t" **[M1997-024]**.

**VERDICT: IN.** The economics of the deciding part, a fee on cost for labour and engineering on government sites with almost no capital, can be pictured ten years out within a range **[M2012-065]**, **[M2011-084]**: the margin band and the capital need are set by the customer's contract types and held in the filer's and its predecessors' statements in every year read; the budget level is width, not understanding **[M2000-094]**. Where the company will stand within the industry is passed to Q2 **[M2012-067]**.
