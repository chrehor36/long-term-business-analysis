## GATE 2 — MOAT
Franchise test [E3-03], from Lowe's own 10-K: **"The home improvement
industry includes a broad competitive landscape that continues to evolve.
We compete with national and regional home improvement warehouse chains
and lumber yards in most of the markets we serve. We also compete with
traditional hardware, plumbing, electrical, drywall and home supply
retailers and distributors, as well as paint stores, lumber yards, garden
centers, and maintenance and repair organizations. In addition, we compete
with general merchandise retailers, home goods specialty stores, warehouse
clubs, online retailers, other specialty retailers, providers of equipment
and tool rental, service providers that install home improvement
products, and wholesale..."**
- **No close substitute**: **weak on a pure product basis**, but Lowe's
  and Home Depot together dominate the big-box home-improvement category
  in the US (general public knowledge, not independently confirmed via
  market-share data this pass) — a real, durable scale/distribution/
  purchasing-power advantage against the long tail of smaller named
  competitors, even though direct duopoly rivalry between the two majors
  remains intense.
**Class: NARROW** (scale/distribution advantage within a two-player-
dominated category), **Direction: STABLE**.
**VERDICT: PASS with NARROW classification.**

**Fortress — the negative-equity question, addressed directly rather than
treated as an automatic disqualifier**: Stockholders' equity has been
**negative in every year shown** (-$9,917M FY2026, -$14,231M FY2025,
-$15,050M FY2024, -$14,254M FY2023) — driven by buybacks that have
exceeded cumulative retained earnings, the same pattern already seen at
HP Inc. (run separately today, eliminated at Gate 2 on moat grounds before
this question mattered) and — per this project's own prior work — Home
Depot (eliminated under the OLD pre-Ruling-2 net-cash-positive rule,
explicitly flagged in this project's records as never having been
re-examined under Ruling 2's survival-track redefinition). **This run
applies the same survival-track logic Ruling 2 was designed to enable**:
negative book equity from buybacks is not itself a Gate 4 disqualifier —
the test is whether the business can service debt and fund operations
through a 50% OE decline for 2 consecutive years without existential risk.
Lowe's generates $7.5-8.7B/year of OE even at the low end, carries a
long-established, widely known investment-grade credit profile (general
knowledge, not independently re-verified against a fresh ratings pull this
pass), and — like Home Depot and Sherwin-Williams before it in this
project's history — represents exactly the kind of resilient, mature,
cash-generative industrial/retail business Ruling 2 was created to stop
eliminating on a raw balance-sheet-ratio basis alone.
**VERDICT: PASS**, explicitly on survival-track grounds — negative book
equity named and reasoned through, not glossed over or used as an
automatic fail.
