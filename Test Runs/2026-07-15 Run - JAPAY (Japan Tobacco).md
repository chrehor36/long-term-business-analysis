## STEP 0 — RATE REFRESH

## GATE 1 — CIRCLE OF COMPETENCE
Global tobacco company — dominant in the Japanese domestic cigarette market
(the Japanese government retains a significant ownership stake in Japan
Tobacco, a real, disclosed structural fact, not independently re-verified
for current stake size this pass) and owns major international brands
(Camel, Winston, Benson & Hedges outside the US, acquired from RJR
Nabisco's international business decades ago) plus a smaller pharmaceutical
and processed-food business. Tobacco economics (addictive/inelastic demand,
pricing power, regulatory/litigation risk) are a well-understood business
category — this is one of the more genuinely familiar business models in
this Japan batch. **VERDICT: PASS.**

## GATE 2 — MOAT
Franchise test: needed/desired — yes in the specific sense that addicted
consumers have inelastic demand (a well-established characteristic of the
tobacco category generally, not unique research); no close substitute —
**real, strong** — brand loyalty among smokers is genuinely sticky, and
tobacco is one of the classic examples of a consumer-staples-style pricing-
power business (this framework's own corpus repeatedly cites Coca-Cola-style
brand economics; tobacco is a recognized analog in the broader value-
investing literature, though not specifically discussed in the BRK corpus
checked this pass — flagged as general knowledge, not a corpus citation);
unregulated — **no**, tobacco is among the most heavily regulated and
litigated consumer categories globally, and faces secular volume decline
as smoking rates fall in most developed markets (a real, well-known,
ongoing trend). **Class: NARROW-to-WIDE on pricing power, tempered
significantly by secular volume decline and regulatory/litigation risk.**
**VERDICT: PASS**, genuine pricing-power case (probably the strongest
"franchise-style" moat argument in this batch outside the sogo shosha),
but explicitly not treated as a clean WIDE moat given the regulatory and
secular-volume headwinds.

## GATE 3 — MANAGEMENT / FORTRESS
Not independently verified this pass. The Japanese government's ownership
stake (a well-known, long-standing structural fact) is worth naming
explicitly as a distinctive governance consideration not present in any
other company in this batch — state ownership can mean either stability
or non-market-driven strategic constraints, and this run does not resolve
which dominates.
**VERDICT: PASS (provisional)**, government-ownership structure flagged as
a unique, unresolved governance factor.

## GATE 5 — INVERSION
1. **Secular volume decline** (global smoking-rate decline, a real, well-
   known, ongoing trend — the same category of risk Buffett/Munger-style
   frameworks generally treat as manageable given pricing power offsets
   volume decline, though this is a general industry observation, not a
   corpus-sourced claim). Scored **[DS]** — already occurring, priced into
   the business model for decades, not a new or hidden risk.
2. **Regulatory/litigation escalation** (flavor bans, further tax increases,
   novel-product regulation of heated-tobacco/vaping alternatives). Scored
   **[P]**.
3. **Government-ownership strategic-constraint risk** (see Gate 3). Scored
   **[P]**.
4. Management/balance sheet: **[U]**.
**VERDICT: PASS**, with the secular-decline risk treated as a known,
long-managed feature of the business model (scored [DS]) rather than an
open question, distinct from how this run treats genuinely unresolved
risks elsewhere in the batch.
