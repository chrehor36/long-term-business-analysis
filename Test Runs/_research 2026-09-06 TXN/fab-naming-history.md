# TXN — the fab disclosure across 10-K vintages, FY2019–FY2025

Supports TASK 2. The question asked for SM1–SM4, RFAB2 and LFAB from the FY2025 10-K. **They are not in
it.** They were in every 10-K from FY2021 to FY2024 and were removed in FY2025. This file records the
whole arc verbatim so the deletion can be verified in under two minutes.

## THE ARC, VERBATIM

### FY2019 10-K (filed 2020-02-20) — one fab, unnamed
> "To strengthen this advantage, we are moving forward with our plan to build our new 300-millimeter wafer
> fabrication facility in Richardson, Texas, as 300-millimeter wafers will continue to support the majority
> of our Analog growth."

### FY2020 10-K (filed 2021-02-05)
> "To strengthen this advantage, construction is underway on our new 300-millimeter wafer fabrication
> facility in Richardson, Texas, as 300-millimeter wafers will continue to support the majority of our
> Analog growth."

### FY2021 10-K (filed 2022-02-04) — the plan is laid out in full
> "• continuing construction of **RFAB2 (Richardson, Texas)**, our next 300-millimeter wafer fabrication
> facility, to begin production in the second half of 2022;
> • purchasing a 300-millimeter wafer fabrication facility in **Lehi, Utah**, to support analog and embedded
> processing manufacturing, which is expected to begin production in early 2023; and
> • planning construction on our next two 300-millimeter wafer fabrication facilities in **Sherman, Texas**,
> to begin in 2022. **This North Texas site has the potential for up to four fabrication facilities** to meet
> demand over time, as semiconductor growth in electronics, particularly in industrial and automotive
> markets, is expected to continue well into the future. **Production is expected to begin in 2025.**"

### FY2022 10-K (filed 2023-02-03) — SM1/SM2/LFAB named for the first time
> "• starting production in 300-mm wafer fabrication facility **RFAB2 (Richardson, Texas)**;
> • starting production in 300-mm wafer fabrication facility **LFAB (Lehi, Utah)**; and
> • starting construction on our next two 300-mm wafer fabrication facilities in Sherman, Texas, **SM1 and
> SM2**. This North Texas site has the potential for four fabrication facilities to meet demand over time…
> **SM1 production is expected to begin in 2025.**"

### FY2023 10-K (filed 2024-02-02) — the fullest disclosure TI ever gave
> "We continue to invest to strengthen our competitive advantage in manufacturing and technology as part of
> our long-term capacity plan to meet demand over time. Semiconductor growth in electronics, particularly in
> industrial and automotive markets, is expected to continue well into the future. Progress and investments
> include:
> • Ramping production in 300mm wafer fabrication facility **RFAB2** in Richardson, Texas.
> • Ramping production in 300mm wafer fabrication facility **LFAB1** in Lehi, Utah.
> • Continuing construction on **SM1 and SM2** in Sherman, Texas, where we are **building four 300mm wafer
> fabrication facilities**.
> • Starting construction on **LFAB2**, another 300mm wafer fabrication facility in Lehi, Utah.
> Together, these investments are designed to strengthen our manufacturing and technology competitive
> advantage, provide us with lower costs and greater control of our supply chain, and **support growth over
> the next 10 to 15 years**."

And, in the same section:
> "In 2023, we sourced **about 80% of our total wafers and about 65% of our assembly/test production
> internally**. With our planned capacity expansions, we expect these percentages to increase."

### FY2024 10-K (filed 2025-02-14) — SM3/SM4 still absent, horizon shortened, percentages removed
> "• Ramping production in 300mm wafer fabrication facilities **RFAB2** in Richardson, Texas, and **LFAB1**
> in Lehi, Utah.
> • **Equipping SM1** and continuing construction on **SM2** in Sherman, Texas, where we are building four
> 300mm wafer fabrication facilities.
> • Continuing construction on **LFAB2**, another 300mm wafer fabrication facility in Lehi, Utah.
> Together, these investments are designed to … support growth **in the years ahead**."

> "In 2024, we sourced **the majority** of our wafer fabrication, as well as assembly and test, internally."

### FY2025 10-K (filed 2026-02-06) — the bullets are gone
The entire "Progress and investments include:" bullet list is deleted. What replaces it, complete (L211):
> "We continue to strengthen our competitive advantage in manufacturing and technology as part of our
> long-term capacity plan to meet demand over time. Semiconductor growth in electronics, particularly in
> industrial, automotive and data center markets, is expected to continue well into the future. In 2025, we
> continued qualifying and ramping production at our newest 300mm wafer fabs in Richardson and Sherman,
> Texas, and Lehi, Utah. These fabs are well positioned to support customer demand, external foundry
> transfers and internal transfers from our legacy 150mm facilities."

> "In 2025, we sourced the majority of our wafer fabrication, as well as assembly and test, internally."

## THE FOUR DISCLOSURE ITEMS DELETED IN FY2025

1. **Named fabs.** RFAB2, LFAB1, LFAB2, SM1, SM2 — every one removed. A reader of the FY2025 10-K alone
   cannot tell how many fabs are ramping, how many are under construction, or which.
2. **The construction pipeline.** FY2024 told you SM2 and LFAB2 were still being built. FY2025 says nothing
   about anything under construction. There is no way to tell from the FY2025 10-K whether SM2 and LFAB2
   are proceeding, paused, or shelved — and this matters directly, because it is the same document that
   cuts 2026 capex guidance to $2–3 billion from $4.55 billion actual.
3. **The growth horizon.** "support growth over the next 10 to 15 years" (FY2023) → "in the years ahead"
   (FY2024) → deleted (FY2025).
4. **The internal-sourcing percentages.** "about 80% of our total wafers and about 65% of our assembly/test
   production internally" (FY2023) → "the majority" (FY2024, FY2025). This was the only quantified
   in-house-versus-foundry ratio TI ever published and it is now qualitative.

A fifth deletion sits in MD&A rather than Item 1:

5. **The cumulative CHIPS Act expectation.** FY2024: "We expect to receive **between $7.5 billion to $9.5
   billion through 2034** from the CHIPS Act." The FY2025 10-K contains no such figure — 0 occurrences of
   "9.5 billion". Given that the OBBBA *raised* the ITC from 25% to 35%, dropping the cumulative estimate
   in the year it became more favourable is a disclosure choice, not an arithmetic necessity.

**Interpretation is deferred to the run file. As a bare finding: in the year TI cut capex guidance by
roughly half, it simultaneously removed the fab-by-fab construction detail, the internal-sourcing
percentages, the growth-horizon statement, and the cumulative CHIPS Act expectation from its 10-K.** Every
one of these deletions removes a number against which a future year could have been checked. That is
directly relevant to framework Q3 (are they honest, and are they rational?) — not because any statement is
false, but because the checkable content of the disclosure fell in a year when the story changed.
