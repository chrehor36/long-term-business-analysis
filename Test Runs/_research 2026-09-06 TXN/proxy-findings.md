> total compensation of all employees was approximately 272:1.
>
> There has been no change in the company's employee population or employee compensation arrangements
> that would result in a significant change in the pay ratio disclosure. Further, there has been no
> change in the circumstances of the employee identified as the median employee in 2023. Accordingly,
> the pay ratio calculation has been made using the 2025 compensation for the median employee
> identified in 2023.
>
> To identify the median of the annual total compensation of all our employees, as well as to
> determine the annual total compensation of the "median employee," we used the following methodology
> and material assumptions, adjustments and estimates to identify the median employee in 2023:
>
> • We selected October 1, 2023, as the date upon which we would identify the "median employee."
> • As of this date, our employee population consisted of approximately 34,387 individuals working at
> the company and consolidated subsidiaries, excluding employees on leaves of absence who are not
> expected to return to work.
> • To identify the "median employee," we used base salary and profit sharing information, each of
> which is paid to all employees. Salaries were annualized for all permanent employees who were
> employees for less than the full fiscal year or who were on an unpaid leave of absence during a
> portion of the year.
> • We identified and calculated the elements of the median employee's compensation for 2025 in
> accordance with the requirements of Item 402(c)(2)(x) of Regulation S-K, including the value of the
> employee's health and welfare benefits (estimated for the employee and the employee's eligible
> dependents) and retirement-related benefits.
> • We applied the exchange rate that we utilize in our payroll system, as of the identification date,
> to convert foreign currency to U.S. dollars.
>
> The annual total compensation of our CEO is the amount reported in the "Total" column of our 2025

> CEO and his eligible dependents) and retirement-related benefits. This resulted in annual total
> compensation for purposes of determining the ratio in the amount of $22,702,299.

# C. INCENTIVE METRICS — THE CENTRAL SECTION

## C.1 The annual bonus / short-term incentive: what it pays against

**Answer: it is DISCRETIONARY, not formulaic. There are no weightings, because there are no weights.**

The plan-description box, verbatim in full (2026 DEF 14A page 24, "Performance Bonus"):

> **Purpose** — To motivate executives and reward them according to the company's relative and
> absolute performance and the executive's individual performance.
>
> **Strategy** — Determined primarily on the basis of one-year and three-year company performance on
> certain measures (revenue growth percent, operating profit margin and total shareholder return¹) as
> compared with peer companies and on our strategic progress. These factors have been chosen to
> reflect our near-term financial performance as well as our progress in building long-term
> shareholder value. The committee aims to pay total cash compensation (base salary, profit sharing
> and bonus) appropriately above market median if company performance is above that of peer companies
> and pay total cash compensation appropriately below market median if company performance is below
> peer companies. **The committee does not rely on formulas or performance targets or thresholds.
> Instead, it uses its judgment based on its assessment of the factors described above.**
>
> **Terms** — Determined by the committee and paid in a single payment after the performance year.

The measures used, verbatim (page 29):

> In January 2026, the committee set the 2025 bonus compensation for executive officers based on its
> assessment of 2025 performance. In setting the bonuses, the committee used the following performance
> measures to assess the company:
> • The relative one-year and three-year performance of TI as compared with Semiconductor Peers, as
> measured by:
> ◦ revenue growth,
> ◦ operating profit margin,
> ◦ total shareholder return, and
> • The absolute one-year and three-year performance of TI on the above measures.
> • In addition, the committee considered strategic progress by reviewing company and strategic
> positioning (including ESG), operating performance, revenue growth, including TI competitiveness in
> key markets with core products and technologies, and the strength of relationships with customers.

**Metrics and weightings:** three quantitative measures — **revenue growth, operating profit margin,
total shareholder return** — each assessed on a **one-year and three-year, absolute and relative**
basis, plus a **qualitative "strategic progress"** assessment. **No weightings are disclosed and none
exist**; the proxy states the committee "does not rely on formulas."

Individual performance, verbatim:

> Before setting the bonuses for the named executive officers, the committee considered the officers'
> individual performance. The performance of Mr. Ilan was judged according to the performance of the
> company. For the other officers, the committee considered the factors described below in assessing
> individual performance. **In making this assessment, the committee did not apply any formula or
> performance targets.**

## C.2 Profit sharing — the ONE formulaic element in the program

Verbatim (page 25 box, "Profit Sharing"):

> **Strategy** — Pay according to a formula that is the same for all employees to focus them on a
> company goal, and set payout potential at a level that will affect behavior. Profit sharing is paid
> in addition to any performance bonus awarded for the year. The formula is based on company-level
> annual operating profit margin. The formula was set by the TI board. The committee's practice has
> been not to adjust amounts earned under the formula.
>
> **Terms** — Payable in a single cash payment shortly after the end of the performance year. As in
> recent years, profit sharing for 2025 was calculated as follows: If operating profit margin was:
> • Below 10%: no profit sharing
> • At 10%: profit sharing = 2% of base salary
> • Above 10%: profit sharing increases by 0.5% of base salary for each percentage point between 10%
> and 24% and by 1% of base salary for each percentage point above 24%. The maximum profit sharing is
> 20% of base salary.
> In 2025, TI delivered operating profit margin of 34.1%. As a result, all eligible employees,
> including executive officers, received profit sharing of 19.06% of base salary.

This is a genuine formulaic gate (operating profit margin ≥10%), but it is company-wide, capped at
20% of base salary, and worth only ~$247k to the CEO — **1.1% of his SCT total**.

# SUMMARY OF THE FINDINGS THAT MATTER

1. **The pay program has essentially no performance conditions.** No PSUs, no TSR modifier, no EPS or
   ROIC gate, no bonus targets or thresholds. RSUs are four-year cliff time-vesting; options vest
   25%/year over four years. The proxy says four separate times that the committee "does not rely on
   formulas or performance targets or thresholds." The annual bonus is reported in the SCT **"Bonus"**
   column — i.e. as discretionary compensation — while the only formulaic item, company-wide profit
   sharing (operating-margin-linked, 20%-of-salary cap), is what fills the "Non-Equity Incentive Plan
   Compensation" column. This is a genuinely simple, judgment-based program; whether that is a virtue
   or a governance weakness is the question, but it is unambiguously what it is.
2. **The stated corporate objective and the pay metrics do not match.** TI says "Our primary objective
   is the long-term growth of free cash flow per share," but FCF/share is nowhere in the bonus
   metrics or the Item 402(v) tabular list. The Company-Selected Measure is **operating profit** —
   a pre-capex measure, during a multi-year 300mm capex build.
3. **Bonuses rose 10% in a year in which TSR was −4.5%, below median on both the one- and three-year
   basis, and three-year revenue CAGR was −4.1%, also below median.** The "holistic" method absorbed
   the miss.
4. **Say-on-pay support is chronically weak and got weaker.** 83.0% (2026) after 87.0% (2025), 85.2%
   (2024), 84.4% (2023), 83.3% (2022) on the convention TXN appears to use. The committee's response
   is verbatim identical each year: no material changes.
5. **Insider ownership is small and mostly optioned.** The CEO's 760,717 shares include 561,511
   options exercisable within 60 days and 153,125 RSUs — roughly **46,000 shares of actual owned
   stock**. The whole board-plus-officers group holds 0.60% of the company, 0.23% net of options.
6. **Pledging: clean and affirmatively so.** "No director or executive officer has pledged shares of
   TI common stock." Hedging and pledging are both prohibited by policy for directors and executive
   officers.
7. **Two disclosure gaps:** no ownership guideline for non-employee directors at all, and no
   statement anywhere that anyone actually complies with the executive guidelines.
8. **Chairman and CEO recombined effective January 1, 2026** on Templeton's retirement; Janet Clark
   (also audit chair) is lead director.
9. **Auditor tenure 74 years** (E&Y or predecessor since 1952), with 14.6% of 2025 fees non-audit and
   all services pre-approved.
10. **Clean structural governance:** single class of stock, one vote per share, annual election,
    majority voting, proxy access, no employment contracts, no gross-ups, double-trigger CoC, 0.5%
    net annual equity dilution against a 2% cap, no related-party transactions disclosed.
