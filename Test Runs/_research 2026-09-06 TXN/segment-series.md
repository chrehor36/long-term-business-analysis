### The three carve-outs that limit what the segment note can tell you, verbatim (L742–745):

> "We report the results of our remaining business activities in Other. Other includes operating segments
> that do not meet the quantitative thresholds for individually reportable segments and cannot be aggregated
> with other operating segments. Other includes DLP® products, calculators and custom ASIC products."
>
> "In Other, we also include items that are not used in evaluating the results of or in allocating resources
> to our segments. Examples of these items include acquisition, integration and restructuring charges (see
> Note 11); and certain corporate-level items, such as litigation expenses, environmental costs, insurance
> settlements, and gains and losses from other activities, including asset dispositions. We allocate the
> remainder of our expenses associated with corporate activities to our operating segments based on specific
> methodologies, such as percentage of operating expenses or headcount."
>
> "**Costs incurred by our centralized manufacturing and support organizations, including depreciation, are
> charged to the operating segments, including those in Other, on a per-unit basis. Consequently,
> depreciation expense is not an independently identifiable component within the segments' results and,
> therefore, is not provided.**"
>
> "**With the exception of goodwill, we do not identify or allocate assets by operating segment, nor does the
> CODM evaluate operating segments using discrete asset information.** We have no material intersegment
> revenue. The accounting policies of the segments are consistent with those described in the significant
> accounting policies and practices."

### FY2025 MD&A segment commentary, verbatim (L435–456):

> **Analog** — Revenue $14,006 vs $12,161, +15%. Operating profit $5,412 vs $4,608, +17%. 38.6% vs 37.9%.
> "Analog revenue increased in both product lines about evenly due to higher demand, which was impacted by
> the macroeconomic factors discussed above. Operating profit increased primarily due to higher revenue and
> associated gross profit, partially offset by higher operating expenses."
>
> **Embedded Processing** — Revenue $2,697 vs $2,533, +6%. Operating profit $304 vs $352, **−14%**.
> 11.3% vs 13.9%. "Embedded Processing revenue increased due to higher demand, which was impacted by the
> macroeconomic factors discussed above. Operating profit decreased primarily due to higher manufacturing
> costs and operating expenses, partially offset by higher revenue."
>
> **Other** — Revenue $979 vs $947, +3%. Operating profit $307 vs $505, **−39%**. 31.4% vs 53.3%.
> "*Includes Restructuring charges/other. Other revenue increased $32 million, and operating profit
> decreased $198 million."

### The Embedded Processing explanation, verbatim (MD&A L408):

> "Our LFAB facility, which primarily supports our Embedded Processing business, was purchased as an
> operating fab and is in the early stages of ramping, so we expect factory loadings to increase over time.
> Until LFAB ramps, we expect Embedded to carry manufacturing costs that disproportionately affect Embedded
> Processing operating profit as compared to Analog."

## WHAT THE SEGMENT SERIES SHOWS

**Embedded Processing has been destroyed as a profit centre and has not recovered.** Revenue peaked at
$3,368M in 2023 and is $2,697M in 2025, 20% below peak and *below its 2021 level*. Operating profit went
$1,253M (2022) → $1,008M (2023) → $352M (2024) → $304M (2025), a 76% decline from peak while revenue fell
only 17%. Operating margin collapsed from 38.5% to 11.3%. TI's stated cause is LFAB underloading — the
$893M Lehi fab it bought in 2021 (see `acquisitions.md`) is being carried by the Embedded segment and is
still not ramped five years after purchase. That is the most concrete, most quantified consequence of the
capex cycle anywhere in the filings.

**Analog absorbed the cycle far better but has not recovered its old margin.** 54.4% operating margin in
2022, 38.6% in 2025, on revenue $647M *below* 2022. The segment is more profitable than it was in 2019–2020
in dollars but less profitable in margin than any year 2021–2023.

**Total operating profit in 2025 ($6,023M) is 41% below the 2022 peak ($10,140M) on revenue 12% below
peak.** The operating leverage worked in reverse exactly as the "Our margins vary" risk factor predicted.
