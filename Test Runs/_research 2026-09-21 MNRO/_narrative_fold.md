**Vehicles serviced −38.7% in seven years. Sales per vehicle +57.3%. Dollar sales −3.6%.**
The dollar line held because the price carried it, and the operating income fell **84.2%**
anyway. Return on unleveraged net tangible assets **[E2-43]** went **21.5% to 2.4%** (3.1%
removing the post-ASC-842 right-of-use asset so both years sit on one basis). Tangible book
equity is **negative $152.6M** against $736.4M of goodwill.

**That is Precision Steel's shape [E4-55] with one difference that makes it worse.** At
Precision Steel the price rise at least *held dollar volume level* and preserved the revenue.
Here the 57% price rise held the dollar line level **and the profit still went.** So the
pricing was not pricing power; it was cost pass-through that failed to cover its own costs,
bought by giving up more than a third of the units.

**[E3-03] closes it on the registrant's own words.** Criterion (2), no close substitute,
fails on the 10-K's Competition section: *"Our segment of the retail industry is fragmented
and highly competitive"*; the substitutes named include *"independent garages, and gas
stations"*; and *"competition in the industry is based primarily on price."* And [E3-03]'s
own demonstration test — *"a company's ability to regularly price its product or service
aggressively and thereby to earn high rates of return on capital"* — **is documented running
in reverse across seven filed years.**

### The refuted priors

1. **The ORLY distribution-density moat case does NOT transfer, and it fails in the most
   direct way possible: Monro sold its distribution.** The June 2022 divestiture of the
   wholesale operations (seven locations) and internal tire distribution to American Tire
   Distributors was accompanied by a **managed-services agreement under which ATD provides
   Monro with category management, ordering, dashboard and inventory managed services**, plus
   a distribution agreement for tire supply. O'Reilly owns its network; Monro rents the
   function from a counterparty that serves the field. **[E2-45]**'s attacker test therefore
   comes back with no grizzly at all: the entry cost is one leased building and two lifts,
   there is no proprietary part, no network effect and no switching cost.
2. **The `acq_note` understates the roll-up by a factor of nine.** It reported *"acquisitions
   are $90M, 24% of cap, inside the window"* because its window is five years. The full
   filed history, "Acquisitions, net of cash acquired" FY2010-FY2026, is **$859.7 million —
   227% of the hand-struck cap** — with peaks at $163.3M (FY2013), $142.6M (FY2017) and
   $104.4M (FY2020), **and exactly zero in FY2024, FY2025 and FY2026** while the store count
   fell 1,304 → 1,115 and 145 stores closed in a single quarter. **[E4-36]**'s fourth cause
   of extreme success — *"Catching and riding some sort of big wave"* — and **[E3-51]**'s
   surfer: *"if he gets off the wave, he becomes mired in shallows."* The wave was a
   consolidating fragmented industry and cheap private store prices. **The engine has been
   switched off for three years.**
3. **The ASC 842 discontinuity the KBH run found the day before is ABSENT here**, and the
   reason is specific rather than lucky: operating leases sat in operating cash both before
   and after adoption, and capital/finance leases put principal in financing both before and
   after (Monro already carried $238.1M of non-current capital-lease obligations at FY2019).
   **The operating-cash series is like-for-like and may be averaged across FY2020.** What did
   break is the **balance sheet** — total assets $1,312.3M → $2,049.5M on adoption — so any
   return-on-capital series crossing FY2020 is not, which is why the run's return table
   reports FY2026 twice.
4. **[E4-29] does not fire, and the negative finding is recorded rather than assumed.** The
   CGNX standing instruction was carried out early, before any manager question could be
   reached: both furnished 8-K EX-99.1 releases were pulled and **the word EBITDA appears
   zero times in either.** "EBITDAR" appears in the 10-K only inside the lenders' covenant
   definitions, which is the lenders' metric and not the company's public narrative. **The
   CGNX pattern — clean in the 10-K, firing in the furnished release — is genuinely absent.**
5. **What the release DID carry was a moat claim, so it was tested at Q2 and not at Q3.**
   Peter Fitzsimmons, Q1 FY2027: *"we were able to hold our tire unit volumes flat, and we
   believe this allowed us to take market share."* Tested rather than dismissed **[E4-26]**:
   the same release reports comparable store sales **−1.7%**, attributes the quarter to
   customers who *"traded-down to lower-cost alternatives"*, credits *"the timely expansion of
   our tier four tire offerings"*, and reports **adjusted operating income BELOW the GAAP
   figure at 0.8% of sales** with a net loss of $2.1 million. One flat quarter in units,
   achieved by trading customers down a price tier, against a seven-year 38.7% decline.
   **Recorded as tested and not sustained**, which is a different outcome from ignored.

### The (c) judgment, and why the screen's top end is not merely optimistic but invalid

The `spread_caveat` said *"CANNOT see variation older than the 5-year window; rebuild it
[E4-25]"*, and it was right. Rebuilt over **all seventeen filed years**, with (c) disclosed
as **cash capex plus finance-lease principal payments** — because **783 of 1,115 stores are
leased**, 42 more are owned buildings on leased land, and **finance-lease principal is a
FINANCING outflow that never touches operating cash** while the corresponding asset
depreciates through D&A. Monro paid **$38.7M of finance-lease principal in FY2026 against
$31.7M of cash capex**: taking the capex line alone as (c) omits more than half the capital
actually consumed.

| window | (c)=D&A, the corpus default [E3-44, E2-41] | (c)=cash capex | **(c)=capex + lease principal** |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3 years | $37.1M | $77.1M | **$37.9M** |
| **5 years [E2-42]** | **$66.4M** | **$108.6M** | **$69.3M** |
| 7 years | $70.0M | $105.1M | $68.4M |
| 10 years | $73.5M | $101.2M | $75.5M |
| 17 years | $69.3M | $85.5M | $70.4M |
| **FY2026 alone** | **$4.9M** | $34.9M | **−$3.8M** |

**My disclosed guess and the corpus's own default agree within a few million at all five
windows.** Two independently-built estimates of an unobservable landing that close is the
strongest evidence available that both point at the same number — and it means **the
cash-capex end, which produced the screen's $107M top, is INVALID rather than optimistic.**
This is the **mirror of [E5-20]**: there, depreciation understates renewal and the D&A end is
invalid; here, depreciation runs *above* cash capex precisely because the lease-financed half
of the store estate never reaches the capex line, so it is the **capex end** that is invalid.
**A new shape worth naming for the survival-shapes index: the LEASE-FINANCED ESTATE, where
(c) must be read from the financing section of the cash-flow statement or it is half a
number.** Windage count **1**, spent on the (c) judgment; no end margin, because no Bar is in
use and the file closed at Q2.

Against the hand-struck cap the yield runs from **negative to 18.6%** depending entirely on
which years are averaged. **[E4-25]**: *"Usually, the range must be so wide that no useful
conclusion can be reached."* The seventeen-year mean is high because it averages a company
that no longer exists — 21.5% returns, 6.2 million vehicles, a working acquisition engine —
with the one that filed this year's 10-K.

### Tooling defects found — four, and two of them can fire across the whole queue

1. **The `cap_flag` is a price-decline detector wearing a sanity check's label.** It said
   *"A cap cannot be smaller than a subset of itself. One of the two is wrong."* **Neither is
   wrong.** The float of $541.4M is dated **2025-09-27**, when MNRO last closed at **$18.68**;
   implied non-affiliate count $541,400,000 ÷ $18.68 = **28,983,940** against **30,019,660**
   outstanding = **96.55%**, a proper subset, and the implied cap at that date is **$560.8M,
   LARGER than the float**. The stock then fell **35.2%**. **The filed float is a cover-page
   figure measured on the last business day of the registrant's most recently completed second
   fiscal quarter — up to twelve months before the cap date.** **Fix: compare the float against
   a cap struck AT THE FLOAT DATE.** As written, this flag false-positives on every name down
   more than `1 − float/shares` since its float date, which in a drawdown is a large number of
   names. Nine or more prior runs have hit this flag; this is the first that traces why.
2. **`floor_screen.capital_acquired()` adds a NON-CASH entry to capex and calls it
   maintenance capital.** It uses `RightOfUseAssetObtainedInExchangeForFinanceLeaseLiability`
   — the gross right-of-use asset recognised **once, at lease inception** — as an
   annual capex component. For MNRO it resolves in **five of seventeen years**: $19.2M
   (FY2018), $14.6M (FY2019), **$64.4M (FY2020), $104.2M (FY2021)**, $8.8M (FY2022), and
   **nothing thereafter**. That makes (c) $120.3M in FY2020 against filed cash capex of
   $55.9M, drives FY2020 owner earnings to **−$2.8M**, which is the single negative value
   that trips the straddle-zero refusal, which produces `level_shift_oe n/a`, which produces
   `flags_disagree`. **One non-cash inception entry cascades into three CSV flags.**
   **[E2-23]** is explicit that (c) is *"the average annual amount of capitalized
   expenditures … that the business requires to fully maintain"*; a lease-inception gross
   asset is not an annual amount. **Proposed one-tag fix: use `FinanceLeasePrincipalPayments`**
   — smooth ($27.2M, $33.0M, $39.4M, $39.5M, $39.0M, $39.8M, $38.7M), cash, available every
   year from FY2020, and it lands on the corpus default. **This can fire on every lease-heavy
   filer in the queue: every retailer, every restaurant chain, every service-bay operator.**
3. **Three CSV note columns are sliced mid-sentence, and one of the truncations inverts its
   own meaning.** `Screens/regen_queue.py` writes `level_note[:60]`, `level_note_oe[:70]` and
   `acq_note[:110]`, while `wc_note` gets 260 and `cap_flag` is written in full at 252 — so
   **the two notes carrying the most consequential diagnosis get the shortest slices.** The
   CSV's `level_note_oe` reads *"the pre-window years run from $-2.8M to $1"*, which a reader
   takes as a near-zero range; the recovered full string ends at **$170.3M**. And `level_note`
   is cut at *"a tight spread her"*, losing the words *"e is not safety"* — the warning
   itself.
4. **`best_year_dep_oe` has no note column at all.** The CSV carries the bare number 0.265 and
   throws away the string the function returned: *"TWO YEARS JOINTLY CARRY THE WINDOW - the
   exact two-year-boom shape leave-one-out cannot see; re-price on a window that excludes
   both."* There is a `best_year_note` for the operating-cash series and **no
   `best_year_note_oe` for the series that is actually valued.** Defects 3 and 4 are the class
   the run queue's own FOLD section names: **a diagnostic that exists but never reaches the
   reader** — the same failure as the spread caveat living only in console output.

Also confirmed for the seventh time: **`newest_filing` carries a REPORT date, not a filing
date.** MNRO's row reads `newest_filing 2026-03-28 / newest_periodic 2026-03-28`; 2026-03-28
is the fiscal year end, the 10-K was **filed 2026-05-27**, and **both fields are a quarter
stale** against the CSV's own date of 2026-09-02 — missing the 10-Q of 2026-07-29, the filing
that carried the share count the cap needed. **MNRO is a fiscal-March filer**, `fiscalYearEnd`
0327, which is exactly the class this defect mis-sorts. And a **second cap defect the flag
never saw**: the Class C Convertible Preferred **converted on 2026-06-18** into **1,204,908**
common shares, so any cap struck on the 10-K cover count understates the shares by **4.0%**.
