## Q1 — CAN I UNDERSTAND HOW THIS MAKES MONEY? [E3-31]

- **Unit economics in my own words:** ASML builds the machines that print the circuit
  patterns on chips. It sold 327 systems in 2025 for €24.5B (about €72M average; EUV tools
  run far higher), and every machine sold joins an installed base that then pays service
  and upgrade fees for decades: €8,193.0M of service and field-option sales in 2025, a
  quarter of revenue, growing every year. Customers pay deposits years in advance to hold
  places in the queue: net contract liabilities were **€18.9B** at end-2025. So the
  customers finance the working capital, the installed base pays an annuity, and the
  company keeps a 52.8% gross margin while spending a €4.7B annual R&D toll.
- **The scarce input the business controls:** the only ecosystem on earth that can make an
  EUV machine: exclusive optics from Carl Zeiss SMT (24.9% owned, contractual exclusivity),
  the owned light source (Cymer), and four decades of integration know-how. The delivery
  queue itself is the scarce input; the €18.9B of customer deposits is the proof.
- **Ten years:** lithography has been the gating step of chipmaking for forty years and the
  filed roadmap (EUV scaling "well into the next decade", High-NA, 3D integration) runs
  past 2030. The honest residual is technology supersession (chipmaking that needs fewer
  litho exposures); it is named here and carried to Q4 as the slow death and to Q6 as the
  monitoring question. It does not make the *money-making* unintelligible today.
- **VERDICT: [x] IN**
