## GATE 1 — CIRCLE OF COMPETENCE
CDW buys IT hardware, software, and cloud/services entitlements from ~1,000+
vendor partners (Apple, Cisco, Dell, HP, Lenovo, Microsoft, etc.) and two large
wholesale distributors (Ingram Micro, TD SYNNEX — together >25% of purchases),
then resells to end customers with an advisory/integration/logistics layer on
top — either as principal (point-in-time hardware/software resale) or as agent
(netted commission on cloud/SaaS placements). Three reportable segments in this
filing — Corporate (private-sector >250 employees), Small Business (<250
employees), Public (government/education/healthcare) — plus two non-reportable
operating segments (CDW UK, CDW Canada) folded into "Other." Effective
2026-01-01 the company is realigning to Commercial/Government/Education
segments (noted, not yet reflected in these financials). Unit economics in
plain terms: buy at wholesale, mark up modestly (~22% gross margin), fund the
receivable/inventory gap between paying vendors and collecting from customers,
keep the spread after opex. Simple to understand; no informational edge claimed
or needed — this is a scale/relationship distribution business, not a
specialized-knowledge one. All five segments/geographies are the same
operating model applied to different customer verticals — each evaluable.
**VERDICT: PASS.**

## GATE 2 — MOAT
Franchise test [E3-03]: needed/desired — yes (enterprises, schools, and
governments need IT procurement and integration); **no close substitute — fails
plainly and is disclosed as failing** by the company itself: Item 1A names
"resellers, manufacturers who sell directly to customers, large service
providers and system integrators, cloud providers, hyperscaler marketplaces,
e-commerce companies, and office supply retailers" as direct competitors, and
warns that "growing hyperscaler marketplaces such as AWS Marketplace, Google
Cloud Marketplace, and Microsoft Marketplace... could change the role of
traditional resellers, which may limit access to offerings, pressure margins,
and restrict participation in certain channels." This is the company naming
its own disintermediation risk in writing; unregulated — yes, materially
unregulated (ordinary commercial/government-contracting rules aside);
**pricing power — does not hold.** Gross margin is thin and management
attributes any movement directly to competitive/mix pricing pressure ("Gross
profit margin decreased 20 basis points... primarily driven by decreased rates
in certain hardware categories").
Primary moat metric: Gross margin — 21.77% (2023) -> 21.9% (2024) -> 21.7%
(2025). Flat within a ~20bp band through a demand cycle (net sales dipped
2023->2024, then grew 6.8% in 2025) — margin did not erode with the cycle,
which is a real (if modest) resilience signal, not evidence of pricing power.
**Class: NARROW** — the real edge is switching-cost/relationship-based: 250,000+
customers, top-tier certifications with major vendors that are expensive to
replicate at CDW's breadth, and scale purchasing leverage (each of the three
largest vendor partners contributed >$2.0B of 2025 net sales). This is a
genuine structural advantage but explicitly NOT a pricing-power franchise —
the company's own risk factors concede the "no close substitute" prong.
**Direction: STABLE**, with a company-disclosed secular threat (hyperscaler
marketplace disintermediation) carried forward into Gate 5, not waved away.
**VERDICT: PASS with NARROW classification** (not wide/generational — no
Coca-Cola clause applies in Gate 6).

## GATE 3 — MANAGEMENT
CEO Christine A. Leahy: President & CEO since January 2019, Chair since
January 2023; joined CDW in 2002 as its first General Counsel, then Chief
Legal Officer (2002-2017), SVP-International (2016-2017), Chief Revenue
Officer (2017-2018) before becoming CEO — 24-year internal tenure, deep
company-specific knowledge, not a lateral outside hire. **Integrity**: no
restatement, material weakness, or SEC investigation found in this pass;
clawback policy covers restatements and misconduct; insider trading policy
"prohibits hedging or monetization transactions involving Company securities...
[and] prohibits holding Company securities in a margin account or pledging
Company securities as collateral." No integrity flags. **Competence**: durable
profitability through a demand-cycle dip (net income $1,104.3M 2023 ->
$1,077.8M 2024 -> $1,066.6M 2025 — remarkably stable even as net sales dipped
and recovered), consistent buyback activity ($500-653M/year 2023-2025) funded
from free cash flow rather than debt (except opportunistic refinancing).
**Alignment**: 91% say-on-pay approval at the most recent annual meeting
("approval by approximately 91% of the votes cast... since our IPO" — durable,
not a one-time result); CEO stock ownership guideline set at 6x base salary;
say-on-pay history strong and consistent. Combined Chair/CEO role offset by a
Lead Independent Director and all-independent committees — a governance
structure the company defends explicitly rather than ignores.
**VERDICT: PASS.**

## GATE 4 — FINANCIAL QUALITY (full OE formula, no NI proxy)
No disclosed maintenance-vs-growth capex split (CDW does not break this out —
it is a distribution business with modest owned real estate: two US and one
UK distribution centers, $171.5M net PP&E against $16.0B total assets).
**Band used: ASSET-LIGHT (P44), 20-30% of total capex treated as maintenance**
— reseller/VAR model, no manufacturing or fleet base. Primary point estimate
uses the 25% midpoint; the 30% conservative end changes OE by <$15M/year (capex
itself is only $117-148M/year against >$1B net income, so the band choice is
immaterial here — the real swing factor is working capital).

ΔWC pulled directly from the cash-flow statement's "Changes in assets and
liabilities" (AR + inventory + other assets + AP-trade + other liabilities),
not estimated:

| FY | Net income | D&A | Capex | Maint capex (25%) | ΔWC (cash impact) | **OE = NI+D&A−maint capex±ΔWC** |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2023 | $1,104.3M | $270.7M | $148.2M | $37.1M | **+$118.8M** (WC released cash) | **$1,456.8M** |
| 2024 | $1,077.8M | $275.3M | $122.6M | $30.7M | **−$162.7M** (WC used cash) | **$1,159.8M** |
| 2025 | $1,066.6M | $295.6M | $117.1M | $29.3M | **−$286.3M** (WC used cash) | **$1,046.6M** |

The declining OE trend (2023->2025) is working-capital-driven, not earnings
deterioration: net income is essentially flat across all three years; the
swing is accounts receivable building as net sales recovered (+6.8% in 2025
after a 2024 dip), a growth-funding use of cash rather than distress. The
discipline still applies — the lowest tier anchors regardless of why it's
lowest.
**LOWEST VERIFIED TIER (anchors all buy prices): $1,046.6M (FY2025).**
Equity-based compensation was $83.6M in 2025 = 8.0% of this OE figure — under
the 15% P44 threshold, no SBC excess deduction required.

Fortress check — two tracks (Ruling 2): CDW is an IT reseller/distributor, not
a bank, insurer, or other balance-sheet-levered financial business —
**SURVIVAL TRACK applies.**
- Total debt $5.6B (mostly fixed-rate unsecured notes, laddered 2026-2034: 2026
  $1,009.1M / 2027 $6.4M / 2028 $1,105.3M / 2029 $700.0M / 2030 $1,234.5M /
  thereafter $1,600.0M — no dangerous cliff concentration).
- Liquidity: cash $618.7M + $1.9B undrawn on the new (Dec 2025) $2.25B
  revolving facility = ~$2.5B available, comfortably covering the entire 2026
  maturity ($1,009.1M) more than twice over before any operating cash flow is
  counted.
- Demonstrated capital-markets access: issued $1.2B of new senior notes in
  August 2024 to refinance/extend, in compliance with all covenants as of
  2025-12-31.
- **Gap flagged, not filled**: the 10-K does not state an explicit letter
  credit rating (references "major debt rating agencies" generically but never
  names CDW's rating) — not fabricated here.
- Survives 50% OE decline x 2yr? At the lowest tier ($1,046.6M), a 50% decline
  implies ~$523M/year OE for two years. Against ~$227M/year net interest
  expense and no near-term maturity that isn't covered several times by
  existing liquidity, this is survivable without existential risk. Debt/
  (operating income + D&A) ≈ $5.6B / $1,951.2M ≈ 2.9x — moderate, ordinary
  investment-grade-range leverage, not a disqualifier under the Ruling-2
  survival standard.
**VERDICT: PASS.**

## GATE 5 — INVERSION
1. **Moat destruction** — hyperscaler marketplace disintermediation (AWS/
   Google/Microsoft Marketplace) and direct-from-vendor sales are named by CDW
   itself as active, not hypothetical, risks. Partially mitigated by CDW's
   scale, certifications, and services layer (marketplaces mostly compete on
   commodity hardware/software, not integration services), but this is a real,
   ongoing structural threat to the "no close substitute" gap already
   identified in Gate 2. Scored **[P]** — the one soft score in this set, same
   honesty standard as AVB's regulatory [P].
2. **Management failure/key person** — CEO has 24-year internal tenure and a
   proven track record through a demand downturn; no single-person
   over-dependency evident beyond ordinary CEO risk. Scored **[D]**.
3. **Balance-sheet stress** — laddered maturities, $2.5B liquidity, demonstrated
   market access, moderate leverage. Scored **[D]**.
4. **Thesis-breaking metric / customer-vendor concentration** — top 3 vendor
   partners are each >$2.0B of 2025 net sales (>27% of total combined); loss of
   authorization from any one is a real, disclosed risk ("there is no
   assurance that... vendor partners... will not limit or curtail the
   availability of their products to solutions providers like us"). Scored
   **[P]** — a second soft score, concentrated vendor dependency is a genuine
   structural vulnerability of the reseller model, not fully diversified away.

No [U]. Two [P]s (moat erosion via marketplace disintermediation; vendor
concentration) — both real and disclosed, neither dismissed.
**Thesis-breaking metric:** gross margin below 21.0% for two consecutive fiscal
years (signals the marketplace-pricing-pressure risk materializing), or net
sales decline >10% outside a broad IT-spending recession, or loss of top-tier
certification/authorization status with any of the three largest vendor
partners.
**VERDICT: PASS** — two [P]s carried forward explicitly, not waved away; no
[D/DS/U] combination that would stop the run.
