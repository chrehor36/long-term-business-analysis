## THE FY2026 GAAP OI BRIDGE (filed attribution — matters for the mix-shift question)
- The filer's own stated objective: "operating income leverage ... growing operating income
  at a faster rate than net sales." FY2026 GAAP: OI +1.6% vs net sales +4.7% — NOT met on
  GAAP. Filed drags: ~$0.9bn higher self-insured general-liability claims expense; $0.7bn
  PhonePe share-based-comp modification charge; higher depreciation from capital investment.
- FY2025 GAAP: OI +8.6% vs sales +5.0% — met.
- GP rate +8bp FY2026 "disciplined inventory management, as well as growth in higher margin
  businesses globally"; FY2025 +40bp "managing prices aligned to our competitive historic
  price gaps, as well as growth in higher margin businesses globally."
- ROI (non-GAAP) 15.1% FY2026 vs 15.5% FY2025 — DOWN, "primarily due to an increase in
  average invested capital due to higher purchases of property and equipment."

## Q2 FY2027 10-Q (period 2026-07-31, acc 0000104169-26-000154) — analyst read
- H1 FY2027 comps: Walmart U.S. +3.8% (Q2 +3.3%), "driven by growth in transactions and
  average ticket," grocery strength, health-and-wellness DOWN on prescription "maximum fair
  price regulation" effective Jan 2026 (a filed regulatory price cap touching the pharmacy
  category — [E3-03](3) note, category-level not enterprise).
- eCommerce contributed ~+5.1 points of the +3.8% six-month comp — MORE than the whole
  comp: the arithmetic residual for non-digital is ≈ −1.3 points. Caveat before reading
  that as store-traffic decline: store-fulfilled pickup/delivery counts inside eCommerce,
  and advertising net sales ride inside the eCommerce impact by definition.
- Sam's Club H1 +7.3% (fuel +3.2 impact); Sam's eCommerce contribution +3.1 points.
