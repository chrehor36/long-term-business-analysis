## Q1 — CAN I UNDERSTAND HOW THIS MAKES MONEY? **[E3-31]**
- **Unit economics in my own words, no management language.** Church & Dwight buys commodity inputs (surfactants, soda ash for baking soda, resins, paper, latex, fragrance), makes household and personal-care goods in its own plants and at contract manufacturers, and sells them to retailers who resell them to households: laundry detergent, cat litter, baking soda, stain remover, toothpaste and mouthwash, condoms, pregnancy tests, hair-removal cream, dry shampoo, water flossers, acne patches, cold remedies, hand sanitiser. Walmart takes about 23% of sales and the top four customers 44%. It spends about 11% of sales on advertising (*"Marketing expenses as a percentage of net sales was 11.4% in 2025"*) and more on trade promotion, which is netted out of sales. The money is the gap between what a branded pack sells for and what the chemicals, packaging and freight cost (gross margin 41.9-45.8% in 2009-2025), less advertising, selling and administration, leaving an operating margin of about 17-21% in ordinary years. Two kinds of product: **value brands** (*"value (34% of total worldwide consumer revenue in 2025)"*; the laundry detergents are *"value products, priced at a discount from products identified by the Company as market leaders"*, the words of every 10-K from 2002 to 2016; the phrase leaves the 10-K in 2017) and **premium brands** (66%), most of them bought. A small industrial business (5%) sells sodium bicarbonate and feed additives.
- **The scarce input this business controls.** **Brand names that shoppers recognise, and shelf space at a handful of retailers**; plus a production position in sodium bicarbonate (*"the leading U.S. producer of sodium bicarbonate"*, the company's own description in its releases, EX-99.1 of 2025-12-09 and 2026-07-31). The chemistry is not scarce. Whether the brands are scarce in [E3-03]'s sense (*"thought by its customers to have no close substitute"*) is Q2's question, not Q1's.
- **Will the fundamentals look broadly the same in ten years?** **The mechanism, yes; the brand list, less certainly.** The 2003 10-K describes the same trade (baking-soda products and value laundry sold through the same mass channel); what has changed is the portfolio, by twenty years of purchases and a handful of exits (VitaFusion, Flawless, Spinbrush). Households will buy detergent, litter and toothpaste in ten years; which of the bought brands still command their shelf is the open question, and it is taken up at Q2.
- **Relatively simple and stable in character [E3-31]?** Yes: buy chemicals, make packaged goods, advertise, sell through a concentrated retail channel. Not a business that would take months to understand [E4-46].
- **VERDICT: [x] IN**: the money is made visibly, as a branded-goods margin over commodity inputs sold through a concentrated retail channel. **IN is not a finding on the franchise.**
