## GATE 2 — MOAT
Franchise test [E3-03], from Micron's own 10-K: **"...competition in the
semiconductor memory and storage markets. To remain competitive, we must
continuously develop and implement new products and technologies and
decrease manufacturing costs in spite of inflationary pressures, changing
technologies, rapid market changes..."** — the company's own words
describe a cost-race on a commodity product.
The filed record is the verdict:
- **NI: $5,861M → $8,687M → −$5,833M → $778M → $8,539M** — a $14.5B
  peak-to-trough-to-peak whipsaw in five years. Not the signature of
  pricing power; the signature of a price-taker on DRAM spot.
- **Capex $15,857M vs D&A $8,352M (FY2025)** — spending nearly 2× the
  depreciation charge just to hold competitive position through node
  transitions. This is [E4-04] rendered in numbers: "A moat that must be
  continuously rebuilt will eventually be no moat at all."
**The counter-thesis, recorded honestly**: bulls argue HBM/AI demand has
converted memory into a disciplined 3-player oligopoly with allocated
supply and durable pricing. Filed evidence for it so far: **one boom
year.** The framework's test is "PROVEN by pricing power + returns on
capital" — one year two years after a $5.8B loss proves a cycle, not a
franchise. If the thesis is right, it will show up as something this
industry has never once printed: a full memory cycle without a loss year.
The framework can wait for that evidence; it does not pre-pay for it.
**Class: NONE (commodity, cost-race). VERDICT: FAIL — STOP.**
Project commodity tally: 13 of 13 (8 E&P + SLVM + 4 others incl. this).
