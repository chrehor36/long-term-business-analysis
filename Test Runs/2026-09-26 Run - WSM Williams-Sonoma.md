## Q1 — CAN I UNDERSTAND HOW THIS MAKES MONEY? **[E3-31]**
- **Unit economics in my own words, no management language.** Williams-Sonoma designs furniture, bedding, lighting, rugs, cookware, tableware and decorative goods under its own names (Pottery Barn, West Elm, Williams Sonoma, Pottery Barn Kids and Teen, Rejuvenation, Mark and Graham, GreenRow), has them made mostly by outside factories abroad (81% of fiscal 2025 purchases foreign: 19% China, 16% Vietnam, 15% India; the largest supplier about 3%; upholstery and lighting made in its own plants in Mississippi, North Carolina and Oregon), and sells them only through its own channels: websites and catalogues (about 65% of fiscal 2025 net revenues) and 506 leased stores (about 35%), plus business-to-business and franchise sales. Its revenue is the shelf price less returns and promotions, plus delivery fees and credit-card incentives. Out of each dollar of fiscal 2025 revenue, about 54 cents went to cost of goods sold (the merchandise, tariffs, inbound freight, rent and depreciation of stores and distribution centres, which the 10-K puts at $820.3M of occupancy, and outbound shipping), about 28 cents to selling, general and administrative costs (employment, advertising, card fees, the distribution network's labour), leaving an operating margin of 18.1% ($1,415.7M on $7,806.8M). **The money is the gap between what it pays a factory for an item it designed and what a customer pays for that item under one of its names, less the rent, freight and advertising needed to put it in front of the customer and deliver it.** The business is seasonal (October to January is the peak) and carries inventory ordered *"up to twelve months and generally multiple seasons in advance"* (Item 1A), so a misjudged season is paid for in markdowns.
- **The scarce input this business controls.** **The brand names and the designs sold under them, and the customer file.** Item 1: *"Our in-house teams design our proprietary products"*; the brand names *"are of material importance to us"*; about 380 design and technology patents and over 1,200 copyright registrations; *"our proprietary customer file, which is a unified view of customers across our brands and channels"*; The Key Rewards loyalty and credit-card programme across the brands. The goods are not sold by any other retailer, which is what separates this from a merchant reselling others' brands (the distinction [E4-65] and [E5-57] draw, where the retailer's own name becomes the brand). Nothing else is scarce: the factories are third parties with alternatives (largest supplier 3%), the stores are leased, and delivery is bought from carriers. Whether the names give the customer a product with no close substitute is Q2's question.
- **Will the fundamentals look broadly the same in ten years?** **The mechanism, yes, and it has been the same for decades; the channel mix has moved and will keep moving.** The fiscal 2001 10-K describes the same business (Williams-Sonoma and Pottery Barn stores and catalogues, 415 stores, direct-to-customer 37.4% of revenue); fiscal 2005 42.6% direct; fiscal 2015 *"E-commerce net revenues generated 51% of our total company net revenues"*; fiscal 2025 about 65% e-commerce. Store count rose to 631 (fiscal 2017) and has fallen to 506. The money is made the same way through every channel: own-designed goods at the retailer's price. What the filings cannot say is which styles will sell ([E3-74]'s *"you cannot coast in retailing"*): Item 1A, *"in the specialty home products business, style and color trends are constantly evolving. As a result, consumer preferences cannot be predicted with certainty and may change between selling seasons."* That is a statement about which products, not about how the business earns; it is weighed at Q2 and Q3.
- **Relatively simple and stable in character [E3-31]?** Simple: a designer-retailer paid at the till and the website, with rent, freight, advertising and inventory as the costs that move. Stable in mechanism for fifty years. Not a technology business and not [E4-46]'s months-of-study case. The volatility is in the level (operating income $42.0M before tax in fiscal 2008, $1,498.4M in fiscal 2022), which is Q2's and Q4's subject.
- **VERDICT: [x] IN**: the money is made visibly, as the margin between the factory cost of goods it designs and the price its own channels charge for them, less rent, freight, advertising and markdowns. **IN is not a finding on the franchise.**
