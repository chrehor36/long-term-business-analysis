### THE STRONGEST EVIDENCE AGAINST THIS VERDICT, stated as its holders would state it [E4-51, E4-26, E3-47]
The verdict below is IN, NARROW, so the case for OUT is set out first, at full strength.
1. *"The filer says in its own risk factors that it cannot raise prices without losing customers to rivals, the predecessor said it had over a hundred competitors, and a competitor's 10-K lists Element Solutions as one of five interchangeable chemistry suppliers. That is criterion (2) failing in the registrant's words, exactly as it failed for Fuller, Quaker and Hawkins."* The FY2025 10-K: *"Passing along these costs to our customers by increasing our prices could result in long-term sales declines or loss of market share if our customers were to find alternative suppliers or choose to reformulate their products to use fewer ingredients."* MacDermid's FY2002 10-K: *"MacDermid has many competitors, estimated to be in excess of 100 in some proprietary product areas."* MKS's FY2025 10-K: *"In MSD, Element Solutions Inc., Qnity Electronics, Inc., Uyemura, JCU International, Inc. and Okuno Chemical Industries Co., Ltd. offer products that compete with our chemistry products."* Customers have left: Energy's FY2019 sales fell because of *"loss of certain business in the first quarter of 2019"*, and Graphics lost *"a significant newspaper customer"* in 2023. **Answer:** accepted as the words. Two limits: the same MacDermid sentence goes on *"To the best of the Corporation's knowledge no single competitor competes with all its proprietary products"*, and the alternative-supplier sentence is a conditional about cost pass-through, where the record below shows price held after costs fell. The customer losses named are in Energy (12% of Specialties) and Graphics (sold). What the words cannot be answered by is a price series, and the filer files none: that is why the class is NARROW.
2. *"The industry's selling prices fall, and the filer and its closest peer have said so in identical words: margin is held only by cutting cost and changing mix. The predecessor wrote down a customer list because Asian customers refused its price increases. The closest filed peer reports price down two years running."* The FY2013 10-K: *"Our ability to maintain or increase our gross profit margin will continue to be dependent, in large part, upon our ability to offset decreases in average selling prices by improving production efficiency or by shifting to higher margin chemical products."*; Atotech's 20-F FY2021 carries the same sentence with *"profitability"* for *"gross profit margin"*; the FY2025 10-K lists *"price erosion"* among the industry's conditions. The 2011 MacDermid impairment (FY2013 10-K) was tested on *"Increased pricing pressure for certain products from competitors"*, *"Increases in raw material prices used in manufacturing process that were difficult to pass along to customers"* and *"Customers’ reluctance to accept product price increases"*. Qnity's Interconnect Solutions segment (the DuPont plating-chemistry business): *"Net sales increased primarily due to a 14% increase in volume partially offset by a 2% decline in local price and product mix."* (2025), and the same 2% decline in 2024. **Answer:** accepted as the industry's condition, and it is the strongest fact in the file. The answer is the margin record through the volume downturns (below), which is where a business without pricing power shows it, and it does not show it here.
3. *"The one inflation test on record was failed. ### THE READING
- **The volume downturns, where a business without a franchise gives margin away.** **2001-2002, the electronics depression, on the predecessor's own figures:** MacDermid's Advanced Surface Finishing segment (electronics and industrial plating chemistry, the core of today's Circuitry and Industrial lines) lost about a fifth of its sales, *"The decrease was primarily due to weak demand in worldwide markets for electronics chemistry."*, and its gross margin **rose**: *"gross profit percentage was 47.0% in transition year 2001 as compared to 46.5% for the same period in 2000"*, attributed to *"a product mix shift towards newer technologies and a more efficient pricing structure for certain other products"*, and *"gross profit percentage was 48.3% in fiscal 2002 compared to 46.6% for the same period in 2001"* on sales down 10%. The operating profit fell (selling cost is fixed), but the spread over materials held. **FY2019** (revenue −6%): Electronics' gross margin rose 100 bps on mix (*"The constant currency decrease in gross profit was primarily driven by lower net sales partially offset by favorable product mix."*). **FY2020** (organic −3%, COVID): gross margin 42.4% against 42.9%. **FY2023** (revenue −8%, organic −5%, Electronics organic −7%): gross margin up two points, and the release for the year: *"Though sales and volumes declined, through proactive cost management and prudent pricing discipline, we saw no degradation in our margins."* Adjusted for the metal, the whole company's EBITA margin stayed between 19.2% and 22.4% in every year FY2018-FY2025 and Electronics' gross margin between 50.2% and 55.8%.
- **[E2-44] (1), price with demand soft: met once, in an inflation year, which flatters it.** The release for the third quarter of 2022: *"Electronics markets weakened, particularly in China, in contrast to their normal third quarter ramp. Nonetheless, we grew organically in both our operating segments as pricing actions, new business wins and a focus on high-growth electronics applications helped to offset soft volume."* Price kept after costs fell (2023-2025, above). **Price named as a cause of a margin fall: in no MD&A read FY2016-FY2025**; the falls are attributed to metal, raw materials, logistics, mix and step-up.
- **[E2-44] (2), growth with minor capital: met.** Revenue ex-metal rose from about $1,573M (FY2016) to about $2,097M (FY2025) with capex of $28-68M a year; the added capital was purchase price.
- **Returns on capital [E3-46]: high on the business's own capital** (46-81% pre-tax since FY2019), **low on what the owners paid** (4.3-9.3% GAAP, 9.4-12.2% before amortization).
- **No administered price [E2-59].** **[E3-62], the second step:** customers' process gains from new chemistry (finer lines, fewer defects) are sold as the product; the flat ex-metal margin through a decade of electronics price deflation says the gains have not all flowed through to the customer, and have not all stayed home either.

**[E4-04], applied as the framework rules it: a lead maintained, not rebuilt from zero; not the perimeter close.** The filer's words place it in the have-to-stay-smart class ([E3-74]): *"The specialty chemicals industry is subject to constant and rapid technological change, product obsolescence, price erosion, evolving standards, finite product lifecycles"*. The test is whether the advantage's **basis** must be replaced or merely defended [E5-23]. The basis here is the qualification of a chemistry into a customer's line and the service force that keeps it there (*"operational risks and switching costs make it difficult for our customers to change suppliers which allows us to retain customers and maintain our market positions"*), carried by the same brands (Alpha, Enthone, MacDermid, Kester, ViaForm) against the same named competitors across the filed years: FY2016 *"our most significant competitors are Atotech Inc., The Dow Chemical Company, across its industrial and electronics chemicals businesses, as well as Asahi, Senju, Tamura, E.I. du Pont de Nemours and Company, and Flint Group"*; FY2025 *"MKS Instruments, Qnity Electronics, Inc., Henkel Corporation, Senju Metal Industry Co. and Uyemura International"* (MKS has owned Atotech since August 2022; Qnity is DuPont's former electronics business). What is replaced each generation is the formulation, at a research cost of 2.6% of sales, not the qualification and service base, and the margin record shows the lead held through the smartphone, 5G and AI-server generations. That is the KEYS and KLAC defence case, not the TSM rebuild case. **Durability is judged from filings here**, so the [E4-04] perimeter close does not apply; the low research spend against the row is recorded as a live width. **Key persons [E4-23]:** none shown at the business level (the decentralised structure is the filer's own description).
