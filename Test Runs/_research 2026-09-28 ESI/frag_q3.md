**STEP 1 — DECLARE THE WEIGHT CASE.**
- [x] **Daily execution** — have-to-be-smart-every-day **[E3-38]**: the Q2 finding is a NARROW moat held by qualifying each new chemistry into customers' lines and servicing it, in an industry the filer describes as one of *"constant and rapid technological change, product obsolescence, price erosion"*; a manager who let the formulation or the service force slip would narrow the moat before an owner could react.
- [ ] **Control** — no **[E1-16]**.
- [ ] **Leverage** — not ticked, recorded **[E3-29]**: debt $2,119.4M against cash $189.6M at 2026-06-30 (net $1.93bn, first-lien secured term loans and $800M of notes), about four times the FY2025 operating income before amortization; heavy for a chemicals company and carried to Q4, but not [E3-29]'s 20:1 bank.

Case declared: **binary gate, on daily execution** (the MSA, CAT, ATR, ECL and KEYS precedent for NARROW moats). No price compensates a failure here.

**Honesty — binary [E5-16], filings-based, dated to when each matter became public.** No finding of personal misconduct in the documents read. Matters read, each dated: (1) **Item 4.02, made public 2016-03-11**: the Q3 2015 statements *"should no longer be relied upon as a result of an error related to its income tax provision"*, a hedge loss booked as a temporary rather than permanent tax difference; followed by material weaknesses in the accounting for income taxes (the FY2017 10-K: *"a material weakness in our internal control over financial reporting related to the accounting for income taxes had not been fully remediated"*), none reported since; an error of the vehicle's accounting, not concealment. (2) **The Arysta West Africa payments, made public in the FY2015 10-K (2016-03-11)**: *"we discovered certain payments made to third-party agents in connection with Arysta’s government tender business in West Africa which may be illegal or otherwise inappropriate"*; self-reported (*"We contacted the SEC and the U.S. Department of Justice to voluntarily inform them of this matter"*) and closed (FY2017 10-K: *"the SEC and the U.S. Department of Justice both advised that they had closed out the matter and declined to take any action"*); in the sold agricultural business, and the direction [E2-69] credits. (3) **The Dillard securities class action (March 2016)** on the same West Africa practices, dismissed on the company's motion (*"On December 7, 2016, the Court granted the motion to dismiss"*). (4) **The pay episode of December 2025 and the sale agreement of July 2026**, read under the flags below; disclosed in full, in plain language, by the proxy and the 8-Ks, so a matter of rationality and incentives, not of concealment. **A Q3 pass is the absence of found disqualifiers, not a finding that the managers are honest [E5-17].**

**STEP 2 — THE FLAGS [E4-22, E5-15]** — each a prompt to read.
- [x] **EBITDA [E4-29]: FIRES at full strength.** Adjusted EBITDA is the headline of every release read (*"Record Adjusted EBITDA of $535 million"*, 2025-02-18), the only guided profit figure, the chief operating decision maker's segment measure (*"The CODM utilizes net sales and Adjusted EBITDA to allocate resources"*), the leverage yardstick of the capital-allocation messages (*"all while remaining below net leverage of 3.5x adjusted EBITDA"*, 2020-02-26), and the first metric of both the annual bonus (adjusted EBITDA and adjusted EPS) and the long-term awards (DEF 14A 2026: *"3-year LTI Awards — Constant currency adjusted EBITDA compound annual growth rate"*). In a company whose depreciation and amortization ran $151-167M a year FY2019-FY2025, against $173-344M of GAAP operating income, this is the [E5-41] reverse-float measure in its purest form.
- [x] **Projections [E4-22] third flag, [E5-30]: FIRES, and [E3-48]'s record is kept.** Every release since 2020 gives quarterly and full-year adjusted EBITDA guidance. **Outturn against the first full-year guidance:** 2021 guided *"adjusted EBITDA growth of approximately 7%"*, delivered 20%; **2022 guided *"between $575 million and $590 million"*, cut twice (to *"$565 million to $575 million"* and *"$525 million to $530 million"*), delivered $527M, 9% below the initial bottom**; **2023 guided *"$510 million to $530 million"*, cut to *"$490 million to $500 million"*, delivered $482M**; 2024 guided $510-530M, delivered $535M; 2025 guided $520-540M, delivered $548M. The misses were reported as misses and the cuts made mid-year with reasons (currency, *"lowered macro-economic growth assumptions"*): not the never-miss pattern [E4-30] warns of. Recorded; the guidance culture itself is the ratchet [E5-30] describes.
- [x] **Metric switching and the yardstick [E2-49], with [E4-27]: FIRES at full strength, the sharpest instance on the file.** The DEF 14A of 2026: the 2022 *"stretch"* awards, whose *"performance target for those awards, which was to double adjusted earnings per share from 2021 to 2026"*, were cancelled in 2025 because *"it was established that the target was unattainable"*, and the board then approved *"share grants of 750,000 shares to Mr. Gliklich, 240,000 shares to Mr. Dorman, 160,000 shares to Mr. Liebowitz, 90,000 shares to Mr. Capps and 225,000 shares to the Company's Executive Chairman"*, which *"vested on December 10, 2025"*, worth *"approximately 75% of the value of the original stretch awards"* and $37.1M of expense, *"to partially remediate the discrepancy between executive total compensation and the Company’s performance for the 2022-2025 period"*, with annual pay raised from 2026 and a new peer group. The 10-K confirms the original awards were never charged (*"these grants were cancelled and no compensation expense was recognized"*). The 2025 annual bonus paid 200% after the committee *"approved adjustments to account for the impact of non-recurring events"*, namely *"divestitures, including the sale of our Graphics business in February 2025, movements in foreign-exchange rates and significant fluctuations of precious metals prices"*. And the $37.1M itself is removed from the headline (*"2025 executive share grants | 37.1"* is an adjustment to reach Adjusted EBITDA). A long-lived bullseye was set, missed, discarded, and paid at three-quarters anyway: *"disposition of the yardstick rather than disposition of the manager"* [E2-49], in the corpus's words. **Against it, stated fairly:** the proxy discloses every step in plain English, attributes the trigger partly to owners (*"in discussions with stockholders, the incentive value of the 2022 "stretch" awards was often part of the dialogue with stockholders"*), and the share price had risen over the period; the corpus's bar is the yardstick, not the share price.
- [x] **The except-for flag [E2-57] and the restructuring charge [E3-53]: FIRES.** Every year FY2023-FY2025 carries adjustments for *"Restructuring expense"* ($5.6-11.4M), *"Acquisition, integration and transaction expenses"* ($16.8-34.9M) and *"Other, net"* ($12.1-27.2M), plus the pay grant. Real costs belong in the owner-earnings mean [E5-33]; Q4's construction keeps every cash dollar of them and subtracts stock pay in full.
- [ ] **Serial share issuance [E5-15]: fired in the vehicle years, not since.** Shares went from 103.6 million (2013) to 284.2 million (2016) through purchases paid in stock and offerings (*"September 2016 Equity Offering ... 48,787,878 shares ... at a public offering price of $8.25 per share"*); since 2019 the count has fallen to 243,690,914 through buybacks, with 2.4 million shares issued on exercise and vesting in 2025, the December grant included. Dated to 2014-2016, under the acquisition vehicle.
- [ ] **Weak accounting, unintelligible footnotes: the 2016 non-reliance and the 2015-2017 tax-control weaknesses (above); not found since.** The notes read (acquisitions, derivatives, debt, segments, stock pay) are clear.
- [ ] **Filed-figure tells [E4-30]: not found.** Cash taxes paid exceed book tax in the years read (*"Cash paid for income taxes | 98.8 | 87.2 | 73.7"* against *"Income tax expense"* 74.6 / 44.8 / 13.0, FY2025-FY2023); reported growth is not smooth (revenue −6% FY2019, −8% FY2023).
- [x] **Related party: a prompt.** *"Mariposa Capital, an affiliate of Sir Martin, Executive Chairman of the Board, ... received in 2025 an annual services fee of $2.0 million"* under a 2013 agreement ($3.0M a year until April 2024) that the founder will continue *"for a minimum of three years"* after leaving the board in May 2026; he also received the 225,000-share grant. Small against the company; recorded because a founder paid outside the board's pay structure after he leaves it is a standing agency cost [E3-57].

**STEP 3 — THE PRIMARY TEST [E2-01]**, on unleveraged capital as [E2-43] directs for an acquisitive filer (Q2 table, `roc_out.txt`): pre-tax operating income before acquired amortization on net tangible capital 46-81% in every year FY2019-FY2025. **The goodwill wedge, separately:** on everything the owners' capital bought (equity + debt − cash, $3.6-4.1bn), GAAP operating income 4.3-9.3% and before amortization 9.4-12.2%. Book return on equity (continuing net income on year-end equity): 3.6% (FY2019), 3.3%, 8.2%, 8.0%, 5.0%, 10.2%, 7.1% (FY2025), with net debt $1.0-1.9bn throughout. **The operating business earns five to eight times on its own capital what the owners earn on the price paid for it**; the price was paid by the vehicle's founders in 2013-2016, and the current management has run the business since 2019.

**The half-owner test [E2-26]: passes on disclosure, fails on emphasis.** Every adjustment is quantified line by line in the segment note and the releases, the pay episode is explained in the proxy in plain words, and the deal and its end were filed the day each happened. The reservation is the one the flags record: the company's own account of its year, its guidance and its pay all run on a figure that excludes depreciation, amortization, restructuring, acquisition costs and, in 2025, the executives' own grant.

**The sale of the company, read as capital allocation [E5-24, E5-44].** In July 2026 the board agreed unanimously to sell the whole business for *"0.500 shares ... of Solstice Common Stock"* and *"$10.00 in cash"* a share, *"a cash-and-stock transaction valued at approximately $14.5 billion, including the assumption of net debt"*. Against the June 2026 net debt of $1.93bn that is about $12.6bn for the equity, **roughly $51 a share by this run's subtraction** (the filings give no per-share figure), nearly half again above today's $35.12 and far above this run's value range (Q5). Most of it was to be paid in the buyer's shares, which [E5-44] says must be measured at the buyer's intrinsic value, not its quote; no filing read gives that. The merger agreement accelerated the executives' awards *"at (i) 200% of the target performance level for awards granted in 2024 and 2026 and (ii) 300% of the target performance level for awards granted in 2025"*, and the change-in-control agreements pay *"3 (CEO) or 2 (other NEOs)"* times salary and bonus on a qualifying termination. Seven weeks later, on owners' objection (*"based on constructive feedback from our shareholders"*), both boards terminated it at no cost. **Read both ways:** a sale at a price well above value is not, on its face, against the owners; a sale largely in another company's paper, agreed by managers whose awards it would have tripled, seven months after they replaced their own failed awards, is the [E4-27] and [E4-52] configuration the corpus warns of. The owners, not the board, stopped it.
