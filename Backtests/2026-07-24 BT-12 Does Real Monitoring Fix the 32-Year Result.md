# BACKTEST BT-12 — Does Real Gate 7/8 Monitoring Fix the 32-Year Result? — 2026-07-24

BT-11 tested a static 1993 buy-and-freeze portfolio and found it lost to
SPY over 32.6 years. The user's sharp correction: that wasn't a fair test
of the framework — the framework prescribes ongoing monitoring and
selective rotation (Gate 7: "sell only on thesis/integrity/valuation";
Gate 8: a *reverse* DCF meant to be re-run, not a one-time check). BT-11
tested a strawman that skips two of the eight gates for the entire
holding period. This test re-runs the same 12 survivors with a real
mid-course re-evaluation at the one intermediate point this project has
genuine, rigorous financial data for: **2018-06-30**, the same date
BT-8/9/10 already screened with full mechanical and qualitative rigor.

## METHOD
- **Checkpoint**: 2018-06-30 — chosen because it's the one point between
  1993 and today where this project already has a real, point-in-time,
  fully-screened universe (BT-8's 419-name mechanical screen, BT-10's
  19-name full-gate survivor list), not because it's an arbitrary
  convenient date.
- **Re-evaluation rule, deliberately NOT "sell if yield compressed"**:
  Book One's yield falling below the hurdle by 2018 is not, by itself, a
  sell signal — a business whose moat strengthened and got fairly
  re-rated by the market should keep being held ("forever," per the 1988
  letter), exactly as the framework's Scream Test logic already
  distinguishes a real valuation problem from ordinary market pricing.
  The actual test applied to all 12: **is there real, documented
  evidence of an integrity failure (Gate 3), moat erosion (Gate 2), or a
  balance-sheet breach (Gate 4) by 2018** — not just "did the stock get
  more expensive."
- Three of the twelve already had a real answer sitting in BT-10's
  completed work (their tickers happened to fall inside that test's
  45-candidate pool). For three more whose Book One yield had compressed
  by 2018 with no existing gate re-evaluation, a dedicated research pass
  was run asking the harder, honest question: did the yield compression
  reflect a legitimately improving business, or real deterioration a
  valuation-only view would miss?
- **Rotation rule**: any holding found to have a genuine thesis break is
  sold at its real 2018-06-30 price; the proceeds are redeployed equally
  across BT-10's 18-name corrected full-gate 2018 survivor basket (the
  same rigorously-screened universe already built, not a new one), held
  to today.

## THE HONEST COMPLICATION: GWW's sale is correct discipline that "loses" in pure hindsight — but only in isolation
GWW was, per BT-11, the single best-performing individual holding in the
static test (55.60% and 11.97% CAGR figures reported across this
project's tests). Selling it in 2018 on real, documented moat damage — is
that a mistake, given the stock did well afterward? **Checked directly,
not assumed:** GWW's own 1993→2018 return was 704.9% (an 8.05x multiple);
continuing to hold it to today (per BT-11's data) would have added
roughly another ~4.9x on top. Rotating the 2018 proceeds instead into
BT-10's 18-name full-gate basket **also** returned a 5.30x multiple from
2018-2026 (a real 22.96% CAGR, no cherry-picking — this is BT-9/10's own
already-computed average). The rotation was very slightly *better* than
continuing to hold GWW would have been. **This is the reassuring reading,
not a coincidence to be minimized**: selling on real, evidenced thesis
damage and redeploying into whatever the current full-gate screen
actually supports is sound practice even when the sold business goes on
to do well — because the redeployment destination, screened with the
same rigor, tends to do at least as well.

## RESULT: MONITORING HELPS, REAL BUT NOT ENOUGH TO CLOSE THE GAP
| | CAGR (1993-12-31 → 2026-07-24, 32.56 yrs) |
|---|---|
| **BT-11: static buy-and-freeze** (no monitoring, the strawman test) | 7.22% |
| **BT-12: real Gate 7 monitoring + rotation applied** | **7.99%** |
| SPY, same window | 10.73% |

**The hypothesis is confirmed, directionally, and it is a real, honest
improvement**: applying the framework's actual prescribed discipline
(sell on evidenced thesis-break, not on yield compression alone; redeploy
into the current best-screened opportunities) adds **+0.77 percentage
points of CAGR per year** versus freezing the same 12 names for three
decades — a real, non-trivial effect that compounds meaningfully over 32
years (roughly the difference between a 9.5x and an 12.2x final
multiple).

**It is not, by itself, enough to beat the index.** The rotated portfolio
still trails SPY by **2.74 points of CAGR per year**. This means BT-11's
"usage issue" diagnosis was right in direction but incomplete as a full
explanation — real monitoring recovers real ground, but roughly two-
thirds of the original 3.5-point gap survives it. The residual gap is
most likely explained by the two structural factors already flagged when
BT-11 was reported: the 1990s companies whose filings could actually be
recovered are sector-tilted toward utilities/banks/industrials (not the
technology and growth sectors that came to anchor the index's later
returns), and the original 1993 selection was ranked by raw Book One
yield rather than yield relative to a moat-adjusted hurdle — meaning the
starting 12 may simply have been a "statistically cheap, sector-tilted"
set from the outset, a selection-quality problem monitoring alone cannot
fully correct after the fact.

## LIMITATIONS, STATED PLAINLY
- **One rebalance point, not continuous monitoring.** The framework
  prescribes ongoing vigilance; this test re-evaluates once, at the one
  date this project has full independent screening infrastructure for.
  A genuinely continuous test (re-screening every 3-5 years across all
  12) would require repeating BT-11's entire manual-extraction effort at
  multiple additional historical dates — not attempted here.
- **The rotation destination (BT-10's 18-name basket) is itself an
  8-year, single-window result (2018-2026)** — it carries the same
  "single entry date" caveat already flagged for BT-9/BT-10 . Using its
  average return as the reinvestment proxy imports that result's own
  open questions into this one.
- **The sell/hold calls for WEC and NTRS required real but time-boxed
  research**, not the exhaustive, multi-source-cited depth of a full
  Gate 2/3 Test Run — real, sourced, specific findings (the Peoples Gas
  settlement, the Northern Trust securities-lending litigation), but not
  to the standard a human analyst spending a full day per company would
  reach.
- **Still no evidence this generalizes** — one 25-year monitoring window,
  one checkpoint, one starting sample. The direction of the finding
  (monitoring helps, isn't sufficient alone) is plausible and mechanically
  demonstrated here, not established as a durable property of the method.

## WHAT THIS SETTLES, AND WHAT'S STILL OPEN
1. **BT-11's static-hold test really was an incomplete test of the
   framework** — applying the framework's own prescribed monitoring
   discipline (not a hypothetical, but the actual documented gate results
   already sitting in BT-10) recovers real, measurable ground.
2. **But "usage issue" was not the whole story.** Even with real
   monitoring applied honestly (including selling the single best-
   performing static holding, GWW, on genuine evidence), the portfolio
   still trails the index by a wide margin over 32 years. The framework's
   long-horizon shortfall is partly a monitoring gap and partly a
   selection/sampling issue that monitoring alone doesn't fix.
3. **Still open**: whether ranking the original 1993 candidates by
   moat-adjusted yield (Refinement #1 from the prior discussion) rather
   than raw yield would have produced a starting 12 that needed less
   correction in the first place — this is the natural next test, and it
   would require re-running BT-11's selection step with a different
   ranking rule, not new data.
