# BACKTEST BT-10 — The Full 8-Gate Screen: Every Possible 10-Business Portfolio — 2026-07-22

BT-9 tested Book One alone (the mechanical yield screen) and found it isn't an
edge by itself — random small portfolios from the Book-One-passer pool beat
SPY only 30% of the time. The user's direct correction: **portfolios should
only contain businesses that pass the actual framework** — meaning Gates
1-5 (Circle of Competence, Moat, Management, Financial Quality, Inversion),
not just the mechanical valuation screen. This test does that, on a
41-name shortlist drawn from BT-8/9's real, point-in-time, non-survivor-
biased universe, and then enumerates **every single possible 10-business
portfolio** the survivors can form — not a random sample, the complete set.

## METHOD
- **Starting pool**: the top 45 (of 142) Book-One passers by entry yield as
  of 2018-06-30, excluding AIV (a REIT flagged in BT-9 as a GAAP-net-income
  screening artifact, not a real 96.68%-yield bargain).
- **Gate 4 (Financial Quality + Fortress), done mechanically from XBRL**:
  computed real owner earnings (NI + D&A − capex − ΔWC, full formula, not
  an NI proxy) for the trailing 5 verified years, and the Fortress leverage
  ceiling for financial businesses. This is the same mechanical rigor as
  the rest of this session's XBRL work, extended to a second financial
  metric (not just NI). **6 of 45 mechanically eliminated**: F, CMG, AN
  (negative owner earnings in the lowest verified year, despite passing
  the NI-only Book One screen — exactly the gap the complete OE formula
  exists to catch), and LNC (17.5:1), STT (11.2:1), PFG (20.8:1) — all
  three fail the hard >10:1 leverage ceiling automatically, no exception,
  per the ratified Fortress Test rule.
- **Gates 1, 2, 3, 5 (qualitative), done as real per-company analysis**:
  dispatched to 6 parallel research passes covering the remaining 39
  names, each applying the actual Test Run template criteria (Circle of
  Competence in plain language; the franchise test + a filing-sourced
  moat metric and trend; management integrity as a binary, permanent
  disqualifier with competence/alignment as secondary; mandatory
  inversion scoring). **Point-in-time discipline enforced explicitly**:
  every verdict is constrained to facts knowable as of 2018-06-30 — no
  hindsight, in either direction (e.g. NVDA judged as a gaming/emerging-
  datacenter chip company, not as the later AI giant; GameStop judged as
  a structurally-challenged 2018 retailer, not through the lens of its
  2021 meme-stock trading episode).
- **Portfolio construction**: from the final survivor list, **every
  possible 10-business combination was enumerated exhaustively** — not
  sampled, not cherry-picked. Each portfolio's return is the equal-weight
  mean of its 10 members' real, corporate-action-correct total returns
  (the same return dataset built for BT-9, already accounting for every
  bankruptcy, buyout, and merger among these names).

## WHY THIS IS DIFFERENT FROM BT-9's RESULT
BT-9's Monte Carlo control (500 random portfolios from the 142 raw
Book-One passers) found only a 30% beat rate — because that pool still
contained PBI (a value trap), TGNA and IVZ (moats already eroding), and
dozens of similar names Book One's mechanical NI-yield test cannot see.
**The qualitative gates removed exactly those names.** This is direct,
mechanical evidence for the framework's own design premise: Book One
finds statistically cheap businesses; Gates 1-5 are what separate a real
bargain from a melting ice cube. Applied together, the combination
produces a survivor pool where *almost any* 10-name combination — not
just a carefully-optimized one — outperforms the market.

## LIMITATIONS, STATED PLAINLY
- **Single entry date, single exit date.** This is still one draw
  (2018-06-30 to 2026-07-22), the same limitation flagged in BT-8 and
  BT-9. The next real test is repeating the full pipeline (mechanical
  Gate 4 + qualitative Gates 1-5 + exhaustive portfolio enumeration) at a
  different historical date to see if a ~94% beat rate and a
  median-above-SPY result hold outside this one window.
- **Qualitative gates were done by parallel AI research passes, not by
  the user or a human analyst**, and not with the full filing-by-filing
  citation depth the Test Run template calls for (e.g. Gate 2's "8-quarter
  trend, filing-sourced" was approximated with 1-2 year directional
  evidence, not a literal quarter-by-quarter filing pull for all 39
  names). This is real, reasoned, sourced judgment — multiple verdicts
  cite specific SEC actions, FTC complaints, and disclosed financial
  trends — but it is not the same standard of rigor as a human-run,
  single-company Test Run (like the TXN run done earlier this session).
  One verdict (FITB) was explicitly left as a flagged, unresolved ruling
  rather than forced to a conclusion.
- **Starting pool was pre-filtered to the top 45 by Book One yield**, not
  all 142 Book-One passers — chosen for tractability. A handful of
  lower-yield-but-still-passing names were never qualitatively evaluated
  at all, so the true qualitative-survivor count among all 142 is
  unknown, only among this top-45 slice.
- **Price return only, no dividends**, consistent with every other test
  this session, on both the portfolio and SPY sides.
- **11 corporate-action tickers' exit values (BT-9's research) apply
  unchanged here** — RTN (now RTX, 2.3348 share ratio), PDCO (though PDCO
  failed Gate 2/3 here and isn't in the survivor set), etc. — all already
  verified, not re-derived.

## ⚠ CORRECTION — A LOOK-AHEAD BUG IN THE BOOK ONE SCREEN INVALIDATES THIS DOCUMENT'S RESULTS (2026-07-25)

**The screen that produced this document's candidate list contained a
look-ahead bug. The results below should not be relied on.**

**The bug.** Market cap was computed as `price * shares_as_of(anchor)`, where
the price came from a cached Yahoo series. Both Yahoo price fields (`adjclose`
and `quote.close`) are back-adjusted to **today's** share basis, while the
share count is the true point-in-time figure. Market cap was therefore
understated -- and owner-earnings yield overstated -- by the cumulative split
factor occurring **after** the anchor date. Verified: AAPL at 2013-06-30
computed to $11.7B against a real ~$372B (31.7x understated, exactly its 7:1
2014 and 4:1 2020 splits plus dividend drift).

**Why this is not a rounding error.** Companies that split are overwhelmingly
companies whose stock *rose*. The bug therefore inflated the measured yield of
future winners specifically, and since candidates were selected as the
top-N-by-yield, **it fed look-ahead bias directly into candidate selection.**
The screen was preferentially finding future winners because they were future
winners.

**The fix.** Market cap is split-invariant, so the price needs no adjustment
at all -- the share count is brought onto today's basis instead:
`cap = close(anchor) * shares(at measurement date) * splits effective after
that measurement date`. Every corrected cap is now validated against
`dei:EntityPublicFloat`, the filed 10-K cover-page market value (available for
553/558 filers, measured at the fiscal-Q2 close = our June-30 anchors). The
project had no independent market-cap check before; that is why this survived
four backtests. Corrected screen: `Backtests/scripts/screen_universe_v3b.py`.

**Also affected:** `bt_engine.py` and `bt_engine_wide.py` carry the identical
defect, so the 49.3% mechanical "coin flip" win rate is likewise computed off a
biased selection.

**Impact on BT-10 specifically — this is severe.** Re-tested against the
corrected screen, **only 5 of this document’s 18 full-gate survivors still
clear Book One at 2018-06-30**: AAPL, CMCSA, USB, AFL, PNC.

The 13 that now FAIL are, with their corrected yields against the 4.00% hurdle:
NVDA (0.31%), BKNG (1.08%), MA (1.29%), GOOG (1.63%), CME (1.79%), V (2.00%),
NEE (2.42%), APH (2.43%), ORLY (2.99%), CSX (3.07%), FAST (3.24%), TJX (3.59%),
WMT (3.90%).

Note what that list is: the winners. NVDA — the single largest contributor to
this document’s headline result — misses the hurdle by more than a factor of
ten. The 93.8% beat-rate across all C(18,10) portfolios is therefore **not a
finding about the framework**; it is a measurement of look-ahead bias in the
candidate selection. **The beat rate, the median 25.46% CAGR, and the
asymmetric-payoff mechanism described here are all withdrawn.**
