## New findings

**1. Citigroup 2007 is the strongest result in either batch — and it bears directly on
the pending fortress ruling.** The Statute alone would have bought a 7.8% yield in a
4.8% world. What stopped it was Gate 4's leverage test — MECHANICALLY, no hindsight:
19:1 assets-to-equity was printed in the 10-K. And the test is corpus-anchored, nearly
verbatim: "When assets are twenty times equity — a common ratio in this industry —
mistakes that involve only a small portion of assets can destroy a major portion of
equity." (1990 letter [E3-02 context].) NOTE FOR THE OPEN RULING: this same strict
fortress test is what eliminated Home Depot last run. Softening it to
"survives-a-bad-cycle" would have kept HD in play — and would also have needed a
carve-out to still catch Citi. The strict version has now paid for itself once.

**2. The Nikkei episode validates the earnings-currency rule.** Measured against the
US 30-yr the 1989 verdict is the same, but the framework's own rule — anchor to the
sovereign of the earnings currency — produced the right answer for the right reason
(1.7% yield vs 5.7% JGB), exactly as it did for Nintendo/Sony last week.

**3. Microsoft, both directions.** The same company, the same moat: 70× in 1999 →
FAIL → dead decade; 9.7× in 2011 → PASS → +900%. The framework is a PRICE discipline
wrapped around quality, not a list of good companies — which is the corpus's whole
point ("Time is the friend of the wonderful business" earned MSFT nothing from 1999's
price).

**4. The by-design misses now have a shape.** GEICO '76 (no earnings, turnaround),
AMZN '05 (earnings deferred by reinvestment), NVDA '23 (future priced far beyond
demonstrated OE). All three demand paying for states of the world not yet in the
filings. Notably, Buffett HIMSELF took the GEICO bet — the framework encodes
late-Buffett discipline, not early-Buffett special situations. That is a design
choice inherited from the corpus's own evolution [E1-15 → E2-16], and its cost is
real: the framework will never own the next GEICO '76 or AMZN. Its compensation is
that it also never owns the next Citi '07 at 13× or Avon at 65×.
